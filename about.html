About the Open Developer's Field Guide

University of Illinois
Chicago

The Open Developer's Field Guide is a catalog of open-source hardware. It covers microcontrollers, dev kits, single-board computers, AI boards, robots, cameras, audio boards, wearables, and radios. Its goal is discovery. Tinkerers, makers, students, and teachers can browse it for ideas and then find the right gear.

Naturalists carry field guides to name the birds and plants they spot. This guide does the same for gadgets in the wild. Each entry is one gadget. It shows how to spot it, where it lives and what it can do.

What makes it into the guide

The guide lists only gear with something open. Some entries share full board plans. Others share open coding tools. A gadget with nothing open stays out. Each entry carries one of three openness levels. The open source page explains them.

The guide does not list every sensor and add-on part. It focuses on the brains and kits at the center of a project. The project recipes name the common add-on parts in plain words.

Prices and stores

Each entry lists boards, stores, and prices. We checked each listing once on the date shown. We do not track prices over time. Prices are estimates and can change at any time. Price and availability vary by vendor. The guide earns nothing from any store and has no sponsors.

How we built it

The guide is an AI-assisted experiment. AI research agents read official pages, datasheets, design files, and store listings. Each agent wrote a research file with its sources. Python scripts then checked every file against strict rules. Rules set the openness level, price band, US availability, and scores. A scoring rubric set ease of use and the code you need. AI agents then wrote the plain-language pages from the research files only. A style checker reviewed every sentence.

The methods page explains each step and every score.

Why an AI-assisted experiment?

New boards appear every week. A small team cannot track them all by hand. AI agents can gather and sort the facts quickly. Python checks keep the agents honest. The result is a large, current catalog that people can still check line by line. Every page lists its sources.

AI can make mistakes. Check details with each maker and store before you buy. Tell us about errors so we can fix them.

Who made it

Jeremy Riel, PhD, made this guide at the TRAILblazer Lab at the University of Illinois Chicago. It is a companion project to Homegrown AI, a guide to open AI tools for schools.

Keeping it fresh

The guide is built to grow. Scripts check open board lists, such as the CircuitPython, MicroPython, and Meshtastic hardware lists, for new arrivals. New gadgets join the guide after research and review. Existing entries can be refreshed one at a time.
