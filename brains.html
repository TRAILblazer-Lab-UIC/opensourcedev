Brains: Microcontrollers and Small Computers

Every gadget needs a brain. The brain reads the world, makes choices, and controls things. Tinkerers mostly use two kinds of brains. Both come on small circuit boards about the size of a credit card or smaller.

The microcontroller

A microcontroller is a tiny computer on one chip. It runs a single program over and over. It starts the moment it gets power. It sips very little energy. Some can run for months on a small battery.

Picture a kitchen timer or a TV remote. Each has a microcontroller inside. It does one job well and never needs updates.

Microcontrollers are great for these jobs:

  • Reading sensors, such as temperature or light.
  • Switching lights, buzzers, and motors.
  • Running on batteries for a long time.
  • Reacting fast, within a thousandth of a second.

Popular microcontroller boards include the Raspberry Pi Pico, the Arduino family, and boards built on ESP32 chips. Most cost between $5 and $30.

The single-board computer

A single-board computer is a full computer on one board. It runs an operating system such as Linux. It can use a keyboard, a mouse, and a monitor. It can browse the web, run Python, and stream video.

Picture a small laptop with the screen and keyboard removed. The Raspberry Pi 5 is the best known example.

Single-board computers are great for these jobs:

  • Handling cameras and video.
  • Running websites and databases.
  • Running AI models that recognize faces, objects, or speech.
  • Doing many jobs at once.

They need more power. Most must shut down properly before you unplug them. They cost more, often $35 to $250.

AI boards

AI boards are single-board computers with an extra chip for AI math. That chip is often called an NPU or a GPU. It makes AI tasks much faster. The NVIDIA Jetson and the BeagleY-AI are examples. Some microcontrollers now include small AI helpers too.

Many projects use both. A small computer does the thinking. A microcontroller handles the motors and sensors.

Capacity in this guide

Every entry gets a capacity score from 0 to 100. The score blends processor speed, memory, storage, pins, radios, and AI chips. A low score means the board handles one simple job at a time. A high score means it can run AI on live video. The score helps you compare boards at a glance. Pick the board that fits your project. A bigger score always costs more power and money.
