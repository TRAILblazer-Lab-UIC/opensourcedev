Plug-and-Play Connectors

Wiring sensors by hand can feel fiddly. Plug-in connector systems fix that. One small cable carries power and data. You click it in and start coding. No soldering is needed.

The main families

NameWho makes itWhat it looks like
QwiicSparkFunA tiny 4-pin plug with a black cable
STEMMA QTAdafruitThe same tiny plug as Qwiic
GroveSeeed StudioA wider 4-pin plug, often white

Qwiic and STEMMA QT parts work together. They use the same plug and the same signals. Grove uses a bigger plug. Adapter cables connect Grove parts to Qwiic boards.

How one cable runs many sensors

These systems use a shared data line called I2C. Each sensor has its own address, a bit like a house number. The brain calls each sensor by its address. That means you can chain several sensors on one cable. Plug a temperature sensor into the board. Then plug a light sensor into the temperature sensor. Both work.

Other common ways to connect

  • Header pins are rows of metal posts. Jumper wires slide onto them.
  • A breadboard holds parts and wires with no solder.
  • Screw terminals clamp bare wires for motors and power.
  • Hats, capes, FeatherWings and shields are add-on boards. They stack right on top of a brain.

Tips for beginners

  • Look for a Qwiic, STEMMA QT or Grove port on the board you buy. It makes the first projects much easier.
  • Check the voltage. Most modern boards use 3.3 volts. Some older sensors expect 5 volts.
  • Buy a starter pack of sensors that all share one plug style.
