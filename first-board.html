Choosing Your First Board

There is no single best board. The right board fits your idea, your budget, and your comfort with code. Answer the five questions below. Then use the filters on the field guide to see matching boards.

1. What do you want to make?

Start with the project. The board comes second.

  • Lights, sensors, buttons, and small motors fit any microcontroller.
  • A gadget that talks to your phone needs Bluetooth.
  • A gadget that sends data online needs Wi-Fi.
  • A sensor far from any building may need LoRa.
  • Cameras, voice, and AI usually need a single-board computer or an AI board.

2. How much code do you know?

  • None yet? Pick a board marked "Newcomer friendly". Many work with blocks or ready-made firmware.
  • A little Python? Pick a board with MicroPython or CircuitPython.
  • Comfortable with code? Any board is open to you.

3. What is your budget?

A board alone is only part of the cost. Plan for a USB cable, a power supply, and a few parts. Each entry shows a starter cost estimate. Many great first boards cost under $15.

4. Can you buy it nearby?

Each entry shows how easy it is to buy in the US. Boards sold by several US stores arrive fast and are easy to return. Imported boards can be cheaper but slower.

5. Will you find help?

Big communities answer questions fast. They also share thousands of example projects. Each entry rates its community size and its docs.
