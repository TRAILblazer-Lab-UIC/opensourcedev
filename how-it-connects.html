How a Project Fits Together

Almost every gadget has five parts. Learn these five and you can read nearly any project.

1. Power

Power is the food for your gadget. Most boards get power from a USB cable. Portable projects use batteries. Big motors need their own larger power supply.

2. The brain

The brain is the board that runs your code. It reads the inputs and decides what to do. Then it tells the outputs to act. The Brains page explains the two main kinds.

3. Inputs

Inputs are the senses of your gadget. They tell the brain about the world.

  • Buttons and switches notice a press.
  • Temperature sensors feel heat and cold.
  • Light sensors see brightness.
  • Motion sensors notice movement or tilt.
  • Microphones hear sound.
  • Cameras see pictures.

4. Outputs

Outputs are how your gadget acts on the world.

  • LEDs and light strips glow in any color.
  • Screens show words and pictures.
  • Speakers and buzzers make sound.
  • Motors spin wheels and fans.
  • Servo motors turn to an exact angle.
  • Relays switch big devices on and off.

5. Connections

Connections let the gadget talk to other gadgets or to you. A USB cable is the simplest. Radios such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and LoRa work with no wires. The Radios page explains each one.

How the parts link up

The brain has rows of metal pins along its edges. Each pin can read an input or control an output. Wires link the pins to sensors and other parts.

Beginners often use a breadboard. A breadboard is a plastic board full of holes. You push wires and parts into the holes. Nothing needs solder. You can pull everything apart and try again.

Plug-in connectors make this even easier. One small cable carries power and data together. The connectors page shows how they work.

A worked example: a plant helper

PartWhat you use
PowerA USB phone charger
BrainA small Wi-Fi microcontroller board
InputA soil moisture sensor in the pot
OutputA small water pump and a green LED
ConnectionWi-Fi sends a message to your phone

The code checks the soil every hour. Dry soil turns on the pump for a few seconds. The LED glows green once the soil is damp. A message lets you know your plant had a drink.
