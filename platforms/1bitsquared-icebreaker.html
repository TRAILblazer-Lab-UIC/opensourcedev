Overview

The iCEBreaker is a small board built around a Lattice iCE40 FPGA. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. The board was made for classes and workshops, and it works with fully open design tools. It suits students and hobbyists who want to learn Verilog, Amaranth or how processors work inside.

Specs in plain words

  • 5280 logic cells is room for a small RISC-V processor plus a few extra circuits.
  • 16 MB of flash holds your design and a lot of extra data, about the space of a few thousand photos from an old phone.
  • 8 MB of fast memory gives a soft processor room for bigger programs.
  • Three Pmod ports take plug-in add-ons such as displays, buttons and video outputs.
  • A USB-C port handles power, uploads and a serial link to your computer.

clock_mhz is the 12 MHz crystal shared with the FPGA. The FPGA also has 48 MHz and 10 kHz internal oscillators and a PLL. ram_kb is the 1 Mbit single-port RAM. The FPGA also has 120 Kbit of block RAM. psram_kb is the 64 Mbit PSRAM mounted by default on V1.1. V1.0 boards had no PSRAM fitted. gpio is the 39 I/O-capable FPGA pins in the README, including 24 Pmod pins. The UART and JTAG-style programming go through the FT2232H. The FPGA has two hard SPI and two hard I2C blocks. Other interfaces are soft logic.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink your first LED

    Write a tiny Verilog design that blinks the RGB LED. It shows how hardware design differs from normal code.

  • Button and LED logic lab

    Wire the snap-off buttons to the LEDs through logic gates you design. Students can build AND, OR and XOR gates by hand.

  • Seven segment counter

    Count button presses on a two-digit number display. It is a classic first step into counters and timers.

    Add: seven segment display add-on.

Builder ideas

  • Reaction time game

    Light a random LED and time how fast a player presses the matching button. Show the best score on the LEDs.

  • Hello over the serial port

    Build a simple serial sender in Verilog. Watch your message appear in a terminal on your computer.

  • Tone and melody maker

    Make square wave tones and play a short tune through a small speaker. Each button can play a different note.

    Add: small speaker, small amplifier board.

  • Frequency counter for science class

    Measure how fast a signal flips on and off. Test a buzzer, a fan sensor or a signal generator.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Servo sweep controller

    Make a precise pulse signal that swings a servo back and forth. Use it to wave a flag or point a little sign.

    Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Video test pattern on a monitor

    Draw color bars and moving shapes on a monitor through a video add-on. It teaches how screens are drawn line by line.

    Add: DVI video add-on, monitor.

  • Pong on a big screen

    Build the classic Pong game fully in logic and play it with the board buttons. No processor runs the game.

    Add: DVI video add-on, monitor.

  • LED panel art wall

    Drive a grid of color LEDs with patterns and scrolling text. Hang it in a hallway or maker space.

    Add: LED matrix panel, LED panel driver add-on, 5 V power supply.

  • Build a RISC-V computer

    Load the PicoRV32 soft core and turn the FPGA into a tiny computer. Then run small C programs on a chip you assembled.

  • Weather station on a soft core

    Read a temperature and humidity sensor through the I2C pins. Send the readings to your computer every minute.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

How open is it?

Open Board

KiCad schematics and board layouts are published for the V1.0 boards and the Bitsy. The current V1.1 board has only its schematic online, and the repo names no license. The design tools are open, with Project IceStorm under the ISC license, but the iCE40 chip itself is closed.
