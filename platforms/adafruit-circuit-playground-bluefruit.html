Overview

Circuit Playground Bluefruit is a round learning board from Adafruit with Bluetooth built in. It packs ten color lights, a motion sensor, temperature and light sensors, a microphone, a speaker and two buttons. It suits students, teachers and crafters who want quick results with no soldering. Bluetooth lets it talk to phones and tablets for wireless projects.

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi or internet projects, as it has Bluetooth only
  • Projects that need many pins, as it has just eight pads
  • Camera or screen-heavy projects without an add-on

Specs in plain words

  • A 64 MHz chip runs light shows, sensor reading and Bluetooth at once with ease.
  • 256 KB of memory is plenty for CircuitPython programs that read sensors and blink lights.
  • 2 MB of extra storage holds your code and libraries like files on a USB stick.
  • Bluetooth Low Energy talks to phones and tablets across a room while using little power.
  • Eight big pads take alligator clips or conductive thread. Seven of them sense touch.
  • The board is about 5 cm across and weighs less than a few coins.

Clock, flash and RAM are the nRF52840 values given by Make's board guide. PlatformIO reports 796 KB flash and 243 KB RAM usable after the bootloader and SoftDevice. A separate 2 MB SPI flash holds CircuitPython files. gpio counts the 8 pads. A1 to A6 read analog with a 12-bit ADC. A1 to A6 and TX are capacitive touch. The Audio pad is the speaker output. I2C is on A4 and A5, and UART is on A6 and TX. The imu entry is the LIS3DH 3-axis accelerometer. The 3.3 V regulator gives about 500 mA for the whole board. Pin D35 turns off the NeoPixels, mic and light and temperature sensors to save power. The accelerometer stays on. Weight is 8.9 g.

Starter ideas

  • Shake to roll dice

    Shake the board and the lights show a random number. Use it for any board game at home.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Clap-on light ring

    Clap your hands and the ten lights turn on. Clap again to turn them off.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Banana piano

    Clip fruit to the touch pads with alligator wires. Each banana plays a different note on the speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: alligator clip wires, fruit.

  • Night light that wakes up

    The light sensor checks the room. The lights glow softly as the room gets dark.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Phone controlled mood lamp

    Pick a color in a free phone app. The ring of lights changes to match over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Sound meter for the library

    The lights turn from green to red as the room gets loud. Kids learn to keep their voices low.

    Uses: Hears sound.

Builder ideas

  • Glowing costume badge

    Sew the board onto a costume with conductive thread. Run sparkly light patterns from a small battery pack.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: conductive thread, battery pack.

  • Bike turn signal glove

    Tilt your wrist and the lights flash left or right. Wear it on evening rides.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: glove, battery pack.

  • Classroom temperature logger

    Read the temperature every minute and send it to a phone. Students can graph how the room warms up.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push two nails into a pot as a simple moisture probe. The board beeps and glows red when the soil is dry.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: alligator clip wires, two nails.

  • USB slide clicker

    Press button A or B to move slides on a laptop. The board acts as a tiny keyboard over USB.

  • Step counter bracelet

    Count steps with the motion sensor and show progress on the lights. Send the daily total to a phone.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: wristband, small rechargeable battery.

  • Touch pad music maker

    Turn the seven touch pads into drum keys. Send the notes over USB to music software on a computer.

    Add: alligator clip wires, foil or copper tape.

  • Science fair light lab

    Measure light from different bulbs or windows. Compare the readings on a phone chart.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

Expert ideas

  • Wireless game controller

    Tilt the board to steer a game on a phone or tablet. The buttons fire or jump.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Two board high five

    Program two boards to find each other over Bluetooth. Both light up and cheer when friends meet.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Makes sound. Add: second board.

How open is it?

Open Board

Adafruit shares the full design files under the CC BY-SA 3.0 license. The board is certified open hardware as US000622. CircuitPython is open under the MIT license, but the Bluetooth stack runs on a closed Nordic binary. The nRF52840 chip design stays closed.
