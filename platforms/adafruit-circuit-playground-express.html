Overview

Circuit Playground Express is a round learning board from Adafruit. It has ten color lights, sensors, a microphone and a speaker all on one board. Kids and adults can code it with blocks in a web browser. Teachers love it for first coding lessons, wearables and sound and light projects.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding lessons with no wiring at all
  • Wearables and light-up costumes
  • Sound and music projects
  • Simple science experiments with built-in sensors
  • Classrooms with Chromebooks or shared computers

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth projects, as the board has no radio
  • Projects with many wires, as it has only eight pads
  • Breadboard circuits, as the round shape does not plug in
  • Big programs, as the memory is small

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 32 KB of working memory is small. Short programs fit fine.
  • 2 MB of extra storage holds CircuitPython code and a few sound clips.
  • Ten color lights can show numbers, moods and patterns.
  • Eight big pads take alligator clips, and seven of them sense touch.
  • The board is about 5 cm across, close to the size of a cookie.

The SAMD21G18 has 256 KB internal flash and 32 KB RAM, per Adafruit's SAMD21G18 product text. A separate 2 MB SPI flash chip holds CircuitPython code and libraries. gpio counts the 8 alligator-clip pads A0 to A7. All 8 read analog, A1 to A7 are capacitive touch and A0 is the DAC output. I2C is on A4 and A5 and UART is on A6 and A7. The imu entry is the LIS3DH 3-axis accelerometer. The infrared transmitter and receiver have no matching vocabulary value.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Shake to roll dice

    Shake the board and the lights show a random number. Use it for any board game at home.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Clap-on light ring

    Clap your hands and the ten lights turn on. Clap again to turn them off.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Banana piano

    Clip fruit to the touch pads with alligator wires. Each banana plays a different note.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: alligator clip wires, fruit.

  • Night light that wakes up

    The light sensor checks the room. The lights glow softly as the room gets dark.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Classroom noise meter

    The microphone listens to the room. The light ring turns from green to red as the room gets loud.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Hot potato game

    Pass the board around while it plays a tune. The player holding it as the music stops is out.

    Uses: Makes sound, Sensors built in. Add: battery pack.

Builder ideas

  • Light-up costume

    Sew the board onto a hat or shirt with conductive thread. The lights sparkle as you dance.

    Uses: Battery ready, Sensors built in. Add: battery pack, conductive thread, LED strip.

  • Bike turn signal glove

    Tilt your wrist to flash the lights left or right. Riders can show where they are going.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: glove, battery pack.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Clip a soil moisture sensor to an analog pad. The board beeps once the soil gets too dry.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, alligator clip wires.

  • Step counter

    Wear the board on your shoe and count steps with the motion sensor. The lights fill up as you walk more.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: battery pack, strap.

  • Temperature science log

    Record the room temperature every few minutes. Students graph how a room warms through the day.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Custom computer keyboard

    Turn the touch pads into keys for a computer. Make a game controller out of foil and cardboard.

    Add: alligator clip wires, aluminum foil, cardboard.

  • Spinning color wheel art

    Mount the board on a small motor and spin it. The lights paint glowing circles in a dark room.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: small motor, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • MIDI music controller

    Send notes to music software on a computer over USB. Tilt the board to bend the sound.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Simple robot with motors

    Add a motor add-on board to drive wheels and servos. The robot rolls toward a light.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: motor add-on board, small motors, servo motor, battery pack.
