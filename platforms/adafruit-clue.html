Overview

The Adafruit CLUE is a small learning board shaped like a BBC micro:bit. It adds a bright color screen and a big set of sensors on the front. Bluetooth lets it talk to phones and tablets. It suits students, teachers, and makers who want quick science projects in CircuitPython.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classroom science with built-in sensors
  • Showing readings on its own color screen
  • Bluetooth projects that send data to a phone
  • Beginner coding in CircuitPython with the Mu editor
  • Robot kits built for the micro:bit edge connector

Not great for

  • MakeCode block coding, as Adafruit does not support it on this board
  • Wi-Fi or internet projects, as it has Bluetooth only
  • LiPo battery projects, as it has no built-in charger
  • Most micro:bit cases, as the CLUE is thicker and does not fit

Specs in plain words

  • A 64 MHz chip handles sensor reading, screen drawing, and Bluetooth at a calm pace.
  • 256 KB of working memory is enough for CircuitPython programs that read sensors and draw simple graphics.
  • 2 MB of extra storage holds your code and data logs like a small USB stick.
  • The 1.3 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp with sharp color.
  • The 9-DoF motion sensor feels tilt, spin, and compass direction.
  • The board measures about 5 by 4 centimeters and runs on USB or a 3 to 6 volt battery pack.

flash_kb is the nRF52840's internal 1 MB. The board adds 2 MB of QSPI flash for CircuitPython files. There is no LiPo charger on board, by design. As a result, alkaline and NiMH packs are safe. Eight analog pins are exposed (A0 to A7). The GPIO count on the micro:bit edge connector was not confirmed. SWD is on bottom pads only. Dimensions are 51.7 x 42.2 x 12.3 mm from the Adafruit page. The speaker is a buzzer. In January 2025 Adafruit swapped the discontinued LSM6DS33 for the LSM6DS3TR.

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Adafruit shares the full board design files under the CC BY-SA 3.0 license. OSHWA certified the CLUE as open hardware with the number US000255. CircuitPython is open under the MIT license, but the Arduino Bluetooth stack uses a closed Nordic binary. The nRF52840 chip design stays closed.
