Overview

The Adafruit Feather M0 RFM95 LoRa is a small board with a long-range radio on top. It can send short messages over two kilometers between your own devices in open country. It suits makers who want sensors far from Wi-Fi. A battery charger on the board makes it a good fit for outdoor nodes.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sending sensor readings across a farm, garden, or campus
  • Battery-powered nodes that report every few minutes
  • Joining The Things Network as a LoRaWAN sensor
  • Learning radio links with CircuitPython or Arduino
  • Studying open board files from an OSHWA certified design

Not great for

  • Video, sound, or any large data, as LoRa sends tiny packets slowly
  • Total beginners who do not want to solder an antenna
  • Projects needing many free pins, as the radio uses some of them
  • Ultra long battery life on LoRaWAN without some extra tuning

Specs in plain words

  • A 48 MHz chip with 32 KB of memory runs one simple job well, like reading a sensor and sending it.
  • 256 KB of storage holds your program with room for a radio library.
  • The radio sends up to +20 dBm, which Adafruit tested past two kilometers with plain wire antennas.
  • About 300 microamps in deep sleep lets a small battery last for weeks or months.
  • The board is about the size of a stick of gum and weighs less than two coins.
  • The 900 MHz model works on 868 or 915 MHz, and a twin model covers 433 MHz.

Specs from the Adafruit product page and learn guide. 20 GPIO, 8 PWM, 10 analog inputs and 1 analog output. Some Feather pins are used by the radio. As a result, fewer are free in practice. The 300 uA sleep figure is Adafruit's whole-board estimate. Weight 5.6 g in product data (5.8 g in the description text). No EEPROM and no extra SPI flash chip. Starting in August 2019, the board uses an FCC-certified LoRa module with a metal shield. The 433 MHz version (3179) is otherwise the same. The swd_jtag entry reflects the SWD pads on the SAMD21 and is not a separate debugger. No user button besides reset.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Backyard to kitchen messenger

    Put one board in the shed and one in the kitchen. Press a button outside and a light blinks indoors.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: second LoRa Feather, push button, LED.

  • Garden soil moisture reporter

    Push a soil sensor into a far garden bed. The board radios the reading to a second board by your window.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor, LiPo battery, second LoRa Feather.

  • Mailbox flag alert

    Fit a small switch inside the mailbox lid. You get a blink or a beep at the house each time the mail arrives.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, LiPo battery, second LoRa Feather, buzzer.

  • Classroom range test

    Carry one board across a field while the other stays put. Students map how far the signal travels and what blocks it.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: second LoRa Feather, LiPo battery.

Builder ideas

  • Weather station network

    Spread a few boards with temperature sensors around a yard or campus. A Raspberry Pi collects all the readings in one place.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, LiPo battery, Raspberry Pi with LoRa add-on.

  • Things Network sensor node

    Join the public LoRaWAN network with the LMIC library. Your readings show up online through any nearby community gateway.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: short jumper wire, temperature sensor.

  • Beehive weight logger

    Place a scale under a hive and send its weight each hour. Watch honey stores grow over the season.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, LiPo battery, small solar panel.

  • Water trough level check

    Mount a distance sensor above a farm trough. A message tells you to refill it before the animals run dry.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor, weatherproof box, LiPo battery.

  • Hiking group text pager

    Give each friend a board with a tiny screen. Send short typed notes across a campsite with no phone signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: small OLED display, LiPo battery, push buttons.

  • Wildlife trail motion counter

    Hide a motion sensor near a trail. Count passing animals each night and radio the total home.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: PIR motion sensor, weatherproof box, LiPo battery.

  • Greenhouse frost warning

    Send the greenhouse temperature to the house every few minutes. A buzzer sounds on cold nights.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: temperature sensor, buzzer, second LoRa Feather.

  • Remote art light trigger

    Start a light show across a park from a single button. The radio signal reaches far past Wi-Fi range.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: LED strip, push button, power bank.

Expert ideas

  • Small mesh of relay nodes

    Set boards along a long path and let each one pass messages on to the next. Reach a spot too far for a single hop.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: several LoRa Feathers, LiPo batteries.

  • Deep sleep battery study

    Tune sleep times and measure how long a cell lasts. Aim for months of readings on one charge.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: LiPo battery, USB power meter.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The schematic and board files are on GitHub under a Creative Commons share-alike license. The board holds OSHWA certification US000604. The Arduino core, CircuitPython, and the radio libraries are open source under LGPL and MIT licenses. The main chip and the radio module stay closed.
