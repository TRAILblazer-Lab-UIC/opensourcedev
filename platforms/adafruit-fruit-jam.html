Overview

Fruit Jam is a tiny computer from Adafruit about the size of a credit card. It uses the Raspberry Pi RP2350B chip with extra memory, video out, USB ports for a keyboard and mouse, sound and Wi-Fi. You connect a monitor and keyboard and code it in CircuitPython, Arduino or C. It suits hobbyists, teachers and retro computer fans who want a full little computer in one board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Retro computer emulators such as a classic Mac or Apple IIe
  • Games and art on a real monitor
  • Music toys with headphones or a small speaker
  • Learning to code on a screen of your own

Not great for

  • Battery projects, as it runs from USB power
  • Sharp modern video, as output tops out at 720p
  • Quick setups, as you need a monitor, cable and keyboard too
  • Wireless projects with Bluetooth, as Adafruit only documents Wi-Fi

Specs in plain words

  • Two processor cores at 150 MHz can run a game and play music at the same time.
  • 8 MB of extra memory gives emulators room to hold an old computer's whole memory.
  • 16 MB of storage holds your code plus plenty of images and sounds.
  • The video port sends a picture to a monitor at up to 1280 by 720 dots.
  • Two USB-A ports take a keyboard and mouse like a desktop computer.
  • A headphone jack and speaker port play sound, and a mini speaker comes in the box.

ram_kb is the RP2350 on-chip SRAM. Adafruit says the main SRAM can serve as the DVI frame buffer while the PSRAM holds emulator data. gpio counts the 10 A/D GPIO on the 2x16 socket header. The pinout page lists about 44 RP2350B GPIO numbers in use across the board. adc_channels counts A0 to A5. A0 is only on the 3-pin JST connector. The video port is DVI from HSTX GPIO12 to GPIO19. Adafruit calls it a DVI port and the kit pairs it with an HDMI cable. As a result, it is recorded as hdmi. Notebookcheck says output tops out at 1280 x 720. The ESP32-C6 hardware also has Bluetooth LE and 802.15.4. Adafruit documents it only as a Wi-Fi coprocessor through the esp32spi library. As a result, only wifi is recorded. The IR receiver shares GPIO29 with a red LED. The 3 V and 5 V rails each give about 500 mA peak. A mini speaker ships in the box and plugs into the JST-SH speaker port. It is not mounted on the board. Size is the ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 card size, 3.375 x 2.125 inches.

How open is it?

Open Board

Adafruit shares the full schematic and board files on GitHub under the CC BY-SA 3.0 license. OSHWA certified the board as open hardware with ID US002765. CircuitPython, the Pico SDK and the Arduino core are open, but the Wi-Fi radio uses closed Espressif libraries and the RP2350 chip design is closed.
