Overview

PyBadge is a handheld game board from Adafruit about the size of a credit card. It has a small color screen, eight game buttons, five color lights, a speaker, a light sensor, and a motion sensor. It runs Microsoft MakeCode Arcade, CircuitPython, and Arduino. It suits kids, teachers, and anyone who wants to make their own games or a fun name badge.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Making first video games with drag-and-drop blocks
  • Wearable name badges at events
  • Classroom lessons with motion and light sensors
  • Portable gadgets running on a small battery

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio
  • Big or fast games, as the screen and chip are small
  • Plug-in sensors with the tiny STEMMA QT plug, as they need an adapter cable

Specs in plain words

  • A 120 MHz chip is plenty for simple retro games and sensor reading.
  • 192 KB of working memory holds a small game and its sprites at one time.
  • 2 MB of extra storage holds images, sounds, and game files.
  • The 1.8 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp pair, with 160 by 128 dots.
  • A battery port with a built-in charger lets it run unplugged.

flash_kb is the chip's internal flash. A separate 2 MB QSPI flash holds CircuitPython files and game assets. The total GPIO and ADC counts were not stated. The Feather headers break out the standard Feather pins. D2 and D3 on JST ports work as digital or analog. A6 reads battery voltage and A7 reads the light sensor. The three STEMMA ports are 2 mm JST PH, two 3-pin and one 4-pin I2C. Adafruit says the I2C port fits Grove sensors. The buttons connect through an 8-channel shift register. It is not straight to GPIO. SWD is an unpopulated 10-pin footprint. The imu entry is the LIS3DH 3-axis accelerometer. Weight is 29.3 g. The PyBadge LC drops the accelerometer, STEMMA ports, Feather headers and speaker connector and has 1 NeoPixel.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Your first arcade game

    Build a simple dodge game with MakeCode Arcade blocks in your browser. Drag the file onto the badge and play it anywhere.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Glowing name badge

    Show your name and a picture on the screen with the lights glowing below. Wear it at a meetup or school event.

    Uses: Screen. Add: lanyard.

  • Tilt maze game

    Roll a ball through a maze by tilting the badge. The motion sensor reads each tilt.

    Uses: Screen, Sensors built in.

  • Pocket music maker

    Turn each button into a musical note and play tunes on the speaker. Kids can compose short songs.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Night light that wakes up

    Turn on the color lights as the room gets dark. Set the glow color with the buttons.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • Step and shake counter

    Count shakes or steps with the motion sensor and show the total on screen. It makes a fun gym class challenge.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small rechargeable battery.

  • Light meter for science class

    Read the light sensor and draw a live graph of brightness. Compare a sunny window to a dark closet.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Plant watering reminder

    Plug a soil moisture sensor into a side port and show a sad face for dry soil. A beep reminds you to water.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, plug-in sensor cable.

  • Flash card study buddy

    Show a word or math problem and reveal the answer with a button press. It fits nicely in a backpack.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: small rechargeable battery.

  • Reaction time tester

    Light the screen at a random moment and time how fast a player presses a button. Keep a list of top scores.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Pocket weather station

    Add a temperature and humidity sensor and show the readings on the screen. Take it on a nature walk.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, plug-in sensor cable, small rechargeable battery.

Expert ideas

  • Platform game with your own art

    Draw your own sprites and levels and code the game in CircuitPython or Arduino. Share it with friends on their badges.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

How open is it?

Wild Open

Adafruit shares the schematic and board files on GitHub under the CC BY-SA 3.0 license. OSHWA certified the board as open hardware with ID US000249. CircuitPython, MakeCode Arcade, and the Arduino core are all open source, but the ATSAMD51 chip design is closed.
