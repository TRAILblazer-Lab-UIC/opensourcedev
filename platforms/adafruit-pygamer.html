Overview

PyGamer is a pocket game board from Adafruit and the bigger sibling of the PyBadge. It has a small color screen, an analog thumbstick, four buttons, five color lights, a motion sensor, and a light sensor. It adds a memory card slot and a headphone jack. It runs Microsoft MakeCode Arcade, CircuitPython, and Arduino, and it suits kids and makers who want to build and play their own games.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Making video games with drag-and-drop blocks
  • Games that use a thumbstick and motion controls
  • Music and sound projects with headphones
  • Portable gadgets running on a small battery

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio
  • Unboxing and playing at once, as button caps, a speaker, and a battery are sold separately
  • Fast or detailed games, as the chip and screen are small
  • Easy buying, as most stores were out of stock on the check date

Specs in plain words

  • A 120 MHz chip is plenty for simple retro games and sensor reading.
  • 192 KB of working memory holds a small game and its sprites at one time.
  • 8 MB of extra storage plus a memory card slot holds lots of games, pictures and songs.
  • The 1.8 inch screen has 160 by 128 dots, about the size of a large stamp.
  • A stereo headphone jack and a speaker port play sound, and the speaker mutes when headphones go in.
  • A battery port with a built-in charger lets it run unplugged.

flash_kb is the chip's internal flash. A separate 8 MB QSPI flash holds CircuitPython files and game assets. The total GPIO and ADC counts were not stated. Named analog pins run A6 to A11. A6 is battery sense, A7 the light sensor, A8 and A9 the D2 and D3 JST ports, and A10 and A11 the joystick. The three STEMMA ports are 2 mm JST PH, two 3-pin and one 4-pin I2C that fits Grove sensors. The buttons connect through a shift register. It is not straight to GPIO. No speaker is mounted on the board. A Molex PicoBlade port drives an external 4 to 8 ohm speaker. The headphone jack mutes the speaker when plugged in. The imu entry is the LIS3DH 3-axis accelerometer. The size includes the thumbstick height. Weight is 37.5 g. The Learn pinout page does not mention SWD pads.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Your first arcade game

    Build a simple chase game with MakeCode Arcade blocks in your browser. Drag the file onto the PyGamer and steer with the thumbstick.

    Uses: Screen. Add: button caps.

  • Tilt racing game

    Steer a race car by tilting the handheld. The motion sensor turns every tilt into a turn.

    Uses: Screen, Sensors built in. Add: button caps.

  • Drawing pad with the thumbstick

    Move a cursor with the thumbstick and paint pixel art in bright colors. Save each picture to the memory card.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Night light that wakes up

    Turn on the color lights as the room gets dark. Choose colors with the buttons.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Reaction time challenge

    Flash the screen at random and time each player's button press. Keep a top score list on the memory card.

    Uses: Screen. Add: button caps, microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Pocket music player

    Play songs from a memory card through headphones. Pick tracks with the thumbstick.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen. Add: microSD card, headphones.

  • Field notes data logger

    Read a temperature sensor on a nature walk and save the readings to the memory card. Graph them back at school.

    Uses: Battery ready, Screen. Add: temperature sensor, plug-in sensor cable, small rechargeable battery, microSD card.

  • Garden moisture checker

    Poke a soil moisture sensor into each pot and see the reading on screen. A sound plays for dry soil.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, plug-in sensor cable, small speaker.

  • Light level science lab

    Graph the light sensor live as clouds pass or lamps switch on. Students can compare rooms and times of day.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Handheld balance game for health class

    Hold the handheld level while walking a line. The motion sensor scores how steady you stay.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen, Battery ready. Add: small rechargeable battery.

Expert ideas

  • Spoken word helper

    Train a tiny speech model to spot a couple of words. Adafruit has a guide for this kind of TinyML demo.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microphone add-on.

  • Retro style platform game

    Draw your own sprites and levels and code a side scroller in CircuitPython or Arduino. Add music for the headphone jack.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: button caps, headphones.

  • Simple retro emulator

    Run a light emulator of an old game system on the small screen. Adafruit says it can handle a little retro emulation.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: button caps, microSD card, headphones.
