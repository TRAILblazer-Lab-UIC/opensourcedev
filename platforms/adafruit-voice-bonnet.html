Overview

The Voice Bonnet is a small add-on board for the Raspberry Pi. It gives the Pi two microphones, two speaker outputs, and a headphone jack. It suits makers who want to build voice assistants, talking gadgets or speech projects. Adafruit no longer stocks it and points buyers to the BrainCraft HAT. A few resellers still list it, but most show it out of stock.

Specs in plain words

  • The WM8960 sound chip turns sound into numbers and back again in stereo.
  • Two 1 watt speaker outputs can fill a small room with clear speech.
  • Two microphones on the left and right can hint at where a sound comes from.
  • A privacy switch cuts the audio for times you want the microphones off.
  • The board is about 65 by 33 millimeters. That is about the size of a Raspberry Pi Zero.
  • A small plug-in port takes add-on sensors without soldering.

Size65.2 x 33.3 x 11.0 mm

The bonnet has no MCU, memory or ADC of its own. The WM8960 codec handles the stereo ADC and DAC. Pins used on the Pi are I2S on GPIO 18, 19, 20 and 21, I2C on SDA and SCL, the button on GPIO 17, DotStar data and clock on GPIO 5 and 6, and the JST STEMMA signal on GPIO 12. The codec shows up at I2C address 0x1A per the audio setup page. Speakers attach to two JST 2-PH ports. 5 V feeds the audio amp. Weight is 6.0 g. The PCB silkscreen was updated on December 15, 2023.
