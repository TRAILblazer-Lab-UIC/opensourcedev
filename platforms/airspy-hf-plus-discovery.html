Overview

The Airspy HF+ Discovery is a small software defined radio that only receives. It plugs into a computer by USB and lets free apps tune shortwave, AM, FM, and airband signals. It suits radio listeners who want very clear reception on a budget. Reviewers praise its low noise and its ability to handle strong signals.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Shortwave and AM listening from around the world
  • Airband, FM, and weather radio on the VHF band
  • Receiving weather satellite pictures near 137 MHz
  • Sharing one receiver online with SpyServer
  • Learning radio with no risk of breaking transmit rules

Not great for

  • Sending any signal, as it cannot transmit at all
  • Watching a wide slice of the airwaves, as it shows about 660 kHz at once
  • Frequencies above 260 MHz such as airplane position signals
  • People who want open hardware or custom firmware

Specs in plain words

  • It covers 0.5 kHz to 31 MHz and 60 to 260 MHz, which spans shortwave, AM, FM, and airband.
  • A window of about 660 kHz shows dozens of AM stations side by side on screen.
  • A sensitivity near -140 dBm means it can hear extremely faint stations.
  • Built-in filters cut out strong nearby stations that would drown weaker ones.
  • The box is about the size of a deck of cards and weighs about as much as a few coins.
  • Your computer needs at least a 1 GHz processor, 1 GB of memory and USB 2.0.

Specs from the official product page. HF 0.5 kHz to 31 MHz. VHF 64 to 260 MHz in the page spec table (60 to 260 MHz in the airspy.us listing and reviews), with 118 to 260 MHz on a second tuner path with reduced performance. Sensitivity -141.5 dBm in FM broadcast and -141.0 dBm in VHF aviation. BDR 95 dB on VHF. Max input +10 dBm. Operating range -45 to 85 C. The rtl-sdr.com preview reports a light plastic case at 28 g with a fully shielded PCB. iTead lists 105.6 g and 75 x 45 x 10 mm, which likely includes packaging. The chips are not named in official material. The antenna jack type (likely SMA) and USB connector type were not confirmed. As a result, connectors is empty. radios lists sub_ghz only because the vocab has no general SDR value. Receive only. Host needs a 1 GHz CPU, 1 GB RAM and USB 2.0 high speed.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Listen to world radio

    Tune into shortwave stations from other countries on your computer. Hear news and music from across the globe.

    Add: wire or loop antenna.

  • Mediumwave night hunt

    Scan the AM band after dark for faraway stations. Keep a log of every city you hear.

    Add: loop antenna.

  • Airband listening

    Listen to public air traffic voice channels near your local airport. Follow a plane from approach to landing.

    Add: VHF antenna.

  • Hear the time signals

    Pick up the shortwave time stations that tick off each second. Use them to check your clocks.

    Add: wire antenna.

  • Weather radio receiver

    Listen to public weather broadcasts on the VHF band. Keep track of storm warnings for your area.

    Add: VHF antenna.

  • Hunt for noisy gadgets

    Tune to an empty shortwave frequency and switch devices on and off. Find the charger or light that adds buzz.

    Add: loop antenna.

Builder ideas

  • Weather satellite pictures

    Receive images from polar weather satellites near 137 MHz. Free decoding software turns the tones into cloud maps.

    Add: satellite antenna.

  • Ham radio listening post

    Listen to amateur radio operators talking across the world. Learn the bands before you study for a license.

    Add: wire antenna.

  • Watch the ionosphere change

    Record signal strength from a distant station each day. Students graph how sunrise and sunset change radio reach.

    Add: wire antenna.

  • Share a receiver online

    Run SpyServer on a Raspberry Pi in a quiet spot. Friends tune in from their own computers.

    Add: Raspberry Pi, outdoor antenna.

  • Decode digital shortwave modes

    Feed the audio into free decoders for weather fax or text modes. Watch messages appear on your screen.

    Add: wire antenna.

  • Antenna comparison lab

    Try different homemade antennas on the same station. Measure which design hears best at your home.

    Add: homemade antennas, antenna adapters.

Expert ideas

  • Very low frequency nature sounds

    Listen at the very bottom of its range for crackles from lightning far away. Record the sounds for a science project.

    Add: long wire antenna, ground rod.

  • Write your own receiver app

    Use the open libairspyhf library in C to grab raw radio samples. Build a custom display or logger.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The host library libairspyhf and its tools are open source on GitHub under BSD-3-Clause, with some GPL-2.0 files. The schematics and board files are not published. The device firmware is a closed binary, and the chips are undisclosed. Popular apps such as SDR# are closed freeware. GQRX is an open app that also works with it.
