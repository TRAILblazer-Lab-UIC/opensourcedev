Alif Ensemble E3

Alif Semiconductor

A tiny AI chip with two brains and two AI helpers for smart cameras.

Overview

The Alif Ensemble E3 is a chip built for small AI gadgets that run on batteries. It has two Arm Cortex-M55 processors and two AI helpers called NPUs. Most makers meet it inside the OpenMV AE3, a tiny camera board that runs object detection in MicroPython. It suits people who want a smart camera that does its own thinking.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart cameras that spot faces, pets, or objects
  • Battery projects that sleep for long stretches
  • Learning computer vision with MicroPython
  • Sound and motion sensing with small AI models

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as boards start near 150 US dollars
  • Wireless projects on Alif's own kits, as the chip has no radio
  • Total beginners who want Alif's SDK path
  • Projects that need a full Linux computer

Specs in plain words

  • Two NPUs give about 250 billion math steps each second for AI work. That is enough for live object detection at about 30 frames per second.
  • 13.5 MB of fast memory holds camera frames and an AI model at the same time.
  • 5.5 MB of MRAM keeps your program safe with the power off.
  • A deep sleep draw of 1.7 microamps lets a small battery last for months between wakes.
  • Camera and display inputs let the chip see the world and show results on a screen.
  • The OpenMV AE3 board is about one inch square, about the size of a postage stamp.

Memory figures are the top E3 parts (13.5 MB SRAM, 5.5 MB MRAM), which is what the OpenMV AE3 uses. The OpenMV AE3 adds 32 MB of storage flash. The second core runs at 160 MHz and the clock field shows the fast core. GPIO is up to 120 1.8 V pins plus up to 8 pins that can switch between 1.8 V and 3.3 V. The OpenMV AE3 exposes 10 I/O pins. ADC count is the 18 single-ended inputs of the three 12-bit ADCs. The 24-bit ADC adds 4 differential inputs. ai_tops is the combined 204 plus 46 GOPS from Alif. The chip has no radio. The OpenMV AE3 adds Wi-Fi and BLE through a separate module. Connectors, onboard parts and size describe the OpenMV AE3.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Bird feeder visitor counter

    Point the camera at a feeder and count each bird that lands. The chip spots birds on its own with no computer attached.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: weatherproof case, USB power bank.

  • Spot the face in the frame

    Run a ready-made face detection example in MicroPython. A box appears around each face on the camera preview.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Color tracker for a classroom

    Track a bright ball as it rolls across a table. Students can graph its path and work out its speed.

    Uses: Camera. Add: brightly colored ball.

  • Plug-in sensor logger

    Add temperature and light sensors through the plug-in connector. Log readings next to the camera images.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in temperature sensor, plug-in light sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Pet door watcher

    Train the camera to tell your cat from other animals. It can flash a light or log each visit.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: LED, small enclosure.

  • Garden pest spotter

    Watch a vegetable bed for rabbits or squirrels. The board logs each sighting with the time of day.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Sips power. Add: weatherproof case, battery pack.

  • Hand gesture light switch

    Wave a hand in front of the distance sensor to turn a lamp on or off. Different gestures can pick different colors.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: LED strip, relay module.

  • Clap and voice command box

    Use the microphone to spot a clap or a short keyword. A small AI model can trigger a light or a buzzer.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip. Add: buzzer.

  • Shelf stock checker

    Aim the camera at a pantry shelf and count jars or cans. It can warn you before you run out.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Posture and fall helper

    Use the motion sensor to notice a sudden fall. A loud alarm can call for help in the next room.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: buzzer.

  • Sound level map

    Measure how loud a room gets over a day. Show the noise level as a bar graph on a small screen.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: small display.

Expert ideas

  • Trail camera that sleeps for months

    Keep the chip in deep sleep and wake it on motion. It snaps a picture and runs object detection on each wake.

    Uses: Camera, Sips power, AI chip. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, microSD card.

  • Smart doorbell screen

    Show the camera view on a small display beside the door. The chip can label each person or package it sees.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: small MIPI display, push button.

  • Wired science camera station

    Record plant growth or a melting ice cube on a timer. Send images over a wired network to a lab computer.

    Uses: Camera, Wired network. Add: Ethernet adapter board, network cable.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Coding toolsMostly open

The chip itself is closed. Alif's E7 DevKit can act as an E3. Its schematics and Altium design files are free to download. No license is stated for them. MicroPython support is MIT licensed and the Zephyr code is Apache-2.0. Alif's SDK uses its own license and works only on Alif chips.
