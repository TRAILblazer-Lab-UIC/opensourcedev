Overview

AmazingHand is an open-source robot hand from Pollen Robotics, which Hugging Face bought in 2025. It has four fingers with two joints each, driven by eight small servo motors inside the hand. You can print the parts yourself or buy a kit. It suits makers, robotics clubs, and researchers who want an affordable humanlike hand.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how robot hands bend and grip
  • Robotics classes and clubs
  • Webcam hand tracking demos
  • Adding a hand to an open robot arm

Not great for

  • Quick weekend projects, as building takes hours
  • Lifting heavy things, as the servos are small
  • Long or complex grasping tasks, which the makers have not tested
  • Running on its own, as it needs a separate controller

Specs in plain words

  • Eight degrees of freedom means eight separate motions, two for each finger.
  • Each finger bends and also tilts side to side about 20 degrees.
  • The hand stands about 195 millimeters tall, about the size of an adult hand.
  • It weighs about 400 grams without servos, about as much as a can of soup.
  • Smart servos report their position, force, and temperature back to your code.
  • The servos need a 5 volt, 2 amp supply, as USB power is too weak.

Size195 x 105 mm

Eight Feetech SCS0009 servos rated 5 V, 1.89 kg-cm and 300 mA, two per finger. CNX Software lists 195 mm height, a palm 105 mm wide and 120 mm deep, about 180 mm finger span, about 80 and 86 degrees of flexion at the two joints and plus or minus 20 degrees sideways. Weight is about 400 g. Seeed and CNX say this excludes servos. USB power is not enough for the servos. An external supply is needed. The Enhanced version uses Feetech STS3032 servos in about the same volume.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wave hello on command

    Run the demo script and make the hand wave. It is a fun first test after you build it.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo driver board, 5 volt power supply.

  • Count to four with fingers

    Program each finger to lift one at a time. Young learners can count along with the hand.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo driver board, 5 volt power supply.

  • Rock paper scissors player

    Make the hand throw rock, paper, or scissors at random. A button press starts each round.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: microcontroller board, push button, 5 volt power supply.

Builder ideas

  • Webcam copycat hand

    Use the hand tracking demo with a webcam. The robot hand copies the moves of your own hand.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: webcam, computer, servo driver board.

  • Sign language alphabet lab

    Program finger shapes for a few letters of the sign alphabet. Students can test which letters four fingers can make.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo driver board, 5 volt power supply.

  • Glove that drives the hand

    Wear a glove with bend sensors on each finger. The robot hand follows your fingers as you move.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: glove, bend sensors, microcontroller board.

  • Two-hand puppet show

    Build a left and a right hand on one servo bus. Stage a short puppet play with both hands.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: second hand kit, servo driver board.

  • Piano key presser

    Set the hand over a small keyboard and press keys with each finger. Write a short tune as a list of finger moves.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small electronic keyboard, servo driver board.

  • Finger motion science lab

    Log servo angles and temperatures as each finger bends. Graph the data to compare the two joints.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo driver board, computer.

Expert ideas

  • Soft grip tester

    Read the servo load feedback to close gently on fruit or a paper cup. It shows how robots learn a light touch.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo driver board, assorted soft objects.

  • Robot arm add-on

    Mount the hand on the end of an open robot arm. The arm moves it into place and the hand grabs.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: robot arm, wrist mount.

  • Custom fingertip designs

    Edit the open CAD files to try new fingertip shapes. Print them and test which shape grips best.

    Add: 3D printer, flexible printing material.
