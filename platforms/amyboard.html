Overview

AMYboard is a small synthesizer board built on an ESP32-S3 chip. It is the home hardware for AMY, an open-source synth engine from Shore Pine Sound Systems. Ten jacks handle stereo audio, digital audio, MIDI, and control voltage for modular synths. It suits musicians, synth tinkerers, and teachers who want a low-cost way into sound coding.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Making synth sounds with short MicroPython programs
  • Adding a cheap voice module to a Eurorack case
  • Playing vintage Juno-6 and DX7 style patches
  • Teaching how sound waves and synthesis work

Not great for

  • Hands-on playing out of the box, as it has no knobs
  • Plugging in a USB keyboard, as the USB port works only as a device
  • Buyers in the US who want fast shipping, as it ships from China
  • Projects that need a screen or sensors built in

Specs in plain words

  • 8 MB of extra memory gives room for samples and many synth voices at once.
  • Two CV inputs and two CV outputs each swing from minus 10 to plus 10 volts, the range modular synths use.
  • 10HP is a Eurorack width of about two inches across the front panel.
  • TRS MIDI jacks connect keyboards and drum machines with small 3.5 mm cables.
  • S/PDIF jacks pass clean digital audio to and from other gear.
  • Wi-Fi lets you send changes to the board without a cable.

The 8 MB RAM figure comes from Synthtopia and is taken as the module's PSRAM. Module flash size was not stated on any fetched page. adc_channels counts the two CV inputs on the ADS1015 ADC. It is not raw ESP32-S3 pins. The troubleshooting docs list I2C devices at 0x40 (PCM9211, an S/PDIF and audio interface chip), 0x48 (ADS1015) and 0x58 (GP8413). The full analog codec chain was not confirmed from a fetched page. USB is device mode only. The board cannot host a USB MIDI keyboard. BLE is listed because LinuxGizmos describes the module as having Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. amyboard.com lists only Wi-Fi.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Play classic synth patches

    Load one of the 128 Juno-6 or 128 DX7 sounds and play it from a MIDI keyboard. You get vintage tones on day one.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: MIDI keyboard, headphones or speakers.

  • Browser sound sketchpad

    Write a few lines of MicroPython in the browser editor to play notes and chords. Change one number and hear the result.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones or speakers.

  • Plucked string instrument

    Use the Karplus-Strong voice to make guitar and harp sounds. Map them to keys on a MIDI controller.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: MIDI controller, speakers.

Builder ideas

  • Knob box for live tweaks

    Add rotary encoders and a small screen through the Grove port. Turn a knob to change the filter and see the value.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Makes sound. Add: rotary encoders, small OLED screen, Grove cables.

  • Step sequencer

    Program a looping pattern of eight or sixteen notes. Change the tempo and notes while it plays.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones or speakers.

  • Eurorack voice module

    Mount the board in a modular synth case as a 10HP module. Feed it CV from other modules to play notes.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Eurorack case, Eurorack power cable, patch cables.

  • Guitar effects box

    Run a guitar or phone through the line input. Add echo or filter sweeps and send it back out.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio cables, small amplifier.

  • Sample player for school band

    Load drum and sound effect samples and trigger them from MIDI pads. Students can score a short play or video.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: MIDI pad controller, speakers.

  • Wavetable sound lab

    Draw simple waves in code and play them as wavetables. Students hear how shape changes sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • CV random voltage generator

    Send slow random voltages out the CV jacks. They make other modular gear wander and change on its own.

    Add: patch cables, modular synth.

  • Garden wind chime synth

    Read a wind or light sensor through the Grove port. The board turns the readings into soft chime notes.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Makes sound. Add: Grove light sensor, speakers.

Expert ideas

  • Wireless patch control

    Send sound changes to the board over Wi-Fi from a phone or laptop. Change patches from across the room.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: speakers.

  • Digital audio bridge

    Pass sound between gear over the S/PDIF jacks. Add effects in the middle with AMY code.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: S/PDIF cables.

  • Custom Arduino synth voice

    Build your own synth engine with AMY in Arduino. Share the code with friends who use other AMY boards.

    Uses: Makes sound.

EAGLE schematics, board files, and front panel files are published for every board revision. The repo uses the MIT license, and the AMY synth engine is MIT too. The ESP32-S3 chip is closed, and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code ships only as closed files.
