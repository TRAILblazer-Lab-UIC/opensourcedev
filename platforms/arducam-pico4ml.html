Overview

The Arducam Pico4ML is a small board built on the Raspberry Pi RP2040 chip. It packs a camera, a tiny screen, a microphone, and a motion sensor onto one board. It suits learners who want to try tiny machine learning with no extra wiring. The original board is now retired, and the Pico4ML-Pro with a bigger screen followed it.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Trying person detection, gesture, and wake word demos
  • Learning TinyML with everything already on the board
  • Reusing Raspberry Pi Pico code and add-ons through its matching pinout
  • Small handheld gadgets with a screen

Not great for

  • Sharp or color images, as the camera is low resolution and grayscale
  • Fast vision, as person detection runs at about one frame per second
  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Long-term buying, as the original board is discontinued at most stores

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 133 MHz handle one small AI model at a time.
  • 264 KB of working memory is just enough for a tiny camera model.
  • 2 MB of storage holds your program plus a trained model.
  • The camera sees 320 by 240 pixels in shades of gray.
  • The 0.96 inch screen shows 160 by 80 pixels, about the size of a postage stamp.
  • The board measures 51 by 21 mm, about the size of a stick of gum.

Values are for the original Pico4ML (B0302). SparkFun lists about 40 mA standby and 60 mA while running models. Person detection runs about 860 ms per frame per CNX Software. The Pico4ML-Pro (B0432) keeps the RP2040 and 2 MB flash, adds a 2.4-inch LCD, four SPI ports for Arducam Mega cameras plus an HM01B0 port, and ships with an enclosure. Its IMU model and screen resolution are not listed.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Clap counter

    Use the microphone to count claps and show the number on the screen. Students race to fit the most claps in ten seconds.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Room noise meter

    Show a bar on the screen that grows with sound level. A library or lunchroom gets a gentle reminder.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Step counter with screen

    Count steps with the motion sensor and show the total on the screen. Compare walks across a week.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small battery pack.

Builder ideas

  • Person detector badge

    Run the person detection demo and show a smiley on the screen each time someone appears. It makes a fun science fair display.

    Uses: Camera, Screen.

  • Magic wand spell caster

    Wave the board in a shape and the gesture model names the spell. Each spell shows a new picture on the screen.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Pocket compass

    Read the 9-axis motion sensor and draw a needle on the screen. Take it on a nature walk.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small battery pack.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Watch the feeder with the camera and count each time something moves in view. Check the tally at dinner.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Tilt maze game

    Tilt the board to roll a dot through a maze drawn on the little screen. The motion sensor does the steering.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Posture reminder

    Clip the board to a shirt and watch its tilt. Flash a message on screen after you slouch for a while.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small battery pack, clip.

Expert ideas

  • Wake word lamp

    Teach the board to hear "yes" and "no". Use the words to switch a lamp on and off.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: relay module, lamp.

  • Handwritten digit reader

    Point the camera at a number drawn on paper. A small model guesses the digit and shows it on screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen.

  • Doorway people counter

    Mount the camera over a door and count people who walk past. Track how busy a classroom or shop gets.

    Uses: Camera. Add: USB power adapter.
