Overview

The Arduino MKR WAN 1310 is a small Arduino board built for LoRa and LoRaWAN projects. A Murata radio module runs the whole LoRaWAN network stack for you. Your sketch sends short commands through the MKRWAN library. It suits makers and teachers who want battery sensors that report from far away.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sensor nodes on The Things Network or Arduino Cloud
  • Battery-powered projects with deep sleep
  • Classroom lessons with Arduino's step by step tutorials
  • Projects that need secure key storage on a crypto chip
  • Using MKR shields that share the same pin layout

Not great for

  • Sending pictures, sound, or large files
  • Low-level radio tinkering, as the module hides the radio chip
  • 5 volt sensors, as the pins accept 3.3 volts only
  • Buyers in a hurry, as stores showed it sold out in late 2026

Specs in plain words

  • A 48 MHz chip with 32 KB of memory handles one simple job at a time, like reading a sensor and sending it.
  • An extra 2 MB flash chip holds thousands of saved sensor readings.
  • The radio covers 433, 868, and 915 MHz, which matches the licence-free bands in Europe and the US.
  • Arduino says it can idle near 104 microamps, which helps a battery last a long time.
  • The crypto chip keeps network keys locked away from prying code.
  • The board is about as long as a house key and comes with an antenna.

Specs from the Arduino store tech specs and the ABX00029 datasheet. The 2 MB SPI flash is extra storage beside the 256 KB internal flash. The headers expose D0 to D14 and A0 to A6. As a result, 22 pins can act as GPIO. The store table lists 8 digital I/O, 13 PWM, 7 analog inputs and 1 DAC output. The 104 uA figure is Arduino's claim, and the datasheet power table still reads TBC. The charger needs a battery that accepts at least 512 mA charge current. I/O is 3.3 V only and not 5 V tolerant. USB host works only when powered through VUSB with a jumper shorted. The antenna attaches by U.FL (not in the connector vocab). A 2 dBi pentaband antenna is bundled. Weight 32 g. SWD pads sit under the SAMD21. The datasheet says a Mini-B cable is needed, but the CNX report and the product line use micro-USB.

Starter ideas

  • Two board button test

    Press a button on one board and light an LED on the other. It is the classic first LoRa link from the Arduino tutorials.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: second MKR WAN 1310, push button, LED.

  • Garden soil moisture reporter

    Send soil moisture from a far garden bed to The Things Network. Check the reading on your phone before you water.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor, LiPo battery.

  • Mailbox arrival alert

    A small switch in the mailbox lid sends a message to the house. You stop walking out to an empty box.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, LiPo battery, second MKR WAN 1310.

  • Greenhouse frost alarm

    Watch the greenhouse temperature from the house. Get an alert on cold nights.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: temperature sensor, LiPo battery.

  • Classroom signal range map

    Walk a board around the neighborhood and record where messages still arrive. Draw a coverage map together.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: LiPo battery, second MKR WAN 1310.

Builder ideas

  • School weather station

    Log temperature, humidity, and air pressure on the school roof. Students graph the data in Arduino Cloud.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: temperature humidity and pressure sensor, LiPo battery, weatherproof box.

  • Parking spot counter

    Count cars entering a small lot with a distance sensor. Share the free spots with your neighbors online.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Beehive health logger

    Weigh a hive and read its inside temperature. Send both each hour to a dashboard.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, temperature sensor, small solar panel.

  • River level watcher

    Hang a distance sensor over a creek. Track the water height after storms for a community science log.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor, LiPo battery, small solar panel.

  • Offline data logger

    Store readings in the 2 MB flash chip between sends. Nothing is lost during a gap in coverage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Trail visitor counter

    Count hikers passing a trail sign with a motion sensor. Send a daily total to the park team.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: PIR motion sensor, weatherproof box, LiPo battery.

Expert ideas

    Program several boards to pass messages along a valley in point to point mode. Reach spots out of range of one hop.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: several MKR WAN 1310 boards, LiPo batteries.

    Uses: Battery ready, LoRa long range radio. Add: LiPo battery, USB power meter.
