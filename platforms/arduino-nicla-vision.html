Overview

The Nicla Vision is an Arduino camera board just under one inch square. It packs a camera, a distance sensor, a motion sensor and a microphone onto one tiny board. It also has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and a battery charger. Makers, students and engineers use it for small vision projects that run on the board itself.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning machine vision with ready-made MicroPython examples
  • Counting people, birds or objects in a small space
  • Battery-powered sensor nodes with a camera
  • Training tiny AI models with Edge Impulse
  • Projects that need many sensors in a very small space

Not great for

  • Sharp photos or video. The camera is a fixed-focus 2 megapixel sensor.
  • Large AI models. The memory limits image size and model size.
  • Plugging in a common battery pack. The battery plug is a small 3-pin type and needs an adapter.
  • Projects that need many pins for wires and add-ons

Specs in plain words

  • The main chip has two cores at 480 and 240 megahertz. One core can watch the camera and the other can handle other jobs.
  • 1 megabyte of working memory holds a small picture and a tiny AI model at the same time.
  • The 2 megapixel camera sees about 80 degrees wide. That is roughly the view of a phone camera held at arm's length.
  • The distance sensor measures up to about 4 meters. That is about the length of a small car.
  • The board sips about 374 microamps in deep sleep. A small battery can last a long time between wake-ups.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth let it send results to a phone or a computer.

Flash is the 2 MB internal flash. The board adds 16 MB of external QSPI flash (AT25QL128A). RAM is the STM32H747's 1 MB of SRAM per the DigiKey Maker.io article. DigiKey counts 19 usable GPIO with 10 digital and 2 analog. The GPIO value here is the digital count. The time-of-flight sensor is listed under light_sensor because the vocabulary has no distance-sensor value. The 374 uA figure is the datasheet average in deep sleep. Image capture draws about 105 mA. The ESLOV 5-pin connector carries I2C. The board has a U.FL antenna connector and a 24-pin board-to-board connector. DigiKey describes the board as just under one inch square. Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Color sorting game

    Hold up colored cards and let the camera name each color. It makes a quick party game for kids.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Face finder demo

    Run the face detection example and watch a box follow your face. It is a fun first taste of machine vision.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Clap and knock detector

    Use the microphone to listen for claps or knocks. Flash the onboard light each time it hears one.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Distance alarm for a shelf

    Use the distance sensor to notice a hand reaching toward a cookie jar. The light blinks to catch the snacker.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder visitor counter

    Point the camera at a feeder and count each bird that lands. You get a daily tally of feathered guests.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready. Add: small battery, weatherproof box.

  • Room people counter

    Mount the board over a doorway and count people walking in and out. A club or library can see its busy hours.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power adapter.

  • Wildlife trail camera

    Run the board on a battery near a garden path. It snaps a picture each time an animal walks by.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready, Sensors built in. Add: small battery, weatherproof box.

  • Phone alert over Bluetooth

    Send a message to your phone each time the camera spots a chosen object. It works across a room.

    Uses: Camera, Bluetooth.

  • Tilt and shake logger

    Use the motion sensor to record bumps and tilts on a bike ride. Look at the bumpy spots on a chart later.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: small battery.

  • Science class motion lab

    Students roll a ball past the distance sensor and record its speed. The class graphs the results together.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Wi-Fi snapshot sender

    Take a photo every hour and send it to a computer over Wi-Fi. You can watch a seed sprout in a time-lapse.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power adapter.

Expert ideas

  • Plant leaf health checker

    Train a small model on photos of healthy and sick leaves. The board flags plants that may need care.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Parts inspection station

    Train a model to spot a missing screw or a bent part on a small item. A makerspace can check prints this way.

    Uses: Camera. Add: desk lamp.

  • Line following robot eye

    Mount the board on a small robot car and let the camera track a taped line. The board tells the motors where to steer.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small robot car kit, motor driver.

How open is it?

Open Board

Arduino publishes the schematics under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license, along with a pinout and 3D models. The PCB layout files are not posted. The OpenMV firmware is mostly MIT licensed, and the Arduino core is open code. The main chip and the Wi-Fi firmware stay closed.
