Arduino Plug and Make Kit

Arduino

A solder-free Arduino starter kit with seven snap-together sensor and light modules.

Overview

The Arduino Plug and Make Kit is a starter kit built around the Arduino UNO R4 WiFi board. It adds seven small modules for motion, distance, temperature, a knob, a buzzer, colored lights, and buttons. The modules link together with plug-in cables and need no soldering. It suits curious beginners, families, and classrooms who want guided projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First projects with zero soldering or breadboard wiring
  • Guided lessons that follow a free online course
  • Sensor projects that show data on a phone through Arduino Cloud
  • Classrooms that want a tidy build on one plate

Not great for

  • Projects that need a camera or heavy number crunching
  • Battery projects that must run for weeks
  • Budget builds, at about 70 to 90 US dollars for the kit
  • MicroPython fans. Support on this board is unconfirmed.

Specs in plain words

  • The main chip runs at 48 MHz. It handles one simple job at a time, like reading a sensor and blinking a light.
  • 32 KB of memory holds a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • A second chip adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth for phone and cloud projects.
  • The base plate is 140 mm square, about the size of a CD case.
  • Each module is about the size of a postage stamp pair and plugs into the next one in a chain.
  • The board has a 12 by 8 grid of red lights for simple pictures and scrolling text.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorRenesas RA4M1 (Arm Cortex-M4F) on the UNO R4 WiFi, plus an ESP32-S3-MINI-1 coprocessor, 1 core at 48 MHz
Memory32 KB
Storage256 KB
Pins14 pins, 6 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), CAN bus, USB device
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsUSB-C, Qwiic or STEMMA QT, Pin headers
Built inLED grid, motion sensor, temperature sensor, buttons, color LED, speaker
Power5 V over USB-C, or 6 to 24 V on VIN or the barrel jack
Size140 x 140 mm

Core, clock, RAM and flash values are for the RA4M1 on the UNO R4 WiFi, from the kit datasheet. It also lists 8 KB EEPROM, a 14-bit ADC, a 12-bit DAC, an RTC and a 12 x 8 red LED matrix. The digital and analog pin counts follow the UNO R4 WiFi layout. The onboard list counts the Modulino nodes in the kit. speaker stands for the piezo buzzer on Modulino Buzzer. The distance sensor (VL53L4CD, 0 to 1200 mm) has no vocabulary slot. Size is the Modulino Base plate (140 x 140 x 1.6 mm). Each Modulino node is 41 x 25.36 mm with JST SM 1.0 mm Qwiic-style connectors. The kit datasheet lists the board as SKU ABX00080. By contrast, the standalone UNO R4 WiFi is sold as ABX00087.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Desk weather station

    Read the temperature and humidity sensor every minute. Show the numbers scrolling across the red light grid on the board.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Gesture lamp

    Wave a hand over the distance sensor to switch the eight colored lights on or off. Turn the knob to pick a color.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Reaction time game

    The lights flash at a random moment. Players race to press a button first, and the buzzer cheers the winner.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Knob music box

    Turn the knob to change the pitch of the buzzer. The three buttons play short tunes you write yourself.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Door greeter

    The distance sensor notices someone walking up. The buzzer chimes and the lights glow a welcome color.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound.

Builder ideas

  • Tilt maze on the light grid

    Tilt the motion sensor to roll a dot around the light grid. Avoid the walls to reach the goal.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Phone dashboard for your room

    Send temperature and humidity over Wi-Fi to an Arduino Cloud dashboard. Check your room from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sensors built in.

  • Classroom quiz buzzers

    Each of the three buttons belongs to a team. The first press lights up that team color and locks out the others.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Posture coach

    Clip the motion sensor to a chair back. The buzzer beeps after you slouch for too long.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound. Add: longer connector cable.

  • Plant corner climate log

    Track humidity and temperature near your houseplants all day. The lights turn blue once the air gets too dry.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi.

  • Parking distance helper

    Mount the distance sensor on a garage wall. The lights shift from green to red as the car gets close.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: USB power adapter, long USB cable.

  • Step and jump counter

    The motion sensor counts jumps for a jump rope challenge. The light grid shows the running total.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Bluetooth game controller

    Use the knob and buttons as a controller for a game on a computer or tablet. The board sends moves over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Science fair motion lab

    Record tilt and shaking from the motion sensor during an experiment. Send the readings to a cloud chart for the class.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi.
