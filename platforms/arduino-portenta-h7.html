Arduino Portenta H7

Overview

The Portenta H7 is Arduino's professional module. It packs two processor cores, extra memory, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a wired network chip on a stick about the size of a stick of gum. It suits makers, labs, and schools that have outgrown a basic Arduino. Its standout trait is speed for camera and vision work.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Camera and vision projects with the Vision Shield
  • Lab and factory gadgets that need a wired network
  • Fast sensor work that splits jobs across two cores
  • Screens and dashboards through a carrier board
  • People who already know the Arduino IDE

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as the module and carriers cost a lot
  • First-time beginners, as most pins sit on dense connectors that need a carrier
  • Fully open hardware, as only a PDF schematic is shared

Specs in plain words

  • A 480 MHz main core is fast for a microcontroller. It can handle a small camera feed.
  • A second 240 MHz core can run its own program at the same time.
  • 8 MB of extra memory holds whole camera images.
  • 16 MB of flash stores programs, small models, and sound clips.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a wired network chip let it talk to almost anything.
  • A built-in battery charger lets it run from a rechargeable LiPo battery.

The M7 runs at 480 MHz and the M4 at 240 MHz. RAM is the 1 MB inside the STM32H747, and psram_kb holds the 8 MB external SDRAM. flash_kb is the 16 MB external QSPI flash. The chip also has 2 MB of internal flash for code. The high-density connectors carry 7 GPIO, 10 PWM, 8 ADC inputs, 4 UARTs, 3 I2C, 1 SPI, 1 I2S, 1 PDM, CAN, SD card, Ethernet, 2-lane MIPI DSI and an 8-bit camera bus. The MKR headers carry 7 PWM, 7 ADC, 1 SPI, 1 UART and 1 I2C, many shared with the HD pins. As a result, a single GPIO total is not given. The STM32H747 chip has 3 ADCs with up to 36 channels and 16-bit resolution, plus two 12-bit DACs. The board also has an ESLOV I2C connector, a U.FL antenna connector and the 80-pin HD connectors, which are not in the connector vocabulary. DisplayPort over USB-C, the SE050C2 and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth are only on the full H7. The H7 Lite drops Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, DisplayPort and the SE050C2, and the H7 Lite Connected keeps Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Deep sleep draws 0.67 mA at 3.7 V per the datasheet, which is recorded as 670 uA. The size comes from a Wevolver search snippet (25 mm x 66 mm). Bluetooth is BLE 5 with the Cordio stack or BLE 4.2 with the Arduino stack, per the datasheet.

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and Wi-Fi hello

    Run the Blink example and then join your home Wi-Fi. Print the board's address in the Serial Monitor.

    Uses: Wi-Fi.

  • Room temperature web page

    Read a temperature sensor and serve a small web page over Wi-Fi. Check the room from any phone at home.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Bluetooth plant monitor

    Send soil moisture readings to your phone over Bluetooth. Water the plants before they droop.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: soil moisture sensor, breakout carrier board.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder camera

    Snap a photo each time a bird lands on the feeder. Save the pictures to an SD card for later.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera shield, microSD card.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board and read each key press. Build a typing game for the classroom.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB-C adapter.

  • Sound effects box

    Play short sound clips through a speaker at the press of a button. Add sound to a school play or a board game.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: speaker, amplifier board, push buttons.

  • Greenhouse controller

    Read temperature and humidity and switch a fan and pump. Watch the numbers on a web page over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, relay module, small fan, small water pump.

  • Community air quality station

    Measure dust in the air and post readings online over Wi-Fi. Neighbors can see how clean the air is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Gesture music controller

    Read a motion sensor and send music notes to a computer. Wave your hand to play a melody.

    Add: motion sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Person counter at the door

    Run a small vision model that spots people in the camera view. Count visitors to a library or event.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera shield.

  • Wired lab data logger

    Read several sensors fast and send the data over a wired network. Lab benches get a steady feed with no Wi-Fi dropouts.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: carrier board with Ethernet jack, network cable, lab sensors.

  • Touch screen control panel

    Drive a screen and draw buttons and gauges. Control lights or fans from a wall panel.

    Uses: Screen. Add: display with touch, carrier board.

  • Spoken word light switch

    Train a speech model to hear a few words through a microphone. Turn lamps on and off by voice.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone, relay module, carrier board.

  • Battery trail camera

    Run from a rechargeable battery and wake on motion. Photograph animals on a forest trail.

    Uses: Battery ready, Camera. Add: camera shield, LiPo battery, motion sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Color sorting robot

    Look at objects with the camera and steer a servo to sort them by color. Use it for a science fair demo.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera shield, servo motor, small conveyor or chute.

  • Two-core dashboard

    Let one core read sensors while the other draws a live chart on a screen. Learn how two brains share a job.

    Uses: Screen. Add: display, carrier board, sensors.

Arduino shares a PDF schematic, a pinout, and 3D models. No board layout files or hardware license were found. The Arduino core code is public, and the newer Zephyr core uses the Apache 2.0 license. The Wi-Fi radio firmware and the main chip stay closed.
