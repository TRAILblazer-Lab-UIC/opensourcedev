Arduino Portenta X8

Overview

The Arduino Portenta X8 is a small module that runs Linux. It also carries a second chip for real-time Arduino sketches. It suits makers and engineers who build gateways, monitors, and machine controllers. Its two brains on one tiny board make it stand out.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Projects that need both Linux apps and precise Arduino timing
  • Gateways that collect sensor data and send it over Wi-Fi or wired network
  • Long-running apps that update over the air
  • Learners who already know Arduino and want to try Linux

Not great for

  • First-time makers on a budget
  • Quick projects with only a USB cable
  • Use without a carrier board, which holds most of the pins
  • Battery gadgets that sip power

Specs in plain words

  • Four main cores at up to 1.8 GHz give it the speed of a modest single board computer.
  • Two gigabytes of memory let it run several small apps at once.
  • Sixteen gigabytes of built-in storage hold the operating system plus room for your files and logs.
  • The extra STM32H747 chip runs Arduino sketches with steady timing for motors and sensors.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a wired network chip let it talk to phones, sensors and routers.
  • The whole module is about 66 by 25 millimeters, close to the size of a stick of gum.

cores and clock_mhz describe the Cortex-A53 cluster. The i.MX 8M Mini also has a 400 MHz Cortex-M4 that the datasheet says is reserved for future use. The STM32H747 has a 480 MHz M7 running Arduino's I/O firmware and a 240 MHz M4 for user sketches. flash_kb is the 16 GB eMMC. The STM32H747 adds 2 MB of internal flash and 1 MB of RAM. The high-density connectors carry 7 GPIO, 8 ADC inputs, 4 PWM, 4 UART, 3 I2C, 2 SPI, I2S, PDM, SD card, two USB HS, Gigabit Ethernet, PCIe, DSI and CSI. The datasheet lists low power mode at 200 mW, busy loop at 2350 mW and maximum at 4000 mW. As a result, low_power_ua is null. The module has a U.FL antenna connector and two 80-pin HD connectors, which are outside the connector vocabulary. The GPU vendor name Vivante is from general i.MX 8M Mini knowledge and was not on the datasheet. A carrier with DIP switches is needed for board recovery.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Home sensor dashboard

    Read temperature and humidity sensors with an Arduino sketch. The Linux side shows the readings on a web page you open from any phone.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi. Add: carrier board, temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Greenhouse watering controller

    Check soil moisture and switch a small pump on a schedule. Watch the plant data from your laptop over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • School weather station

    Log wind, rain, and temperature every minute. Students download the data as a spreadsheet for science class.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: carrier board, weather sensors, network cable.

  • Small home server

    Run a few apps in containers on the Linux side. Host a family photo page or a shared calendar on your home network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: carrier board, network cable.

  • Lobby info screen

    Plug a monitor into the USB-C port. Show the day's schedule, news, or weather in a library or club room.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: USB-C to DisplayPort adapter, monitor.

  • Community air quality node

    Measure dust in the air outside a school or park. Share the numbers on a public map for neighbors.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, air quality sensor.

  • USB scanner kiosk

    Plug a USB barcode reader into the module. Check out books or tools in a classroom library and log each one.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, USB barcode scanner.

Expert ideas

  • Wildlife camera station

    Connect a camera module to the carrier board. Record clips of birds at a feeder and save them to storage.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Workshop machine monitor

    Watch a motor or fan for odd temperature changes. Get a message on your network once something runs hot.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: carrier board, temperature sensor, current sensor.

  • Bluetooth gadget hub

    Collect readings from Bluetooth sensors around the house. Keep them all in one log on the module.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, Bluetooth sensors.

  • Sound level logger

    Listen with a digital microphone and record noise levels through the day. Spot the loudest hours on a street or in a classroom.

    Uses: Hears sound, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, digital microphone.

  • Talking doorbell

    Press a button at the door and play a recorded greeting through a speaker. Send a note to your phone at the same time.

    Uses: Makes sound, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, push button, small amplifier, speaker.

  • Robot arm brain

    Let the Arduino side drive servos with steady timing. The Linux side plans the moves and serves a control page.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, servo motors, robot arm kit, power supply.

  • Beehive health tracker

    Weigh a hive and record its inside temperature each hour. Beekeepers see how the colony grows across the season.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: carrier board, load cell, temperature sensor, weatherproof box.

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Arduino publishes the schematic, pinout, and 3D files under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license. The Linux build recipes are public, and the helper firmware uses GPL-3.0 with kernel modules under GPL-2.0. The main chips are closed, and the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip needs closed firmware.
