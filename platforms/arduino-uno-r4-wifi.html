Overview

The Arduino UNO R4 WiFi keeps the classic UNO shape and header layout. A faster 32-bit chip runs your code, and a second chip adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. A built-in grid of red lights can show words, faces and simple games. It suits beginners, classrooms, and anyone with old UNO shields.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First Arduino projects with tons of tutorials
  • Internet of things projects with Wi-Fi
  • Reusing classic UNO shields and add-on boards
  • Classroom lessons with a built-in light display
  • Plug-in sensors through the Qwiic connector

Not great for

  • Cameras or sound recording
  • Running Python, as the main path is Arduino code
  • Driving motors straight from the board without an outside supply
  • Battery projects that must last for months

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 32 KB of working memory is small. Short sensor programs fit fine.
  • 256 KB of flash holds a program of a few thousand lines.
  • The 12 by 8 light grid can show short words, icons and tiny games.
  • The headers run at 5 volts, which suits many classic sensors and shields.
  • The plug-in Qwiic port runs at 3.3 volts on its own separate channel.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorRenesas RA4M1 (R7FA4M1AB3CFM, Arm Cortex-M4F) plus ESP32-S3-MINI-1-N8 coprocessor (Xtensa LX7 dual core), 1 core at 48 MHz
Memory32 KB
Storage256 KB
Pins14 pins, 6 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), CAN bus, USB device
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsUSB-C, Qwiic or STEMMA QT, Pin headers
Built inLED grid
Power5 V over USB-C, or 6 to 24 V on VIN or the barrel jack
Size68.6 x 53.4 mm

Core, clock, RAM and flash values are for the RA4M1 main MCU. The ESP32-S3 module has 8 MB flash and 512 KB SRAM and runs the Wi-Fi and USB bridge firmware. The datasheet lists 14 digital I/O and 6 analog channels, 1 UART, 1 SPI, 2 I2C (one on Qwiic) and 1 CAN that needs an external transceiver. The 12-bit DAC and RTC have no vocabulary slot. USB HID is supported, recorded as usb_device. The Qwiic port runs at 3.3 V while the headers run at 5 V. Size is the standard UNO footprint from Wikipedia, which states the R4 boards keep the R3 form factor. The datasheet mechanical drawing was not read as text.

Starter ideas

  • Scrolling name badge

    Type a message in a sketch and watch it scroll across the red light grid. Prop it on your desk at school.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil moisture sensor into a pot. The grid shows a sad face once the soil gets dry.

    Uses: Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Simple electronic piano

    Wire eight buttons to play eight notes on a buzzer. The grid shows each note as you play.

    Uses: Screen. Add: push buttons, small speaker, breadboard.

  • Heartbeat light grid

    Clip a pulse sensor to your finger. The grid draws a heart that beats in time with you.

    Uses: Screen. Add: pulse sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Weather on the light grid

    Fetch the local weather over Wi-Fi. The light grid shows a sun, a cloud, or rain drops.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Room temperature dashboard

    Send temperature readings to an online dashboard over Wi-Fi. Check your room from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in temperature and humidity sensor, connector cable.

  • Snake game on the grid

    Wire two buttons to steer a snake across the light grid. Eat dots and grow longer.

    Uses: Screen. Add: push buttons, breadboard, jumper wires.

  • Phone-controlled robot car

    Drive a small car from a phone app over Bluetooth. Add a motor shield on top of the headers.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: robot car kit, motor driver shield, battery pack.

  • Garden watering timer

    Switch a small pump on for a few minutes each day. Your seedlings stay watered on a schedule.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: relay module, small water pump, power supply.

  • Doorbell that pings your phone

    Press a button at the door. The board sends a message to your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: push button, jumper wires.

  • Classroom air quality monitor

    Plug in an air quality sensor and log readings online. Students see the air get stale during a long class.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in air quality sensor, connector cable.

  • Custom game keyboard

    The board can act as a USB keyboard. Build big arcade buttons that press keys in a computer game.

    Add: arcade buttons, jumper wires.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Count birds with a motion sensor and post a daily tally online. Learn when your feeder is busiest.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: motion sensor, weatherproof box, power supply.

  • Mood lamp from your phone

    Pick a color in a phone app over Bluetooth. An LED strip glows to match your mood.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: LED strip, power supply.

Expert ideas
