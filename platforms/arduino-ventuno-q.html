Overview

The Arduino VENTUNO Q is a large board with two brains. A Qualcomm processor runs Ubuntu Linux and has an AI engine rated at 40 trillion operations per second. An STM32 microcontroller runs Arduino sketches for steady, real-time control. It suits makers building robots, smart cameras, and local AI projects that also need pins and shields.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Vision projects with up to three cameras
  • Running AI models on the board with no cloud service
  • Robots that mix smart planning with real-time motor control
  • Reusing UNO shields, Qwiic sensors, and 40-pin HAT boards
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and fast wired network projects

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as it costs about 399 US dollars
  • Battery gadgets that need to sleep for months
  • Small builds, as the board is about 16 by 10 centimeters
  • Absolute beginners who want a simple one-cable Arduino

Specs in plain words

  • Eight processor cores at up to 2.36 GHz give desktop-class speed for Linux apps.
  • 16 GB of memory holds large AI models and many apps at once.
  • 64 GB of built-in storage fits the operating system, your projects and thousands of photos.
  • The 40 TOPS AI engine can spot objects in live video in real time.
  • The 250 MHz microcontroller handles fast, steady jobs like motors and blinking lights.
  • Power comes from a 5 V 3 A USB-C supply or a 12 to 24 V barrel jack.

Core, clock, RAM and eMMC values are for the Qualcomm MPU. CNX lists the CPU as Kryo Gen 6 with Cortex-A78 and A55 cores but gives no split. The STM32H5F5 has 4 MB flash and 1.5 MB RAM. The NPU figure is 40 dense TOPS. Board height is 25.8 mm. GPIO and analog input counts were not stated on a page I read. SPI, UART and I2S are likely on the headers but were not confirmed. Audio (two mic inputs, headphone and line out) is on the JMISC header. CNX notes a 104-LED matrix and a fan connector.

Things you could build

18 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Scrolling message board

    Write a short sketch that scrolls words across the built-in LED grid. Show a name, a score, or a daily quote.

  • Plug-and-play weather station

    Snap a temperature and humidity sensor onto the Qwiic port. A Python app shows the readings on a web page.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, Qwiic cable.

  • Phone-controlled lamp

    Drive a relay from the Arduino pins and serve a simple web page from Linux. Tap a button on your phone to switch the lamp.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: relay module.

  • Science fair data logger

    Read light and temperature sensors with a short sketch. Python saves each value to a spreadsheet file.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plug-in connectors. Add: light sensor, temperature sensor, Qwiic cables.

Builder ideas

  • Object spotting camera

    Connect a camera and run a vision model on the AI engine. The board names the things it sees and lights the LED grid.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module.

  • Bird feeder visitor log

    Point a camera at a feeder and let a vision model count birds. Each visit goes into a log with a photo.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Smart plant waterer

    Read a soil sensor with the microcontroller and run a small pump. Linux keeps a watering diary you can view over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Home dashboard screen

    Show the weather, bus times, and your calendar on a monitor. The board refreshes it over Wi-Fi each minute.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: HDMI screen.

  • Classroom desktop computer

    Connect a screen, keyboard, and mouse to use Ubuntu as a desktop. Students can browse, write code, and test circuits on one board.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: HDMI screen, keyboard, mouse.

  • Shield-based music box

    Stack an audio shield on the UNO headers and trigger sounds with buttons. Python picks the next song from a playlist.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: audio shield, push buttons, small speaker.

  • Bluetooth sensor hub

    Collect readings from Bluetooth tags around the house. Linux stores them and draws simple charts.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Runs Linux. Add: Bluetooth sensor tags.

  • Garden greenhouse controller

    Read temperature and soil sensors and switch fans and pumps on a schedule. Check the greenhouse from your phone.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature sensor, soil moisture sensor, relay module, small fan.

Expert ideas

  • Hand gesture light show

    Track hand poses with a camera and a pose model. Each pose sets a different color on an LED strip.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, LED strip.

  • Talking helper on your desk

    Run a small local language model on the AI engine. Ask it questions through a USB microphone and hear replies on a speaker.

    Uses: AI chip, Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB microphone, USB speaker.

  • Vision-guided robot car

    Let a camera model find a path or a ball. The microcontroller drives the wheels with steady timing.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, motor driver shield, robot car chassis, battery pack.

  • Three-camera wildlife watch

    Use three cameras to watch a yard from different sides. A vision model flags foxes, deer, or neighborhood cats.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera modules, weatherproof boxes.

  • Robot arm with CAN motors

    Drive smart motors over the CAN-FD buses. Linux plans each move and the microcontroller keeps the timing tight.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: CAN motors, robot arm kit, power supply.

  • Community center people counter

    Count visitors at a doorway with a camera model. Show the daily total on the LED grid and a web page.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux, Wi-Fi. Add: camera module.
