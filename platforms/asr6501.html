ASR6501

ASR Microelectronics

A tiny chip that pairs a small brain with a long-range LoRa radio.

Field marks: Look for a little CubeCell board with a solar wire and a stubby antenna, sunbathing on a fence post and whispering to gateways miles away.

Overview

The ASR6501 combines a small Arm brain and a Semtech LoRa radio in one chip about the size of a fingernail. Heltec used it in its first CubeCell boards. It suits makers who want long-range sensors that run for months on a battery or a small solar panel. The boards are now older stock, as newer CubeCell boards use the ASR6502.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Long-range sensors that send small readings miles away
  • Battery and solar projects that sleep most of the time
  • LoRaWAN networks such as The Things Network
  • Outdoor gadgets on farms, gardens and creeks

Not great for

  • Big programs, as memory is very small
  • Screens, sound or cameras
  • Buying new, as these boards are mostly sold out
  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth links to a phone

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 128 KB of flash holds a sensor program and the LoRaWAN code.
  • 16 KB of working memory is tiny. It fits a handful of readings at once.
  • About 3.5 microamps in deep sleep lets a small battery last a long time.
  • The LoRa radio can reach several miles in open country with a good antenna.
  • Eight input and output pins are enough for a few sensors.

GPIO, ADC, connectors, onboard parts and size are for the HTCC-AB01 V1 dev board. It exposes 8 GPIO, 1 UART, 1 SPI, 1 I2C, SWD and one 12-bit ADC input. The board ADC input range is 0 to 2400 mV per the Heltec core README. The chip's own GPIO count was not found in a fetched source. The package is QFN48, 6 x 6 mm, per LCSC. The radio covers 470 to 510 MHz (L version) or 863 to 928 MHz (F version). Deep sleep is 3.5 uA when powered from the 3.3 V header and 11 uA from the battery input, per the AB01 datasheet. TX current runs from 70 mA at 10 dBm to 105 mA at 20 dBm, and RX is about 10 mA. The AB01 has a CP2102 USB-to-serial chip. The RGB LED and buttons come from the CNX Software description of the dev board.

Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Long-range soil moisture sensor

    Read a soil sensor and send the value over LoRa to a gateway. Check a far corner of the garden from home.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LoRa antenna.

  • Mailbox alert

    Sense the mailbox door with a magnet switch. Send a LoRa message the moment mail arrives.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, Li-ion battery.

  • Button that calls for help

    Press a big button to send a LoRa message to a receiver indoors. An older relative can ask for help from the garden.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: large push button, Li-ion battery.

  • Frost warning for the garden

    Watch the air temperature at night and send an alert near freezing. Cover tender plants in time.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power. Add: temperature sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Solar weather node

    Read temperature and humidity every ten minutes and send them over LoRaWAN. A small solar panel keeps it running.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small solar panel, Li-ion battery.

  • Water tank level reporter

    Measure the water level in a rain barrel. Send the reading to your phone through a LoRaWAN network.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: distance sensor, Li-ion battery, weatherproof box.

  • Beehive temperature watch

    Place a sensor inside a hive and report its warmth each hour. Beekeepers spot trouble without opening the box.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: waterproof temperature probe, Li-ion battery.

  • Gate open alarm on a farm

    Report each time a far gate opens. Farmers know when animals might wander out.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, weatherproof box.

  • Two-board LoRa messenger

    Set up two boards and send short messages between them. Test how far the signal reaches across a park.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: second CubeCell board, LoRa antennas.

Expert ideas

  • River level station for the community

    Measure a creek level and share it over a public LoRaWAN network. Neighbors can watch for floods.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: distance sensor, small solar panel, Li-ion battery, weatherproof box.

How open is it?

Open Board

Heltec shares PDF schematics and pinouts for its CubeCell boards. Its documents allow only personal use, which keeps the hardware closed. The Arduino core is public, but part of it ships as a closed library file. The LoRaWAN code uses a Revised BSD license.

Hardware license: None, Heltec documents are copyright with only non-commercial personal use allowed · Software license: Revised BSD (LoRaMac-node stack under cores/asr650x/lora), rest of Heltec core has no stated license
