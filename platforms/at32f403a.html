Overview

The AT32F403A is a microcontroller from Artery Technology in Taiwan. It runs at 240 MHz and has far more memory than the older STM32F103 chips it often replaces. Makers meet it on the WeAct Black Pill, which costs only a few dollars. It suits hobbyists who already know Arduino and want more speed for little money.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Fast projects on a tiny budget
  • USB gadgets such as keyboards and game pads
  • Projects with sound output or a parallel screen
  • Machines that talk over CAN bus
  • Upgrading old STM32F103 designs

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Buying in the US, as no US store was found
  • Beginners who need many tutorials, as most help is scattered across forums
  • Drop-in STM32 swaps, as some code and pins need changes

Specs in plain words

  • 240 MHz with a math unit handles audio, screens, and control loops with ease.
  • 1 MB of storage fits big programs, fonts, and sound clips.
  • 224 KB of working memory holds a screen buffer or lots of sensor data.
  • Built-in USB works without an extra crystal, which keeps boards cheap.
  • Two CAN buses let it join the wiring found in cars and robots.
  • Two 12-bit DAC outputs can play simple sounds and tones.

ConnectorsUSB-C, Pin headers
Built inbuttons
Power5 V over USB-C or 3.3 to 5 V on the headers (WeAct Black Pill)
Size52.4 x 20.8 mm

Flash, RAM, GPIO, ADC, connectors and size are for the AT32F403ACGU7 on the WeAct Black Pill. The chip has up to 80 I/O (37, 51 or 80 by package) and up to 16 ADC channels on larger packages. SRAM is 96 KB plus 128 KB that can be traded against zero-wait flash through option bytes. The LCD interface is the XMC parallel bus for 8080 or 6800 displays. Supply is 2.6 to 3.6 V. Packages are QFN48, LQFP48, LQFP64 and LQFP100. Sleep current was not found in a fetched source.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blinking light sequencer

    Program a row of LEDs to chase, fade, and flash. It makes a fun first project for the Arduino IDE.

    Add: LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Plant watering timer

    Check soil moisture and run a small pump for a few seconds. Houseplants get water while you travel.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Tone generator for science class

    Use the built-in DAC to play steady tones at set pitches. Students hear how frequency changes pitch.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker, amplifier board.

Builder ideas

  • Indoor air quality monitor

    Read an air sensor and show the numbers on a small screen. Learn the right moment to open a window.

    Uses: Screen. Add: air quality sensor, small screen.

  • Simple drum machine

    Play drum sounds from its two-channel audio output at the press of a button. Make beats for music class.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push buttons, small speaker, amplifier board.

  • Data logger to SD card

    Record sensor readings to a memory card every minute. Graph a week of temperatures in a spreadsheet.

    Add: microSD card module, temperature sensor.

  • USB game controller

    Wire a joystick and buttons and make the board show up as a game pad. Play computer games with your own controller.

    Add: joystick module, push buttons.

  • Retro handheld game

    Draw a simple game on a color screen and read a few buttons. Build a pocket game for long car rides.

    Uses: Screen. Add: color screen, push buttons, battery pack.

  • Sound level meter

    Read a microphone on an analog pin and show a level bar on screen. Watch how noise changes through the day.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: microphone module, small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Custom mechanical keyboard

    Wire a grid of key switches and make the board act as a USB keyboard. Others have built one on this chip.

    Add: key switches, diodes, keyboard case.

  • Car and robot CAN bus explorer

    Listen to messages on a CAN bus with the built-in controller. Learn how robots and cars pass data.

    Add: CAN transceiver module.

  • Open firmware oscilloscope study

    Explore the community OpenScope firmware for a pocket scope built on this chip. Learn how measuring tools work inside.

    Uses: Screen. Add: FNIRSI 2C53T pocket scope.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

WeAct publishes a schematic PDF for its Black Pill in an MIT-licensed repo, but no layout files. Artery's firmware library is BSD-3-Clause, and its PlatformIO platform is Apache-2.0. Code builds and flashes with open GNU and OpenOCD tools. The chip itself is a closed design.
