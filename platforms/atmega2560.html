ATmega2560

Microchip Technology (formerly Atmel)

The many-pinned chip inside the Arduino Mega and classic 3D printer boards.

Overview

The ATmega2560 is an 8-bit chip from Microchip, first made by Atmel. It is the brain of the Arduino Mega 2560 and many 3D printer boards. Its big draw is pins. The Mega offers 54 digital pins, 16 analog inputs, and four serial ports.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Projects that run out of pins on an Arduino Uno
  • 3D printers and CNC machines with the open Marlin firmware
  • Robots with many motors, buttons, and lights
  • Talking to several serial gadgets at once, such as GPS and a screen
  • Learners who already know the Uno and want more room

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Camera, voice, or AI projects with their large memory needs
  • Tiny wearables, as the Mega board is large and long
  • Many modern 3.3 volt sensors, which need a level shifter on its 5 volt pins

Specs in plain words

  • 16 MHz is a modest speed. It is plenty for reading sensors and moving motors.
  • 256 KB of flash holds a large program, such as full 3D printer firmware.
  • 8 KB of working memory holds a few hundred sensor readings at a time.
  • 86 input and output lines give you room for dozens of buttons, lights and motors.
  • Four serial ports let the board talk to four gadgets at the same time.
  • The chip can sleep on about a tenth of a millionth of an amp.
Full spec sheet
Processor8-bit AVR (megaAVR), 1 core at 16 MHz
Memory8 KB
Storage256 KB
Pins86 pins, 16 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), Debug port
ConnectorsPin headers
Built inbuttons
Power7 to 12 V barrel jack or 5 V over USB on the Arduino Mega 2560 Rev3
Deep sleep draw0.1 microamps

Values are for the chip. The Arduino Mega 2560 Rev3 exposes 54 digital pins and 16 analog inputs. The Mega uses a USB-B port with an ATmega16U2 USB-serial bridge. The chip has no native USB. The chip can address up to 64 KB of external memory. low_power_ua is the datasheet power-down figure at 1.8 V for the low-voltage V part. The 16 MHz speed grade needs 4.5 to 5.5 V.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Big LED scoreboard

    Wire a grid of LEDs to the many pins and add two buttons. Keep score at family game night in bright digits.

    Add: LED matrix panels, push buttons.

  • Reaction time game

    Light up one of many buttons at random and time each press. Friends race to beat the best score.

    Add: arcade buttons with lights, small buzzer.

  • Classroom weather station

    Read temperature, humidity, and light sensors all at once. Students watch the numbers change across a school day.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, light sensor, small screen.

  • Science fair data logger

    Record several sensors each second to a memory card. Students graph the results later in a spreadsheet.

    Add: SD card module, temperature sensor, light sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Garden bed watering helper

    Check soil moisture in several garden beds at once. The board switches a small pump on for each dry bed.

    Add: soil moisture sensors, relay module, small water pump.

  • Small CNC drawing machine

    Drive two stepper motors to move a pen across paper. The machine draws patterns and lettering from your files.

    Add: stepper motors, stepper drivers, pen holder, power supply.

  • Walking robot with many servos

    Control a dozen servo motors from one board. Your robot can walk, wave, or dance on a timed routine.

    Add: servo motors, servo power supply, robot frame.

  • Model railway control panel

    Use dozens of pins to read track switches and drive signal lights. The board runs a whole layout from one panel.

    Add: toggle switches, small LEDs, relay module.

  • Homemade MIDI drum pads

    Read many pressure pads and send notes out a serial port. A music program on your computer plays the drum sounds.

    Add: piezo pads, MIDI connector.

  • Hive weight logger

    Weigh a beehive with a load cell and save readings to a memory card. The board sleeps between readings to save battery.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: load cell with amplifier, SD card module, battery pack.

  • Escape room puzzle box

    Hide a lock behind a set of switches, keypads, and lights. Players must find the right combination to open it.

    Add: keypad, toggle switches, servo motor, small LEDs.

  • GPS trip tracker

    Listen to a GPS module on one serial port and a screen on another. The board shows speed and distance on a bike ride.

    Add: GPS module, small screen, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Build your own 3D printer

    Stack a printer driver shield on the Mega and load the open Marlin firmware. The board moves the motors and heats the nozzle.

    Add: 3D printer driver shield, stepper motors, stepper drivers, heated nozzle kit, power supply.

  • Pinball machine brain

    Read many target switches and flash lights around a wooden playfield. The board keeps score and plays sound effects.

    Add: micro switches, LED strip, small buzzer, solenoids, power supply.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

The Arduino Mega design files are public under a Creative Commons share-alike license. The Olimexino-2560 board is certified open hardware with KiCad files. The compiler and upload tools are open source, and the Arduino libraries use the LGPL. The chip itself is a closed Microchip design.
