Overview

The ATmega328P is an 8-bit chip from Microchip, first made by Atmel. It is the brain of the Arduino Uno R3, the Nano and the Pro Mini. Thousands of tutorials, add-on boards, and code libraries target it. That makes it the classic first chip for learners.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First electronics projects with lights, buttons, and sensors
  • Classrooms, thanks to a huge pile of free lessons
  • Small robots and motor projects
  • Simple CNC machines with the open GRBL firmware
  • Building your own Arduino on a breadboard

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Big screens, sound, or AI with their large memory needs
  • Projects with many sensors and lights, which can run out of pins

Specs in plain words

  • 16 MHz is a modest speed. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 32 KB of flash holds a program of a few hundred lines.
  • 2 KB of working memory is tiny. Long text and screen images fill it fast.
  • 1 KB of EEPROM keeps small settings safe after the power goes off.
  • The chip runs on 5 volts, which suits many classic sensors and motors.
  • The DIP version has legs that plug right into a breadboard or socket.

Deep sleep draw0.1 microamps

The chip supports 20 MHz at 4.5 to 5.5 V. Most boards run it at 16 MHz. As a result, clock_mhz shows 16. The TQFP and QFN packages have 8 ADC channels and the DIP package has 6. The Uno R3 uses a USB-B port and an ATmega16U2 as its USB-serial bridge. The Nano uses a Mini-B USB port and measures 45 x 18 mm. low_power_ua is the datasheet power-down figure at 1.8 V and 25 C. The chip has no native USB. As a result, boards add a separate USB-serial chip.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blinking traffic light

    Wire a red, a yellow, and a green LED to three pins. The board runs them through a real traffic light cycle.

    Add: red, yellow and green LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Night light that wakes up

    Read a light sensor and turn on an LED as the room gets dark. A great first project for a bedroom or hallway.

    Add: light sensor, LED, resistors.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil moisture sensor into a pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets too dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small buzzer.

  • Simple electronic piano

    Wire eight buttons to play eight notes on a speaker. Kids can compose and play short tunes.

    Add: push buttons, small speaker.

  • Room temperature display

    Show the temperature and humidity on a small screen. Place it in a classroom or a reptile tank.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED after a random pause and time the button press. Two players race to see who is faster.

    Add: LEDs, push buttons, small screen.

  • Mood lamp with color knobs

    Turn three knobs to mix red, green, and blue light. The lamp glows in any color you like.

    Add: RGB LED, three knobs, resistors.

Builder ideas

  • Obstacle avoiding robot car

    Mount a distance sensor on a two-wheel car. The car turns away from walls on its own.

    Add: robot car kit, motor driver, ultrasonic distance sensor.

  • Battery bird feeder counter

    Count visits with a motion sensor at a feeder. The chip sleeps between visits to make the batteries last.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Heartbeat light show

    Clip a pulse sensor to your finger. An LED strip glows in time with each beat.

    Add: pulse sensor, LED strip.

  • Garden watering timer

    Switch a small pump on for a few minutes each day. Your seedlings stay watered on a schedule.

    Add: relay module, small water pump, real-time clock module.

  • Weather data logger

    Save temperature and pressure readings to a memory card every few minutes. Graph a week of weather in a spreadsheet.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: pressure and temperature sensor, SD card module, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Desktop CNC with GRBL

    Load the open GRBL firmware and connect three stepper drivers. The board turns a small mill or laser into a computer-controlled tool.

    Add: CNC driver shield, stepper motors, stepper drivers, power supply.

  • Breadboard Arduino from scratch

    Put a bare chip on a breadboard with a crystal and a few parts. You learn what every part of an Uno does.

    Add: breadboard, 16 MHz crystal, capacitors, USB to serial adapter.

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

The Arduino Uno design files are public under the CC BY-SA 2.5 license. Several Adafruit and SparkFun boards with this chip are certified open hardware. The compiler and upload tools are open source, and the Arduino libraries use the LGPL. The chip itself is a closed Microchip design.
