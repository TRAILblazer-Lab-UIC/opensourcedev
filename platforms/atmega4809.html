Overview

The ATmega4809 is an 8-bit chip from Microchip. It is the brain of the Arduino Nano Every and the Arduino Uno WiFi Rev2. It is a roomier step up from the classic Arduino Uno chip. Beginners can use it with the free Arduino IDE and thousands of Arduino tutorials.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First Arduino projects on a small and cheap board
  • Robots, sensors and gadgets that fit in a breadboard
  • Projects that talk to several serial modules at once
  • Upgrading old Nano projects that ran out of memory

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio of its own
  • Sound files, cameras or screens with lots of pictures
  • Some older libraries written for the Uno chip, which may not compile

Specs in plain words

  • The 20 MHz processor is plenty for reading sensors and driving motors.
  • 6 KB of working memory is six times what the Arduino Uno chip has.
  • 48 KB of program space fits a long sketch of well over a thousand lines.
  • Four serial ports let it talk to four separate modules at the same time.
  • In deep sleep it draws about a tenth of a microamp, which lets batteries last a long time.
  • The Nano Every is about 4.5 by 1.8 centimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorAVR 8-bit (megaAVR 0-series), 1 core at 20 MHz
Memory6 KB
Storage48 KB
Pins41 pins, 16 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART)
ConnectorsMicro USB, Pin headers
Power5 V over USB or up to 21 V on VIN (Nano Every)
Deep sleep draw0.1 microamps
Size45 x 18 mm

Chip values are for the 48-pin ATmega4809, which has 41 GPIO and 16 ADC channels. The 40-pin PDIP version has 33 GPIO. Full 20 MHz speed needs 4.5 V to 5.5 V, and 10 MHz needs 2.7 V or more. The power-down figure is the datasheet typical value at 3 V with all peripherals stopped. Connectors and size are for the Arduino Nano Every, the most common board. The Nano Every uses a SAMD11 chip as its USB-to-serial bridge. The radios list is empty because the chip has no radio. The Uno WiFi Rev2 adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth through a separate u-blox NINA-W102 module.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Plant thirst light

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A red LED lights up once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, LED.

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a friend presses the button. The score shows on your computer screen.

    Add: push button, LED.

  • Classroom quiz buzzers

    Wire one big button for each team. The first press lights its team light and sounds a beep.

    Add: arcade buttons, LEDs, piezo buzzer.

  • Room temperature display

    Show the temperature and humidity on a small screen by your bed. Watch the room cool down overnight.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

Builder ideas

  • Tiny robot car

    Drive two small motors through a motor driver. Add a distance sensor and the car turns away from walls.

    Add: motor driver board, two small motors with wheels, distance sensor, battery pack.

  • Garage parking helper

    Measure the distance to your car bumper with a distance sensor. A light turns from green to red as the car gets close.

    Add: distance sensor, LED strip.

  • Pet feeder timer

    Turn a servo motor at set times to open a food flap. Your cat gets breakfast on schedule.

    Add: servo motor, real time clock module, food container.

  • Push button music box

    Play a different note for each button through a small buzzer. Kids can pick out simple tunes by ear.

    Add: push buttons, piezo buzzer.

  • Indoor air quality monitor

    Read a dust sensor and a carbon dioxide sensor on two of the chip's four serial ports. A light tells you to open a window.

    Add: dust sensor, carbon dioxide sensor, LED strip.

  • Battery garden weather station

    Read temperature and light in the garden every few minutes. The chip sleeps between readings to save its batteries.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, light sensor, battery pack, small screen.

  • Upgrade an old Nano project

    Swap an older Arduino Nano for a Nano Every in a finished build. You get six times the working memory for longer programs.

Expert ideas

  • Light chaser in hardware logic

    Set up the chip's custom logic blocks to blink a row of LEDs. The pattern keeps running with almost no work from the processor.

    Add: LEDs, resistors.

  • Bare chip breadboard build

    Put a bare ATmega4809 on a breadboard and program it with a UPDI programmer. Then design your own tiny circuit board around it.

    Add: UPDI programmer, breadboard, jumper wires.

  • Step through your own code

    Use the debugger on a Microchip Curiosity Nano board to pause a program line by line. Watch each value change as it runs.

How open is it?

Wild Open

Arduino shares the design files for the Nano Every and Uno WiFi Rev2 under CC BY-SA 4.0. The compiler, the upload tool and the MegaCoreX board package are open source, and MegaCoreX uses the LGPL-2.1 license. The chip itself is a closed Microchip design.
