ATSAM3X8E

Microchip Technology (formerly Atmel)

The 32-bit chip inside the Arduino Due, built for USB, audio, and car data.

Field marks: Look for a long Mega-sized board with 54 pins, two USB ports, and 3.3 volt pins ready to fry any 5 volt shield stacked on top.

Overview

The ATSAM3X8E is a 32-bit chip from Microchip, first sold by Atmel. It powers the Arduino Due, the first 32-bit Arduino from 2012. It stands out for its USB host port, two sound output pins, and two CAN buses for car data. It suits Arduino users who want more speed and pins than an Uno or Mega.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Arduino fans who want a faster board with lots of pins
  • Sound projects that need real audio output pins
  • Car hacking with CAN bus data
  • Projects with a USB keyboard, mouse, or MIDI device plugged in

Not great for

  • Old 5 volt shields, as its pins run at 3.3 volts and can be damaged
  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Buyers who want a fresh product, as the Due is aging and stock is drying up
  • Cameras, Linux, or AI work

Specs in plain words

  • 84 MHz is fast for a microcontroller and handles several jobs at once.
  • 512 KB of flash holds a large program with many libraries.
  • 96 KB of working memory is room for sound buffers and long lists of readings.
  • The 12-bit sensor reader splits a voltage into about 4,000 steps for fine readings.
  • The two 12-bit DAC pins output real voltage waves for sound.
  • The Due is about the size of a long playing card at 101 by 53 millimeters.

GPIO, ADC, connectors and onboard parts are for the Arduino Due, which exposes 54 digital pins (12 PWM), 12 analog inputs and 2 DAC outputs. The chip has 103 I/O lines, 16 ADC channels (one for the temperature sensor), 3 USARTs plus 2 UARTs, 2 TWI and 1 SPI in the LQFP144. SRAM is 64 KB plus 32 KB. Supply is 1.62 to 3.6 V. The Ethernet MAC needs an external PHY and is not wired on the Due. The 2.5 uA figure is chip backup mode with RTC and RTT. The Due size is the standard Arduino Mega footprint and was not confirmed in a fetched source.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blinking light pattern board

    Wire a row of LEDs to the many pins and program chasing light patterns. It makes a fine first project on a big board.

    Add: LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Desk weather station

    Read temperature, humidity, and air pressure from a sensor. Show the numbers on a small screen beside your desk.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, pressure sensor, small screen.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil moisture sensor into a pot. A buzzer sounds once the soil gets too dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Two-voice tone synthesizer

    Send sound waves out of the two built-in DAC pins to a small amplifier. Knobs change the pitch and the tone.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small amplifier, speaker, knobs.

  • Sound effect box for a play

    Store short sound clips on a memory card and play them out of a speaker. Buttons trigger each effect on cue.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: SD card module, small amplifier, speaker, push buttons.

  • Clap-reactive light show

    Read a microphone with the fast analog input. LED strip colors jump with each clap or drum beat.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone module, LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • USB keyboard for a game booth

    Plug a USB keyboard or mouse into the native USB port. The chip reads the keys and drives lights or a small game.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB on-the-go adapter.

  • Custom game controller

    Wire arcade buttons and a joystick to the pins. The native USB port makes the board act as a controller for a computer.

    Add: arcade buttons, joystick.

  • Science lab data logger

    Sample a sensor many thousands of times a second with the 12-bit ADC. Save the readings to a memory card for graphs.

    Add: SD card module, analog sensor.

  • Battery garden logger with sleep

    Wake the chip on a timer to log soil and air readings. Deep backup mode keeps power use tiny between readings.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, temperature sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

Expert ideas

  • Car data dashboard

    Read speed and engine data from a car's CAN bus with a transceiver module. Show the values on a small screen.

    Add: CAN transceiver module, OBD2 cable, small screen.

  • Robot arm controller

    Drive several servo motors from the many PWM pins. Record a set of moves and play them back.

    Add: servo motors, robot arm kit, 5 volt power supply.

  • Wired room sensor hub

    Add an Ethernet shield and publish room readings on your home network. A simple web page shows the latest values.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet shield, temperature sensor, network cable.

  • Simple 3D printer controller

    Pair the board with a printer shield and stepper drivers. Run open printer firmware on a homemade machine.

    Add: 3D printer shield, stepper motor drivers, stepper motors, 12 volt power supply.
