Overview

The ATSAMD21 is a 32-bit Arm chip from Microchip, first made by Atmel in 2014. It has built-in USB and runs at 48 MHz. Many boards show up as a USB drive. You save your code to the drive and it runs right away. It was the first popular chip for CircuitPython.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding with CircuitPython on a cheap board
  • Wearables and costumes, thanks to tiny sewable boards
  • USB gadgets like keyboards, mice, and MIDI controllers
  • Simple sound projects with the built-in analog output
  • Learning real step-by-step debugging on the Arduino Zero

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on most boards, as the chip has no radio
  • Large CircuitPython programs, which fill the 32 KB of working memory fast
  • Camera, voice, or AI projects with their heavy power needs
  • New long-term designs, as newer chips now get more attention

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is about three times the speed of an Arduino Uno.
  • 32 KB of working memory holds a few thousand sensor readings.
  • 256 KB of flash holds your program. Express boards add 2 MB more for CircuitPython files.
  • Built-in USB lets the board act as a drive, keyboard, mouse, or music device.
  • The 12-bit analog input reads sensors in finer steps than an Uno.
  • The chip runs on 3.3 volts, which suits most modern sensors.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorArm Cortex-M0+, 1 core at 48 MHz
Memory32 KB
Storage256 KB
Pins38 pins, 14 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), Digital audio (I2S), USB device, USB host, Debug port
ConnectorsMicro USB, Pin headers, Battery plug
Built incolor LED, battery charger, buttons
Power3.3 V logic. Boards take 5 V over USB, and some take 7 to 9 V on a barrel jack
Size51 x 23 mm

Chip values are for the 48-pin ATSAMD21G18, which has 38 GPIO, 14 ADC channels and six SERCOMs. The 32-pin ATSAMD21E18 used on the QT Py and Gemma M0 has 26 GPIO, 10 ADC channels and four SERCOMs. The 64-pin SAMD21J has 52 GPIO and 20 ADC channels. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Adafruit Feather M0 Express, a common board with micro-USB, a LiPo charger and a NeoPixel. Express boards add 2 MB of external SPI flash for CircuitPython files. That flash is not counted in flash_kb. The datasheet summary does not give a single standby current. As a result, low_power_ua is null.

Starter ideas

  • Glowing costume badge

    Sew a round board into a costume and add a ring of color LEDs. Touch a pad to change the light pattern.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: LED ring, small rechargeable battery, conductive thread.

  • Touch pad drum kit

    Use the built-in touch inputs as drum pads made of foil or fruit. The board plays a drum sound for each touch.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: aluminum foil, small speaker, alligator clips.

  • Shortcut button for your laptop

    Make the board act as a USB keyboard. One button press types a phrase or fires a shortcut.

    Add: large push button.

  • Plant thirst light

    Read a soil moisture sensor and glow red once the soil gets dry. A first CircuitPython project in a few lines.

    Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Rainbow desk lamp

    Drive an LED strip with a short CircuitPython program. Edit the colors and save the file to see changes right away.

    Add: LED strip.

  • Classroom quiz buzzers

    Wire one big button for each team. The first press lights its team color and plays a beep.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: arcade buttons, LED strip, small speaker.

Builder ideas

  • Clap counter

    Listen to a small microphone and count claps or loud sounds. Students see a sound level meter on LEDs.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED strip.

  • USB MIDI controller

    Wire knobs and buttons that send MIDI over USB. Play and tweak sounds in music software.

    Add: knobs, push buttons, project box.

  • Simple sound effects box

    Play short sound clips through a speaker from the built-in analog output. Add one button per sound for a game night.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker with amplifier, push buttons.

  • Pocket weather logger

    Log temperature and humidity to the board's own storage. Copy the file off like a USB stick later.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small rechargeable battery.

  • Bike light with turn signals

    Mount LED strips on a backpack with two handlebar buttons. Each button flashes the matching arrow.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: LED strips, push buttons, small rechargeable battery.

  • Step counter wristband

    Read a motion sensor on a tiny board strapped to your wrist. LEDs show progress toward a daily step goal.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, small rechargeable battery, wrist strap.

  • Theremin style noise maker

    Read a distance sensor and change the pitch of a tone. Wave your hand to play spooky music.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: distance sensor, small speaker with amplifier.

Expert ideas

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board in host mode. Turn each key press into lights or messages for a game.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB on-the-go adapter.

  • Step through your own code

    Use the debugger built into the Arduino Zero to pause a program line by line. Watch the values change as it runs.

How open is it?

Wild Open

Adafruit shares its SAMD21 board designs under CC BY-SA, and SparkFun shares its breakout under CC BY-SA 4.0. Several of these boards are certified open hardware. The compiler, Arduino core, and CircuitPython are open source under the LGPL and MIT licenses. The chip itself is a closed Arm-based design from Microchip.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA (Adafruit Feather M0 Express and other Adafruit M0 boards), CC BY-SA 4.0 (SparkFun SAMD21 Mini Breakout), Arduino Zero CAD files license not stated on the docs page · Software license: LGPL-2.1 (ArduinoCore-samd), MIT (CircuitPython)
