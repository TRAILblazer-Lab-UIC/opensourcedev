Overview

The ATSAMD51 is a fast 32-bit Arm chip from Microchip. It runs at 120 MHz and has built-in USB. Adafruit built its M4 board line on it, including the Feather M4 Express. Boards show up as a USB drive, and you save CircuitPython code straight to them. It shines at sound, lights, and small games.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding with CircuitPython on a quick board
  • Synths, drum machines, and other sound projects
  • LED strips, LED panels, and small handheld games
  • USB gadgets like keyboards and MIDI controllers
  • Battery projects with the charger built into Feather boards

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on most boards, as the chip has no radio
  • Voice assistants or real AI, which need far more memory
  • Saving settings in EEPROM, which the chip lacks
  • The lowest price, as newer chips now cost less

Specs in plain words

  • 120 MHz is about seven times the speed of an Arduino Uno.
  • 192 KB of working memory is room for big CircuitPython programs and sound buffers.
  • 512 KB of program space holds CircuitPython itself. Express boards add 2 MB or more for your files and sounds.
  • Two 12-bit analog outputs can play real audio straight to an amplifier.
  • Built-in USB lets the board act as a drive, keyboard, mouse, or music device.
  • The Feather M4 is about 5 by 2.3 centimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.

Chip values are for the 64-pin ATSAMD51J19 used on the Feather M4 Express and Metro M4. It has 512 KB flash, 192 KB SRAM and 51 I/O pins. The datasheet configuration table gives the J parts 16 channels on ADC0 and 8 on ADC1, and adc_channels sums them. The Grand Central M4 uses the 128-pin ATSAMD51P20 with 1 MB flash, 256 KB SRAM and up to 99 I/O. The Thing Plus SAMD51 uses the ATSAMD51J20 with 1 MB flash and 256 KB SRAM. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Adafruit Feather M4 Express. Express boards add 2 MB to 8 MB of QSPI flash that is not counted in flash_kb. The low-power figure is the typical Backup mode current at 3.3 V and 25 C with no RTC running. The camera_dvp entry reflects the parallel capture controller.

Adafruit shares its M4 board designs under CC BY-SA 3.0, and SparkFun shares the Thing Plus SAMD51 under CC BY-SA 4.0. Several of these boards are certified open hardware. The compiler, the LGPL Arduino core, and CircuitPython are open source. The chip itself is a closed Arm-based design from Microchip.
