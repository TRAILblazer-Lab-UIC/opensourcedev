ATtiny1616

Microchip Technology

A tiny 20-pin chip for touch buttons, sensors, and long-lasting battery gadgets.

Field marks: Look for a fingernail-sized speck on a little breakout, linked by plug-in cables and quietly feeling for touch.

Overview

The ATtiny1616 is a tiny 8-bit chip from Microchip with 20 pins. It can sense touch on its own, and it sips very little power in sleep. Makers program it from the Arduino IDE with the free megaTinyCore package. Adafruit sells it on a small breakout with plug-in cable ports. It suits patient tinkerers more than first-time beginners.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Touch buttons, sliders, and keypads
  • Small battery gadgets that sleep most of the time
  • Helper chips that give a bigger board extra pins
  • Shrinking a simple project into a tiny permanent build

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio
  • Screens, sound files, or anything that needs much memory
  • Newcomers who want a plug-in board that just works
  • Quick reprogramming, as it needs a separate programmer

Specs in plain words

  • The 20 MHz processor is plenty for blinking lights and reading a sensor.
  • 2 KB of working memory holds a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • 16 KB of program space fits a short sketch of several hundred lines.
  • Eighteen pins connect to the outside world, and up to twelve can sense touch on their own.
  • In deep sleep it draws about a tenth of a microamp, which lets a coin cell last a very long time.
  • The breakout costs about 5 US dollars, close to the price of a sandwich.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorAVR 8-bit (tinyAVR 1-series), 1 core at 20 MHz
Memory2 KB
Storage16 KB
Pins18 pins, 12 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART)
ConnectorsQwiic or STEMMA QT, Pin headers
Power2 V to 5 V on VIN (Adafruit breakout)
Deep sleep draw0.1 microamps

Chip values are for the 20-pin ATtiny1616, which has 18 GPIO. ADC0 has 12 channels and ADC1 adds 8 more on shared pins. As a result, adc_channels counts ADC0 only. The PTC touch controller takes over ADC0 while it is in use. Full 20 MHz speed needs 4.5 V to 5.5 V, and 10 MHz needs 2.7 V or more. The power-down figure is the datasheet typical value at 3 V with all peripherals stopped. Connectors and power are for the Adafruit ATtiny1616 Breakout, which has two STEMMA QT ports. Its size was not stated on the pages read.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Extra buttons for your board

    Plug the Adafruit breakout into a bigger board with a plug-in cable. Read extra buttons through its ready-made firmware.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: main microcontroller board, plug-in sensor cable, push buttons.

Builder ideas

  • Touch lamp switch

    Turn a metal plate into a touch pad with the built-in touch sensing. Tap it to switch an LED lamp on and off.

    Add: metal plate, LEDs, small battery pack.

  • Fruit piano

    Wire bananas or apples to the touch pins. Each fruit plays a different note on a small buzzer.

    Add: fruit, alligator clips, piezo buzzer.

  • Coin cell blinky badge

    Blink a few LEDs on a small badge for a school event. The chip sleeps between blinks and the coin cell lasts for weeks.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: LEDs, coin cell holder, pin back.

  • Dark sensing night light

    Read a light sensor and fade on an LED as the room gets dark. It makes a gentle guide light for night trips.

    Add: light sensor, LEDs, small battery pack.

  • Doorbell melody player

    Play a short tune on a buzzer at the press of a button. Write your own melody note by note.

    Add: push button, piezo buzzer.

  • Bike reflector blinker

    Flash a red LED in a steady pattern on the back of a bike. Deep sleep keeps the batteries going for months.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: red LEDs, small battery pack, push button.

  • Plant check stick

    Read a soil moisture sensor once an hour and blink an LED once the pot is dry. It sleeps the rest of the time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LED, small battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Touch keypad door chime

    Use the touch controller to read a 12 key touch pad. Each key plays its own sound on a buzzer.

    Add: touch keypad pad, piezo buzzer.

  • Solar supercap firefly

    Charge a supercapacitor from a small solar cell by day. Blink an LED all night like a firefly.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: supercapacitor, small solar cell, LED.

  • Build your own sensor module

    Write firmware that turns the chip into a helper for a bigger board. It reads a sensor and reports over a plug-in cable.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: sensor of your choice, plug-in sensor cable.

  • Small fan speed control

    Use the fine 12-bit timer to set the speed of a small fan. A knob turns the breeze up or down.

    Add: small fan, transistor, knob.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The tools are fully open. Code builds with avr-gcc through megaTinyCore, which uses the LGPL-2.1 license. The Adafruit breakout design is shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 and is OSHWA certified. The chip itself is a closed Microchip design.
