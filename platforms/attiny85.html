ATtiny85

Microchip Technology (originally Atmel)

A tiny eight-legged chip for small permanent light and sensor gadgets.

Field marks: Eight pins, five usable, 512 bytes of RAM, and one reset pin that every ATtiny85 owner eventually tries to turn into a sixth GPIO.

Overview

The ATtiny85 is a tiny chip with eight legs from Microchip, first made by Atmel in 2005. It runs small Arduino programs and sips very little power. Makers love it for shrinking a working breadboard project into a small permanent build. It suits patient tinkerers more than first-time beginners.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Shrinking a simple Arduino project into a tiny lasting build
  • Blinking lights, jewelry, and small art pieces
  • Battery gadgets that sleep most of the time
  • Learning how a microcontroller works at the lowest level

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio
  • Screens, sound files, or anything that needs much memory
  • Newcomers who want a plug-in board that just works
  • Projects with many buttons or sensors, as only five pins are free on most boards

Specs in plain words

  • The 20 MHz processor is plenty for blinking lights and reading a sensor.
  • 512 bytes of working memory holds about a sentence or two of text at once.
  • 8 KB of program space fits a short sketch of a few hundred lines.
  • Six pins connect to the outside world, and one of them doubles as reset.
  • In deep sleep it draws about a tenth of a microamp, which lets a coin cell last a very long time.
  • The Adafruit Trinket board is about 3 by 1.5 centimeters, roughly the size of a postage stamp.
Full spec sheet
Processor8-bit AVR RISC, 1 core at 20 MHz
Memory0.5 KB
Storage8 KB
Pins6 pins, 4 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI
ConnectorsMicro USB, Pin headers
Built inbuttons
Power1.8 to 5.5 V for the chip. Boards take 5 V over USB or up to 16 V on the Trinket

Chip values come from the Atmel 2586Q datasheet. The 20 MHz top speed needs 4.5 to 5.5 V. RAM is 512 bytes, shown as 0.5 KB. I2C and SPI come from the USI block, which needs software help. There is no hardware UART and no native USB. One of the six I/O lines is the reset pin. As a result, most boards expose five usable pins. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Adafruit Trinket 5V, which runs at 8 or 16 MHz from the internal oscillator. Bootloaders take about 1.5 to 2.75 KB of flash, leaving about 5.25 to 6 KB for sketches. The 0.1 µA power-down figure is at 1.8 V with the watchdog off.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Firefly jar night light

    Blink a few LEDs in slow random pulses inside a glass jar. It looks like fireflies drifting on a summer night.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: LEDs, coin cell holder, glass jar.

  • Flickering pumpkin candle

    Make yellow LEDs flicker like a real flame. Drop it inside a carved pumpkin for a safe glow.

    Add: yellow LEDs, small battery pack.

  • Dark sensing hallway light

    Read a light sensor and switch on an LED as the room gets dark. It makes a gentle guide light for night trips.

    Add: light sensor, LEDs, small battery pack.

Builder ideas

  • Glowing pendant or badge

    Drive a few color-changing LEDs in a pendant or a name badge. Pick slow rainbows or quick sparkles.

    Add: addressable RGB LEDs, small battery pack.

  • Plant thirst blinker

    Check a soil sensor once an hour and sleep in between. An LED blinks to say the pot needs water.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LED, coin cell holder.

  • Electronic pocket dice

    Press a button and watch LEDs show a random roll from one to six. It fits in a matchbox for board game night.

    Add: push button, LEDs, coin cell holder.

  • Tiny tune player

    Play a short melody on a buzzer at the press of a button. It makes a fun greeting card surprise.

    Add: piezo buzzer, push button, coin cell holder.

  • Fridge door alarm

    Watch a magnetic switch on the fridge door. A buzzer beeps after the door stays open too long.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, piezo buzzer, small battery pack.

  • Wiggling cat toy

    Sweep a servo arm with a feather at random times. Your cat gets a new target to pounce on.

    Add: servo motor, feather toy, small battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Shed temperature logger

    Wake up each hour and save a temperature reading in the chip's own memory. Read the week of readings back later on a computer.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, small battery pack.

  • USB shortcut key

    Use a USB stick style board to act as a tiny keyboard. One tap types a phrase or a shortcut on your computer.

    Add: push button.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

The tools are fully open. Code builds with the free avr-gcc compiler and uploads with avrdude or the GPL Micronucleus bootloader. Board designs such as the Adafruit Trinket are shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 and are OSHWA certified, but the chip itself is a closed Atmel design.
