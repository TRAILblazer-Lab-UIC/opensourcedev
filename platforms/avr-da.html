AVR DA

Microchip Technology

A roomier, faster 8-bit chip for Arduino-style sensor and LED projects.

Field marks: This 8-bit chip programs over a single UPDI wire with a cheap USB serial adapter and exactly one diode, the whole toolkit in one coin pocket.

Overview

The AVR DA is a family of 8-bit chips from Microchip. The biggest parts hold four times the program space of the chip in an Arduino Uno. They also add a finer sensor reader, a sound output pin, and touch sensing. The free DxCore package lets you program them in the Arduino IDE.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Arduino fans who have outgrown the Uno's memory
  • Sensor projects that need careful readings
  • Battery gadgets that sleep most of the time
  • Projects that talk to several serial devices at once

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Absolute beginners, as ready-made boards are rare in maker shops
  • Plugging straight into USB, as the chip has no USB of its own
  • Big screens, cameras, or AI work

Specs in plain words

  • 24 MHz is a quick speed for an 8-bit chip. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 128 KB of flash holds a program of a few thousand lines.
  • 16 KB of working memory is room for long text and decent LED strip animations.
  • The 12-bit sensor reader splits a voltage into about 4,000 steps for fine readings.
  • The chip runs on anything from 1.8 to 5.5 volts and works with old 5 volt parts.
  • Deep sleep draws under one millionth of an amp and stretches battery life a long way.
Full spec sheet
Processor8-bit AVR with hardware multiplier, 1 core at 24 MHz
Memory16 KB
Storage128 KB
Pins41 pins, 18 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART)
ConnectorsPin headers
Power1.8 to 5.5 V for the chip
Deep sleep draw0.65 microamps

Values are for the AVR128DA48 from datasheet DS40002183B. The 48-pin part has 41 GPIO, 18 ADC channels, 5 USARTs, 2 SPI, 2 I2C, 2 TCA timers, 4 TCB timers and 6 LUTs. The 64-pin part has 55 GPIO, 22 ADC channels and 6 USARTs. The 28-pin part has 23 GPIO, 10 ADC channels and 3 USARTs. The AVR64DA parts have 8 KB SRAM and the AVR32DA parts have 4 KB SRAM. Typical Power-Down current is 650 nA with all peripherals off. There is no native USB and no radio. Programming uses UPDI, a one-wire interface that does not fit the swd_jtag value. Connectors, onboard parts and size vary by board and were not confirmed.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Touch-sensitive night light

    Turn a strip of copper tape into a touch pad with the built-in touch sensing. A tap turns a soft LED glow on or off.

    Add: copper tape, LED, resistors.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil moisture sensor into a pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets too dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small buzzer.

  • Classroom traffic light

    Wire a red, a yellow, and a green LED to three pins. The chip runs a full traffic light cycle for a lesson on timing.

    Add: red, yellow and green LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Room comfort display

    Show temperature and humidity on a small screen. Set it on a desk or beside a pet tank.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

Builder ideas

  • Rainbow LED strip lamp

    Drive a strip of color LEDs with smooth fades and patterns. The extra memory holds longer light shows than a classic Uno.

    Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • Tiny tone synthesizer

    Send waves out of the built-in DAC pin to a small amplifier. Knobs change the pitch and the sound shape.

    Add: small amplifier, speaker, knobs.

  • Precise battery voltage meter

    Use the 12-bit ADC to read battery voltages in fine steps. Show the result on a small screen for a science lab.

    Add: resistors, small screen.

  • Battery bird feeder counter

    Count visits with a motion sensor at a feeder. The chip sleeps deeply between visits and the batteries last for months.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Garden weather logger

    Save temperature and pressure readings to a memory card every few minutes. Graph a week of garden weather later.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: pressure and temperature sensor, SD card module, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Many-port serial hub

    Use the extra serial ports to talk to a GPS module, a display and a computer at the same time. Each device gets its own line.

    Add: GPS module, serial display.

  • Logic gates without code

    Set up the built-in custom logic blocks to act like small logic chips. Buttons and LEDs show the truth tables for a class.

    Add: push buttons, LEDs, resistors.

  • Build your own breakout board

    Order a copy of the open Feather-style board design and solder it up. You learn how a chip becomes a usable board.

    Add: printed circuit board, soldering iron, small parts kit.
