AVR DB

Microchip Technology

A modern 8-bit chip with built-in amplifiers and mixed-voltage pins.

Field marks: Spot a 5 volt chip with a side door for 1.8 volt parts, three op-amps on board, and a breakout seller who is probably on a break.

Overview

The AVR DB is a family of modern 8-bit chips from Microchip. The largest parts hold 128 KB of program space and run at up to 24 MHz. Built-in op-amps boost weak sensor signals with no extra chips. One port can talk to 1.8 or 3.3 volt parts while the rest runs at 5 volts.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sensor projects with weak analog signals
  • Mixing classic 5 volt parts with modern low-voltage sensors
  • Battery gadgets that sleep most of the time
  • Arduino fans who want more memory than an Uno

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Plugging straight into USB, as the chip has no USB of its own
  • Shoppers who want maker-store boards, as hobby boards are rare
  • Screens with big graphics, cameras, or AI work

Specs in plain words

  • 24 MHz is a quick speed for an 8-bit chip. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 128 KB of flash holds a program of a few thousand lines.
  • 16 KB of working memory is room for long text and decent LED strip animations.
  • The 12-bit sensor reader splits a voltage into about 4,000 steps for fine readings.
  • The on-chip op-amps make tiny sensor signals big enough to measure.
  • Deep sleep draws under one millionth of an amp and stretches battery life a long way.

Values are for the AVR128DB48 from preliminary datasheet DS40002247A (2020). The 48-pin part has 41 GPIO (40 usable beside reset and UPDI per DxCore), 18 ADC channels, 5 USARTs, 2 SPI, 2 I2C, 3 op-amps, 2 zero-cross detectors and 6 LUTs. The 64-pin part has 55 GPIO, 22 ADC channels and 6 USARTs. The 28-pin part has 22 GPIO, 9 ADC channels, 3 USARTs and 2 op-amps. The AVR64DB parts have 8 KB SRAM and the AVR32DB parts have 4 KB SRAM. Unlike the DA, the DB has no Peripheral Touch Controller. Low-power value is the typical Power-Down current with all peripherals disabled. There is no native USB and no radio. Programming uses UPDI, a one-wire interface that does not fit the swd_jtag value. Connectors and onboard parts are for the AVR128DB48 Curiosity Nano, which has micro-USB, one user button, one LED, a 32.768 kHz crystal and a 16 MHz crystal.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Classroom traffic light

    Wire a red, a yellow, and a green LED to three pins. The chip runs a full traffic light cycle for a lesson on timing.

    Add: red, yellow and green LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil moisture sensor into a pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets too dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small buzzer.

  • Room comfort display

    Show temperature and humidity on a small screen. Set it on a desk or beside a pet tank.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

Builder ideas

  • Tiny tone player

    Send tones out of the built-in DAC pin to a small amplifier. Buttons play the notes of a simple tune.

    Add: small amplifier, speaker, push buttons.

  • Light meter for plants

    Boost a light sensor signal with a built-in op-amp. The chip reports how bright each windowsill gets.

    Add: light sensor, small screen.

  • Heartbeat sensor demo

    Amplify a pulse sensor signal with the on-chip op-amps. An LED flashes with each heartbeat for a health class.

    Add: pulse sensor, LED, resistors.

  • Old and new parts together

    Run the chip at 5 volts for classic parts. Talk to a modern 3.3 volt sensor on port C with no level shifter.

    Add: 3.3 volt sensor, 5 volt display.

  • Battery bird feeder counter

    Count visits with a motion sensor at a feeder. The chip sleeps deeply between visits and the batteries last for months.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Garden weather logger

    Save temperature and pressure readings to a memory card every few minutes. Graph a week of garden weather later.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: pressure and temperature sensor, SD card module, battery pack.

  • Window security sensor

    Watch a window with a magnet switch and a motion sensor. A buzzer sounds once the window opens at night.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, motion sensor, small buzzer.

Expert ideas

  • Precise scale for the kitchen

    Read a load cell through the differential ADC and an op-amp gain stage. Show the weight on a small screen.

    Add: load cell, small screen.

  • Many-port serial hub

    Use the extra serial ports to talk to a GPS module, a display, and a computer at the same time. Each device gets its own line.

    Add: GPS module, serial display.

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

DxCore builds with the free avr-gcc compiler and uploads with open tools, but its license is not clearly stated. Microchip publishes a schematic for the Curiosity Nano kit. No AVR DB board with open layout files and a license was found. The chip itself is a closed Microchip design.
