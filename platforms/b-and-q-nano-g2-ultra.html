Overview

The B&Q Nano G2 Ultra is a handheld LoRa radio that comes with Meshtastic already installed. It pairs a Nordic nRF52840 chip with a LoRa radio, a GPS receiver, and a 1.3 inch screen. It suits hikers, campers, and community groups who want to text without cell service. Its standout traits are long battery life and real hardware switches for GPS power and alerts.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends off the grid with the Meshtastic phone app
  • Sharing locations on a map with its built-in GPS
  • Multi-day trips on one battery charge
  • Travel across regions with its wideband antenna

Not great for

  • Wet weather, as the stacked case is not sealed
  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has Bluetooth only
  • Quick buying in the US, as it ships mainly from China
  • A ready-to-go kit, as the battery is sold separately

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF52840 runs at 64 MHz with 256 KB of memory. That is plenty for messages and sensor readings.
  • The radio averages about 14 milliamps. The maker says a 1200 mAh battery lasts about 5 days.
  • The built-in antenna covers 815 to 940 MHz. One radio fits most countries' LoRa bands.
  • The 1.3 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It shows messages, battery level, and position.
  • A GPS switch cuts power to the GPS chip. Turning it off saves about 22 milliamps.
  • The case is about 10 by 6 centimeters and weighs about 123 grams with a battery.

Clock, RAM and flash are nRF52840 chip values. The board adds a 16 Mbit QSPI flash. The Grove and Qwiic sockets are on the optional I2C extension board that plugs into the 10-pin FPC port (1x I2C, 6x GPIO, 3.3 V, GND). The speaker entry stands for the active buzzer used for message alerts. Measured draw from the maker is 14.24 mA average at 4.3 V with GPS and screen off, and 14.43 mA in LoRa receive. Size is for the cased device. Weight is about 123 g with the 1200 mAh battery. The battery is not included. The wiki lists Bluetooth 5.4 and GPS plus BDS. Meshtastic docs also list GLONASS.
