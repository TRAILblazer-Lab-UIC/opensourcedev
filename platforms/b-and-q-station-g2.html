Overview

The B&Q Station G2 is a Meshtastic base station board built on an ESP32-S3 and a LoRa radio. It adds a strong power amplifier and a very quiet receiver for long links. People used it as a fixed rooftop repeater or a node in a vehicle. The maker has discontinued it and now points buyers to the Station G3.

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 has two cores at 240 MHz and 8 MB of extra memory. That is a lot of room for Meshtastic and its web features.
  • The amplifier reaches about 35 dBm. That is a few watts, enough to carry messages many miles with a good antenna.
  • The low-noise receiver hears faint signals well. The maker measured about 4 dB better sensitivity than the Station G1.
  • It needs 15 V over USB-C PD or 9 to 19 V DC. Plain 5 V USB runs everything except the amplifier.
  • The board is about 11 by 6 centimeters. It fits in a small weatherproof box.

The gpio value counts the 8 user GPIOs on the 10-pin IO extension socket. The Grove GPS socket is a UART for an optional GPS module. On plain 5 V USB the 7.5 V PA supply stays off. As a result, everything works except the power amplifier. Measured draw is about 855 mA at 14.16 V in LoRa TX (35.5 dBm) and about 46 mA at 14.16 V in RX with the screen on. LoRa range is 815 to 940 MHz per the maker, sold in US915 and EU868 versions. Weight is about 87.5 g without antenna. Output above 30 dBm in the US 915 MHz ISM band needs an amateur radio license and unencrypted operation.

Things you could build

Starter ideas

  • Home base message station

    Set the board on a shelf with a window antenna. Your family's pocket radios reach it from far across the area.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen. Add: 15 V USB-C power supply.

  • Phone chat over Bluetooth

    Pair the station with your phone. You can read and send mesh messages from your desk.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: 15 V USB-C power supply.

Builder ideas

  • Rooftop neighborhood repeater

    Mount the board on a roof with a good antenna. It relays Meshtastic messages for radios across a whole town.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: outdoor LoRa antenna, antenna cable, weatherproof box, 15 V USB-C power supply.

  • Car-mounted mesh node

    Power the board from the car's 12 V system. It keeps a group convoy in touch on back roads.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: car power adapter, magnetic mount antenna.

  • Mesh status on Wi-Fi

    Connect the board to your home Wi-Fi. A browser on any computer can show mesh messages and nodes.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Wi-Fi. Add: 15 V USB-C power supply.

  • Community emergency hub

    Place the station at a library or fire hall. It links neighborhood radios during storms and outages.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen. Add: battery backup unit, outdoor LoRa antenna.

  • Base station with GPS

    Plug a GPS module into the Grove socket. The station shares its fixed spot on the mesh map.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Plug-in connectors. Add: Grove GPS module.

  • Weather station node

    Add a temperature and pressure sensor on the Qwiic socket. The station shares local weather across the mesh.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and pressure sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Club range experiments

    A radio club can compare antennas and record how far messages travel. The board's strong receiver shows small gains clearly.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: two or more antennas, coax cable.

  • Bare LoRa test sketch

    Load a minimal Arduino LoRa sketch from the community. You can study raw radio packets without Meshtastic.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen.

  • Licensed high-power link

    Licensed ham operators can raise the output above 30 dBm. A distant hilltop link becomes possible with encryption off.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: high-gain antenna, 15 V USB-C power supply.
