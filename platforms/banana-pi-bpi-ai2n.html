Overview

The Banana Pi BPI-AI2N is a vision AI kit with a small computer module and a carrier board. The module uses a Renesas RZ/V2N chip with four main cores and a DRP-AI accelerator. It suits engineers and advanced makers who build smart cameras, robots, and industrial vision gear. Its standouts are two camera ports, two Ethernet ports, and published schematics.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart cameras that spot objects in live video
  • Robots that need vision and real-time control
  • Industrial projects that face heat or cold
  • Recording video to a fast NVMe drive

Not great for

  • Beginners, as setup needs Linux images and vendor AI tools
  • Hobby camera projects under Armbian, as camera ISP access needs an NDA
  • Plug-and-play use, as a reviewer found the fan and camera failed under Armbian
  • Tight budgets, as the module alone costs over 200 US dollars

Specs in plain words

  • Four Cortex-A55 cores run at 1.8 GHz. That is enough for a full Linux desktop and several apps at once.
  • The DRP-AI accelerator gives 4 TOPS in normal use. It can run object detection on live video.
  • The 15 TOPS figure in ads is a best case for trimmed AI models.
  • It has 8 GB of memory and 32 GB of built-in storage. That is room for an operating system, models, and some video.
  • The module is about the size of a laptop memory stick. The carrier is about 10 by 8 centimeters.
  • It runs from about -40 to 85 C. It can work in a cold garage or a hot factory.

Size100 x 79 mm

Size is the carrier board. The module is 69.6 x 45 mm. ai_tops uses the dense figure. Banana Pi markets 15 TOPS, which is the sparse figure. The gpio value is the 40-pin header size. The bus functions on that header were not listed on pages I read. Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 4.2 come from an RTL8821CU USB module per CNX Software. The official page says only that a Wi-Fi module is onboard. Buttons are sleep, power and recovery. A USB-C console port and a PWM fan header are also present. Renesas lists H.264 encode and decode at 1080p60.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Banana Pi publishes PDF schematics and assembly drawings for both the module and the carrier. No layout files are posted, and no hardware license is named. The Yocto build layers are on GitHub, and the Linux kernel is GPL-2.0. The Renesas chip, its DRP-AI tools, and camera ISP access stay closed.
