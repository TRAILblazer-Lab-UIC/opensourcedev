Banana Pi BPI-M7

Banana Pi (SINOVOIP), co-designed with ArmSoM

A slim eight-core Linux computer with fast networking and an AI chip.

Field marks: Look for a thin RK3588 board with two names, two 2.5 gigabit ports, two USB-C ports, and exactly one that will power it.

Overview

The Banana Pi BPI-M7 is a slim single-board computer built on the Rockchip RK3588 chip. It is the same board that ArmSoM sells as the Sige7. It suits tinkerers who want a home server, a router, or a local AI box. Its standouts are two 2.5 gigabit network ports, an NVMe slot, and a 6 TOPS AI chip.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Home servers and media streaming
  • Network projects like routers and firewalls
  • Local AI with the RKNN toolkit
  • Running several virtual machines with Proxmox

Not great for

  • Beginners, as setup can need a Windows repair tool
  • Low budgets, as prices rose sharply in 2026
  • Fanless builds, as the chip slows down above about 80 C
  • Battery projects, as it needs a 9 to 15 V USB-C PD supply

Specs in plain words

  • It has eight cores, with four fast ones at 2.4 GHz. That is close to a budget laptop in speed.
  • The 6 TOPS NPU runs AI models on the board. It can spot objects in video or run a small chat model.
  • Memory ranges from 4 GB to 32 GB. More memory lets you run bigger AI models and more servers at once.
  • The NVMe slot uses four fast PCIe lanes. A solid-state drive there loads files much faster than an SD card.
  • Two 2.5 gigabit ports move data about two and a half times faster than normal home Ethernet.
  • The board is about 9 by 6 centimeters. That is about the size of a playing card.

Specs from the Banana Pi docs page. RAM and eMMC vary: the docs list 8, 16 or 32 GB RAM and 32, 64 or 128 GB eMMC with 8 GB and 64 GB as default. The official shop and ArmSoM list 4 GB/32 GB, 8 GB/64 GB and 32 GB/128 GB variants. The 40-pin header carries UART, SPI, I2C, I2S, PWM, CAN and one SARADC input (pin 22). The exact count of free GPIO lines was not stated. USB is one USB 3.0 Type-A, one USB 2.0 port and one USB-C 3.1 port with DP 1.4 and OTG. A second USB-C port is for power only. Other parts are a 5 V PWM fan header, an RTC battery connector (HYM8563TS), a headphone output, power, reset and Maskrom buttons and two LEDs. The PCB is 12 layers and the board weighs 46.6 g. The JTAG listing reflects JTAG alternate functions on header pins 8 and 10.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Classroom Linux desktop

    Hook up a monitor, keyboard, and mouse. Students can browse, code, and learn Linux on one small board.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: monitor, keyboard and mouse, HDMI cable.

  • Music practice player

    Connect headphones to the audio jack. The board loops songs and slows them down for practice.

    Uses: Makes sound, Runs Linux. Add: headphones.

  • Digital art display

    Drive a large TV over HDMI. The board shows a rotating gallery of student art in a hallway.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: TV or monitor, HDMI cable.

Builder ideas

  • Home media server

    Put movies and music on an NVMe drive. The board streams them to TVs and phones around the house.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe drive, 12 V USB-C PD charger, heatsink with fan.

  • Family photo backup box

    Run a photo backup app on the board. Phones copy new pictures to the drive each night over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Living room game console

    Install a retro game emulator. Plug in game controllers and play classic games on the TV.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB game controllers, HDMI cable.

  • Smart home control hub

    Run a home automation app. It links Wi-Fi and Bluetooth lights, plugs, and sensors in one dashboard.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth.

  • Science data logger

    Wire sensors to the 40-pin header. Python scripts record readings and plot them over time.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

  • Community file share

    Set up a shared drive at a makerspace. Members store project files on the fast local network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe drive.

Expert ideas

  • Fast home router

    Use the two 2.5 gigabit ports to build a custom router. Linux tools filter ads and guard the network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: heatsink with fan.

  • Local AI chat helper

    Run a small language model on the board. You can ask questions without sending data to the cloud.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip. Add: heatsink with fan, NVMe drive.

  • Smart security camera

    Connect a MIPI camera and run object detection on the NPU. The board alerts you to people at the door.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: MIPI camera module.

  • Home lab for virtual machines

    Install Proxmox and run several small servers on one board. It is a quiet way to practice IT skills.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Voice note transcriber

    Plug a microphone into the board. A speech model turns spoken notes into text files.

    Uses: Hears sound, Runs Linux, AI chip. Add: microphone.

  • Bird feeder AI camera

    Point a camera at a feeder. The NPU names each bird and logs a photo for later.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: MIPI camera module, weatherproof box.

    Follow the official guide to add a Hailo-8 card in the M.2 slot. Vision models get far more AI power.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip, Camera. Add: M.2 AI accelerator card, MIPI camera module.

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Banana Pi shares a schematic PDF, board outlines, and a 3D model. No layout files or hardware license are posted. The Linux kernel and U-Boot forks are open under GPL-2.0, and Armbian supports the board. The Rockchip chip, the NPU runtime, and some GPU and boot parts stay closed.
