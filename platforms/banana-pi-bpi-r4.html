Banana Pi BPI-R4

Banana Pi (Shenzhen SINOVOIP)

A powerful router board for building your own fast home network.

Overview

The Banana Pi BPI-R4 is a router development board built on a MediaTek Filogic 880 chip. It has two fiber cages for 10 gigabit links and four wired network ports. It runs OpenWrt and Debian Linux. It suits network tinkerers who want a fast and flexible home router.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building a very fast home router with OpenWrt
  • Wi-Fi 7 access points with the add-on card
  • 4G or 5G internet gateways
  • Small file servers with an NVMe SSD
  • Learning how networks really work

Not great for

  • Beginners who want a ready router in a box
  • Sensor and gadget projects with lots of pins
  • Quiet shelves. The chip runs hot and needs a fan.
  • Tight budgets. The full kit costs about 370 US dollars.

Specs in plain words

  • Four Cortex-A73 cores at 1.8 GHz route traffic for a busy house with ease.
  • 4 or 8 GB of memory is plenty for routing, filters and a file share together.
  • Two SFP cages take fiber modules for 10 gigabit speeds, about ten times a common home port.
  • Four Gigabit ports connect wired computers, game consoles, and TVs.
  • Wi-Fi is not built in. A mini PCIe card adds Wi-Fi 7.
  • The board is about 15 by 10 centimeters, close to the size of a paperback book cover.

RAM is 4 GB on the BPI-R4-4G and 8 GB on the BPI-R4-8G. The 4E and 8E variants have PoE support with no PoE module, and the 4P and 8P variants have the PoE module fitted. flash_kb is the 8 GB eMMC. There is also 128 MB SPI-NAND. Wi-Fi is not onboard and needs a mini PCIe card such as the BPI-R4-NIC-BE14. Cellular needs an M.2 Key-B modem. The 26-pin header carries UART, I2C, SPI, PWM and I2S. Its free GPIO count was not stated. The board weighs 250 g. A 1.25 mm RTC battery connector is fitted.

How open is it?

Open Board

Banana Pi publishes a schematic archive, DXF outlines, and 3D case files. No board layout or Gerber files are public, and no hardware license is stated. The Linux and OpenWrt code is open under GPL-2.0. MediaTek keeps its best Wi-Fi 7 driver closed, and an open MT76 driver offers a fully open path with fewer Wi-Fi features.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: GPL-2.0 (Linux and OpenWrt BSP), MediaTek MP Wi-Fi SDK driver closed
