Banana Pi BPI-SM10

Banana Pi (Sinovoip) with SpacemiT

A RISC-V developer kit with eight AI cores for running language models at home.

Field marks: Look for a boxy kit with a cooler, a 19 volt barrel jack, and a price tag inflated by the 2026 memory market.

Overview

The Banana Pi BPI-SM10 is a developer kit built from a K3-CoM260 compute module and a carrier board. Its SpacemiT K3 chip has eight RISC-V cores plus eight AI cores rated at 60 TOPS. Banana Pi says it runs 30B-parameter language models at more than 10 words per second. It suits experienced Linux users who want local AI on the new RISC-V chip family.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running local language models and chatbots with no cloud
  • Camera AI projects with two camera connectors
  • Exploring RISC-V Linux on one of the first RVA23 boards
  • Moving designs from a Jetson Orin Nano carrier

Not great for

  • First-time builders, as setup needs a serial cable and a flashing tool
  • Tight budgets, as kits start at about 370 to 450 US dollars
  • Wireless out of the box, as Wi-Fi needs an extra M.2 card
  • Battery projects, as the module draws 18 to 35 watts

Specs in plain words

  • 60 TOPS of AI power is enough to spot objects in live video or chat with a mid-sized language model.
  • 8, 16, or 32 GB of memory sets how big a model fits. The 32 GB kit holds the largest ones.
  • 128 GB of built-in storage holds the operating system and several AI models.
  • Three M.2 slots take a fast SSD, a second drive, and a Wi-Fi card.
  • Two camera connectors and a display connector let it see and show results without USB adapters.
  • The 40-pin header talks to sensors and small add-on boards.

RAM is the base 8 GB kit. All shop variants list 128 GB storage, taken as onboard UFS. 60 TOPS is INT4 per CNX Software. The module draws 18 to 35 W. Size includes feet and cooler, with a height of 35 mm. The GPU is an Imagination BXM4-64-MC1 per CNX. Wi-Fi needs an M.2 2230 E-key card. The getting-started guide shows a Realtek RTL8852BE card. The module also supports CAN-FD, eSPI and SDIO, but carrier exposure was not confirmed. ameriDroid describes dual Gigabit Ethernet and Wi-Fi 6, which conflicts with the official carrier spec.

Key terms on this page

AI model
An AI model is a trained program that spots patterns and makes guesses.
Carrier board
A carrier board holds a compute module and adds ports.
Cloud
The cloud means computers in big data centers that you reach over the internet.
Compute module
A compute module is the brain of a computer on a small card.
Edge AI
Edge AI means running AI on the device itself.
GPU
A GPU is a chip part built to do many small math jobs at once.
MIPI DSI
MIPI DSI is a fast link between a processor and a screen.
On-device language model
An on-device language model is a chatbot-style AI that runs on local hardware.
Operating system
An operating system is the main software that runs a computer.
RISC-V
RISC-V is a free and open set of rules for how a processor works.
TOPS
TOPS stands for trillion operations per second.
USB-to-serial chip
A USB-to-serial chip lets a computer talk to a board's UART over USB.
