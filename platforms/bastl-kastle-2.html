Overview

Kastle 2 is a pocket-size sound instrument from Bastl Instruments in the Czech Republic. One box can run FX Wizard effects, the Wave Bard sampler, or the Alchemist synth. It works with no code at all and runs on three AA batteries. Coders can also write new sound apps with its open C++ code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Musicians who want a cheap-to-run portable effects box or sampler
  • Learning how sound and synths work by turning knobs and patching wires
  • Playing music outdoors on battery power
  • Coders who want to write their own audio effects

Not great for

  • People with big fingers, as reviewers found the tiny header patch bay fiddly
  • General gadget projects, as it has no radio and few spare pins
  • Budget buyers, as it costs about 150 to 195 US dollars
  • Cloning the board, as Bastl keeps the layout files private

Specs in plain words

  • 44 kHz 16-bit stereo audio is close to CD sound quality.
  • 18 analog inputs on the patch bay let wires, sensors, or other gear change the sound.
  • 8 MB of storage holds the firmware and a set of short sound samples.
  • Three AA batteries last about 15 to 18 hours of play.
  • USB MIDI and sync jacks keep it in time with other music gear.

Specs come from the kastle2 repo README. The RP2040 runs at 176 MHz in this design in place of the stock 133 MHz. adc_channels counts the 18 patchable analog inputs (2 direct and 16 through a multiplexer). It is not raw RP2040 ADC pins. Analog outputs are filtered 10-bit PWM. Inputs and outputs work in a 0 to 5 V range. Size and 132 g weight come from the Thomann listing for the assembled FX Wizard. The audio codec part was not named on a fetched page.

Starter ideas

  • Pocket effects for any instrument

    Run a guitar, keyboard, or phone through the FX Wizard effects. Add echo, delay, and more on the go.

  • Park bench beat making

    Load your own sounds into Wave Bard and play patterns anywhere. Three AA batteries last a full day.

    Uses: Battery ready, Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • First synth sound design

    Turn knobs on the Alchemist synth and patch wires to change the sound. It is a hands-on way to learn how synths work.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • Classroom sound science

    Let students hear how delay, pitch, and filters change a voice. Compare the results with a tone from a phone.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone with line output, small powered speaker.

  • Sample field recordings

    Record birdsong or rain on a phone and load the clips with the web sample loader. Play them back as music.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: USB-C cable.

Builder ideas

  • Synced jam with a drum machine

    Link the sync jacks or USB MIDI to a drum machine. Both boxes then keep the same tempo.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small drum machine, sync cable.

  • Light-controlled sound

    Patch a light sensor into an analog input on the header. Waving your hand over it bends the sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: light sensor, jumper wires.

  • Build it from the DIY kit

    Solder the DIY kit and end up with a working instrument. It is a satisfying weekend project.

    Add: soldering iron, solder.

  • Generative ambient box

    Patch the outputs back into the inputs and let the sound change slowly on its own. Leave it running in a gallery corner.

    Uses: Makes sound, Battery ready. Add: small powered speaker.

Expert ideas

  • Write your own effect in C++

    Use the open DSP library to code a new effect, such as a bit crusher. Flash it over USB and play.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: USB-C cable.

  • Eurorack bridge

    Use the 0 to 5 V analog outputs to control a modular synth. The same firmware also runs on the Citadel module.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: patch cable adapters, modular synth.
