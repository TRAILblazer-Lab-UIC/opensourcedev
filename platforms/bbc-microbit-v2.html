Overview

The BBC micro:bit V2 is a small classroom computer from the Micro:bit Educational Foundation. It has a grid of lights, two buttons, motion sensors, a microphone, and a speaker. Students code it in a web browser with blocks, JavaScript or Python. Its built-in radio lets boards talk to each other for games and group projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding lessons in school
  • Radio games with several boards
  • Simple science experiments and data logging
  • Small robots with a carrier board
  • Music and sound projects

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the board has no Wi-Fi
  • Driving motors straight from the board without a carrier board
  • Big screens or cameras
  • Projects that need many free pins, as many pins are shared

Specs in plain words

  • 64 MHz is a modest speed. It can juggle a few jobs at once.
  • 128 KB of working memory is enough for games and sensor programs.
  • 512 KB of flash holds your program and a small Python script.
  • The 5 by 5 light grid shows letters, icons and simple pictures.
  • The board is about 5 by 4 cm, close to the size of a credit card cut in half.
  • Two AAA batteries can power it away from a computer.

Size51.6 x 42 mm

Values are for the nRF52833 application processor. A separate interface chip (KL27 on V2.00, nRF52820 or nRF52833 on V2.2x) handles USB and is not user programmable in normal use. GPIO count is the 19 numbered edge pins (P0 to P16, P19, P20) from the schematic page, many shared with the LED matrix, buttons and I2C. The analog-capable pin count was not confirmed on a page I read. The regulator gives 190 mA to accessories. Thickness is 11.65 mm. The edge connector is not a standard header. As a result, connectors lists only USB and battery. The micro-USB connector type comes from common knowledge of the board and was not stated on a page I read.

Things you could build

18 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Name badge

    Scroll your name across the light grid. Press a button to show a smiley face.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Shake to roll dice

    Shake the board and the light grid shows a number from one to six. Use it for any board game.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Step counter

    Wear the board on your ankle and count steps. Press a button to see the total.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready, Screen. Add: battery pack, strap.

  • Digital compass

    Use the magnetometer to find north. An arrow on the light grid points the way on a hike.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: battery pack.

  • Classroom noise meter

    The microphone listens to the room. The light grid fills up as the class gets louder.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Radio messenger

    Two boards send short messages to each other by radio. Friends can pass notes across the room.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Clip two wires into the soil with crocodile clips. The board plays a sad tune once the soil gets dry.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: crocodile clip wires, two nails.

  • Simple tune player

    Program a song with music blocks and play it on the built-in speaker. Touch the logo to start it.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Reaction timer game

    Wait for the light grid to flash and press a button fast. The board shows your reaction time.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Rock paper scissors duel

    Shake two boards at once and each picks a move. The radio tells each player who won.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Temperature data logger

    Record the temperature every few minutes on the board. Students graph how a fridge or garden changes.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: battery pack.

  • Garden frost warning

    Place the board outside in a box. It beeps loudly once the air gets close to freezing.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound, Battery ready. Add: battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Simple robot car

    Plug the board into a robot carrier with motors. Steer it with blocks or a second board as a remote.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: robot carrier board with motors, battery pack.

  • Light-up costume

    Clip an LED strip to the edge pads. The lights change color as you dance.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: LED strip, crocodile clip wires, battery pack.

  • Phone-controlled game buttons

    Pair the board with a phone over Bluetooth. Its buttons work as a remote for a phone app.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Class voting machine

    Each student votes with a button on their own board. One board collects every vote by radio and shows the result.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Clap counter science test

    Count claps or loud sounds with the microphone. Compare how noise changes through the school day.

    Uses: Hears sound.

Expert ideas

  • Python sensor station

    Write MicroPython code to read several sensors at once. Send the readings to a computer for a science fair.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: extra sensors, crocodile clip wires.
