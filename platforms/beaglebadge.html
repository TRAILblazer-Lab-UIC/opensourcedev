BeagleBadge

BeagleBoard.org Foundation

A battery-powered Linux computer shaped like a conference badge with an e-paper screen.

Field marks: Spot it hanging from a lanyard at tech events, its gray e-paper face showing a name long after the battery naps.

Overview

BeagleBadge is a small Linux computer built into a wearable badge. It has a 4.2 inch e-paper screen, a battery charger, and three radios for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and LoRa. It suits makers and students who want to learn embedded Linux or Zephyr on a fun gadget. The full board design files are open for anyone to study.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Interactive name badges at events and meetups
  • Learning embedded Linux and Zephyr on real hardware
  • Long-range LoRa messaging away from phone networks
  • Sensor projects with plug-in Qwiic and Grove parts
  • Battery-powered displays that stay readable in sunlight

Not great for

  • Desktop Linux apps, as 256 MB of memory is small
  • Fast moving graphics, as e-paper refreshes slowly
  • Total beginners, as setup needs an OS image and some coding
  • Buyers in a hurry, as stores still showed pre-orders in late 2026

Specs in plain words

  • Two Arm cores at 1.25 GHz give it enough speed for a lean Linux system and small programs.
  • 256 MB of memory fits light tools and simple screens. It is too small for a web browser.
  • The 4.2 inch e-paper screen is about the size of a playing card. It keeps its picture with no power.
  • Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth LE connect it to home networks and phones.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages across a park or campus with no internet.
  • Qwiic and Grove ports let you snap on sensors with no soldering.

RAM is a single ISSI IS43LQ16128A 2 Gbit LPDDR4 part (256 MB), per the V1.0 BOM, README, TI page and Zephyr docs. The Seeed listing says 2 GB, which looks like a Gb and GB mix-up. Storage is a 256 Mbit (32 MB) ISSI OSPI flash plus a 32 Kbit EEPROM and microSD. The BeagleBoard, DigiKey, Seeed and CNX pages list 4 GB eMMC, but the V1.0 BOM has no eMMC part and the README and TI page omit it. Store pages also list a humidity sensor, two 7-segment displays and a mikroBUS header. The V1.0 BOM and README show none of these. As a result, they are left out here. The README lists BLE 5.4 (CC3301 silicon). Store and Zephyr pages say Bluetooth 5.2. Size is from CNX Software. JTAG is through an edge connector per the Zephyr docs. The MIPI DSI connector appears on the official page and Zephyr docs.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Name badge that updates itself

    Show your name, talk title, and a QR code on the e-paper screen. Change the details from your laptop over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi.

  • Art frame with slow changes

    Show a new black and white drawing on the screen every hour. It hangs on a wall like a tiny gallery.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Bird sighting tally

    Press a button each time you spot a bird type on a nature walk. The badge keeps the count and saves it to the card.

    Uses: Battery ready, Screen. Add: microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Event schedule on your chest

    Pull the day's talk schedule over Wi-Fi and show the next session. Use the joystick to scroll through the day.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi.

  • Room weather station

    Plug a weather sensor into a Qwiic port and show temperature and humidity on the screen. The e-paper keeps the reading visible with almost no power.

    Uses: Screen, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Garden soil check

    Add a soil moisture sensor through the Grove port. The badge shows a happy or thirsty plant face on the screen.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Step and tilt tracker

    Use the built-in motion sensor to count steps or tilts during a walk. Show the total on the screen at the end.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Daylight logger for science class

    Record light levels every few minutes with the onboard light sensor. Save the data to the microSD card for a graph later.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: microSD card.

  • Tilt maze game

    Tilt the badge to roll a ball through a maze on the screen. The motion sensor reads each tilt.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Pocket Linux learning lab

    Log in over USB and practice Linux commands on a real tiny computer. It is a gentle way to learn how servers and gadgets work.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Keyboard-powered note pad

    Plug a small USB keyboard into the USB-A port and type notes onto the screen. The e-paper is easy to read in bright sun.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen, Runs Linux. Add: small USB keyboard.

Expert ideas

  • Off-grid text messenger

    Send short messages to a friend's badge over LoRa radio. It works at campsites and festivals with no phone signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen, Battery ready. Add: LoRa antenna.

  • Mesh network for a meetup

    Link several badges into a small radio mesh. Each badge passes messages along to reach the others.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: LoRa antenna.

  • Bluetooth phone notifier

    Pair your phone over Bluetooth LE and show new message alerts on the badge. The buzzer gives a short beep for each one.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

How open is it?

Open Board

The board schematic, layout, gerbers, and parts list are public under CC BY-SA 4.0. The docs use the MIT license and the software runs on open Linux and Zephyr. The TI processor is closed, and the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip needs closed TI firmware.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA-4.0 · Software license: MIT (docs), Linux GPL-2.0 and Zephyr Apache-2.0 for the software stacks
