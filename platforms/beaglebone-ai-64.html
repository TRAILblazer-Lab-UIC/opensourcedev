BeagleBone AI-64

BeagleBoard.org

An open hardware Linux board with a strong built-in AI engine for camera vision.

Field marks: A heatsinked board with two long cape headers, two camera ports, and a spec sheet listing more DSPs, accelerators, and real-time cores than most owners can name.

Overview

BeagleBone AI-64 is an open hardware Linux computer from BeagleBoard.org built with Seeed Studio. It launched in 2022 with a Texas Instruments chip made for cars and robots. Special AI cores reach up to 8 TOPS for camera vision work. It suits makers, students, and robot builders who already know some Linux.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Camera vision projects that spot and count objects
  • Robots that need AI and steady motor timing on one board
  • Learning embedded Linux on a certified open hardware design
  • Reusing classic BeagleBone capes for sensors and motors
  • Two camera projects that measure depth

Not great for

  • First-time builders who want plug-and-play ease
  • Battery projects with its hungry 5 volt 3 amp power needs
  • Wireless projects without an added Wi-Fi card
  • Tight budgets, with prices near 186 to 227 US dollars

Specs in plain words

  • Two fast 2 GHz main cores run Linux and everyday programs at a good pace.
  • The 8 TOPS AI engine can check many camera frames each second for objects.
  • 4 GB of memory holds Linux, an AI model, and your own program at once.
  • 16 GB of built-in storage holds the operating system, and a microSD slot adds more room.
  • Two camera ports let you plug in a pair of cameras for depth or wide views.
  • The board is about 10 by 8 centimeters, a bit bigger than a postcard folded in half.

Size102.5 x 80 mm

GPIO (72 digital pins) and ADC (7 analog inputs) come from the CNX Software spec list for the cape headers. The PCB is 102.5 x 80 mm and the full board is 104 x 83 x 37 mm per the docs. Video out is Mini DisplayPort, which has no matching interface value. As a result, it is left out of interfaces. The M.2 E-key slot takes a Wi-Fi card but no radio is fitted. The 16-bit LCD interface on the headers is recorded as lcd. GPU is PowerVR Rogue 8XE GE8430.

Starter ideas

    Plug in a screen, keyboard, and mouse to explore Debian Linux. Students practice the command line on a real open board.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: Mini DisplayPort to HDMI adapter, screen, keyboard, mouse.

  • Blinking LED first lesson

    Wire an LED to a header pin and blink it with a short Python script. It is a calm first step into Linux hardware.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: LED, resistor, jumper wires.

  • Board temperature web page

    Read the onboard temperature sensor and show it on a small web page. Anyone on your home network can check how warm the board runs.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: network cable.

  • Club room door sign

    Show meeting times and news on a screen by the door. Members update it from any computer on the wired network.

    Uses: Screen, Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: screen, Mini DisplayPort to HDMI adapter, network cable.

BeagleBone AI-64 is OSHWA certified as US002120. Its schematic, board layout, parts list, and 3D model are public under CC-BY-4.0 on OpenBeagle. Debian and the Linux kernel are open under GPL. The TI chip is closed, and parts of the AI and graphics software likely rely on closed driver files.
