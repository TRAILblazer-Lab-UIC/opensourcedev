Overview

BeaglePlay is an open hardware Linux computer from BeagleBoard.org, built with Seeed Studio. It has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and a long-range sub-GHz radio on one board. Grove, Qwiic, and mikroBUS ports let sensors plug in without a breadboard. It suits makers, teachers, and students who want a fully open board for smart home and sensor projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Plug-in sensor projects with no wiring
  • Smart home hubs with Zigbee and Bluetooth
  • Long-range sensor networks around a yard or farm
  • Learning Linux and Python on open hardware
  • Studying a fully open board design

Not great for

  • Heavy AI or vision models. It has no AI accelerator.
  • Battery gadgets that sleep for months
  • Absolute beginners who want a two-minute start
  • Old BeagleBone add-on capes. The big cape headers are gone.

Specs in plain words

  • Four 1.4 GHz cores run Debian Linux with a desktop.
  • 2 GB of memory handles a web page, a few sensors and a camera stream at once.
  • 16 GB of built-in storage holds the operating system and many logs.
  • The sub-GHz radio reaches sensor nodes up to about a kilometer away.
  • Grove, Qwiic, and mikroBUS ports connect thousands of ready-made add-on modules.
  • The board is about 8 by 8 centimeters, close to the size of a coaster.

Size80 x 80 mm

The PCB is 80 x 80 mm and the overall board is 82.5 x 80 x 20 mm, 55.3 g. Typical draw is 400 mA at 5 V. The 2 ADC channels come from an external ADC102S051 SPI ADC. The docs text says 10-bit while a figure caption says 12-bit. OLDI (LVDS) display is recorded as lcd. CAN and I2S (McASP) come out on the mikroBUS socket. The mikroBUS socket is recorded as headers. The GPU is a PowerVR Rogue AXE-1-16M. The board has an RTC with a coin-cell backup socket and Tag-Connect JTAG pads. No total GPIO count was given on the pages I read.

BeaglePlay is OSHWA certified as US002174. Its schematics, board layout, Gerbers, and parts list are public under a Creative Commons license. The Debian image, Linux kernel, and radio firmware are open. The Wi-Fi firmware and graphics driver use closed files.

Key terms on this page

Bill of materials
A bill of materials is the full list of parts on a board.
Breadboard
A breadboard is a plastic block full of small holes linked in rows.
Gerber files
Gerber files are the standard files sent to a PCB factory.
Grove
Grove is a plug-in system from Seeed Studio.
Node
A node is one device in a network.
NPU
An NPU is a chip part made just for running AI models.
Open Board
Open Board is this guide's middle openness label.
Operating system
An operating system is the main software that runs a computer.
OSHWA certification
OSHWA certification is a free check run by the Open Source Hardware Association.
Qwiic
Qwiic is a small four-pin plug system from SparkFun.
Sub-GHz
Sub-GHz means radio bands below one gigahertz.
Zigbee
Zigbee is a low-power mesh radio for smart home devices.
