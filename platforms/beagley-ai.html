Overview

BeagleY-AI is a credit-card sized Linux computer from BeagleBoard.org, built with Seeed Studio. A Texas Instruments chip pairs four processor cores with AI helpers rated at 4 TOPS. It uses the familiar 40-pin header, and its full design files are public. It suits makers and students who want vision AI on a truly open board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Camera projects with on-board AI
  • Learning Linux and Python on open hardware
  • Driving up to three screens at once
  • Robots that need a steady real-time helper core
  • Reusing many 40-pin add-on boards

Not great for

  • Absolute beginners who want a quick plug-and-play start
  • Large language models or heavy AI work
  • Battery gadgets that need to sleep for months
  • Shoppers who want one steady price. Store prices vary widely.

Specs in plain words

  • 4 TOPS means the AI helpers do about 4 trillion small math steps each second.
  • 4 GB of memory runs Debian Linux, a web browser and a small vision model together.
  • The four-core 1.4 GHz processor handles everyday Linux tasks.
  • Two camera connectors let you add one or two camera modules.
  • Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.4 come built in, plus a wired network port.
  • The board is about 8.5 by 5.6 centimeters, roughly the size of a credit card.

The board boots from microSD and has no eMMC. OLDI (LVDS) display is recorded as lcd. MIPI DSI is muxed with one CSI port. PCIe needs an M.2 HAT or other adapter. The OpenBeagle README and Seeed list 5 V at 5 A. By contrast, DigiKey lists 5 V at 3 A. The RTC needs an external coin cell. The GPU model was not named on the pages I read.

Starter ideas

  • Home weather dashboard

    Read a temperature and humidity sensor on the 40-pin header. Show the readings on a screen and a web page.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, HDMI screen.

  • Classroom Linux lab

    Set up the board as a small desktop computer. Students learn the command line and blink an LED with Python.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: HDMI screen, keyboard, mouse, LED.

  • Plant growth time-lapse

    Snap a photo of a seedling every hour. The board stitches the pictures into a short movie.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box, power bank.

How open is it?

Open Board

BeagleY-AI is OSHWA certified as US002616. Its schematics, board layout, Gerbers and parts list are public under CC-BY-4.0. The Debian image and Linux kernel are open, and the docs use CC BY-SA 4.0. The Wi-Fi firmware and parts of the AI stack likely use closed TI files.
