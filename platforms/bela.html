Overview

Bela is an open hardware board for making instruments and interactive sound projects. It sits on top of a small BeagleBoard computer and handles audio and sensors with under a millisecond of delay. You write code in a browser-based IDE that runs right on the board. Musicians, sound artists, teachers and researchers use it to build instruments that feel instant to play.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building new musical instruments that respond instantly
  • Turning sensors into sound for art installations
  • Learning real-time audio coding with a free course
  • Making custom Eurorack synth modules
  • Using Pure Data, SuperCollider, Csound or C++ on one board

Not great for

  • Total beginners with no coding background
  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth onboard
  • Tight budgets, with the board plus host computer costing over 100 dollars

Specs in plain words

  • Under 1 millisecond of delay means sound comes out faster than you can notice.
  • 24-bit audio at 96 kHz is studio-quality sound.
  • 8 analog inputs let you read 8 knobs, sliders or sensors at once.
  • The Gem Multi has 10 audio inputs and 10 outputs for multichannel speaker setups.
  • It runs a special real-time Linux that keeps audio on schedule.
  • 512 MB of memory on the host board is room for many sounds and effects at once.

Specs are for the original Bela cape on a BeagleBone Black (AM3358 1 GHz, 512 MB DDR3L, 4 GB eMMC). Analog I/O on the cape is 8 x 16-bit in and 8 x 16-bit out. Audio is 2 in and 2 out. The Gem boards use a PocketBeagle 2 instead (TI AM6254 quad Cortex-A53 up to 1.4 GHz, 512 MB DDR4, microSD boot) with 2x2 (Stereo) or 10x10 (Multi) 24-bit 96 kHz audio, 8 x 16-bit analog inputs, 16 GPIO, USB-A host, USB-C device and Qwiic I2C.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Light-controlled theremin

    Use light sensors as invisible knobs. Waving your hands over them bends the pitch and volume.

    Add: light sensors, small speaker.

  • Talking plant installation

    Add a soil moisture sensor to a potted plant. The plant hums happily or groans as the soil dries.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small speaker.

  • Classroom sound science lab

    Students watch sound waves on the built-in browser oscilloscope. They see how pitch and volume change the shape.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: microphone, headphones.

Builder ideas

  • Touch-sensitive drum pad

    Wire pressure sensors to the analog inputs. Each tap plays a drum sound the instant you hit it.

    Add: pressure sensors, headphones.

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Run your guitar through the audio input. A Pure Data patch adds echo or distortion with no lag you can hear.

    Add: guitar cable adapter, foot switch, small amplifier.

  • Interactive gallery sound piece

    Place distance sensors around an art piece. The soundscape changes as visitors walk closer.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: distance sensors, speakers.

  • USB MIDI keyboard synth

    Plug a USB keyboard into the host port. Bela becomes a standalone synth with sounds you design.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB MIDI keyboard, headphones.

  • Accessible music controller

    Build big soft buttons or a breath sensor for players with limited movement. Each input triggers notes with instant response.

    Add: large arcade buttons, breath pressure sensor.

  • Bird song recorder and filter

    Record birds in the garden through a microphone. A live filter cleans up wind noise as it records.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: microphone, microSD card.

Expert ideas

  • Eurorack synth module

    Build a custom module for a modular synth. The analog outputs send control voltages to other modules.

    Add: Eurorack case, patch cables.

  • Haptic feedback glove

    Drive small vibration motors from the analog outputs. The glove buzzes in time with music or sensor readings.

    Add: vibration motors, motor driver board, glove.

  • Networked jam station

    Connect two boards over a wired network. Players in different rooms share control of one soundscape.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: network cable.

  • Custom DSP in C++

    Write your own audio effects in C++ with the free Bela course. You learn real-time audio programming step by step.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: headphones.

  • SuperCollider live coding rig

    Run SuperCollider on the board for live coded music. Knobs on the analog inputs tweak the sound as you play.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: knobs, speakers.

How open is it?

Wild Open

Bela is OSHWA certified as UK000070. The board design files are on GitHub in EAGLE and KiCad formats under CC BY-SA. The core software is LGPL-3.0 and the IDE is GPL-3.0. The Texas Instruments chip on the host board stays closed.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA-3.0 (cape and Mini), CC-BY-SA-4.0 (Gem) · Software license: LGPL-3.0 (core), GPL-3.0 (IDE)
