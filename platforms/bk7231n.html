Overview

The BK7231N is a low-cost Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip from Beken. It sits inside Tuya modules found in many cheap smart plugs, bulbs and switches. Tinkerers reflash these devices with open firmware such as OpenBeken or ESPHome. The result is smart-home gear that answers to you and skips the vendor cloud.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Freeing cheap smart plugs and bulbs from the vendor cloud
  • Home Assistant fans who want local control
  • Learning how store-bought gadgets work inside
  • Very low-cost Wi-Fi modules for simple builds

Not great for

  • First-time builders, as most paths need soldering inside a device
  • Sensor-heavy projects, as Tuya modules expose only one analog input
  • Projects that need a USB plug, as the modules have none
  • Arduino-style tutorials, as few exist for this chip

Specs in plain words

  • 120 MHz is plenty of speed to run a smart plug and a small web page.
  • 256 KB of working memory handles Wi-Fi, a settings page, and a few timers at once.
  • 2 MB of flash holds the full firmware with room for updates over Wi-Fi.
  • A built-in recovery mode means a bad flash can always be fixed.
  • The CBU module is about the size of a postage stamp.

Chip values come from the Beken product page and LibreTiny docs. Beken lists 2 MB or 4 MB flash, 2 I2C, 1 SPI, 1 SDIO, 6 PWM and an ADC with up to 6 channels. Tuya modules expose only ADC3 on pin P23. As a result, most devices have one usable ADC input. GPIO is the 14 pins of the common CB3S module. The CB2S exposes 11. Size is the Tuya CBU module. The Beken page headline says Bluetooth LE 4.2 while its overview says Bluetooth 5.2. LibreTiny lists BLE 5.1 for the CB2S and CB3S and Tuya lists BLE 5.2 for the CBU. No deep-sleep current was found. The CBU datasheet lists 330 µA with the module disabled through CEN. Modules have castellated pads and no USB. As a result, flashing needs a 3.3 V USB-serial adapter.

How open is it?

Open Code

The LibreTiny, ltchiptool, and tuya-cloudcutter projects are open under the MIT license. OpenBeken source code is public on GitHub, but its license is not stated. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth parts likely come from closed Beken libraries. The chip and the Tuya module layouts are closed.

Software license: MIT (LibreTiny, ltchiptool, tuya-cloudcutter), OpenBeken license not stated
