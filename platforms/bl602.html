Overview

The BL602 is a low-cost Wi-Fi and Bluetooth LE chip from Bouffalo Lab. It runs on a 32-bit RISC-V core at up to 192 MHz. The Pine64 PineCone board made it famous at about four dollars. It suits makers who want to learn RISC-V and already know some C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning RISC-V on real hardware for very little money
  • Wi-Fi sensors and smart-home gadgets
  • Reflashing BL602 smart devices with OpenBeken
  • Trying Apache NuttX or Rust on a small chip

Not great for

  • Beginners, as the setup needs a C SDK and a boot jumper dance
  • Arduino-style projects, as few libraries exist
  • Following old guides, as the newer SDK changed many names
  • Long-range Bluetooth, as the chip skips newer Bluetooth range modes

Specs in plain words

  • 192 MHz is a brisk speed for a small chip. It can juggle a few jobs at once.
  • 276 KB of working memory holds Wi-Fi, a small web page, and sensor data together.
  • 2 MB of flash on the PineCone stores your program with room to spare.
  • Twelve analog inputs read many sensors such as light, soil and temperature.
  • Deep sleep draws about 12 millionths of an amp on some modules and suits battery gadgets.
  • The PineCone is about one inch wide and fits across a breadboard.

Chip values come from the Bouffalo BL602/BL604 datasheet v1.6 and the official product page. The BL604 has 23 GPIO. Flash is embedded as an option (none or 16 Mbit) or external over QSPI. 2 MB is the PineCone value. Ai-Thinker modules use 2 MB or 4 MB. The 12 µA deep-sleep figure is from Ai-Thinker Ai-WB2 module specs reported by CNX Software. The Bouffalo page lists about 40 µA for networked standby. BLE 5.0 on this chip does not support 2M PHY, Coded PHY or advertising extensions. Connectors, onboard LEDs and size are for the Pine64 PineCone, which uses a CH340 USB-serial chip.

Things you could build

Builder ideas

  • Wi-Fi blinking light

    Flash a small program that blinks the onboard color LED. Then change the colors from a web page on your network.

    Uses: Wi-Fi.

  • Room temperature reporter

    Read a temperature sensor and send the value over Wi-Fi every minute. A home dashboard draws a daily graph.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Plant moisture alert

    Read a soil moisture sensor with the analog inputs. Your phone gets a message once the pot dries out.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Battery door sensor

    Watch a door with a magnetic switch and sleep between events. The board wakes up and reports each opening over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery pack.

  • Smart plug with OpenBeken

    Flash OpenBeken onto a BL602 smart plug. You control it locally from Home Assistant.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: smart plug with this chip.

  • Simple tone player

    Send a tone from one of the DAC pins to a small amplifier. Play a doorbell chime or a short melody.

    Add: small amplifier, speaker.

  • LED strip mood lamp

    Drive an LED strip with the PWM pins. Pick colors from a small web page hosted on the board.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

Expert ideas

  • Bluetooth plant tag

    Broadcast soil readings over Bluetooth LE. A phone app shows the reading as you walk past the garden bed.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • IR remote bridge

    Use the built-in infrared block with an IR LED. Your phone can now switch the TV or the air conditioner.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: infrared LED, infrared receiver.

  • Garden weather station

    Log temperature, pressure, and light over Wi-Fi. The deep sleep mode stretches the battery between readings.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: pressure and temperature sensor, light sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Learn RISC-V with NuttX

    Boot Apache NuttX on a PineCone and explore its command shell. Students see how a small operating system runs on an open instruction set.

  • Rust on a microcontroller

    Try the community Rust crates for this chip. Blink the LED and read a button in a modern language.

    Add: push button.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Bouffalo SDK is open under the Apache 2.0 license and builds with a free RISC-V compiler. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth parts ship as closed prebuilt libraries. Pine64 shares the PineCone schematic and KiCad files for a smaller module, but no license is stated. The chip itself is a closed design.
