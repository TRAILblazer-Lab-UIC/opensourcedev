Overview

The BL702 is a small, low-cost RISC-V chip from Bouffalo Lab. It has Bluetooth LE, a Zigbee and Thread radio, and a USB port. Most hobbyists meet it inside a USB debug probe or the Pinecil V2 soldering iron. It suits makers who are comfortable with C and do not mind hunting for docs.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Cheap USB to JTAG and USB to serial adapters
  • Zigbee and Thread smart home sensors
  • Tiny boards such as the M0sense with a motion sensor and microphone
  • Tinkering with the open IronOS firmware on a Pinecil V2

Not great for

  • Beginners, as the SDK is C based and often documented in Chinese first
  • Bluetooth projects with the newest SDK, which marks BLE as unsupported on this chip
  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi radio
  • Big data jobs, as memory is small

Specs in plain words

  • 144 MHz is quick enough to read several sensors and run a radio at the same time.
  • 132 KB of RAM is enough for small programs and short lists of readings.
  • 512 KB of flash on the M0sense holds your program and a little saved data.
  • The M0sense board is 23 by 18 millimeters, about the size of a postage stamp.
  • The BL704 and BL706 versions add more pins, Ethernet, and on the BL706 a camera port.

Chip values come from the BL702/704/706 datasheet v2.6. GPIO is 15 on the BL702, 23 on the BL704 and 31 on the BL706. Ethernet RMII is on the BL704 and BL706 only, and the camera interface is on the BL706 only. Flash is 512 KB on the Sipeed M0sense. Part options in the datasheet carry 4 Mbit, 8 Mbit or 16 Mbit flash, and the BL706C-22 adds 16 Mbit pSRAM. The 12 ADC channels are the external inputs of the 12-bit ADC. There is also a 10-bit DAC. The datasheet lists hibernate and power-down sleep modes but gives no sleep current. As a result, low_power_ua is null. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Sipeed M0sense, which has a six-axis IMU, an analog microphone, an RGB LED and an FPC screen port.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Motion-sensing LED badge

    Use the M0sense motion sensor to change the RGB LED color as you tilt the board. Wear it on a lanyard at school events.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: small battery pack.

  • Clap-activated light

    Listen through the onboard microphone and toggle a light on a loud clap. It is a fun bedroom gadget.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: relay module, lamp.

  • Homemade debug probe

    Load the open RV-Debugger firmware and turn a BL702 board into a USB to JTAG and serial adapter. Use it to program other boards.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Step counter toy

    Count steps with the motion sensor and show the total on the small optional screen. Kids can race to a daily goal.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: 0.68 inch screen.

  • Simple tone player

    Use the DAC output to play beeps and simple tunes through a little speaker. Make a doorbell chime or a game sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker, small amplifier board.

Expert ideas

  • Zigbee door sensor

    Build a door sensor that reports open or closed to a Zigbee home hub. Get an alert from the hub app.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: magnetic reed switch, coin cell holder.

  • Thread garden soil sensor

    Send soil moisture readings over a Thread mesh to your smart home system. Water only when the plants need it.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • Zigbee mesh classroom map

    Place a few BL702 boards around a room and form a mesh. Students can see how messages hop from node to node.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: several BL702 boards, USB power banks.

  • Wired network sensor on BL706

    Use a BL706 board with Ethernet to post sensor readings to a local server. It works where Wi-Fi does not reach.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet PHY board, temperature sensor.

  • Tiny camera snapshot on BL706

    Hook a small camera to the BL706 camera port and grab still frames. Check on a bird box from time to time.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small DVP camera module.

  • Custom soldering iron firmware

    Build and flash your own copy of the open IronOS firmware onto a Pinecil V2 iron. Try new temperature menus.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: Pinecil V2 soldering iron.

  • Knock pattern lock

    Detect a secret knock pattern with the microphone and motion sensor. Light the LED green on a correct pattern.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The official bouffalo_sdk is Apache-2.0, and the blisp flasher is MIT. Parts of the radio stack ship as closed prebuilt libraries. Sipeed and Pine64 publish PDF schematics for their BL70x boards, but no layout files or hardware license. The chip itself is closed.
