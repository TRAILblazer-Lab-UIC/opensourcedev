BL808

Bouffalo Lab

A three-core RISC-V chip that can boot a tiny Linux and run small AI tasks.

Field marks: Spot a slim board about the length of a stick of gum, with a camera ribbon socket and a microSD slot, usually in the hands of a RISC-V explorer.

Overview

The BL808 is a wireless chip from Bouffalo Lab with three RISC-V cores. Its biggest core is fast enough to boot a small Linux system. It also has a small AI engine, a video encoder and camera inputs. Boards such as the eight dollar Pine64 Ox64 make it a favorite of RISC-V explorers with some Linux experience.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Trying Linux on a 64-bit RISC-V chip for very little money
  • Camera and AI demos on the Sipeed M1s Dock kit
  • Learning how Linux boots on tiny hardware
  • Wireless gadgets with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Zigbee on one chip

Not great for

  • Beginners, as flashing needs a serial adapter and a careful button routine
  • Plug and play setup, as the official SDK no longer lists the chip
  • Heavy desktop software, as 64 MB of memory is very small for Linux
  • Fully open builds, as the Wi-Fi and AI parts ship as closed libraries

Specs in plain words

  • Three cores share the work. The big one runs Linux and the small ones handle radios and quick jobs.
  • 64 MB of built-in memory sits inside the chip package. It is enough for a slim Linux with a command line.
  • 16 MB of flash on the Linux version of the Ox64 stores the boot files.
  • The AI engine is rated at about 100 billion simple math steps per second. That suits spotting a person or a sound.
  • The video encoder compresses camera video up to full HD. It helps stream video over Wi-Fi.
  • About 40 pins give room for buttons, sensors and screens.

SRAM is 768 KB from the datasheet memory map (64 KB OCRAM, 160 KB WRAM, 512 KB DRAM and 32 KB VRAM) and Sipeed's M1s Dock page. Pine64 and CNX quote 728 KB. PSRAM is the 64 MB part used on the Ox64 and M1s. The datasheet also lists a 32 MB option. Flash is the 16 MB (128 Mbit) Ox64 model. A 2 MB (16 Mbit) Ox64 model also exists. ai_tops is Sipeed's 100 GOPS claim for the BLAI-100, seen only in a search snippet. GPIO is 40 for the BL808C package. The datasheet says 36 or 40. The datasheet lists the off mode at about 1 uA. Connectors, button and size are for the Pine64 Ox64, which has a 2-lane MIPI CSI camera connector, a microSD slot, two 20-pin headers, a JTAG header and a BOOT button. Bluetooth is listed as 5.x dual mode in the datasheet. The Pine64 store page says BLE 5.0.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pine64 publishes the Ox64 schematic as a PDF, and Sipeed shares the M1s Dock schematic, but no editable layout files or hardware license were found. The Bouffalo SDK, Sipeed's M1s SDK and the Linux build tools are open code under Apache 2.0. The Wi-Fi driver and AI engine library ship closed, and the DevCube flasher and the chip design are closed too.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Apache-2.0 (Bouffalo SDK, Sipeed M1s SDK, bl808_linux)
