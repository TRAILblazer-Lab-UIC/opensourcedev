Brilliant Labs Frame

Brilliant Labs

Open-source smart glasses with a tiny display, a camera, and a microphone.

Field marks: Round glasses with a camera in the bridge and a 640 by 400 display in the right lens, charged by a USB-C cradle that carries a battery of its own.

Overview

Frame is a pair of open-source smart glasses from Brilliant Labs. It has a tiny color display in the right lens, a camera in the bridge and a microphone. You write apps in Python on a phone or computer, and they send small Lua scripts to the glasses over Bluetooth. Frame is now a legacy product, and Brilliant Labs sells the newer Halo glasses.

Specs in plain words

  • The 640 by 400 display fills a small patch of your view, about 20 degrees wide.
  • The glasses weigh about 40 grams, close to regular glasses.
  • The Bluetooth chip runs short Lua scripts and talks to your phone.
  • An FPGA chip handles the camera and graphics work.
  • The camera captures 1280 by 720 pictures and crops them to a square.
  • Two small batteries in the frame recharge in a cradle with a USB-C port.

Sizeunknown mm

The USB-C port is on the charging cradle. It is not on the glasses. The FPGA has no physical programming interface and is loaded by the nRF52840. The camera captures 1280 x 720 and crops to 720 x 720. The display shows up to 16 colors per frame. Weight of 40 g is from Liliputing launch coverage. The microphone is a TDK ICS-41351 MEMS mic.

    Uses: Hears sound, Bluetooth, Screen.

    Have a phone app translate what you hear. The translated text appears in your view.

    Uses: Hears sound, Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Ask the AI what you see

    Snap a photo with the bridge camera and send it to a vision model. The answer appears on the lens.

    Uses: Camera, Bluetooth, Screen.

    Show one recipe step at a time on the lens. A nod of your head moves to the next step.

    Uses: Screen, Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

    Take a picture of a plant or bird on a walk. A phone app names it and shows the answer on the lens.

    Uses: Camera, Bluetooth, Screen.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen, Battery ready.

  • Step-by-step repair guide

    Show wiring or assembly steps on the lens. Your hands stay free to hold tools.

    Uses: Screen, Bluetooth.

  • Name reminder at events

    Type quick notes about people you meet on a phone app. The note pops up on the lens the next time you need it.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Science field notes by voice

    Speak observations during a nature walk. A phone app saves them with a photo from the glasses.

    Uses: Hears sound, Camera, Bluetooth.

    Uses: Screen, Sensors built in.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The firmware and FPGA code are published under the ISC license. The schematics come as PDFs alongside 3D models and drawings, all under the ISC license of the docs repo. The Python SDK is MIT licensed. Rebuilding the camera pipeline needs closed Lattice tools and paid IP cores, and the chips themselves are closed.
