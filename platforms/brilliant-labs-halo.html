AI smart glasses with a tiny color display, camera, and open firmware.

Overview

Halo is a pair of AI smart glasses from Brilliant Labs. A tiny color display sits above the right eye, with a camera, two microphones and bone conduction speakers built in. Developers push small Lua apps to the glasses from Python, Flutter or web code on a phone or computer. It suits coders who want to build their own heads-up and voice projects.

Specs in plain words

  • The Cortex-M55 chip at 160 MHz is a small, efficient brain. Most heavy work happens on your phone.
  • The Ethos-U55 NPU runs tiny AI models such as sound or gesture spotting on the glasses.
  • 2 MB of working memory fits small scripts and short tasks. Big AI models stay on the phone.
  • The 640 x 480 camera takes small, sharp photos with little blur.
  • Bone conduction speakers send sound through your skull bones. Your ears stay free to hear the room.
  • The 300 mAh battery is rated for up to 14 hours of use.

MCU memory (2.0 MB SRAM, 1.8 MB MRAM) and the core are from the Halo hardware manual. Flash is 1.8 MB MRAM, shown as 1843 KB. The 160 MHz clock and 46 GOPS NPU figure are from Alif's Balletto family page. The 46 GOPS figure is for the family, unconfirmed for the B1. The display panel is a Guozhao VGA020 with 640 x 480 pixels, of which 256 x 256 is drawable. It connects over 2-lane MIPI D-PHY or Quad SPI. The IMU is a 3-axis accelerometer plus a 3-axis magnetometer, with no gyroscope. Weight is just over 40 g per press coverage. The magnetic charger has a USB-C input. The glasses themselves have no exposed connectors.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Heads-up shopping list

    Send your shopping list from a phone to the glasses. Each item floats in view as you walk the aisles.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Tap-through recipe cards

    Show one recipe step at a time in the display. Tap the frame to move to the next step with messy hands.

    Uses: Screen, Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Talk speaker notes

    Push presentation notes to the glasses from a laptop. A tap moves to the next note during your talk.

    Uses: Screen, Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Compass for hikes

    Read the built-in e-compass and show your heading in the corner of your view. It helps on trails with no signal.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Posture nudge

    Use the accelerometer to notice a long head tilt over a desk. A soft sound reminds you to sit up.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound.

  • Science walk photo log

    Take a photo at each stop on a field trip with a tap. The phone app saves the pictures with the time.

    Uses: Camera, Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Music practice metronome

    Play a steady beat through the bone conduction speakers and flash the count in the display. Your ears stay open to your instrument.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Live captions for conversations

    Stream sound from the microphones to a phone app for speech to text. The words appear in your view as people speak.

    Uses: Hears sound, Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Voice assistant in your ear

    Ask a question out loud and hear the answer through the bone conduction speakers. A phone app sends the question to an AI service.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Bluetooth.

  • Plant and bird namer

    Snap a photo of a leaf or bird with the glasses camera. A phone app names it and shows the answer in the display.

    Uses: Camera, Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Wake word on the glasses

    Train a tiny sound model to run on the NPU. The glasses react to one spoken word with no phone in the loop.

    Uses: AI chip, Hears sound.

  • Custom firmware experiments

    Build your own Zephyr firmware and flash it over Bluetooth. A bad image rolls back on its own.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Only the firmware and an STL model of the outer shell are public, with no schematics or board layout. The Brilliant Labs firmware code uses Apache-2.0 and the SDKs use BSD-3-Clause. The Alif SDK inside the firmware has a restricted license that limits use to Alif chips.
