Overview

The CamThink NeoEyes NE301 is a ready-made AI camera in a weatherproof case. It runs on four AA batteries, USB-C, or a network cable. A built-in chip recognizes animals, people, and objects without sending video to the cloud. It suits nature fans, gardeners, teachers, and anyone who wants a smart camera they can tinker with.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wildlife and garden watching outdoors in rain or dust
  • Long battery life thanks to a tiny wake-up chip
  • Setting up from a phone or laptop browser with no code
  • Counting people, cars, or animals on the device itself
  • Remote spots with the cellular or Ethernet versions

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, with prices near 200 US dollars and up
  • Smooth all-day video streaming on batteries
  • Fully open projects, given the CamThink-only license
  • Buyers in the US who want a local store

Specs in plain words

  • The 4 MP camera takes clear photos, and you can pick a narrow, medium, or wide lens.
  • The 600 GOPS AI engine runs object-spotting models fast enough for real-time detection.
  • 64 MB of extra memory and 128 MB of storage leave room for a model, photos, and settings. A microSD slot holds more.
  • A sleep current of about 7 to 8 microamps means the camera barely sips power while it waits.
  • The IP67 rating means the case handles heavy rain and brief dunks in water.
  • A box of 77 by 77 by 48 millimeters is about the size of a coffee mug.

Specs come from CNX Software, the CamThink product page and the AndroidPIMP review. ram_kb is the STM32N6 on-chip SRAM. Flash is 128 MB. CNX calls it HyperFlash, and the review names an MX66UM1G45G octal flash. The repo also holds GD25LX512 and GD55LX01 datasheets and a migration note. As a result, the flash part may vary by batch. gpio of 2 is the free GPIO count on the 16-pin expansion header. The header also carries UART, RS485, I2C, SPI and switchable 3.3 or 5 V power. Audio in and out are wafer connectors, listed here as audio_jack because the vocab has no wafer type. Cellular is the optional Quectel EG912U-GL or EG915Q-NA Cat.1 module. Ethernet needs the PoE variant or add-on. Active current is 170 to 180 mA per CNX. The review gives no measured battery life. The repo README says it is based on the STM32N6570 Discovery Kit design.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Backyard wildlife camera

    Strap it to a tree and let it snap photos of the animals it spots. You get a gallery of visitors without hours of empty footage.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Battery ready.

  • Bird feeder visitor log

    Point it at a feeder and count each bird that lands. Share the daily tally with your family or class.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Front porch package watch

    Mount it by the door and get a message whenever a person or parcel shows up. It sleeps the rest of the time to save batteries.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi, Sips power.

  • Garden pest spotter

    Watch a vegetable bed for deer, rabbits, or raccoons. Get an alert before the lettuce disappears.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Motion-triggered trail camera

    Add a motion sensor to wake the camera only when something moves. The batteries can last much longer this way.

    Uses: Camera, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: PIR motion sensor.

  • Library people counter

    Count how many people walk through a doorway each hour. A school or library can see its busiest times.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Farm gate monitor

    Watch a far-off gate over a cellular link and report vehicles or animals passing through. It works with no Wi-Fi nearby.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Cellular, Battery ready.

  • Solar powered nature station

    Pair it with a small solar panel for a camera that never needs a battery swap. Leave it in a field or park all season.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: small solar panel kit.

  • Parking spot checker

    Aim it at a few parking spaces and report which ones are free. Neighbors can check a web page before they drive over.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Hallway safety camera over Ethernet

    Run a single network cable for both power and data. The camera can watch a shed or garage all day without batteries.

    Uses: Camera, Wired network. Add: PoE network switch.

  • Chicken coop guard

    Spot foxes or other visitors near the coop at night with the fill light on. Send a phone alert right away.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Science class time-lapse with labels

    Photograph a plant or pond every hour and label what changes. Students can graph growth or animal visits over weeks.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: microSD card.

  • Bluetooth wake-up camera

    Wake the camera from your phone over Bluetooth for a quick check. It stays asleep until you ask.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power, Camera.

Expert ideas

  • Sound and sight doorbell

    Add a small speaker and microphone to talk with visitors the camera recognizes. It turns the box into a smart doorbell.

    Uses: Camera, Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi. Add: small speaker, microphone.

  • Train your own detector

    Collect photos of something special, such as your pet or a rare plant, and train a custom model. Load it through the web page and watch it work.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Weather station with a view

    Wire temperature and humidity sensors to the expansion header. Send readings and a matching photo to your dashboard.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.
