CC1352P

Texas Instruments

A dual-band TI radio chip with a strong amplifier for long-range wireless links.

Overview

The CC1352P is a wireless microcontroller chip from Texas Instruments. It talks on both the sub-GHz bands and 2.4 GHz from one chip. A built-in power amplifier gives it extra range. Most hobbyists meet it on a TI LaunchPad running as a Zigbee coordinator for Home Assistant or Zigbee2MQTT.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running a local Zigbee smart home with no cloud account
  • Long-range sensors on the sub-GHz bands
  • Battery sensors that sleep for months
  • Learning Thread, Zigbee, and Bluetooth Low Energy on one board
  • Radio experiments with Zephyr, RIOT, or Contiki-NG

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi radio
  • Beginner coding, as the tools are mostly C
  • Cheap starter kits, as LaunchPads cost about 50 dollars
  • Big programs, as memory is small next to newer chips

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It is plenty for handling radio messages from many devices.
  • A second tiny core runs only the radio. The main core stays free for your program.
  • 352 KB of flash holds the full Zigbee coordinator firmware.
  • 88 KB of working memory keeps track of a long list of paired devices.
  • The plus 20 dBm amplifier is a strong boost. It pushes signals much farther than a typical Bluetooth chip.
  • Standby uses under one microamp. A coin cell can keep a sleeping sensor alive for a long time.

RAM is 80 KB ultra-low-leakage SRAM plus 8 KB cache that can be used as RAM. The sensor controller has a further 4 KB. The radio core is a separate Cortex-M0, which is not counted in cores. The CC1352P comes in a 7 x 7 mm 48-pin VQFN only. Connectors and onboard parts are for the TI LaunchPads, which have a micro-USB XDS110 debugger, two buttons, two LEDs and BoosterPack headers. PA placement depends on the board. The P1 puts 20 dBm on sub-GHz, the P-2 puts 20 dBm on 2.4 GHz and the P-4 targets 433 MHz. LaunchPad size was not found.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Zigbee coordinator for Home Assistant

    Flash the free Z-Stack firmware onto a LaunchPad and plug it into your home server. Pair Zigbee bulbs, plugs, and sensors with no cloud account.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging.

  • Whole house Zigbee mesh

    Add powered Zigbee plugs around the house. Each one passes messages along and extends the range.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: Zigbee smart plugs.

  • Leak alarm for the basement

    Pair a Zigbee water sensor through the coordinator. Your phone buzzes the moment water touches the floor.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: Zigbee water sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Bluetooth classroom thermometer

    Read a temperature sensor and send it over Bluetooth Low Energy. Students watch the value on a phone app.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Radio range science lab

    Send test packets on 2.4 GHz and sub-GHz from the same board. Students walk away and record where each signal drops.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Far field soil sensor

    Send soil readings over the sub-GHz band from a distant garden plot. The strong amplifier helps the signal reach the house.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack, outdoor antenna.

  • Long-range mailbox alert

    Wire a switch to the mailbox door at the end of a long driveway. A sub-GHz message tells you the mail has arrived.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: door switch, battery pack.

  • Coin cell weather node

    Let the low-power sensor controller read a temperature sensor once a second. The main core sleeps and a battery lasts a long time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, coin cell holder.

  • Thread sensor network

    Run Thread with Zephyr or the TI SDK and link several boards. Each node shares readings across the mesh.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Beehive weight logger

    Weigh a hive every hour and send the number over the sub-GHz band. Beekeepers spot the honey flow from home.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, battery pack.

  • Two protocol bridge

    Run Bluetooth and Zigbee at the same time. Let a phone app control Zigbee lights in the same room.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Thread or Zigbee.

  • Digital audio beep box

    Send short sound clips to a small amplifier over the I2S pins. Play an alarm tone after a sensor trips.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: I2S amplifier board, small speaker.

How open is it?

Open Board

TI shares LaunchPad design files with schematics and layouts, but no open hardware license was found. The SimpleLink SDK uses a TI license that limits use to TI chips, and some stack parts ship as closed code. Zephyr, RIOT, and Contiki-NG offer fully open code paths. The debug probe firmware and the chip itself stay closed.

Hardware license: TI design-file terms (no open license found) · Software license: TI limited license, TI devices only (SimpleLink SDK), BSD-3-Clause (Contiki-NG), Apache-2.0 (Zephyr)
