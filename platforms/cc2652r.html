Overview

The CC2652R is a small wireless chip from Texas Instruments. It speaks Zigbee, Thread, and Bluetooth Low Energy from one radio. Most hobbyists meet it inside a USB coordinator stick for Home Assistant or Zigbee2MQTT. It suits smart home fans who want local control of their gadgets.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running a Zigbee smart home with no cloud account
  • Linking dozens of sensors, plugs, and bulbs into one mesh
  • Trying Thread with the open OpenThread project
  • Battery sensors that sleep for months between readings
  • Learning wireless firmware with Zephyr or the TI SDK

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi radio
  • Beginner coding, with mostly C tools and few starter lessons
  • Buying an open stick today, with both open designs sold out
  • Plugging straight into USB, as every stick adds a separate USB chip

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It is plenty for handling radio messages from many devices.
  • A second tiny core runs only the radio. The main core stays free for your program.
  • 352 KB of flash holds the full Zigbee coordinator firmware with room to spare.
  • 88 KB of working memory tracks a long list of paired devices.
  • Standby uses under one microamp. A coin cell can keep a sleeping sensor alive a long time.
  • Built-in encryption hardware keeps your smart home messages private.

Chip values come from the TI product page for the 48-pin 7 x 7 mm VQFN. RAM is 80 KB of SRAM plus 8 KB of cache SRAM usable as general RAM. The Cortex-M0 runs only the radio. As a result, cores counts the user core. Connectors and onboard parts describe the LAUNCHXL-CC26X2R1, which has micro USB, BoosterPack headers, two buttons and two LEDs. The swd_jtag tag reflects TI cJTAG with SWD support through the XDS110 debugger. LaunchPad board size and the chip supply range were not read in a fetched source.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, coin cell holder.

    Wire a motion sensor to a small board and count steps. The count shows up on a phone over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, coin cell holder.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power, Mesh messaging. Add: break-beam sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

How open is it?

Open Board

Two hobby sticks publish full board files under the Solderpad Hardware License 2.0. The slaesh stick is certified open hardware by OSHWA. Zephyr, OpenThread, and Contiki-NG support the chip with open code, and the Z-Stack firmware repo uses the MIT license. Parts of the TI wireless stacks ship as closed libraries, and the chip itself is a closed TI design.

Hardware license: Solderpad Hardware License 2.0 (Electrolama zzh and slaesh CC2652RB stick), TI LaunchPad design files under TI terms · Software license: TI SimpleLink SDK under TI BSD-style and TI license terms (NOASSERTION on GitHub), Z-Stack-firmware repo MIT, Zephyr Apache-2.0, Contiki-NG BSD-3-Clause, OpenThread BSD-3-Clause
