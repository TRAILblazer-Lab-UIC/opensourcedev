CH32H417

WCH (Nanjing Qinheng Microelectronics)

A fast, cheap dual-core RISC-V chip with built-in USB 3.0 and Ethernet.

Overview

The CH32H417 is a microcontroller from WCH with two RISC-V cores and lots of memory. It is the first WCH chip with built-in USB 3.0, which moves data about ten times faster than common USB 2.0 chips. It also has Ethernet, a camera port, and a display controller. Boards cost under 20 dollars, but the tools and docs suit experienced makers.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Fast USB 3.0 gadgets such as drives and data loggers
  • Wired network projects with Ethernet built in
  • Small screens and simple graphics
  • Learning about dual-core chips on a budget

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on the chip
  • Beginners who need step-by-step English guides
  • Battery gadgets, given its higher sleep current
  • Projects that need a big community or many add-on boards

Specs in plain words

  • A 400 MHz main core plus a smaller helper core lets two jobs run side by side.
  • 896 KB of RAM is a lot for a microcontroller and holds a small screen image with room to spare.
  • 960 KB of flash stores a large program plus fonts and pictures.
  • USB 3.0 moved about 450 MB per second in testing, enough to copy a movie in seconds.
  • Up to 95 pins let you wire in many buttons, lights, and sensors.
  • The chip has no radio. All connections go through cables.

Chip values come from the CH32H417 datasheet V1.4 and the openwch README. The V3F clock is 150 MHz in the datasheet, 144 MHz in CNX coverage and 160 MHz in the openwch README. Zephyr runs the V5F at 480 MHz, above the 400 MHz rating. GPIO is 95 on the QFN128 CH32H417QEU6, 65 on the QFN88 MEU6 and 50 on the QFN68 WEU6. The CH32H416 has 480 KB flash. The CH32H415 lacks USB 3.0, Ethernet and UHSIF. The 16 ADC channels are the external inputs of the 12-bit ADCs. The HSADC adds up to 7 more. Low power is the typical stop-mode current with the regulator in low-power mode (1.38 mA). The RTC on VBAT alone draws 3.5 uA. Connectors are for the MuseLab nanoCH32H417, which has USB 3.0 Type-A, two USB-C ports, Ethernet, microSD, an FPC SPI LCD connector and an onboard WCH-LinkE debugger. Its size was not published.

Builder ideas

  • Mini dashboard screen

    Plug a small screen into the board and show the time, weather, or class schedule. The graphics helper keeps drawing smooth.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small SPI LCD screen.

  • Network status light

    Connect the board to your router by cable and blink a light when the internet drops. A web page on the board shows details.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: LED.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board and show typed letters on a screen. It becomes a tiny typewriter.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, small SPI LCD screen.

  • Sound level meter

    Read a microphone and show how loud a room is on a screen. Classrooms can use it to keep noise down.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: microphone module, small SPI LCD screen.

  • Ethernet sensor station

    Read temperature and light sensors and serve the numbers on a web page over Ethernet. A school lab can log data all day.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: temperature sensor, light sensor.

  • Touch button music box

    Use the touch inputs as piano keys and play notes through a speaker. Kids can play tunes on copper tape.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: copper tape, small speaker.

  • Blink both cores

    Run a small program on each core that blinks its own LED. It is a friendly first look at dual-core chips.

    Add: LED.

Expert ideas

  • Fast USB flash drive

    Turn the board into a speedy USB 3.0 drive that stores files on a microSD card. It shows how USB devices work.

    Add: microSD card.

  • Retro handheld game

    Build a small game console with buttons and a color screen. Two cores let one handle graphics and one handle sound.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: small SPI LCD screen, push buttons, small speaker.

  • Simple camera snapshot box

    Connect a camera module to the camera port and save photos to a microSD card. Use it to watch seedlings grow.

    Uses: Camera. Add: DVP camera module, microSD card.

  • Fast data logger

    Sample a signal with the high-speed ADC and stream it to a computer over USB 3.0. It works like a simple scope for science class.

  • USB game controller adapter

    Read an old USB controller and pass its buttons to another device. It helps revive old gaming gear.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB game controller.
