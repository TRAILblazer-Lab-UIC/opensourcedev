CH32V003

WCH (Nanjing Qinheng Microelectronics)

A dirt-cheap RISC-V chip for tiny light, button and sensor gadgets.

Field marks: Found in great flocks for pennies each, usually trailing a small WCH-LinkE programmer on a single wire like a leash.

Overview

The CH32V003 is a 32-bit RISC-V chip from the Chinese maker WCH. It costs under 10 cents each in large batches and boards sell for about 1.50 to 5 US dollars. It suits tinkerers who enjoy writing small C programs and squeezing a lot out of very little. A friendly open community SDK called ch32fun makes it easy to program with free tools.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Very cheap gadgets with lights, buttons and simple sensors
  • Learning RISC-V and low-level C on a tiny budget
  • Helper chips that handle small jobs for a bigger board
  • Building many copies of one small project

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio
  • Beginners who expect to plug in a USB cable and upload
  • Programs that need much memory, such as graphics or sound files
  • Long battery projects, as its sleep current was not confirmed

Specs in plain words

  • The 48 MHz processor is quick enough for lights, buttons and sensor reading.
  • 2 KB of working memory holds a few hundred numbers at once.
  • 16 KB of program space fits a modest program written in lean C.
  • Up to 18 pins connect to LEDs, buttons and sensors on the larger chip versions.
  • It runs on 3.3 or 5 volts and works with most common hobby parts.
  • The Olimex CH32V003-EVB board is about 3 by 2 centimeters, roughly the size of a postage stamp.

Chip values are for the 20-pin CH32V003F4P6 and F4U6, which expose 18 GPIO. The SOP8 J4M6 exposes 6 GPIO. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Olimex CH32V003-EVB, whose USB-C port only supplies power. Programming and debugging use the single-wire SDI pin on PD1, which is not SWD or JTAG. There is no hardware USB. Some boards add software USB. The datasheet was not readable. As a result, standby current is null. RV32EC as the core subset comes from ch32fun-related sources and CNX coverage of the RISC-V2A core.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • LED chaser name badge

    Light a row of LEDs one after another on a small badge. Change the pattern with a button press.

    Add: LEDs, push button, coin cell holder.

  • Reaction time game

    Light an LED after a random wait and time how fast a player hits the button. Friends can race for the best score.

    Add: LEDs, push buttons, piezo buzzer.

  • Electronic dice for game night

    Press a button to roll a number from one to six on a ring of LEDs. It never rolls under the couch.

    Add: LEDs, push button.

  • Plant moisture bar graph

    Read a soil sensor and show the water level on a row of LEDs. Green means happy and red means thirsty.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, LEDs.

  • Rainbow mood lamp

    Drive a short strip of color-changing LEDs inside a paper lantern. A knob picks the color and the speed.

    Add: LED strip, knob, 5 volt power supply.

  • Light theremin

    Wave your hand over a light sensor to change the pitch of a buzzer. It makes a spooky instrument for a music class.

    Add: light sensor, piezo buzzer.

  • Morse code trainer

    Tap a button to send dots and dashes and hear them on a buzzer. The chip prints each decoded letter back to your computer.

    Add: push button, piezo buzzer.

  • Tiny screen counter

    Count button presses or visitors and show the number on a small screen. Keep the display to plain text and numbers.

    Add: small OLED screen, push button.

Expert ideas

  • Sun tracking flower

    Compare two light sensors and turn a servo toward the brighter side. A paper flower follows the sun across a window sill.

    Add: light sensors, servo motor.

  • Rain gauge tip counter

    Count each tip of a rain gauge bucket with a magnetic switch. Read the total rainfall from the chip at the end of the day.

    Add: tipping bucket rain gauge, magnetic switch.

  • Helper chip for a bigger board

    Let the chip read knobs and buttons and pass the values over two wires to a larger board. The main board stays free for bigger jobs.

    Add: knobs, push buttons.

  • Learn RISC-V up close

    Step through tiny C programs with the debugger and watch the processor work. It suits a computer architecture class on a small budget.

How open is it?

Open Board

The ch32fun SDK is open under the MIT license and builds with the free RISC-V compiler. WCH shares its evaluation board files on GitHub with no license stated, and no board is OSHWA certified. The RISC-V instruction set is open, but WCH keeps its processor core design closed.

Hardware license: none stated (WCH EVT board files), schematics published for nanoCH32V003 and CH32-Ant without a license · Software license: MIT (ch32fun)
