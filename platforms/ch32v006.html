Overview

The CH32V006 is a low-cost 32-bit chip from the Chinese maker WCH. It uses the open RISC-V instruction set. It is a roomier sibling of the famous ten-cent CH32V003. Tinkerers like it for small gadgets, LED controllers and cheap RISC-V lessons.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Very cheap gadgets that do one job well
  • Touch buttons, thanks to eight built-in touch channels
  • Measuring signals with its fast analog input
  • Learning how a RISC-V chip works with open tools
  • Projects that mix 3.3 volt and 5 volt parts

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio
  • Plugging straight into USB, as most boards need a separate programmer
  • Big programs or screens full of images in 8 KB of memory
  • Beginners, with few guides and early Arduino support

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is a modest speed. It handles one simple job at a time.
  • 62 KB of flash holds a program of a few thousand lines.
  • 8 KB of working memory is small. Long text and images fill it fast.
  • The analog input takes three million readings each second. That is fast enough to watch quick signals.
  • It runs on anything from 2 to 5.5 volts. It works with both 3.3 volt and 5 volt parts.
  • The WeAct board is about the size of a stick of gum.

Chip values come from the CH32V006 datasheet V2.0 for the QFN32 K8U parts with 31 GPIO. The QSOP24 E8R parts have 22 GPIO. Standby current is the datasheet typical value at 3.3 V with the watchdog and LSI off. Connectors, onboard parts and size describe the WeAct CH32V006F8U6 Mini Core, whose USB-C port carries power and debug lines. The chip has no hardware USB. Debug uses the SDI pin on PD1. A CNX reader comment says the ADC needs at least 2.4 V supply.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Touch lamp switch

    Use the built-in touch sensing on a copper pad. A tap turns a small LED lamp on and off.

    Add: copper tape, LED, transistor, resistors.

  • LED strip color controller

    Drive an addressable LED strip with a knob for color. Your shelf or desk glows in any shade.

    Add: LED strip, knob, 5 volt power supply.

  • Plant thirst blinker

    Read a soil moisture sensor once an hour. A red LED blinks once the pot gets too dry.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LED, battery pack.

  • Tiny reaction game

    Light an LED after a random pause and time the button press. Friends race for the fastest score.

    Add: LEDs, push buttons.

  • Small fan speed controller

    Read a temperature sensor and speed a fan up as things warm. It keeps a 3D printer cabinet or a terrarium cool.

    Add: temperature sensor, small fan, transistor.

  • Doorbell melody box

    Play short tunes on a buzzer at the press of a button. Each button can play a different song.

    Add: piezo buzzer, push buttons.

Expert ideas

  • Pocket voltmeter

    Use the fast analog input to read voltages and show them on a small screen. The open PidiMet project shows how far this can go.

    Add: small screen, resistors, probe leads.

  • Mini color screen badge

    Drive a small color screen with the open display library. Show your name or a tiny animation on a lanyard.

    Add: small color screen, battery pack.

  • Light sensor science logger

    Sample a light sensor many times each second. Students see how a lamp flickers faster than the eye can notice.

    Add: light sensor, USB to serial adapter.

  • Small motor driver

    Control a small DC motor's speed and direction with a driver chip. It can power a toy car or a moving art piece.

    Add: motor driver, small DC motor, battery pack.

  • Learn RISC-V from the ground up

    Write bare C programs with the open ch32fun SDK. You see every register the chip uses.

    Add: WCH-LinkE programmer.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The 74th board and the ADBeta breakout publish KiCad design files under the MIT license. WCH shares its evaluation board files with no stated license. The ch32fun SDK is MIT, and Zephyr uses Apache-2.0. The RISC-V instruction set is open, but WCH keeps the chip's core design closed.

Hardware license: MIT (74th CH32V006 board and ADBeta QFN32 breakout), WCH EVT files have no stated license · Software license: MIT (ch32fun), Apache-2.0 (Zephyr)
