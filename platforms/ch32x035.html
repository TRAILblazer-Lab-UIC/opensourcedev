CH32X035

WCH (Nanjing Qinheng Microelectronics)

Overview

The CH32X035 is a tiny, low-cost microcontroller from WCH with a RISC-V core. Its big trick is built-in USB-C power delivery. It can ask a charger for higher voltages. It also has a regular USB port for keyboards and other gadgets. Breakout boards start around five dollars, and makers love it for USB power projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • USB-C power triggers and testers
  • Homemade USB keyboards, keypads, and other USB gadgets
  • LED strips with the built-in protocol helper
  • Very cheap projects with an open toolchain

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on the chip
  • Large programs, with only 62 KB of flash and 20 KB of RAM
  • Beginners who want lots of step-by-step tutorials
  • Buyers who want a US store, as few stock it

Specs in plain words

  • A 48 MHz RISC-V core is plenty for reading buttons, sensors, and USB traffic.
  • 20 KB of RAM holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 62 KB of flash fits a USB gadget program with room for simple extras.
  • USB PD support lets the chip ask a charger for 9, 12, or 20 volts.
  • The small QFN20 version has 19 pins, enough for a keypad or a few sensors.
  • A breakout of 31 by 18 millimeters is about the size of a postage stamp.

Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), USB device, USB host

Power3.3 V or 5 V chip supply. Boards take 5 V over USB-C, and some accept PD voltages up to 20 V or more
Deep sleep draw54 microamps
Size31 x 18 mm

GPIO and ADC counts are for the QFN20 CH32X035F8U6 used on the WeAct and Polos boards. The LQFP64 R8T6 has 60 GPIO and 14 ADC channels. The F8U6 has no USB host and three UARTs. The G8U6, C8T6 and R8T6 have USB host and four UARTs. Flash is 62 KB of user code flash. Low-power value is the datasheet typical standby current with the watchdog off. Size is for the Polos CH32X035 Alef breakout. Programming uses WCH's 2-wire SDI debug or the USB bootloader.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • USB mouse jiggler

    Make a tiny USB gadget that nudges the mouse now and then. It keeps a display from going to sleep during a talk.

Builder ideas

  • USB-C power trigger

    Ask a USB-C charger for 9, 12, or 20 volts. Power old gadgets, LED strips, or a soldering iron from a phone charger.

    Add: USB-C charger.

  • USB PD tester

    Read what voltages a charger offers and show them on a tiny screen. It helps sort good chargers from bad ones.

    Add: small OLED screen, USB-C charger.

  • Custom macro keypad

    Build a small USB keypad with shortcut buttons for drawing, video editing, or games. Your computer sees it as a keyboard.

    Add: push buttons.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a strip of colorful LEDs with the built-in protocol helper. Make a glowing lamp or a party display.

    Add: LED strip.

  • Touch sensor piano

    Use the touch-key inputs as keys on a paper piano. Play notes through a small buzzer.

    Add: copper tape, buzzer.

  • Plant light timer

    Turn a grow light on and off on a schedule. It runs from one USB-C charger.

    Add: relay module.

  • Light meter for science class

    Read a light sensor with the ADC and send numbers to a computer over USB. Students can graph light through the day.

    Add: light sensor.

  • Low-power door counter

    Count door openings with a magnetic switch and save the total. The chip rests in standby between visits.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: magnetic reed switch.

Expert ideas

  • Bench power supply controller

    Set an exact voltage from a USB-C charger and show it on a screen. Use it to test circuits at your desk.

    Add: small OLED screen, buck converter.

  • USB serial adapter

    Turn the board into a USB-to-serial bridge for talking to other boards. It is handy on any electronics bench.

  • USB game pad adapter

    Read a USB game pad with the host port and turn its buttons into actions. Use a larger chip version with USB host.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB game pad.
