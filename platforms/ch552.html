WCH (Nanjing Qinheng Microelectronics)

A tiny, very cheap chip for homemade USB keyboards, knobs, and buttons.

Overview

The CH552 is a tiny 8-bit USB chip from WCH. It costs only a few dollars on a ready board. Makers love it for homemade keyboards, knobs, and other USB gadgets. You can program it from the Arduino IDE with plain C code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Custom USB keyboards and macro pads
  • Volume knobs and other desk controls
  • Touch buttons made from copper tape
  • Learning about simple chips on a tiny budget

Not great for

  • Big programs, with only a little memory to work with
  • Python, which this chip cannot run
  • Wireless projects, with no radio on board
  • Arduino libraries written in C++, which do not work here

Specs in plain words

  • 16 KB of program space fits a keyboard program with room for a few dozen shortcuts.
  • About 1 KB of working memory is enough for a short list of numbers.
  • Built-in USB lets the chip act as a keyboard or mouse with no extra parts.
  • Six touch inputs can turn bits of metal foil into buttons.
  • Deep sleep uses about 4 millionths of an amp, which barely sips a battery.
  • The flash may only take about 200 uploads at 5 volts. A spare board is handy.

Memory1.25 KB
Storage16 KB
Pins17 pins, 4 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsSPI, Serial (UART), USB device
ConnectorsUSB-C, Qwiic or STEMMA QT

Power5 V or 3.3 V, down to 2.8 V. Boards take 5 V over USB
Deep sleep draw4 microamps

Chip values are from the WCH CH552 datasheet V1E. RAM is 256 B iRAM plus 1 KB xRAM. Up to 17 GPIO counts XI/XO, RST and the USB pins. As a result, usable pins are fewer, especially on the 16-pin CH552G. There is no hardware I2C. Boards use software I2C. Sleep current is 4 uA typical at 3.3 V (ISLP3L), or 8 uA at 5 V. Adafruit runs the chip at 16 MHz at 3.3 V. Connectors and onboard parts are for the Adafruit CH552 QT Py, which uses the Seeed XIAO footprint. Flash can be programmed only about 200 times at 5 V according to the datasheet.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • One-button shortcut key

    Make a single big button that types a shortcut on your computer. Mute a video call with one slap.

    Add: large push button.

  • Rainbow status light

    Blink the onboard color LED in different colors from simple Arduino code. It is a gentle first project.

  • Volume knob for your desk

    Turn a rotary knob to raise or lower the computer volume. Press the knob to pause music.

    Add: rotary encoder knob.

Builder ideas

  • Mini macro pad

    Build a pad of three or four keys that each type a word or shortcut. Artists and coders save many clicks.

    Add: mechanical key switches, keycaps, small case.

  • Touch pad piano keys

    Use the touch inputs to turn strips of copper tape into keys. Each tap sends a note to a music app on the computer.

    Add: copper tape.

  • Game controller for a class game

    Wire a few arcade buttons and have them act as keyboard keys. Students can play a simple computer game with a big homemade controller.

    Add: arcade buttons, cardboard box.

  • Plug-in sensor reader

    Plug a small sensor into the cable connector and print its readings to your computer. Try a temperature sensor for a weather log.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature sensor with cable connector.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor and flash the color LED red for a dry pot. Green means the plant is happy.

    Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Accessible big-button switch

    Turn a large soft switch into a keyboard key for a person who finds small keys hard. It works with many communication apps.

    Add: large soft switch, headphone jack socket.

Expert ideas

  • Caps lock warning light

    Light an LED for caps lock. You will stop shouting in emails by mistake.

    Add: LED.

  • Coin cell night badge

    Glow a soft LED for a few seconds after a button press and then sleep. The deep sleep mode helps a coin cell last.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: coin cell holder, LED, push button.

  • Tiny USB to serial adapter

    Turn the board into a USB serial adapter for talking to other boards. Keep one in your toolbox for fixing gadgets.

    Add: jumper wires.
