Overview

The Organelle is a keyboard music computer made in the USA by Critter and Guitari. It runs Linux and loads patches that turn it into a synth, sampler, effects unit, or sequencer. Musicians can play it right out of the box. Tinkerers can write their own patches in Pure Data, Faust, and Lua.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Playing over a hundred ready-made synth and effect patches
  • Musicians who want one box that changes roles
  • Learning sound design with Pure Data
  • Live shows, looping, and band practice

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, at $499 for the S2
  • Electronics projects with wires and sensors on pins
  • People who want open hardware design files
  • Power-hungry USB gear without a powered hub

Specs in plain words

  • A 1.8 GHz quad-core processor runs complex sound patches smoothly.
  • 1 GB of memory holds several sound patches and samples at once.
  • An 8 GB microSD card stores the system and your patches.
  • Audio runs at 44.1 kHz and 16 bits, the same quality as a music CD.
  • It has 25 keys and weighs about a pound. It is a bit shorter than a school ruler.
  • Each USB port gives at most 0.5 A. A big controller may need a powered hub.

Values are for the current Organelle S2 from the official spec list. It boots from an 8 GB microSD card split into OS and patch partitions. As a result, flash_kb is null. Audio is 44.1 kHz 16-bit in and out. Wi-Fi comes from the included USB Wi-Fi adapter. It is not an onboard radio. The Organelle_OS README maps the Organelle M to a Raspberry Pi CM3 and the Organelle 4 / S2 to a Raspberry Pi CM4. The official page says Cortex-A53. The two do not agree. See open questions. Weight is 495 g.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Organelle OS is open source under the BSD 3-Clause license. Patches use open tools such as Pure Data, Faust, and Lua. The official patch library is public, but it has no license file. The hardware is closed, and no schematics or board files are published.
