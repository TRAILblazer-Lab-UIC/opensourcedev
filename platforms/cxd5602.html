Overview

The CXD5602 is a microcontroller chip from Sony. It packs six processor cores, a GPS receiver, and a high quality sound path. Most people meet it on the Sony Spresense main board. It suits makers who want location, sound, or camera projects on a small battery board.

Specs in plain words

  • Six cores at 156 MHz can each handle a different job at once.
  • 1.5 MB of working memory holds short sound clips and small photos.
  • 8 MB of storage on the main board holds your programs with room to spare.
  • The built-in GPS reads signals from several satellite systems around the world.
  • The sound path accepts up to eight microphones for clear recordings.
  • The main board measures about 5 by 2 centimeters, close to a stick of gum.

Memory, flash and size are for the Spresense main board CXD5602PWBMAIN1, which is the only common carrier. The chip comes in a 185-pin 6.5 x 6.5 mm FCBGA package. Sony lists 2 analog inputs with a 0.7 V range on the main board, and CircuitPython lists 6 ADC channels with the extension board. The extension board adds the microSD slot, headphone jack, microphone pins and a second USB port. No GPIO count or sleep current was found on a fetched page.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Sony shares schematics, parts lists, and 3D models for the Spresense boards under CC BY-SA 3.0. Board layout files are missing from the repo. The SDK is open source under BSD-3-Clause and Apache-2.0, and the Arduino core uses LGPL-2.1. Every image still needs closed Sony loader and GPS firmware files, and the chip design stays closed.
