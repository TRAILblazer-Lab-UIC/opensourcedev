Cytron EDU PICO

Cytron Technologies

A classroom project board for the Raspberry Pi Pico with dozens of built-in parts.

Field marks: This green board crams an OLED, a pile of sensors, a motor driver, and nine Grove ports around one Pico, then ships a propeller in the box for good measure.

Overview

The Cytron EDU PICO is a project board with almost every classroom part already wired in. You plug a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W into its socket and follow a printed guidebook. It has a small screen, several sensors, a microphone, lights, a buzzer, and a motor driver. It suits teachers and beginners who want many projects without loose wires.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classrooms that want a printed step-by-step guidebook
  • Learning CircuitPython with sensors already connected
  • Motor and servo projects with the parts included in the kit
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth projects through the Pico 2 W
  • Battery projects with the built-in battery charger

Not great for

  • Studying or remaking the board design. No schematic is published.
  • Shoppers in the US. Few US stores list the current version.
  • Projects that need many free pins. Most pins are already wired to parts.

Specs in plain words

  • The Pico 2 W chip runs at 150 MHz with two cores. It can juggle a few jobs at once, like reading several sensors and sending the results over a radio.
  • 520 KB of working memory is room for thousands of sensor readings at once.
  • 4 MB of storage holds your programs and plenty of small data files.
  • The 0.96 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp.
  • The motor driver powers two small motors at up to 1 amp each.
  • Nine plug-in Grove ports accept extra sensors with no soldering.

Core, clock, RAM, flash, GPIO and ADC values are for the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W that comes with the standard kit, from the Pico 2 W datasheet. The -NB variant ships without a Pico. The V1 board used a Pico W (RP2040, 133 MHz, 264 KB SRAM, 2 MB flash). Most GPIO are already wired to onboard parts through the Grove ports. The USB-A port is a relay-switched 5 V output limited to 500 mA. Speaker stands for the piezo buzzer. The gesture and proximity sensor has no vocabulary slot. The V2 datasheet names the microphone MP34DT01 while circuitpython.org names it MP34DT05-A. Board size is shown only in a datasheet drawing that was not read as text.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The example code and guidebook code are open under the MIT license. The board runs open CircuitPython, MicroPython, and the Pico C and C++ tools. The board design files are unpublished, and the datasheet says All Rights Reserved. The Wi-Fi chip on the Pico 2 W also needs closed firmware.
