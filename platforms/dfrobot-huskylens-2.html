DFRobot HuskyLens 2

DFRobot

A smart camera with a touchscreen that recognizes faces, objects and gestures.

Overview

HuskyLens 2 is a vision sensor from DFRobot with its own camera, touchscreen, microphone, and speaker. It comes with more than 20 ready-made vision skills such as face, object, hand, and text recognition. You pick a skill on the screen and a small host board reads the results. It suits classrooms and young makers who want camera smarts with very little code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Adding face, color, or object recognition to a micro:bit or Arduino project
  • Students who code with blocks in MakeCode or Mind+
  • Robot cars that follow lines, balls, or people
  • Teaching a new object with a single button press
  • Trying custom YOLO models on a strong AI chip

Not great for

  • Running your own programs on the camera itself, as the firmware is closed
  • Standalone wireless projects, as Wi-Fi needs an extra module
  • Builders who want open hardware files to study or copy

Specs in plain words

  • The AI chip is rated at 6 TOPS. That is enough to spot faces and objects many times each second.
  • The 2.4 inch touchscreen is about the size of a large postage stamp. You can see what the camera sees and pick skills right on it.
  • The 2 megapixel camera gives a sharp picture for recognition jobs and has a lens you can swap.
  • The whole unit is about the size of a deck of cards and weighs about 90 grams.
  • The Gravity connector is a four-pin plug. It carries results to a host board over I2C or UART.

One K230 core runs at 1.6 GHz with RVV 1.0 vector support and the other at 800 MHz (CNX Software). RAM is 1 GB LPDDR4 and flash is the 8 GB eMMC. A TF-card version (SEN0638-TF) also exists. The K230 also has a depth unit for structured light and an H.264/H.265 video unit. The camera is a 2MP GC2093 1/2.9 inch sensor at up to 60 FPS. The display is a 2.4 inch 640x480 IPS touchscreen. Wi-Fi 6 (2.4 GHz) needs the optional slot-in module. As a result, radios is empty for the base unit. The expansion connector is DFRobot's 4-pin Gravity I2C/UART port. Size is 70 x 58 x 19 mm and weight is 90 g.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Face recognition door greeter

    Teach the camera your family faces with one button press. A speaker greets each person by name.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound. Add: micro:bit or similar host board.

  • Color sorting game

    Hold up colored cards in front of the lens. The host board lights a matching LED for each color.

    Uses: Camera. Add: host board, LED lights.

  • Book title reader

    Point the camera at printed words. The text recognition reads the title and shows it on the screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen.

  • Talking object finder

    Train the camera on keys, a phone, and a wallet. It says out loud which item it sees.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound. Add: host board.

  • Rock paper scissors partner

    Show your hand to the camera and the host board picks its own move. A screen keeps score.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board.

Builder ideas

  • Line-following robot car

    Point the camera at a taped path on the floor. The robot reads the line and steers along it.

    Uses: Camera. Add: robot car kit, host board, colored floor tape.

  • Smart recycling sorter

    Train the camera on cans, bottles, and paper. A servo flap tips each item into the right bin.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board, servo motor, cardboard bins.

  • Gesture-controlled game

    Wave your hand left or right to move a player on a screen. The camera tracks your hand pose.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board.

  • Bird feeder visitor counter

    Aim the camera at a feeder and train it on birds. The host board counts each visit and logs the time.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board, outdoor weather cover.

  • Exercise rep counter

    The pose tracking watches your arms during jumping jacks. The host board counts each rep on a display.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board.

  • Face-unlock treasure box

    The box opens only for faces you have taught it. A servo motor works the latch.

    Uses: Camera. Add: host board, servo motor, small wooden box.

  • Robot that follows you

    The camera locks onto a person or a colored ball. The robot car drives to keep it in view.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: robot car kit, host board.

Expert ideas

  • Plant leaf health checker

    Train a custom model on healthy and sick leaves. The screen shows a label for each leaf you hold up.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Voice-chat vision helper

    Connect the camera to a large language model through its built-in MCP service. Ask questions about what it sees.

    Uses: Camera, Hears sound, AI chip. Add: computer, Wi-Fi module.

  • Parking lot plate reader

    Read license plates on toy cars at a model gate. The host board lifts a barrier for known plates.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: host board, servo motor, toy cars.
