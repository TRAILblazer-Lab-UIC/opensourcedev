Overview

The UNIHIKER K10 is a small learning board from DFRobot that packs a color screen, a camera, two microphones, a speaker, and several sensors. It suits kids aged eight and up, teachers, and anyone curious about simple AI. Offline demos for faces, pets, QR codes, and voice commands run right out of the box. Students can then code it with blocks, MicroPython, or Arduino.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First lessons in AI with no extra parts
  • Classroom sensor projects with a built-in screen
  • Block coding in Mind+ that grows into Python
  • Voice and camera projects on a small budget

Not great for

  • Heavy AI tasks such as large language models running on the board itself
  • Projects that need many wires or pins
  • Fully open hardware work, as only a schematic is published
  • Long battery life, as it has no built-in charger

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 chip runs at 240 MHz with two cores. That is quick enough for small AI tricks and a busy screen.
  • 8 MB of extra memory gives room for camera pictures and small AI models.
  • 16 MB of storage holds programs, sounds, and pictures.
  • The 2.8 inch screen is about the size of a large postage stamp.
  • The 2 MP camera takes small pictures that work well for spotting faces and objects.
  • The whole board is about 83 by 52 mm, close to a deck of cards.

Size83 x 51.6 x 11 mm

Module is the ESP32-S3 N16R8 per the official hardware reference. GPIO is an estimate from the edge connector spec of 2 full-function GPIO plus 15 digital IO and one I2C bus. CNX lists 3 GPIO. Interfaces list SPI and UART as ESP32-S3 capabilities. Which are broken out on the edge connector was not confirmed. The board has no onboard battery charger listed. It takes battery power on a 2-pin PH2.0 port. The edge connector is described as micro:bit-like. Physical compatibility with micro:bit accessories was not confirmed. The accelerometer is 3-axis only. No gyroscope is listed.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Face spotter demo

    Turn on the board and pick the face detection demo with the A and B buttons. It draws a box around each face it sees.

    Uses: Camera, Screen.

  • Classroom weather station

    Show the room temperature, humidity, and light level on the screen. Students can track how the room changes over a day.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Talking voice commands

    Say a simple command and the board answers with a light or a sound. It is a fun first taste of offline voice control.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Tilt maze game

    Tilt the board to roll a ball through a maze on the screen. The motion sensor reads each tilt.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Cat and dog greeter

    Point the camera at the door. The board plays a different sound for a cat or a dog.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound.

  • QR code treasure hunt

    Hide QR codes around the school. The board scans each one and shows the next clue on its screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: printed QR codes.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Plug in a soil sensor and watch the moisture level on screen. The speaker beeps once the pot gets dry.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Noise meter for the library

    The microphones measure how loud the room is. The color lights shift from green to red as noise rises.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Phone linked scoreboard

    Send scores from a phone over Bluetooth. The screen shows the score for a game of table tennis.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Portable step counter

    Run the board from a small battery and clip it to a bag. The motion sensor counts steps and shows the total.

    Uses: Battery ready, Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small battery pack.

  • Online weather display

    Join home Wi-Fi and fetch the local forecast. The screen shows sun or rain icons for the day.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Bird feeder camera log

    Aim the camera at a feeder and save a photo each time something moves. Photos go to a memory card for later.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, small battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Train your own image sorter

    Train a tiny model with Edge Impulse to tell your own objects apart. The board names each object it sees.

    Uses: Camera, Screen.

  • Voice chatbot buddy

    Flash the open XiaoZhi chatbot firmware and connect to Wi-Fi. Ask questions out loud and hear spoken answers.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Instant photo booth

    Snap a picture with a button press and show it with a fun frame on screen. Builders have made a polaroid style camera this way.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, Battery ready. Add: small battery pack, microSD card.

  • Robot car brain

    Use the board as the brain of a small robot car. The camera can follow a line or spot objects ahead.

    Uses: Camera. Add: robot car kit.

Openness level

DFRobot shares a schematic PDF and 3D models of the board and its case. Circuit board layout files are missing, and no hardware license is stated. The board works with open tools such as Arduino, ESP-IDF and MicroPython, but the DFRobot board package and the main chip stay closed.
