Overview

The Digilent Cmod A7 is a small FPGA module shaped like a long chip. It plugs straight into a breadboard. An FPGA is a chip you can rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. It suits students, hobbyists, and engineers who want to learn hardware design on a breadboard.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Breadboard labs on logic gates, counters, and timers
  • Building soft processors such as MicroBlaze or RISC-V cores
  • Precise timing jobs such as LED strips and many servos
  • Dropping a real FPGA into your own circuit board
  • Learning open FPGA tools such as openFPGALoader and LiteX

Not great for

  • Total beginners who want a first project in minutes
  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on board
  • Quick setups, with a design tool that is a very large download
  • Projects needing 5 V signals, with 3.3 V pins only

Specs in plain words

  • 20,800 logic cells give room for a small CPU plus several helper circuits at once.
  • 512 KB of fast memory holds the code for a soft processor with no extra memory chip.
  • 4 MB of flash stores your design and reloads it at every power up.
  • 44 pins on the edges plus 8 on a Pmod header give plenty of wires for buttons, sensors, and lights.
  • Two analog inputs read voltages from 0 to 3.3 V for simple sensors.
  • The whole board is about the size of a stick of gum, at 70 by 18 millimeters.

Values are for the current Cmod A7-35T. The Cmod A7-15T (XC7A15T, 10,400 LUTs, 112.5 KB block RAM) is no longer in production per a search snippet. clock_mhz is the onboard 12 MHz clock from the master XDC. The fabric runs much faster internal clocks through its 5 clock management tiles. gpio 52 is 44 DIP I/O plus 8 Pmod I/O. adc_channels 2 counts the DIP analog inputs to the 1 MSPS XADC. size_mm converts the manual's 0.7 by 2.75 inches. I2C and SPI are soft cores built in the fabric. It is not hard blocks. Board power comes from an LTC3569 triple buck regulator.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Blink the RGB LED

    Write a small Verilog design that fades the RGB LED through colors. It is the classic first step in hardware design.

  • Breadboard logic gate lab

    Wire buttons and LEDs on a breadboard and build AND, OR, and XOR gates inside the chip. Students see logic gates come alive.

    Add: breadboard, push buttons, LEDs, resistors.

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a player presses a button. Show the result on a number display.

    Add: seven segment display, breadboard.

  • Serial hello message

    Build a serial sender in Verilog that talks over the USB cable. Your message appears in a terminal on the computer.

  • Digital synth tone maker

    Generate square wave notes and play them through a small speaker. Each button plays a different note.

    Add: small speaker, small amplifier board, push buttons.

  • Light sensor logger

    Read a light sensor through one of the two analog inputs. Send the readings to the computer for a science graph.

    Add: light sensor, resistor.

Expert ideas

  • Retro VGA test pattern

    Draw color bars and a bouncing square on an old monitor through a simple VGA add-on. It shows how screens draw line by line.

    Add: VGA add-on board, monitor.

  • Run a tiny soft CPU

    Build a MicroBlaze or RISC-V soft processor inside the chip and run C programs on it. The fast SRAM holds its code.

  • LiteX system on a chip

    Generate a full LiteX system with a CPU, serial port, and LED control. It is a hands-on lesson in how computers are built.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a long strip of addressable LEDs with precise timing from the chip. Make patterns for an art piece or a stage.

    Add: LED strip, level shifter, 5 V power supply.

  • Many-servo robot arm

    Make clean control signals for several servo motors at once. The chip can steer a small robot arm smoothly.

    Add: servo motors, robot arm kit, 5 V power supply.

  • Custom carrier board

    Design your own circuit board with a socket for this module. Swap the module between projects like a chip.

    Add: custom circuit board, DIP socket.
