Overview

The EFR32BG22 is a low-power Bluetooth chip from Silicon Labs. It sips so little power in sleep that a coin cell can run a sensor for years. Makers meet it on small kits such as the BGM220 Explorer Kit and the Thunderboard BG22. It suits builders of beacons, tags, and simple wireless sensors.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Bluetooth beacons and tags that run for years on a coin cell
  • Simple sensors that report to a phone app
  • Plug-in sensor projects through the Qwiic connector
  • Bluetooth Mesh networks of small devices
  • Cheap experiments with the BGM220 Explorer Kit

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, with Bluetooth only on board
  • Matter smart home gear, with too little memory
  • Screens, cameras, and other memory-hungry jobs
  • Total beginners, with a bootloader step before Arduino works

Specs in plain words

  • A 76.8 MHz processor is plenty for reading sensors and sending Bluetooth messages.
  • 32 KB of memory is small. It fits a sensor program and the Bluetooth stack with little room to spare.
  • 512 KB of flash holds your program and the Bluetooth software.
  • Deep sleep draws about 1.4 millionths of an amp. That is why a coin cell can last for years.
  • A digital microphone input lets it listen for sounds and noise levels.
  • Up to 26 pins connect buttons, lights, and sensors.

Chip values come from the CNX Software launch article, which lists the data sheet figures. Flash and RAM are the top options and the common kits use them. GPIO is the QFN40 maximum. The BGM220P module exposes up to 25 GPIO. The 1.4 µA figure is EM2 with 32 KB RAM retention and the RTC on LFXO. EM3 is 1.05 µA. The USARTs can run I2S, which is why i2s is listed. Connectors and onboard parts are for the BGM220 Explorer Kit, which has a mikroBUS socket, a Qwiic connector, one LED and one button. The kit connector type was not confirmed on a fetched page. The SparkFun listing mentions a USB virtual COM port. The chip has no native USB. The kits use an onboard J-Link debugger chip.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Silicon Labs publishes schematics for its Thunderboard BG22 kits, with no open hardware license stated. Code builds with free GCC tools through Zephyr or the Silicon Labs Arduino core. The Bluetooth stack ships as closed precompiled files under the Silicon Labs MSLA, and the chip itself is proprietary.

Hardware license: none stated (Silicon Labs kit schematics) · Software license: Simplicity SDK under the Silicon Labs MSLA with some Zlib parts, Zephyr is Apache-2.0
