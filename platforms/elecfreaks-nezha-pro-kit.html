ELECFREAKS

Overview

Nezha Pro is a family of brick-building robot kits from ELECFREAKS for the BBC micro:bit. Every kit centers on one battery box with four motor ports and eight sensor ports. Themed kits such as Sports and Ocean add smart motors, sensors and hundreds of bricks with over 20 guided builds. Young students can build and spin motors with the box buttons before they write any code.

Specs in plain words

  • The box holds a 900 mAh battery that charges over USB-C in about 50 minutes.
  • Four lights on the box show how full the battery is.
  • Smart motors know their own speed and angle. A robot arm can stop at exactly the right spot.
  • RJ11 plugs look like old phone plugs and only fit one way. That cuts down on wiring mistakes.
  • The box is about 80 by 60 by 44 mm, close to a small juice box.
  • The micro:bit brain has 128 KB of memory, which is plenty for robot programs.

The breakout board has no user-programmable processor. As a result, CPU, RAM, flash and radio values are for the BBC micro:bit V2 that plugs into it. The board has 8 sensor ports (I2C ports with blue labels, analog IO with yellow labels, digital IO with red labels) and 4 motor ports, all RJ11. RJ11 is not in the connector vocabulary. Motor ports deliver 6.4 to 8.4 V at 1 A rated and 2 A max. Sensor ports supply 3.3 V at up to 1 A total. Size and weight (142 g) are for the breakout board without the micro:bit. Runtime is about 45 minutes with an AI lens, four smart motors and two LED modules. The kit page claims up to 4 hours. The OLED, ultrasonic, line-tracking, color and other sensors are separate PlanetX modules in each kit.

Starter ideas

    Build a windmill from bricks and spin it with the buttons on the box. Young builders need no code for this one.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready.

    Build the guided weightlifting robot and program it in MakeCode blocks. It raises a tiny barbell up and down.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small ball.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: line tracking sensor, black tape.

Builder ideas

  • Phone remote car

    Drive a brick car from a phone or a second micro:bit over Bluetooth. Race friends on a living room track.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Color sorting machine

    A color sensor reads each brick on a ramp. A motor pushes it into the right bin.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: color sensor.

  • Obstacle dodging rover

    An ultrasonic sensor sees walls ahead. The rover turns away before it bumps into them.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: ultrasonic sensor.

  • Rainbow dance bot

    Program a small robot to wiggle in time with a light show. A rainbow LED module flashes along with each move.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: rainbow LED module.

  • Garden gate opener model

    Build a model gate that opens at the press of a button. It shows how real automatic gates work.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: button module.

  • Precise drawing robot

    Attach a pen to a two wheeled robot. The encoder motors turn exact angles to draw squares and stars.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: marker pen.

    Read the speed of each smart motor and show it on a small screen. Students compare gears and wheel sizes.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small OLED screen.
