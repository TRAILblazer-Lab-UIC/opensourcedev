Overview

The CrowPanel Advance 5.0 is a 5 inch touch screen from Elecrow with an ESP32-S3 chip built onto the back. It adds a microphone, a speaker output, a clock chip, a memory card slot, and a battery charger. Its standout feature is a slot for small radio modules that add LoRa, Zigbee, Thread, or Wi-Fi 6. It suits makers who want a smart home panel, a dashboard or a Meshtastic touch terminal.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wall-mounted smart home control panels
  • Weather and data dashboards with a big touch screen
  • Meshtastic messaging with the LoRa module
  • Voice assistant projects with the built-in microphone

Not great for

  • First-time coders, as touch screen apps need real programming
  • Projects that need many free pins
  • Using the microphone and memory card at the same time
  • Camera projects, as it has no camera port

Specs in plain words

  • The 5 inch screen shows 800 by 480 dots. That is sharp enough for clear text, buttons, and charts.
  • 8 MB of extra memory lets the chip draw smooth screen graphics.
  • 16 MB of storage holds programs, fonts, and pictures.
  • The radio slot lets you swap in a new radio for a few dollars.
  • The panel is about 136 by 85 mm, close to the size of a paperback cover.
  • A battery port with a charger lets it run away from a wall plug.

Wi-Fi and BLE come from the ESP32-S3. LoRa (SX1262) and Thread or Zigbee (ESP32-H2) need the optional plug-in wireless module. ESP32-C6 (Wi-Fi 6) and nRF2401 modules are also offered. GPIO is limited to what the HY2.0-4P UART0, UART1 and I2C ports and the wireless slot expose. No general header count is published. The microphone and microSD share pins, and V1.0 used a DIP switch to pick microphone and speaker, wireless module or SD card. The speaker is not included. The board has an amplifier output for an 8 ohm 2 W speaker on PH2.0-2P. Grove means the HY2.0-4P Crowtail/Grove-style ports. Buttons are reset and boot. The board has an RTC per the product page.

Elecrow publishes Eagle schematic and circuit board files for four hardware versions on GitHub. The repo states no license, and the board has no OSHWA certification.
