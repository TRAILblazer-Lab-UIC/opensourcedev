Overview

The Elecrow ThinkNode M1 is a finished handheld radio for Meshtastic text messaging. It packs a LoRa radio, GPS, a paper-like screen, and a 1200 mAh battery into a sturdy case. It arrives with Meshtastic already loaded. It suits hikers, campers, and community groups who want off-grid messaging with no soldering.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting off the grid with a phone over Bluetooth
  • Reading messages on the screen in bright sunlight
  • Trips of four to five days on one charge with GPS off
  • Sharing your location with a group on a map
  • A cheap first Meshtastic node with a case included

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi
  • Adding sensors, as it has no expansion port
  • Quick screen updates, as each e-paper refresh takes about two seconds
  • Fast GPS fixes, as a first lock can take five to ten minutes

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF52840 chip runs at 64 MHz and sips power. It suits small battery gadgets.
  • 256 KB of working memory is plenty for messaging and simple screens.
  • The 1.54 inch e-paper screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It keeps its picture with no power.
  • The LoRa radio comes in a 915 MHz or 868 MHz version. Buy the band your country allows for licence-free use.
  • The 1200 mAh battery lasts about a day with GPS on and several days with GPS off.
  • The case is about the size of a deck of cards cut in half and weighs about as much as two AA batteries.

Elecrow lists 3 MB flash, which is the nRF52840's 1 MB internal flash plus an external 2 MB QSPI chip. The Meshtastic variant names the external chip as an MX25R1635F. Max current is about 85 mA with the CPU and LoRa active. Low power draw is about 5.6 uA per Elecrow. The device has no user expansion port. A reviewer noted SWD pads on the PCB. The antenna port is RP-SMA. Weight is 58 g bare and 81 g with the case. The Meshtastic variant also defines a buzzer, an L76K GPS and an e-ink front light pin.

How open is it?

Open Code

The software is open. Meshtastic firmware uses the GPL-3.0 license and has an official M1 build. Elecrow shares only a 3D case file and a manual, with no schematic or board layout. The Nordic chip and its Bluetooth stack stay closed.
