Electrosmith Daisy Seed

Electrosmith (now trading as Daisy, Qu-Bit Electronix Inc.)

A tiny audio computer for building effects pedals, synths, and music gadgets.

Overview

The Daisy Seed is a small board made for sound projects. It pairs a fast Arm processor with 64 MB of memory and a stereo audio chip. Musicians and makers use it for guitar pedals, synths, and samplers. You can code it in C++, Arduino, CircuitPython, or with visual patch tools such as Pure Data.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Guitar effects pedals and loopers
  • Desktop synths and Eurorack modules
  • Samplers and long delays that need lots of memory
  • Turning a Pure Data or Max patch into a standalone gadget

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio
  • Plugging a guitar straight in, as the audio jacks need extra buffer circuits
  • Screen-heavy apps, as it has no display connector or operating system

Specs in plain words

  • A 480 MHz processor does millions of math steps per second, enough for rich reverbs and synth voices.
  • 64 MB of memory holds up to about ten minutes of recorded audio.
  • Stereo in and out at studio quality means clean sound for music work.
  • 12 analog inputs read knobs, sliders, and sensors to control the sound.
  • 31 pins connect buttons, LEDs, and switches.
  • The board is about 51 by 18 millimeters, close to a stick of gum.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorSTMicroelectronics STM32H750IB Arm Cortex-M7, 1 core at 480 MHz
Memory plus 64 MB extra (PSRAM)
Storage8 MB
Pins31 pins, 12 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), Digital audio (I2S), USB device, USB host, SD card bus, Debug port
ConnectorsMicro USB, USB-C, Pin headers
Power5 to 17 V on VIN, or 5 V over USB
Size51.15 x 18 mm

Values are for the 65 MB Seed and Seed3. psram_kb holds the 64 MB external SDRAM. flash_kb is the 8 MB QSPI flash. The internal SRAM of the STM32H750 was not confirmed on a fetched page. As a result, ram_kb is null. 1 MB variants of the Seed2 DFM and Seed3 drop the large SDRAM. The original Seed uses micro USB and the Seed3 uses USB-C. The Seed2 DFM has 33 GPIO and 14 ADC inputs and no JTAG header. Codec by revision is AK4556 (Rev 4), WM8731 (Rev 5), PCM3060 (Rev 7) and TAC5242 (Seed3). Size is from the Seed datasheet technical drawing.

Starter ideas

  • First sound test

    Flash a ready example with the web programmer and hear a tone through headphones. It proves your wiring works.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio jack breakout, headphones, breadboard.

  • Simple synth with knobs

    Turn a few knobs to change pitch and tone of a simple synth voice. Copy an Arduino example and tweak the numbers.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, audio jack breakout, breadboard.

  • Echo box for your voice

    Plug in a line level source and add a long echo to it. The large memory holds many seconds of sound.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio jack breakouts, breadboard.

  • Sound effects for a puppet show

    Map buttons to spooky, silly, or animal sounds. Kids can trigger them live during a show.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push buttons, small powered speaker.

Builder ideas

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Build a distortion, chorus, or reverb pedal for your guitar. A buffer circuit brings the guitar signal to the right level.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: pedal enclosure, audio jacks, foot switch, knobs, buffer circuit parts.

  • Looper pedal

    Record a short phrase and play it back on repeat. Then layer new parts on top for a one person band.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: foot switch, audio jacks, enclosure.

  • Drum machine

    Play drum sounds in a pattern you set with buttons. Change the tempo with a knob.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push buttons, knobs, audio jack breakout.

  • Sampler for sound clips

    Load sound clips from a microSD card and trigger them with buttons. Great for school plays and podcasts.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: microSD card breakout, push buttons, audio jack breakout.

  • Pure Data patch in a box

    Design a sound patch in Pure Data on your computer. Then run it on the board with no computer attached.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, audio jack breakout.

  • MIDI keyboard synth

    Plug a USB MIDI keyboard into the board and play your own synth sound. It makes a small desktop instrument.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: USB MIDI keyboard, USB adapter cable, audio jack breakout.

  • Science class sound lab

    Generate pure tones at chosen pitches and listen to how they mix. Students can explore beats and echoes by ear.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, headphones, audio jack breakout.

  • Nature soundscape player

    Blend bird song and rain loops from a microSD card. Let a light sensor fade them up at dawn.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: microSD card breakout, light sensor, small powered speaker.

Expert ideas

  • Eurorack synth module

    Build a filter, oscillator, or effect for a modular synth rack. Control voltages from other modules steer the sound.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: Eurorack panel, jacks, knobs, level shifting circuit parts.

  • Vocal harmonizer

    Sing into a microphone and add harmony voices in real time. The fast processor shifts the pitch as you sing.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone preamp board, audio jacks, headphones.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The libDaisy, DaisyExamples, and DaisyDuino code is open under the MIT license, and the build tools are open too. The Seed module itself only has a reduced schematic and no stated hardware license. The Seed3 pedal, desktop, and Eurorack carrier boards are fully open under CERN-OHL-P-2.0, but the STM32 chip is closed.

Hardware license: None stated for the Seed modules, Seed3 dev kit carrier boards are CERN-OHL-P-2.0 · Software license: MIT (libDaisy, DaisyExamples, DaisyDuino, bootloader)
