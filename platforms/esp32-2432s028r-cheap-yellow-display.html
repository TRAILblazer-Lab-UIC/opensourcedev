A low-cost Wi-Fi board with a built-in 2.8 inch touch screen.

Overview

The Cheap Yellow Display is a small board that pairs an ESP32 chip with a 2.8 inch touch screen. It costs about as much as a lunch out. The maker offers almost no help, but a large community has filled the gap with guides and ready-made projects. It suits hobbyists who want a screen gadget without wiring one up.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Desk screens that show the weather, time, or news
  • Smart home touch panels with ESPHome or Tasmota
  • Status screens for 3D printers and music players
  • Retro games and small touch apps
  • Trying many ready-made community projects

Not great for

  • Projects that need many extra sensors or wires, with only three easy free pins
  • Newcomers who want official support from the maker
  • Battery gadgets, with no battery charger on the board
  • Camera or voice projects

Specs in plain words

  • The 2.8 inch screen is about the size of a large matchbox and shows 320 by 240 dots.
  • The touch layer responds to a firm press from a finger or stylus.
  • A 240 MHz dual-core chip with 520 KB of memory runs a busy screen and Wi-Fi at the same time.
  • 4 MB of storage holds your program plus pictures and fonts.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth let it talk to your home network and to phones.
  • A microSD slot adds room for photos, logs, and game files.

Specs come from the seller specification PDF archived in the community repo. RAM is the ESP32 520 KB SRAM. There is no PSRAM. GPIO counts only the three easily reached pins (IO35 input only, IO22, IO27) per the community PINS page. Others need an SD-card breakout or desoldering. The original board has micro-USB only. The later two-USB revision adds a USB-C port and an ST7789 panel, and its USB-C lacks CC resistors so C-to-C cables may not power it. USB-serial is a CH340. Buttons are reset and boot. The speaker output on IO26 goes through an onboard amplifier to a 2-pin connector. As a result, no speaker is fitted. A DHT11 sensor header is also listed by the seller.

Starter ideas

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

    Show the album art and song title of the music playing at home. Tap the screen to skip or pause.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • 3D printer status panel

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

    Read the built-in light sensor and fade up the RGB LED at dusk. The screen can show the room light level too.
