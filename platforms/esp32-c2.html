Overview

The ESP32-C2 is the lowest-cost chip in Espressif's ESP32 family. It also sells as the ESP8684. It has one RISC-V core, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth LE in a package about the size of a grain of rice. It suits simple smart home gadgets and cheap Wi-Fi sensors.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Very cheap Wi-Fi sensors around the house
  • Simple smart plugs, switches, and lights
  • Battery gadgets that sleep most of the day
  • Adding Wi-Fi to another microcontroller board
  • Learning MicroPython or Rust on a low budget

Not great for

  • Projects with many buttons, sensors, and lights at once
  • Plug-and-play Arduino use
  • Screens, cameras, or sound projects
  • First-time makers, with the ESP32-C3 as an easier start

Specs in plain words

  • A 120 MHz RISC-V core is plenty for reading sensors and sending data.
  • 272 KB of memory holds a small program and a few thousand sensor readings.
  • 4 MB of flash stores your program and a small web page.
  • 14 GPIO pins give room for a handful of sensors and switches.
  • 5 µA deep sleep lets a battery gadget last for months.
  • The chip has no USB of its own. Boards add a USB-to-serial chip for programming.

Chip values come from the ESP8684 data sheet v2.3. 16 KB of the 272 KB SRAM serves as cache. Flash is 4 MB on the common DevKitM-1-H4X and DevKitC-02C-N4X boards. A 2 MB part also exists. The chip has no native USB. Boards use a USB-to-UART bridge. The chip has one I2C master. JTAG appears in the data sheet block diagram. Connectors and onboard parts are for the ESP8684-DevKitM-1, with an RGB LED, BOOT and RESET buttons.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi room thermometer

    Read a temperature sensor and show the number on a tiny web page. Check any room from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Bluetooth fridge beacon

    Broadcast the fridge temperature over Bluetooth every few seconds. A phone app nearby shows how cold it is.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: waterproof temperature probe.

  • Phone switched desk lamp

    Wire a relay to a small lamp and switch it from a web page. It works like a homemade smart plug.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: relay module, lamp.

Builder ideas

  • Mailbox mail alert

    Sense the mailbox door opening and send a message over Wi-Fi. The chip sleeps the rest of the day to save the battery.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery holder.

  • Plant thirst checker

    Push a soil sensor into a pot and post the reading a few times a day. Your phone tells you which plant needs water.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery holder.

  • Garage door status light

    Watch the garage door with a switch and report open or closed. A web page shows the state from anywhere at home.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: magnetic door switch.

  • Classroom air checker

    Measure carbon dioxide in a classroom and post the number to a shared page. Students learn to open a window at the right time.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: carbon dioxide sensor.

  • Rain gauge counter

    Count the tips of a rain gauge bucket and log the total. The garden page shows how much rain fell this week.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: tipping bucket rain gauge.

  • Bluetooth step counter tag

    Clip a motion sensor to a backpack and count steps through the day. A phone reads the total over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, coin cell holder.

Expert ideas

  • Wi-Fi helper for another board

    Flash Espressif's ready AT firmware and connect the chip to a bigger board. The other board gets Wi-Fi through simple text commands.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: second microcontroller board, jumper wires.

  • Rust sensor node

    Write a small Wi-Fi sensor program in Rust with the esp-hal library. It is a cheap way to learn embedded Rust.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Espressif publishes the DevKitM-1 schematic and board layout as PDFs, with no license stated. The ESP-IDF toolkit is open under Apache-2.0, and arduino-esp32 uses LGPL-2.1. The chip design and the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers stay closed.
