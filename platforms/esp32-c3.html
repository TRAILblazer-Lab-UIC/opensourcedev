Overview

The ESP32-C3 is a small Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip from Espressif, first shown in late 2020. It was the company's first chip built on the open RISC-V instruction set. It sits on many thumb-sized boards that cost about 5 to 10 US dollars. It suits makers who want cheap wireless sensors, LED controllers, and Bluetooth gadgets.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Cheap Wi-Fi sensors and smart home nodes
  • Ready-made firmware such as WLED and ESPHome
  • Bluetooth LE gadgets that talk to phones
  • Tiny builds that need a board the size of a thumbnail
  • Learning Arduino, MicroPython, or Rust on a low budget

Not great for

  • Cameras, as the chip has no camera interface
  • Bluetooth audio, as it lacks classic Bluetooth
  • Thread or Zigbee smart home networks
  • Heavy jobs that need two cores or extra memory

Specs in plain words

  • One 160 MHz processor core handles Wi-Fi and a small program with ease.
  • 400 KB of working memory holds a small web page and thousands of sensor readings.
  • 4 MB of flash storage on most boards fits your program plus a few small files.
  • Up to 22 pins connect sensors, lights, and buttons.
  • Built-in USB lets one cable upload code and show messages with no extra chip.
  • Deep sleep draws about 5 microamps for the chip alone, and a whole XIAO board measured about 44.

Chip values come from the ESP32-C3 Series datasheet v2.5. The chip does not support PSRAM. Flash is 4 MB on nearly all boards (ESP32-C3-MINI-1 module, ESP32-C3FH4 or FH4X chips). The FH8X part has 8 MB. Connectors and onboard items describe the official ESP32-C3-DevKitM-1, which has micro-USB through a USB-UART bridge, an RGB LED on GPIO8 and Boot and Reset buttons. Most third-party boards use USB-C wired to the chip's native USB Serial/JTAG. The 22 GPIO applies to parts with external or FH4/FH8X flash. The FH4X and FH4AZ parts expose 16. The 5 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current for the chip alone. CNX Software measured 44 uA for a whole XIAO ESP32C3 board. The datasheet USB block is a Serial/JTAG controller. It is not a general USB device or host.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi weather station

    Read temperature and humidity and show them on a small web page. Check the porch from your phone on the home network.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Phone controlled LED strip

    Flash the free WLED firmware and wire up an LED strip. Pick colors and effects from a phone app with no coding.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • Internet desk clock

    Fetch the exact time and the forecast over Wi-Fi and show them on a small screen. It fits on a shelf next to your books.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: small OLED screen.

  • Bluetooth room beacon

    Broadcast the room temperature over Bluetooth every few seconds. Any phone nearby can read how warm the room is.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: temperature sensor.

Builder ideas

  • ESPHome door sensor

    Describe a door switch in a short settings file and let ESPHome build the firmware. Your smart home dashboard shows each open and close.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery holder.

  • Smart plant watering helper

    Watch soil moisture and run a small pump once the pot gets dry. Your phone shows how thirsty each plant is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Bluetooth plant thirst tag

    Push a soil sensor into a pot and send the reading over Bluetooth a few times a day. The board sleeps in between to save power.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery holder.

  • Phone driven robot car

    Steer a small two-wheel car from a phone over Bluetooth. Kids can race it around the classroom floor.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: motor driver board, small DC motors with wheels, battery holder.

  • Doorbell chime

    Play a short tune on a speaker each time the doorbell button is pressed. A message also lands on your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier board, small speaker, push button.

  • Classroom noise meter

    Measure the sound level with a microphone and post it to a web page. A row of lights shows the volume at a glance.

    Uses: Hears sound, Wi-Fi. Add: digital microphone board, LED strip.

  • Community air quality monitor

    Measure dust in the air and post the readings to a shared map. Neighbors can compare air across the block.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor, 5 volt power supply.

  • Mailbox mail alert

    Sleep inside the mailbox and wake up the moment the lid opens. A message lands on your phone right away.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery holder.

  • Heart rate display for gym class

    Clip a pulse sensor to a fingertip and send the beats to a tablet over Bluetooth. Students graph their heart rate before and after exercise.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: pulse sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Long range trail counter

    Count hikers on a trail with a motion sensor and send the total over long range Bluetooth. A ranger can collect counts from a distance.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery holder, weatherproof box.

  • Learn embedded Rust

    Write small Rust programs with the esp-hal project and run them on a cheap board. It suits a coding club ready for a new challenge.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

Several ESP32-C3 boards are certified open hardware. Olimex shares KiCad files under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and Adafruit and SparkFun share their files under CC BY-SA. ESP-IDF is open under Apache-2.0, but the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth stacks ship as closed files and Espressif keeps its core design closed.
