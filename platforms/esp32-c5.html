ESP32-C5

Espressif Systems

A low-cost chip with dual-band Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth, Thread, and Zigbee.

Field marks: Espressif's first 5 GHz Wi-Fi 6 chip also lists Bluetooth, Thread, Zigbee, and Matter, a radio roster longer than the seven-dollar XIAO board carrying it.

Overview

The ESP32-C5 is a low-cost microcontroller chip from Espressif. It is the first Espressif chip with Wi-Fi 6 on both the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands. It also carries Bluetooth Low Energy and a radio for Thread, Zigbee and Matter. Makers can buy it on thumb-sized boards for about seven dollars.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart home sensors and Matter devices
  • Wi-Fi projects in busy buildings with crowded networks
  • Battery sensors that sleep most of the time
  • Low-cost classroom kits
  • Home Assistant gadgets

Not great for

  • Camera projects or big color screens
  • Heavy AI tasks such as image recognition
  • Total beginners who want a no-setup first board
  • Running Linux or desktop programs

Specs in plain words

  • One main core at 240 MHz handles Wi-Fi, sensors and small web pages with ease.
  • A second low-power core can watch a sensor while the main core sleeps.
  • 384 KB of working memory is plenty for sensor readings and simple web pages.
  • Many boards add 8 MB of storage and 8 MB of extra memory for bigger programs.
  • The 5 GHz band is often quieter than 2.4 GHz in apartments and schools.
  • Deep sleep draws about 12 microamps on the chip alone. A battery can last a long time.

Chip values come from the ESP32-C5 datasheet v1.5. The core count covers the HP core only. The 48 MHz LP core is extra. Flash and PSRAM are for the common 8 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM boards such as the DevKitC-1-N8R8, XIAO ESP32-C5 and SparkFun Thing Plus. Some boards ship with 4 MB flash and no PSRAM. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the ESP32-C5-DevKitC-1, which has two USB-C ports. USB is full speed only. SDIO is a slave interface and is missing on chip revisions v0.0 and v0.1. The chip has one RF path. As a result, it uses 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi at a time, per a reader comment citing Espressif's FAQ.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi weather station

    Read temperature and humidity and post them to a small web page. Check the backyard from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Smart LED strip mood light

    Pick light colors from a phone browser. Choose calm colors for reading and bright patterns for parties.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • Wi-Fi network scanner

    List nearby networks on both the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands. See which band is less crowded at home or school.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: dual-band antenna.

  • Doorbell chime player

    Play a short tune on a speaker each time someone presses a button. Swap the tune for each holiday.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push button, small speaker, amplifier board.

Builder ideas

  • Zigbee door sensor

    Tell your smart home hub each time a door opens. It talks over Zigbee like store-bought sensors.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: door sensor, battery pack.

  • Home Assistant room sensor

    Report temperature, light, and motion to Home Assistant. Your lights can react to people coming and going.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature sensor, light sensor, motion sensor.

  • Battery soil moisture probe

    Check soil moisture a few times an hour and sleep in between. One battery can last a long garden season.

    Uses: Sips power, Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Plant watering helper

    Run a small pump once the soil gets dry. Your phone shows how thirsty each plant is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Room noise meter

    Measure how loud a classroom or library gets through the day. Share a simple chart of busy and quiet hours.

    Uses: Hears sound, Wi-Fi. Add: digital microphone.

  • Internet radio

    Stream music from an online station to a small speaker. Pick stations from a phone web page.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Bluetooth classroom buzzer

    Give each team a button that sends a Bluetooth signal. The first press lights up the winning team.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: push buttons, small battery.

  • Community air quality post

    Measure dust in the air on a street corner. Neighbors can look up the readings on a shared map.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: dust sensor, weatherproof box, 5 volt power supply.

  • Beehive weight logger

    Weigh a beehive every hour and send the reading over Wi-Fi. A rising weight shows the bees are making honey.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Matter smart plug

    Build a switch that joins a Matter smart home over Thread or Wi-Fi. Phones and voice assistants can then turn a lamp on.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Wi-Fi. Add: relay module, push button.

  • Thread border router lab

    Bridge a small Thread mesh to your home Wi-Fi. Watch messages hop from board to board in class.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging, Wi-Fi.

  • Electric bike data logger

    Listen to the CAN data line on an electric bike. Log speed and battery level for later study.

    Add: CAN transceiver board, microSD card.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Olimex shares KiCad files for its ESP32-C5 board under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. Seeed, SparkFun, and Espressif also publish schematics or layout files for their boards. The ESP-IDF toolkit is open source under Apache-2.0, and the Arduino core uses LGPL-2.1. The radio code ships as closed files, and the chip design stays closed.
