A cheap chip with Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth, Thread, and Zigbee for smart home gadgets.

Overview

The ESP32-C6 is a low-cost microcontroller chip from Espressif. It carries Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth Low Energy, and a third radio for Thread and Zigbee. That mix makes it a natural fit for smart home gadgets and Matter devices. Makers can buy it on thumb-sized boards for about five dollars.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart home sensors and switches
  • Zigbee and Thread experiments
  • Battery sensors that sleep most of the time
  • Wi-Fi projects that report to a phone or web page
  • Low-cost classroom kits

Not great for

  • Camera projects or big color screens
  • AI tasks such as voice or image recognition
  • Total beginners who want a no-setup first board
  • Running Linux or desktop programs

Specs in plain words

  • One main core at 160 MHz handles Wi-Fi and a handful of sensors with ease.
  • A second tiny core can watch a sensor while the main core sleeps.
  • 512 KB of working memory is plenty for sensor readings and small web pages.
  • 4 MB of storage on most boards holds your program with room to spare.
  • Three radios let it talk to Wi-Fi routers, phones and Zigbee or Thread smart home gear.
  • Deep sleep draws about 7 microamps on the chip alone. A battery can last a long time.

Chip values come from the ESP32-C6 Series datasheet v1.6. cores counts the main high-performance core. The chip also has a 20 MHz low-power RISC-V core for sleep tasks. ram_kb is the HP SRAM. There is also 16 KB of LP SRAM. The chip does not support PSRAM. Flash varies by board. 4 MB is the most common value (XIAO, Feather, NanoC6, Qwiic Pocket, C6-Zero). The official DevKitC-1-N8 has 8 MB and the SparkFun Thing Plus has 16 MB. Connectors and onboard items describe the DevKitC-1, which has two USB-C ports (native USB and a USB-UART bridge), an RGB LED on GPIO8 and Boot and Reset buttons. The 30 GPIO is for the QFN40 package. The QFN32 package has 22. The 7 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current for the chip alone. The SDIO block is a slave interface only.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi weather station

    Read temperature and humidity and post them to a small web page. Check the backyard from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Smart LED strip mood light

    Pick light colors from a phone browser. Choose calm colors for reading and bright patterns for parties.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • Doorbell chime player

    Play a short tune on a speaker each time someone presses a button. Swap the tune for each holiday.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push button, small speaker, amplifier board.

Builder ideas

  • Zigbee door sensor

    Build a sensor that tells your smart home hub each time a door opens. It talks over Zigbee like store-bought sensors.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: door sensor, battery pack.

  • Battery soil moisture probe

    Check soil moisture a few times an hour and sleep in between. One battery can last a long season in the garden.

    Uses: Sips power, Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Plant watering helper

    Run a small pump once the soil gets dry. Your phone can show how thirsty each plant is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Bluetooth classroom buzzer

    Give each team a button that sends a Bluetooth signal. The first press lights up the winning team.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: push buttons, small battery.

  • Mailbox arrival alert

    Sleep on a battery and wake once the mailbox door opens. Your phone gets a ping that mail has come.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: door sensor, battery pack.

  • Room noise meter

    Measure how loud a classroom or library gets through the day. Share a simple chart of busy and quiet hours.

    Uses: Hears sound, Wi-Fi. Add: digital microphone.

  • Beehive weight logger

    Weigh a beehive every hour and send the reading over Wi-Fi. A rising weight shows the bees are making honey.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, battery pack.

  • Community air quality post

    Measure dust in the air on a street corner. Neighbors can look up the readings on a shared map.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: dust sensor, weatherproof box, 5 volt power supply.

  • Bluetooth step counter badge

    Count steps with a motion sensor and send the total to a phone over Bluetooth. Wear it on a lanyard during recess.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, small battery.

Expert ideas

  • Matter smart light switch

    Make a light switch that joins a Matter smart home. Phones and voice assistants can then flip it.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Wi-Fi. Add: relay module, push button.

  • Thread network test lab

    Set up a few boards as a small Thread mesh at home or in class. Watch messages hop from board to board.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging.

  • Bike CAN bus reader

    Listen to the data line on an electric bike or car. Log speed and battery level for later study.

    Add: CAN transceiver board.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

Adafruit publishes design files for its ESP32-C6 Feather under CC BY-SA, and that board is certified open hardware. Olimex and SparkFun also share design files for their boards. The ESP-IDF toolkit is open source under Apache-2.0. The radio code ships as closed files, and the chip design itself stays closed.

Hardware license: CC BY-SA (Adafruit ESP32-C6 Feather, SparkFun boards), CERN-OHL-1.2 per README (Olimex ESP32-C6-EVB), Espressif DevKitC-1 schematic and layout PDFs without a stated license · Software license: Apache-2.0
