ESP32-CAM

Ai-Thinker

A tiny, cheap Wi-Fi camera board for streaming and snapping photos.

Overview

The ESP32-CAM is a tiny camera board built on the ESP32 chip with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. It packs a 2 megapixel camera, a microSD slot, and a bright flash LED. Clones sell for about 10 to 15 US dollars. It suits makers who want a cheap Wi-Fi camera and do not mind a little setup.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Streaming live video to a browser over Wi-Fi
  • Security, pet, and wildlife cameras on a budget
  • Timelapse photos saved to a microSD card
  • Watching a 3D printer from another room
  • Robot cars that need a camera view

Not great for

  • First-time builders, as the board has no USB port of its own
  • Battery projects that sleep for months, as deep sleep still draws a lot of power
  • Projects that need many extra pins, as most pins are shared with the card slot or flash
  • Face recognition with the current Arduino example, which dropped that feature

Specs in plain words

  • The board is about 27 by 40 millimeters, smaller than a matchbox.
  • The 2 megapixel camera takes clear photos for monitoring jobs. It is far from phone camera quality.
  • 4 MB of extra PSRAM memory gives the camera room to hold whole video frames.
  • Only about 9 pins are broken out. Most of them are shared with the microSD slot or the flash LED.
  • The board wants a steady 5 volt supply that can give 2 amps. A weak supply makes it reset over and over.

PSRAM is 4 MB on the original Ai-Thinker spec and on most clones. SunFounder, CircuitPython and the JLCPCB guide say newer Ai-Thinker boards carry 8 MB, but the ESP32 maps only 4 MB at a time. Flash is 32 Mbit (4 MB). The 9 GPIO figure is SunFounder's count of broken-out I/O. Most of those pins are shared with the microSD slot, the flash LED or boot strapping. The deep-sleep figure of 6 mA comes from SunFounder and CircuitPython.org, and it is high because of the regulator and camera circuitry. There is no USB on the board itself. The ESP32-CAM-MB base adds micro USB through a CH340 serial chip, plus reset and boot buttons. Camera output formats are JPEG (OV2640 only), BMP and grayscale. A red status LED sits on GPIO 33 and the flash LED on GPIO 4.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi video stream

    Upload the camera web server example. Watch live video from any browser on your home network.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: programmer base.

  • Pet watcher

    Set the camera near the food bowl. Check on your pet from your phone in another room.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: programmer base, 5 volt power supply.

  • 3D printer monitor

    Mount the camera above a 3D printer. Watch each print from another room and catch failures early.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: 5 volt power supply, small mount.

  • Classroom document camera

    Aim the camera down at a desk. Show a worksheet or a bug under a lens on the classroom screen.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: small stand.

  • Phone-controlled flashlight

    Turn the bright flash LED on and off from a web page. It makes a handy remote light for a dark shed.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: programmer base.

Builder ideas

  • Plant growth timelapse

    Snap a photo every hour and save it to a microSD card. Stitch the photos into a video of a seed sprouting.

    Uses: Camera. Add: microSD card, 5 volt power supply.

  • Wildlife trail camera

    Add a motion sensor to trigger photos. Find out which animals visit your yard at night.

    Uses: Camera. Add: motion sensor, microSD card, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Doorbell snapshot

    Wire a button to the board. Each press takes a photo of the visitor and sends it over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: push button, 5 volt power supply.

  • Robot car camera

    Ride the camera on a small robot car. Drive it from a browser and watch the live view.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: robot car kit, motor driver board.

  • Mailbox alert

    Point the camera inside a mailbox. A photo arrives on your phone after the door opens.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: door switch, battery pack.

  • Bird nest box camera

    Mount the board inside a nest box with a small light. Watch chicks hatch from the house.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: 5 volt power supply, weatherproof box.

  • Weather sky photos

    Photograph the sky every ten minutes and add a temperature reading. Students compare clouds and weather.

    Uses: Camera. Add: temperature sensor, microSD card.

  • Pan and tilt security camera

    Mount the board on two small servos. Turn the camera left, right, up, and down from a web page.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera. Add: two servo motors, pan tilt bracket.

Expert ideas

  • Bluetooth photo trigger

    Use a phone app to send a Bluetooth signal. The board snaps a photo and saves it to the card.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Camera. Add: microSD card.

  • Simple people counter

    Write code to spot motion between frames. Count how many people pass a doorway each hour.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: 5 volt power supply.

How open is it?

Open Board

The software is open. ESP-IDF and the camera driver use Apache-2.0 and Arduino-ESP32 uses LGPL-2.1. A schematic PDF circulates, but no board layout or hardware license is published. The ESP32 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Apache-2.0 (ESP-IDF, esp32-camera), LGPL-2.1 (Arduino-ESP32)
