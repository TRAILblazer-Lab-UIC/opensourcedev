Overview

The ESP32-H2 is a small, low-power chip from Espressif for smart home gadgets. It speaks Zigbee, Thread, Matter, and Bluetooth Low Energy. It has no Wi-Fi at all. It suits makers who want battery sensors and switches that join a home mesh network. Boards cost about four to ten US dollars.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Zigbee sensors that work with Zigbee2MQTT or a smart home hub
  • Thread and Matter switches and lights
  • Battery gadgets that sleep most of the day
  • Bluetooth Low Energy tags and phone gadgets
  • Learning how mesh networks pass messages along

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects or web dashboards, with no Wi-Fi on the chip
  • Gateways on their own, which need a second chip with Wi-Fi
  • Screens, cameras, or heavy number crunching
  • MicroPython Zigbee projects, with only Bluetooth support in that build

Specs in plain words

  • A 96 MHz processor is plenty for reading sensors and sending small radio messages.
  • 320 KB of memory holds a sensor program and the Zigbee or Thread software with room to spare.
  • 4 MB of storage on most boards holds your program and its updates.
  • Deep sleep draws about 7 millionths of an amp. A small battery can last a very long time.
  • 19 pins connect buttons, sensors, and LEDs.
  • The 802.15.4 radio is the same kind of radio found in Zigbee bulbs and Thread sensors.

Chip values come from the ESP32-H2 Series datasheet v1.3 (July 2026). Flash sits inside the chip package. The FH2S part has 2 MB and the FH4S part has 4 MB. Every board checked uses 4 MB. The chip has no PSRAM interface listed and no Wi-Fi. Connectors and onboard items describe the DevKitM-1, which has two USB-C ports (native USB Serial/JTAG and a USB-UART bridge), an RGB LED on GPIO8 and Boot and Reset buttons. The USB port is a Serial/JTAG controller. As a result, the chip cannot act as a general USB device or host. The 7 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current for the chip alone.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Zigbee room temperature sensor

    Read a temperature and humidity sensor and send the numbers to your smart home hub over Zigbee. It can run for a long time on a battery.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, battery holder.

  • Zigbee color lamp

    Start from the light example and control the onboard RGB LED from your smart home app. Swap in an LED strip for a bigger glow.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: LED strip.

  • Wireless wall switch

    Wire a push button to the board and pair it with a Zigbee light. Press the button to flip the lamp on or off.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: push button, battery holder.

  • Bluetooth plant tag

    Broadcast soil readings over Bluetooth to a phone app. Walk past the plant and check its thirst level.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Door and window alarm

    Fit a magnet switch to a door and report each opening to your hub. Your phone can ping you about it.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery holder.

  • Garden soil moisture reporter

    Push a soil sensor into a planter and send readings over the Zigbee mesh. The hub can remind you to water.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery holder.

  • Classroom mesh network demo

    Spread several boards around a room and watch messages hop from one to the next. Students see how a mesh network heals itself.

    Uses: Mesh messaging, Bluetooth.

  • Mailbox delivery notifier

    Put a tilt or motion sensor in the mailbox and send a Zigbee alert on each delivery. The chip sleeps between visits.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery holder.

  • Bluetooth step counter badge

    Count steps with a motion sensor and show the total in a phone app over Bluetooth. Clip it to a backpack.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, small battery.

  • Water leak alarm

    Place a water sensor under the sink and report any drip to your smart home hub. A buzzer adds a local warning.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: water leak sensor, buzzer.

  • Bird box temperature logger

    Hide a sensor in a nest box and send readings over the Zigbee mesh to the house. Kids can chart the nest warmth each day.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, battery holder.

Expert ideas

  • Matter smart plug relay

    Build a Matter device that switches a lamp through a relay. It joins Apple, Google, or Amazon homes through a Thread hub.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: relay module, USB power supply.

  • Doorbell chime with sound

    Play a short chime through a small amplifier on each press of a Zigbee doorbell button. The sound can change with the seasons.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Makes sound. Add: small amplifier, small speaker, push button.

  • Thread network sensor node

    Join a Thread network made by a Wi-Fi ESP32 border router. Send sensor readings to a home server over the internet protocol.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: second ESP32 board with Wi-Fi.

How open is it?

Open Board

The Olimex ESP32-H2-DevKit-LiPo has full design files under the CERN-OHL-S-2.0 license and is OSHWA certified. ESP-IDF and the Zigbee software are open under Apache-2.0, and the Arduino core is LGPL-2.1. The Bluetooth, radio, and Thread libraries ship as closed binaries. The chip design stays closed.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-S-2.0 (Olimex ESP32-H2-DevKit-LiPo), Espressif DevKitM-1 schematic and layout PDFs without a stated license · Software license: Apache-2.0
