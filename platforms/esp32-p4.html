ESP32-P4

Espressif Systems

A fast ESP32 chip built for cameras, screens, video, and sound.

Field marks: A 400 MHz chip with camera ports, display ports, and an H.264 encoder, plus a second ESP32 parked beside it to supply the Wi-Fi it left out.

Overview

The ESP32-P4 is Espressif's fastest microcontroller chip. It has two 400 MHz processor cores, plenty of memory, and special hardware for cameras, screens, and video. The chip has no radio of its own. Most boards add a second ESP32 chip for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. It suits builders who want smart displays, camera gadgets, and audio projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Touch screen panels and smart displays
  • Camera projects with photos or compressed video
  • Audio players and recorders
  • Wired network gadgets over Ethernet
  • USB gadgets that use keyboards, gamepads, or drives

Not great for

  • First-time makers, with tricky setup and chip revisions to check
  • Simple Wi-Fi sensors, which suit a cheaper ESP32
  • Tiny battery projects
  • Running a full desktop operating system

Specs in plain words

  • Two 400 MHz cores give it about twice the speed of an ESP32-S3.
  • 768 KB of fast memory plus 16 or 32 MB of extra memory holds whole screen images and camera frames.
  • Video hardware can squeeze camera footage into H.264 files at 1080p and 30 frames a second.
  • MIPI ports take the same thin ribbon cables found on phone cameras and screens.
  • High-speed USB can talk to keyboards, gamepads, and drives.
  • 55 pins leave lots of room for buttons, sensors and lights.

Chip values come from the ESP32-P4 Series datasheet pre-release v0.7 (July 2026), which covers chip revision v3.x (sold as ESP32-P4X). Revision v1.3 chips (NRW16 and NRW32) are end of life. Their datasheet gives a default 360 MHz clock and 25 uA deep sleep. ram_kb is the HP L2 memory. There is also 32 KB LP SRAM and 8 KB scratchpad memory. PSRAM sits in the package. 32 MB is the most common value (Waveshare NANO, Olimex DevKit, SparkFun RedBoard). The Adafruit Metro uses 16 MB. The chip has no internal flash. 16 MB external flash is the common board value. radios is empty because the chip has no radio. Most boards add an ESP32-C6 (Wi-Fi 6, BLE, 802.15.4) or ESP32-C5 over SDIO using ESP-Hosted. Connectors describe the ESP32-P4-Function-EV-Board, which also has 10/100 Ethernet RJ45, an audio codec with amplifier and four USB ports. The 12 uA figure is the v3.x datasheet deep-sleep current with LP timer and LP memory on. ai_tops is null because Espressif gives no TOPS figure.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Smart home touch panel

    Drive a 7 inch touch screen with buttons for lights and scenes. Mount it on the wall by the door.

    Uses: Screen. Add: MIPI touch screen, ribbon cable.

  • Digital photo frame

    Show pictures from a microSD card on a large screen. Swipe to the next photo with a finger.

    Uses: Screen. Add: MIPI touch screen, microSD card.

  • Bird feeder camera

    Snap JPEG photos of birds at the feeder and save them to a microSD card. Kids can sort the visitors later.

    Uses: Camera. Add: MIPI camera, microSD card, motion sensor.

  • Science lab data dashboard

    Read several sensors and draw live charts on a large screen. Students watch an experiment unfold in real time.

    Uses: Screen. Add: MIPI screen, temperature sensor, light sensor.

  • Voice memo recorder

    Record speech from a microphone to a microSD card. Play the clips back through a speaker.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone, small speaker, microSD card.

  • Music visualizer

    Listen through a microphone and paint dancing color bars on a screen. It brings a party or a classroom to life.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: microphone, MIPI screen.

  • USB keyboard art kiosk

    Plug in a USB keyboard and mouse to paint on a big screen. A museum or library can offer it as a drawing station.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, USB mouse, MIPI screen.

  • Network clock and signage

    Pull the time and notices over Ethernet and show them on a hallway screen. It suits a school front office.

    Uses: Wired network, Screen. Add: MIPI screen, network cable.

  • Talking plant monitor

    Read a soil sensor and show a plant face on screen. A short spoken clip plays out loud for a thirsty plant.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, small speaker, MIPI screen.

  • Timelapse garden camera

    Take a photo every few minutes and save it to a microSD card. Stitch the shots into a video of seedlings growing.

    Uses: Camera. Add: MIPI camera, microSD card.

  • Handheld audio player

    Play music files from a microSD card through headphones. Add a small screen for song titles.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen. Add: audio amplifier board, headphones, microSD card, small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Video doorbell

    Stream camera video from the front door to a screen indoors. Add a button and a speaker for a chime.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, Makes sound. Add: MIPI camera, push button, small speaker.

  • Wired security camera

    Record compressed video from a camera and send it over an Ethernet cable. It keeps watch on a garage or shed.

    Uses: Camera, Wired network. Add: MIPI camera, network cable.

  • Retro game console

    Run a game emulator on a screen with a USB gamepad. Add a speaker for the music.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: MIPI screen, USB gamepad, small speaker.

  • Gesture and face detection demo

    Run a small vision model on camera frames. It can spot a face or a raised hand and react on screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: MIPI camera, MIPI screen.

  • Low-power wildlife trail camera

    Let the low-power core watch a motion sensor and wake the main chip for a photo. A battery lasts longer this way.

    Uses: Camera, Sips power. Add: MIPI camera, motion sensor, battery pack, microSD card.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

The Olimex ESP32-P4-DevKit has full design files under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and the Adafruit Metro ESP32-P4 has files under CC BY-SA. Both boards are OSHWA certified. ESP-IDF is open under Apache-2.0 with no closed P4 libraries. The Wi-Fi helper chip on most boards runs closed radio software. The chip design itself stays closed.
