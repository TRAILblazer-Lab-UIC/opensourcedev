ESP32-S2

Overview

The ESP32-S2 is a Wi-Fi chip from Espressif with USB built right in. Boards show up on a computer as a drive or a keyboard. That made it an early favorite for CircuitPython on Adafruit boards. It suits beginners, teachers, and makers who want Wi-Fi gadgets and USB tools. It has no Bluetooth.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • CircuitPython projects edited straight from the board drive
  • Custom USB keyboards, macro pads, and game controllers
  • Wi-Fi sensors, signs, and smart home devices
  • Battery gadgets on boards with a charger
  • Touch-sensitive art with the built-in touch pins

Not great for

  • Bluetooth projects, with no Bluetooth on the chip
  • Heavy tasks that need two processor cores
  • Voice assistants or machine learning on camera images
  • High-quality camera projects

Specs in plain words

  • A single 240 MHz core handles Wi-Fi, sensors, and small screens with ease.
  • 320 KB of memory plus 2 MB of extra memory on many boards holds CircuitPython programs and fonts.
  • 4 MB of storage holds your code and small data files on the board drive.
  • Native USB lets the board act as a drive, a keyboard or a gamepad.
  • 14 touch pins sense a fingertip with only a wire or a strip of foil.
  • Deep sleep can drop to about 20 millionths of an amp on the bare chip.

Chip values come from the ESP32-S2 Series datasheet v1.9 (July 2026). The ESP32-S2FH4 has 4 MB flash, the FN4R2 has 4 MB flash and 2 MB PSRAM, and the R2 has 2 MB PSRAM. The FH2 is end of life. Flash and PSRAM vary by board. 4 MB flash and 2 MB PSRAM is the most common pairing (Adafruit Feather, QT Py, Metro). The current DevKitC-1-N8R2 has 8 MB flash. Connectors, onboard items, power and size describe the Adafruit ESP32-S2 Feather, which has a NeoPixel, Reset and Boot buttons, LiPo charger and a MAX17048 battery monitor. The 20 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current with the RTC timer only. The Feather draws about 80 to 100 uA in deep sleep. The camera and LCD interfaces reuse the I2S and SPI hardware. USB OTG is full speed (12 Mbps).

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Desk weather display

    Fetch the forecast over Wi-Fi and show it on a small screen. Check the sky before you leave home.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen. Add: small screen.

  • Macro keypad

    Make a row of buttons that act as a USB keyboard. One press can copy, paste, or mute your video call.

    Add: push buttons, key switches.

  • Plant watering reminder

    Read a soil moisture sensor and send a message over Wi-Fi to your phone. The plant asks for water by itself.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Touch-sensitive art piece

    Wire copper tape to the touch pins and light LEDs with each touch. Hang it in a hallway for visitors to play.

    Add: copper tape, LED strip.

  • Classroom science logger

    Plug in a sensor with a Qwiic cable and save readings as a file on the board drive. Students open it in a spreadsheet.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: light sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Battery room sensor

    Read temperature and humidity on battery power and post the numbers online every few minutes. The board sleeps in between.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Battery ready, Sips power, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, LiPo battery.

  • Mini synth with buttons

    Play notes through a small amplifier with a few buttons. Twist a knob to change the pitch.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small amplifier, small speaker, push buttons, knob.

  • Clap-activated lamp

    Listen through a microphone and toggle a lamp at a loud clap. It is a fun first sound project.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone, relay module.

  • Online scoreboard sign

    Pull sports scores over Wi-Fi and scroll them across an LED matrix. Hang it in a game room.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen. Add: LED matrix.

  • USB game controller

    Build a custom gamepad that your computer sees as a real controller. Add big arcade buttons for extra fun.

    Add: arcade buttons, joystick.

  • Community air quality station

    Read a dust sensor and post readings to a shared online map over Wi-Fi. Neighbors can check the air on their phones.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Smart home device with ESPHome

    Flash ESPHome and add the board to Home Assistant over Wi-Fi. Control lights or read sensors from your phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: relay module.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Count visits with a motion sensor and post the daily total online. Kids can graph which hours draw the most birds.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: motion sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Simple security camera

    Attach a small camera and snap a low-resolution photo over Wi-Fi on a schedule. It peeks in on a pet or a shed.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: small camera module.

  • USB flash drive reader

    Plug a USB drive into the board and read files from it. A logger can save data to the stick.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB host adapter, USB flash drive.

How open is it?

Open Board

Adafruit publishes Eagle design files for its Feather, QT Py, and Metro S2 boards under CC BY-SA. Those boards are OSHWA certified, as are Olimex boards under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. ESP-IDF is open under Apache-2.0 and CircuitPython is open too. The Wi-Fi libraries and the Xtensa processor core stay closed.

Hardware license: CC BY-SA (Adafruit Feather, QT Py and Metro ESP32-S2), CERN-OHL-S-2.0 (Olimex ESP32-S2-DevKit-Lipo), Espressif DevKitC-1 schematic and layout PDFs without a stated license · Software license: Apache-2.0
