Overview

The ESP32-S3 is a microcontroller chip from Espressif with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth built in. It sits on many hobby boards, from thumb-sized modules to boards with their own screens. It suits makers who want gadgets that talk to phones and the internet. Its extra math instructions help it run tiny AI models for words, gestures and simple images.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart home gadgets that connect over Wi-Fi
  • Small color screens and dashboards
  • Camera and microphone projects with tiny AI models
  • Bluetooth controllers and wearables
  • Battery sensors that sleep most of the time

Not great for

  • Large AI models or anything like a chatbot
  • Running Linux or desktop programs
  • Total beginners who want a no-setup first board
  • Old Bluetooth audio gear, which needs Bluetooth Classic

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at up to 240 MHz can run Wi-Fi, a screen and sensors together.
  • 512 KB of working memory plus up to 8 MB of extra memory on many boards gives room for pictures and small AI models.
  • 8 MB of storage on common boards fits your program, web pages and sound clips.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy let it chat with your phone and home network.
  • A camera port and a screen port let it see and show things.
  • Deep sleep draws about 7 microamps, which lets a battery last a long time.

Chip values come from the ESP32-S3 datasheet. Flash and PSRAM vary by module and board. Values shown are for the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1-N8R8 (WROOM-1 module, 8 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM). Current DevKitC-1 variants carry 16 MB flash with 8 MB PSRAM or 32 MB flash with 16 MB PSRAM. The DevKitC-1 has a micro-USB port on a USB-UART bridge, a second port on the chip's native USB, an addressable RGB LED and Boot and Reset buttons. Many third-party boards use USB-C instead. The 45 GPIO is the chip count. Boards expose fewer. The 7 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current for the chip alone.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi weather station

    Read temperature and humidity and show them on a web page. Check the backyard from your phone on the home network.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Desk clock with a screen

    Pull the time and forecast from the internet and draw them on a small color screen. It makes a cheerful desk buddy.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen. Add: small color screen.

  • Touch-sensitive music box

    Use the built-in touch pins as piano keys made of foil or fruit. Each tap plays a note on a small speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker, amplifier board, foil or fruit.

  • Smart LED strip mood light

    Control color LEDs from a phone browser. Pick calm colors for reading and party patterns for weekends.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • USB keyboard shortcut deck

    Make a pad of keys that the computer sees as a keyboard. Each key can type a phrase or switch apps.

    Add: push buttons, small enclosure.

Builder ideas

  • Smart plant watering helper

    Watch soil moisture and run a small pump once the pot gets dry. Your phone can show how thirsty each plant is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Bird feeder camera

    Snap photos of visitors at the feeder and save them to a memory card. Review the guest list each evening.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: camera module, microSD card, weatherproof box.

  • Bluetooth game controller

    Build a handheld pad that pairs with a phone or computer over Bluetooth. Play your favorite games with custom buttons.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: push buttons, joystick, battery.

  • Room air quality monitor

    Measure carbon dioxide in a classroom and show a color warning. Open a window once the color turns red.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen. Add: carbon dioxide sensor, small screen.

  • Internet radio player

    Stream online radio stations over Wi-Fi and play them through a speaker. Knobs pick the station and volume.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier board, speaker, rotary knob.

  • Mailbox arrival alert

    Sleep for days on a battery and wake once the mailbox door opens. Your phone gets a ping that mail has come.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: door sensor, battery pack.

  • Community noise logger

    Record sound levels on a street corner through the day. Neighbors can share a chart of busy and quiet hours.

    Uses: Hears sound, Wi-Fi. Add: digital microphone, weatherproof box.

Expert ideas

  • Wake word light switch

    Train a tiny model to hear one spoken word and toggle a lamp. It runs on the board with no cloud service.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone, relay module.

  • Gesture sensing wearable

    Teach a tiny model to recognize arm waves from a motion sensor. A wave could flip slides or start music.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, small battery.

  • Simple object spotter camera

    Run a small vision model that notices a person or a pet in view. It can flash a light or send a note.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: camera module.

  • Home sensor network hub

    Gather readings from Bluetooth sensors around the house and pass them to a home dashboard over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Wi-Fi. Add: Bluetooth temperature sensors.

SparkFun and Adafruit publish board design files for their ESP32-S3 boards under CC-BY-SA. The ESP-IDF toolkit is open source under Apache-2.0 and the Arduino core is LGPL-2.1. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code ships as closed files, and the chip design itself is closed.
