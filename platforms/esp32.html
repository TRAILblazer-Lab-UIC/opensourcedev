The classic low-cost Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip behind countless smart gadgets.

Overview

The ESP32 is the original Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chip from Espressif, released in 2016. It has two processor cores and plenty of pins. Thousands of boards use it, from cheap generic devkits to Adafruit Feathers. It is the only chip in the family with classic Bluetooth, which makes it the pick for Bluetooth audio projects.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart home sensors and switches on Wi-Fi
  • Ready-made firmware such as WLED, ESPHome, and Tasmota
  • Bluetooth audio and wireless game controllers
  • Simple camera projects on ESP32 camera boards
  • Learning Arduino or MicroPython on a cheap wireless board

Not great for

  • Long battery life on most boards, as Wi-Fi uses a lot of power
  • Smooth video or big AI models
  • Plug-and-go USB, as the chip has no built-in USB and some computers need a driver
  • Projects that need Thread or Zigbee radios

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 240 MHz let it run Wi-Fi on one core and your program on the other.
  • 520 KB of working memory holds a small web page and thousands of sensor readings.
  • 4 MB of flash storage on most boards fits your program plus a few small files.
  • 34 pins connect sensors, lights, and buttons, and boards expose fewer of them.
  • Ten touch pins can sense a fingertip with just a piece of metal or foil.
  • Deep sleep draws about 10 microamps for the chip alone. A battery sensor can nap between readings.

Chip values come from the ESP32 Series datasheet v5.3. Flash and PSRAM vary by module and board. Values shown are for the ESP32-DevKitC-32E (WROOM-32E module, 4 MB flash, no PSRAM). WROVER-E based DevKitC boards and the Adafruit Feather V2 add PSRAM (8 MB and 2 MB). The chip has no native USB. As a result, boards use a USB-UART bridge. Camera support uses the I2S parallel mode through the esp32-camera driver, as on ESP32-CAM boards. There is no dedicated DVP block. The 34 GPIO is the chip count, and six of those are input only. Boards expose fewer. The 10 uA figure is the datasheet deep-sleep current for the chip alone. Several older part numbers (ESP32-D0WD, D0WDQ6, S0WD) are marked NRND. The ESP32-D0WD-V3 (chip revision 3) is the current part.

Things you could build

18 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wi-Fi weather station

    Read temperature and humidity and show them on a small web page. Check the backyard from your phone on the home network.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Phone controlled LED strip

    Flash the free WLED firmware and wire up an LED strip. Pick colors and effects from a phone app with no coding.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

  • Home Assistant sensor with ESPHome

    Describe a motion or door sensor in a short settings file and let ESPHome build the firmware. The sensor shows up in your smart home dashboard.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: motion sensor.

  • Internet desk clock

    Fetch the exact time and the forecast over Wi-Fi and show them on a small screen. It makes a cheerful desk buddy.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: small OLED screen.

Builder ideas

  • Bluetooth speaker receiver

    Stream music from your phone over classic Bluetooth to an old pair of speakers. The ESP32 is the only family member with this trick.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier board, speakers.

  • Smart plant watering helper

    Watch soil moisture and run a small pump once the pot gets dry. Your phone can show how thirsty each plant is.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Bird feeder camera

    Use an ESP32 camera board to snap photos of visiting birds. Browse the pictures from your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: ESP32 camera board, weatherproof box, 5 volt power supply.

  • Touch sensitive art panel

    Connect copper tape shapes to the touch pins on the board. Each touch lights a different part of a painting.

    Add: copper tape, LED strip.

  • Classroom noise meter

    Measure the sound level with a microphone and show green, yellow or red lights. Students can see the volume at a glance.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone board, LED strip.

  • Bluetooth game controller

    Wire up a joystick and a few buttons to make a wireless game pad. Pair it with a computer or a phone game.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: joystick module, push buttons.

  • Mailbox mail alert

    Sleep inside the mailbox and wake up the moment the lid opens. A message lands on your phone right away.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery holder.

  • Community air quality monitor

    Measure dust in the air and post the readings to a shared map. Neighbors can compare air across the block.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor, 5 volt power supply.

  • Heart rate display for gym class

    Clip a pulse sensor to a fingertip and send the beats to a tablet over Bluetooth. Students graph their heart rate before and after exercise.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: pulse sensor.

  • Quiz show buzzers

    Give each team a big button on its own board. The boards race over Wi-Fi and the first press lights up.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: large push buttons, LEDs.

Expert ideas

  • Internet radio

    Stream radio stations over Wi-Fi and play them through a small speaker. Add a knob to switch stations.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier board, small speaker, knob.

  • Wired network sensor hub

    Connect the board to your router with a network cable for a steady link. It gathers readings from sensors around a workshop.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet add-on board, temperature sensors.

  • Video doorbell

    Stream live video from the front door to a phone and ring a chime on a button press. Keep the picture small for a smooth stream.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: ESP32 camera board, push button, small speaker.

  • Car data reader

    Listen to the data network inside a car or an electric bike. Show speed or battery level on a small screen.

    Add: CAN transceiver board, small OLED screen.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

Many ESP32 boards are certified open hardware. The Olimex ESP32-DevKit-LiPo shares KiCad files under Apache-2.0, and Adafruit and SparkFun share their files under CC BY-SA. ESP-IDF is open under Apache-2.0, but the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth libraries ship as closed files and the chip design stays proprietary.
