Overview

The ESP32-LyraT is an audio development board from Espressif. It pairs an ESP32 chip with a stereo sound chip, two microphones and two speaker outputs. It was the reference board for Espressif's audio framework, with examples for internet radio, Bluetooth speakers and voice wake-up. Distributors now list it as obsolete. Espressif's smaller ESP32-LyraT-Mini and newer ESP32-S3 audio kits carry on the idea, and many third-party ESP32 audio kits copy its layout.

Not great for

  • Using the microSD card and the JTAG debugger at the same time

Specs in plain words

  • A dual-core 240 MHz chip can play music and handle Wi-Fi together.
  • 8 MB of extra memory holds plenty of audio for streaming and effects.
  • 4 MB of flash stores your program and some sound clips.
  • Two 3 watt amplifiers can drive small speakers loud enough for a room.
  • A battery socket with a charger lets it play music away from the wall.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth Classic, and Bluetooth Low Energy connect it to phones and the internet.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorEspressif ESP32 (dual-core Xtensa LX6) in an ESP32-WROVER-E module, 2 cores at 240 MHz
Memory520 KB plus 8 MB extra (PSRAM)
Storage4 MB
Ways to connect partsDigital audio (I2S), I2C, Serial (UART), SD card bus, Debug port
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Classic, Bluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsMicro USB, microSD slot, Audio jack, Battery plug, Pin headers
Built inmicrophone, buttons, battery charger
Power5 V over micro USB, or a 3.7 V Li-ion battery
Sizeunknown mm

Values are for V4.3. V4.2 used the ESP32-WROVER module and a single amplifier. Clock and SRAM are ESP32 chip values. Flash and PSRAM are module values from the Espressif docs. Most ESP32 GPIO are used by the codec, SD card, touch pads and keys. The board breaks out UART, I2S, I2C and JTAG headers. microSD and JTAG share GPIO 12 to 15 and are chosen with a DIP switch. USB-UART bridge is a CP2102 (schematic) or CP2102N (NuttX docs). The headphone jack supports OMTP headsets only. Board size was not found on any fetched page.

How open is it?

Open Board

Espressif shares a schematic PDF and a parts layout PDF, but no full board files. No hardware license is stated. ESP-ADF uses the Espressif MIT License, and some audio decoders and the radio stacks come as closed binary files.
