Espruino Bangle.js 2

Espruino (Pur3 Ltd)

A hackable smartwatch you program in JavaScript from a web browser.

Field marks: A chunky watch you program in JavaScript from a browser, with GPS, a heart rate sensor, a barometer, and about four weeks of standby on a 175 mAh battery.

Overview

Bangle.js 2 is a smartwatch made for tinkering. It runs the open Espruino JavaScript engine, and you send apps to it over Bluetooth from a web browser. A community app loader offers hundreds of free faces and apps. It suits curious beginners, students learning JavaScript, and makers who want a wrist full of sensors.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning JavaScript on a real gadget you can wear
  • Fitness, hiking, and GPS logging apps
  • Bluetooth remotes for slides, robots, and music
  • Sensor experiments with motion, heart rate, pressure, and direction

Not great for

  • Projects that need extra wires. Every chip pin already drives a part inside the watch.
  • Sound projects. The watch has a buzzing motor and no speaker.
  • Coding over a cable. The charge cable carries no data.
  • Tight budgets. Some stores charge a lot for it.

Specs in plain words

  • 256 KB of working memory holds one small app and its data at a time.
  • 8 MB of extra storage keeps many apps, faces and GPS tracks on the watch.
  • The 1.3 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp, and it stays on all day without draining the battery.
  • Bluetooth LE lets the watch talk to phones, laptops and other small gadgets a few rooms away.
  • The IP67 rating means it survives rain and a quick dunk in water.
  • The battery can last about four weeks on standby.

Size36 x 43 x 12 mm

The nRF52840 has 1 MB of on-chip flash, and the watch adds 8 MB of external SPI flash (GD25Q64). All chip pins drive internal parts. As a result, there is no free GPIO header. The charge cable has no USB data lines, and two of the four rear contacts are SWD pads. Sensors are a KX022 accelerometer, a 3-axis magnetometer, a BMP280 or SPL06 barometer, and a VC31 heart rate monitor. The watch has a vibration motor and no speaker. The official page lists a 175 mAh battery, and the 2021 CNX article listed 200 mAh. The official page gives no sleep current.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Custom watch face

    Pick a free face from the app loader and change its colors or layout. Your wrist shows the time your way.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Daily step and heart tracker

    Install a fitness app from the app loader. The watch counts steps and checks your heart rate through the day.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • GPS walk logger

    Use a GPS recorder app on a walk or bike ride. Load the track on a computer later to see your route.

    Uses: GPS.

  • Wrist compass for hikes

    Install a compass app that uses the built-in magnetometer. The screen points north on the trail.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Classroom JavaScript lab

    Students write short JavaScript programs in the Web IDE and send them to the watch. They see their code run on real hardware right away.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Weather watcher on your wrist

    Show air pressure from the barometer on the watch face. A falling number can hint that rain is coming.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Quiet buzz timer

    Write a timer app that buzzes your wrist with the vibration motor. It works for study breaks or kitchen timing.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Medicine reminder

    Code a reminder that buzzes at set times each day. A tap on the screen marks the dose as taken.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Bird walk tally counter

    Make a touch app with buttons for common birds. Each tap saves a count with a GPS spot.

    Uses: GPS, Screen.

  • Bluetooth slide clicker

    Turn the watch into a remote for a talk. A tap or button press moves your slides forward.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Wrist remote for a robot

    Tilt your wrist to steer a small robot car. The watch sends accelerometer data over Bluetooth to the robot board.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in. Add: Bluetooth robot car kit.

  • Sleep motion study

    Log wrist movement through the night with the accelerometer. Graph the data later to see restless and calm hours.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Altitude logger for a hill climb

    Record barometer readings on a climb. A small chart on the screen shows how high you went.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Gesture music controller

    Send wrist tilts to a music app on a computer over Bluetooth. Waving your arm bends notes or changes the beat.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Plant sensor dashboard

    Read a Bluetooth soil sensor in the garden from your wrist. The watch shows when your plants look thirsty.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen. Add: Bluetooth soil moisture sensor.

  • Bare-metal C experiments

    Connect a debug probe to the rear pads and write firmware in C. This path teaches how chips start up.

    Add: SWD debug probe.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Espruino firmware is open under the MPL-2.0 license, and the community apps are MIT licensed. The watch hardware is closed, with only a case model, a pin map and board photos published. The Nordic Bluetooth stack is a closed binary blob.

Software license: MPL-2.0 (Espruino firmware), MIT (BangleApps app loader)
