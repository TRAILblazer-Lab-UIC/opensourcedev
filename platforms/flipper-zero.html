A pocket gadget that reads and copies remotes, cards, and tags for learning.

Overview

Flipper Zero is a pocket tool that reads, saves, and replays signals from remotes, cards, and tags. It has its own screen, buttons, and battery. Anyone can use it through menus with no computer. Hobbyists and security learners use it to see how everyday wireless gadgets work.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how infrared, radio remotes, and contactless cards work
  • Controlling your own TVs and gadgets from one device
  • Writing small JavaScript or C apps for a handheld screen
  • Hardware hobbyists who want a debug probe and pin header on the go

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi or internet projects without an add-on board
  • Big screens or color graphics, with a small black and white display
  • Tight budgets, at about 169 US dollars
  • Using radio features freely, with bands locked by region and local laws to follow

Specs in plain words

  • The 64 MHz chip with 256 KB of memory runs simple apps and menus quickly.
  • The 1.4 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp and shows simple black and white pictures.
  • A 2100 mAh battery lasts days or even weeks between charges.
  • The sub-GHz radio talks to garage remotes, doorbells, and sensors on common bands.
  • The NFC and RFID readers handle the same contactless cards and tags found in offices and pets.
  • A 13-pin header lets you wire sensors, LEDs, or other boards.

Ways to connect partsSPI, USB device, Debug port
RadiosBluetooth Low Energy, Thread or Zigbee, Sub-GHz radio, NFC

Built inscreen, buttons, speaker, battery charger
Power5 V over USB-C (up to 1 A charging), built-in 2100 mAh LiPo
Size100.3 x 40.1 x 25.6 mm

Cores counts only the user Cortex-M4 core. The 32 MHz Cortex-M0+ runs the radio stack. Flash and RAM are shared between the two cores. Other radios not in the vocabulary are 125 kHz RFID, iButton (1-Wire) and infrared (940 nm TX, 950 nm RX at 38 kHz). The sub-GHz CC1101 TX power is listed as -20 dBm max in the tech specs. BLE 5.4 at 4 dBm. The 802.15.4 radio is in the STM32WB but has little firmware use. GPIO pins are 3.3 V with 5 V tolerant inputs. Weight 102 g. Battery life up to 28 days per the spec page. The microSD card (SPI) is required but not included. The tech specs page does not list I2C or UART pins in text. These are shown only in a pinout image.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Universal TV remote

    Use the built-in infrared library to turn TVs and projectors on and off. One gadget replaces a drawer of lost remotes.

    Uses: Battery ready, Screen.

  • Backup copy of your own remote

    Record the signal from your own fan or air conditioner remote. Replay it from the Flipper on the day the remote goes missing.

    Uses: Battery ready, Screen.

  • Card and tag explorer

    Scan your own library card, hotel key, or pet tag to see what kind of chip it holds. It is a hands-on lesson in how contactless cards work.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Radio signal spotter

    Use the frequency analyzer to see which band a car key or doorbell button uses. Learn how everyday wireless remotes talk.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Infrared signal lesson

    Point a remote at the Flipper and watch it capture the button press. Students see that invisible light carries the message.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Spare key for your own iButton door

    Save the iButton key for your own shed or locker. The Flipper can stand in for a lost key.

    Uses: Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Typing shortcut helper

    Plug into your own computer over USB and play a saved script that types a long setup routine. It saves time on repeated chores.

  • Sensor readout on the go

    Wire a temperature sensor to the pin header and show the reading on the screen. Carry it around the garden to find warm spots.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

  • JavaScript mini apps

    Write short JavaScript files and drop them on the microSD card. Make a dice roller, a timer, or a quiz.

    Uses: Screen.

  • LED light show

    Connect a few LEDs to the pin header and blink them in patterns from a script. It makes a pocket light toy.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: LEDs, resistors, jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Bluetooth sensor tag

    Write a C app that sends a sensor reading over Bluetooth. A phone nearby can show the value.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

  • Beep and buzz games

    Build a small reaction game in C that beeps and vibrates. Challenge friends to press the button fastest.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Hardware debugger for other boards

    Turn the pin header into a debug probe for small microcontroller boards. Step through their code from your computer.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Custom C app for the catalog

    Build your own app with the free app SDK and share it with others. The community catalog lists apps from many makers.

    Uses: Screen.
