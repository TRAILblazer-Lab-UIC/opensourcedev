Overview

The Freenove 4WD Car Kit is a robot car you assemble yourself. A Raspberry Pi rides on top and runs all the code. The kit has four driven wheels, a camera on a tilting head, a distance sensor, line sensors, light sensors, and color LEDs. It suits curious teens, hobbyists, and teachers who want to learn Python robotics.

  • Classrooms that already use Raspberry Pi boards

Not great for

  • Selling products built on its code, as the license forbids commercial use
  • Builders who want to study the circuit board, as no schematic is published

Specs in plain words

  • The kit has no brain of its own. A Raspberry Pi 5, 4B, 3B+, 3B, or 3A+ does all the thinking.
  • Four geared motors drive all four wheels. That gives the car grip on rugs and small bumps.
  • The camera sits on two servo motors. It can look left, right, up, and down.
  • The ultrasonic sensor works like a bat. It sends out a sound pulse and times the echo to measure distance.
  • Two 18650 batteries power the car. Each cell is a bit bigger than a AA battery.

The kit has no processor of its own. The Raspberry Pi runs everything. CPU, RAM, GPIO and radio values are for a Raspberry Pi 4B with 4 GB (official Raspberry Pi spec page lists 1.8 GHz, 1 to 8 GB). A Pi 5 or 3B+ changes them. flash_kb is null because the Pi boots from a microSD card. adc_channels is the 8-channel ADS7830 on the connection board (PCB V2.0. V1.0 used a different ADC, datasheets for ADS7830 and PCF8591 are both in the repo). The PCA9685 PWM chip drives the servos and motors over I2C. On PCB V2.0 the RGB LEDs need SPI enabled. The camera uses the ov5647 overlay. The Pi 5 needs the cam0 overlay variant. The ultrasonic sensor, line tracker and buzzer have no matching vocabulary terms. The assembled car size was not stated on any page read.

    Drive the car from a phone or PC over Wi-Fi. You see what the camera sees in real time.
