Overview

The Freenove Robot Dog Kit is a four-legged robot you build yourself. A Raspberry Pi rides on its back and runs everything. It has twelve leg servos, a head servo, a camera, a distance sensor, a motion sensor, and color lights. It suits hobbyists and older students who have built a simpler robot and want a real challenge.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how walking robots balance and move their legs
  • Python projects with face recognition and ball tracking
  • A step up after a wheeled robot car
  • Robotics clubs that already own Raspberry Pi boards

Not great for

  • Complete beginners, as the build takes about five to six hours
  • Long play sessions, as the servos need rest after a few minutes of hard work
  • Selling products built on its code, as the license forbids commercial use

Specs in plain words

  • The kit has no brain of its own. A Raspberry Pi 5, 4B, 3B+, 3B, or 3A+ does all the thinking.
  • Twelve small metal-gear servos move the legs, with three in each leg. That lets each leg lift, swing, and bend.
  • The MPU6050 motion sensor feels tilt. The dog uses it to stay level on a slope.
  • The camera lets the dog recognize faces and follow a red ball.
  • Two high-drain 18650 batteries power the dog. Each cell is a bit bigger than a AA battery.

The kit has no processor of its own. The Raspberry Pi runs everything. CPU, RAM, GPIO and radio values are for a Raspberry Pi 4B with 4 GB (official spec page lists 1.8 GHz, 1 to 8 GB). A Pi 5 or 3B+ changes them. flash_kb is null because the Pi boots from a microSD card. adc_channels is the 8-channel ADS7830 (I2C 0x48), used to read battery voltage. The PCA9685 (0x40) drives the servos. The MPU6050 sits at 0x68. The USB-C port and battery_charger entry come from the RaspberryTips review, which says the robot has a USB-C charging port that did not work for the reviewer. Board revision details were not confirmed. The ultrasonic sensor and buzzer have no matching vocabulary terms. Assembled size was not stated on any page read.
