FriendlyElec CM3588 NAS Kit

FriendlyElec

A home storage server kit with four slots for fast SSDs.

Field marks: Look for a bare green board with four empty SSD slots, one 2.5 GbE port, and a short list of SSD brands it refuses to meet.

Overview

The CM3588 NAS Kit pairs FriendlyElec's RK3588 compute module with a carrier board made for SSD storage. It has four M.2 slots for NVMe drives and a 2.5 GbE network port. Ready-made images include OpenMediaVault, Debian, Ubuntu, and a Proxmox preview. It suits home lab fans who want a small, quiet, all-flash file server.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building a quiet all-flash home NAS
  • Hosting Docker apps and small virtual machines
  • Media servers that stream around the house
  • Tinkerers who want schematics for the board

Not great for

  • People who want a plug-and-play NAS with no setup
  • Top SSD speed, as each slot gets one PCIe lane
  • Network speed above about 2.3 Gbps from the single port
  • Some SSD models, as FriendlyElec warns that a few WD drives may not work

Specs in plain words

  • Eight Arm cores up to 2.4 GHz give it laptop-class speed for server jobs.
  • The 4 GB entry kit holds a few light apps, and 8 GB to 32 GB versions handle more.
  • Four M.2 slots each reach about 1 GB per second, plenty for a home network.
  • The 2.5 GbE port moves files about two and a half times faster than common Gigabit Ethernet.
  • A 6 TOPS NPU can run small AI models for photos or video.
  • The board idles near 5 W with SSDs, about the draw of a night light.

RAM and eMMC values are for the entry shop SKU, 4 GB LPDDR4X with 64 GB eMMC. The module spec also lists 8 and 16 GB LPDDR4X and 0 or 256 GB eMMC, but the shop offers only 4 or 8 GB with 64 GB. The CM3588 Plus module adds 16 or 32 GB LPDDR5. The module is 55 x 65 mm on four 100-pin board-to-board connectors. The carrier is a 4-layer 160 x 116 mm PCB with a 40-pin header, 5 V fan connector, 3.5 mm audio and MIPI DSI and CSI connectors. GPIO count on the header is not stated.

Starter ideas

  • All-flash family file server

    Fill the four slots with SSDs and run OpenMediaVault. Everyone at home saves photos and files to one quiet box.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe SSDs, 12 V power supply.

Builder ideas

  • Photo backup for every phone

    Run a self-hosted photo app on the NAS. Phones back up pictures over the home network each night.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe SSDs, 12 V power supply.

  • Home movie streaming box

    Host a media server and stream films to TVs in the house. The HDMI output can also play them on a screen nearby.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux, Screen. Add: NVMe SSDs, HDMI cable.

  • Docker playground

    Run small web apps in containers on the board. Try a wiki, a recipe book, or a password manager.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe SSD.

  • School shared drive

    Give a classroom one place to store projects. Students reach it from their laptops over the school network.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe SSDs, case with cooling fan.

  • Dual-screen info kiosk

    Drive two monitors from the two HDMI outputs. Show a schedule on one and a slideshow on the other.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: two monitors, two HDMI cables.

  • Garden camera time-lapse

    Connect a MIPI camera and snap a garden photo every hour. Stitch the shots into a growing season video.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: MIPI camera module, long camera cable.

  • Music library and speaker player

    Store your music on the NAS and play it through the 3.5 mm audio jack. Control it from a phone browser.

    Uses: Makes sound, Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: powered speakers.

  • Community archive server

    Hold local history photos and recordings for a club or library. Members browse the archive over the network.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe SSDs, case with cooling fan.

Expert ideas

  • Proxmox home lab

    Run several virtual machines on the eight cores. It is a cheap way to practice server skills.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe SSDs, 12 V power supply.

  • Home router with FriendlyWrt

    Flash FriendlyWrt and route your home network through the board. Add a USB network adapter for a second port.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB Ethernet adapter.

  • Security camera recorder

    Record network cameras to the SSDs around the clock. The NPU can flag clips that show a person.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network, AI chip. Add: network cameras, NVMe SSDs.

  • HDMI capture and record station

    Feed a game console or camera into the HDMI input. Save the video straight to the SSDs.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: HDMI cable, NVMe SSD.

  • Local AI photo sorter

    Run an image model on the NPU to tag photos by what they show. Your pictures stay at home.

    Uses: AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe SSDs.

FriendlyElec publishes schematic PDFs, a layout PDF, DXF outlines, and a 3D model for the carrier. No editable layout or hardware license is offered. The Linux kernel is open under GPL-2.0. The NPU runtime, the GPU user space, and some boot files are closed.
