Overview

The GD32VF103 is a small 32-bit chip from GigaDevice. It was one of the first cheap RISC-V chips hobbyists could buy. Most people met it on the Sipeed Longan Nano, a tiny board with a color screen. It suits curious tinkerers who want to learn about RISC-V and already know some C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how a RISC-V processor runs simple programs
  • Small screen projects with the Longan Nano display
  • Reading sensors and saving data to a microSD card
  • Acting as a USB or CAN helper chip in bigger projects

Not great for

  • Beginners who want a quick start with lots of tutorials
  • Wireless projects on most boards. Only the Wio Lite adds Wi-Fi.
  • Buying new boards today. Most are discontinued.
  • Big programs or heavy graphics with only 32 KB of working memory

Specs in plain words

  • The 108 MHz clock is quick enough for sensors, small screens and simple sound.
  • 128 KB of flash holds your program and a few small pictures for the screen.
  • 32 KB of working memory is room for a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • The Longan Nano screen is 0.96 inches across, about the size of a fingernail.
  • The board is about the size of a stick of gum at 46 by 20 millimeters.
  • Sleep mode sips about 7.5 millionths of an amp. That helps battery projects last.

Memory, GPIO and ADC values are for the GD32VF103CBT6 in LQFP48, the chip on the Longan Nano and Wio Lite RISC-V. That package has 37 GPIO and 10 ADC channels. LQFP64 and LQFP100 parts have up to 51 and 80 GPIO and 16 ADC channels. The Longan Nano Lite and smaller parts have 64 KB flash and 20 KB SRAM. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Sipeed Longan Nano, which includes a 0.96 inch 160x80 IPS LCD. The 7.5 uA sleep figure and 33.7 mA active figure come from CNX Software's launch coverage. GigaDevice's series page gives no current figures. JTAG is the debug interface. The chip has no SWD.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Scrolling name badge

    Show your name and a few pictures on the built-in screen. Wear it at a club meeting or maker fair.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small battery pack.

  • Room temperature display

    Read a temperature sensor and print the reading on the tiny screen. Keep it on a desk or bookshelf.

    Uses: Screen. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Plant thirst meter

    Check how dry the soil is and show a happy or sad face on the screen. You know when to water your plant.

    Uses: Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Pocket reaction game

    Flash a shape on the screen and time how fast a friend presses a button. The best score stays on top.

    Uses: Screen. Add: push button.

  • Tiny WAV music player

    Play sound files from a microSD card through a small speaker. Pick songs with two buttons.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: microSD card, small amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Sound level meter for class

    Measure how loud the room is with a microphone. A bar on the screen grows as the noise rises.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: microphone board.

  • Garden data logger

    Save light and temperature readings to a microSD card every few minutes. Graph a week of garden weather later.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: light sensor, temperature sensor, microSD card, battery pack.

  • Custom USB keyboard buttons

    Make a small pad of buttons that types shortcuts on your computer. It helps with art apps or video calls.

    Add: push buttons.

Expert ideas

  • USB keyboard to screen reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board and show typed letters on the tiny screen. It makes a fun typing tutor.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, USB OTG adapter.

  • Learn RISC-V assembly

    Write tiny programs in the chip's own instructions and watch the results on the screen. It shows how a processor really works.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Car network listener

    Use the CAN bus to read messages on a model train or robot network. Show the messages scroll by on the screen.

    Uses: Screen. Add: CAN transceiver board.

  • Simple tone synthesizer

    Make tones with the built-in sound outputs and change the pitch with a knob. Play simple tunes on a small speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knob, small amplifier board, small speaker.
