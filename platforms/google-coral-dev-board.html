Overview

The Coral Dev Board is a small Linux computer that Google released in 2019. A removable module holds an NXP processor and a Google Edge TPU chip for fast AI. The Edge TPU runs vision models at 4 TOPS on about 2 watts. Major stores now list the board as discontinued. It suits experienced makers who find one secondhand and want to learn edge AI.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Fast camera vision that runs fully on the board
  • Voice and sound projects with the two built-in microphones
  • Learning how a prototype module moves onto a custom board
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth projects with AI on the side

Not great for

  • Newcomers who want a short setup with current software
  • Buyers who want a board still in active production
  • Training models or compiling them on the board itself
  • Projects that need many plain input and output pins

Specs in plain words

  • Four 1.5 GHz cores run Linux and light programs at a steady pace.
  • The 4 TOPS Edge TPU checks many camera frames each second on about the power of a night light.
  • 1 GB of memory fits Linux plus one small AI model at a time. A 4 GB version also exists.
  • 8 GB of built-in storage holds the Mendel Linux system with some room left for projects.
  • The 40-pin header follows the Raspberry Pi layout, but only 8 pins are plain on and off pins.

RAM and eMMC use the common 1 GB and 8 GB version. A 4 GB RAM and 16 GB eMMC version (G950-06210-01) also exists. The SoM is 40 x 48 mm. Baseboard dimensions appear only in a datasheet figure I did not read. As a result, size is null. The 40-pin header has 8 plain GPIO pins plus I2C, SPI, UART, PWM and SAI (I2S) pins. GPU is Vivante GC7000Lite. The module also has a crypto coprocessor. bt_classic is assumed from Bluetooth 4.2 and should be checked.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Home network dashboard

    Run a small web page that shows weather and to-do lists. Family members check it from any phone on the Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

  • Blink an LED with Python

    Wire an LED to the pin row and blink it with a short script. It is a gentle first test of the board.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: LED, resistor, jumper wires.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder species spotter

    Point a camera at a feeder and let an AI model name each bird. The board keeps a daily list for your backyard log.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Talking plant guide

    Hold a leaf up to the camera and have the board name the plant. It reads the answer out loud through a speaker.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Makes sound, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, small speaker.

  • Classroom image sorter demo

    Show students how a computer sorts pictures into groups. Live results appear on an HDMI screen.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, screen, HDMI cable.

  • Garden pest alert

    Watch a vegetable bed for rabbits or deer. The board sends a message to your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • People counter for a library

    Count visitors passing a doorway with a camera. The board saves only the numbers and keeps faces off any server.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module.

  • Bluetooth sensor collector

    Gather readings from small Bluetooth sensors around the house. The board stores them and draws simple graphs.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Runs Linux. Add: Bluetooth temperature sensors.

  • Smart trash sorting helper

    Show an item to the camera and get told which bin it goes in. A screen or speaker gives the answer.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Makes sound, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, small speaker.

  • Wildlife trail camera

    Save photos only of animals and skip empty frames. The low power AI chip keeps the board running cool in the field.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, microSD card, weatherproof box, large battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Voice keyword light switch

    Say a short keyword into the onboard microphones to flip a lamp on. A small speech model listens on the board itself.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: relay module, lamp.

  • Gesture controlled game

    Play a simple game by waving at the camera. A pose model tracks your arms and the screen shows the action.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, screen, HDMI cable.

  • Custom product prototype

    Build a prototype on the Dev Board and move the module to your own baseboard later. The open baseboard files give you a starting design.

    Uses: AI chip, Runs Linux.

  • Sound event logger

    Listen for dog barks or glass breaking with the onboard microphones. The board writes each event to a log with the time.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Runs Linux.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Google published the baseboard schematic and layout files under Apache-2.0 on GitHub. The Edge TPU runtime and the PyCoral and libcoral libraries are also Apache-2.0. The module with the processor and Edge TPU has no public design. The Edge TPU compiler ships only as a prebuilt package, and the graphics driver is likely closed.

Hardware license: Apache-2.0 · Software license: Apache-2.0 (libedgetpu, PyCoral, libcoral)
