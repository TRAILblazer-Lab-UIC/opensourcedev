Overview

YARD Stick One is a USB dongle that sends and receives digital radio packets below 1 GHz. A Texas Instruments chip handles the radio work, and you control it from Python with RfCat. Security researchers use it to study key fobs, doorbells, and other small gadgets. It suits people who already know some Python and want to learn how simple wireless devices talk.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Studying how your own sub-1 GHz gadgets send data
  • Python scripting with the RfCat shell
  • Security classes with a lab of owned devices
  • Learning about open hardware with full KiCad files

Not great for

  • Beginners with no Python or command line skills
  • General listening, as reviewers call its receiver weak
  • Wide signal pictures, as it is a packet radio with no waterfall view
  • Casual transmitting, which needs a license or strict local rules

Specs in plain words

  • It covers 300 to 348, 391 to 464, and 782 to 928 MHz. Those bands hold many remotes, sensors, and doorbells.
  • Up to 500 kbps is fast enough for the short packets these gadgets send.
  • 4 KB of memory is tiny. The chip does one radio job and passes data to your computer.
  • The SMA port fits standard SMA antennas. Many Wi-Fi antennas use RP-SMA and will not fit.
  • The stick is about 84 mm long and weighs about as much as two paper clips.

The schematic names the CC1111F32RSPR with a 48 MHz crystal, SKY13251 RF switches, an SPF5043Z amplifier and AP7313 regulators. It plugs in with a USB-A connector. TI lists the CC1111Fx with up to 32 KB flash, up to 4 KB RAM, 19 GPIO, an 8-input ADC and 10 dBm max TX power. Values shown are for the F32 part. CPU clock was not confirmed from a fetched TI page. As a result, it is null. GPIO is null because only the P1 expansion header is exposed. The board has three LEDs. Size and 7.8 g weight are from Adafruit.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The board is fully open. KiCad schematics and layout use the CERN-OHL-P-2.0 license, and OSHWA certified it as US000673. RfCat firmware and its Python client are BSD licensed and build with free tools. The Texas Instruments radio chip stays closed.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-P-2.0 · Software license: BSD (RfCat), GPL-2.0 (yardstick repo)
