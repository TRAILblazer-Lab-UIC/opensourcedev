Overview

HackRF Pro is a software defined radio from Great Scott Gadgets and the successor to the HackRF One. It plugs into a computer and turns almost any radio band from 100 kHz to 6 GHz into data you can see and study. It works with HackRF One software and add-ons. It suits students, radio amateurs, and security researchers who want a wide, open radio lab.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Seeing a huge range of radio bands on one device
  • Decoding public signals such as airplane and ship messages
  • GNU Radio, SDR++, and Python experiments
  • Studying fully open board files and firmware
  • Licensed amateur radio work across many bands

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as it costs about 400 USD with no antenna or cable
  • Sending and receiving at the same moment, as it is half duplex
  • Fine detail on weak signals, as normal mode uses 8-bit samples
  • Casual transmitting, which needs a license in most bands

Specs in plain words

  • 100 kHz to 6 GHz covers AM radio, FM radio, airplanes, satellites, and Wi-Fi bands.
  • 20 million samples a second lets it watch a slice of the airwaves about 20 MHz wide at once.
  • Half duplex means it can listen or send, one at a time.
  • The built-in TCXO keeps its tuning steady. Stations stay where you left them.
  • USB-C power and data come from your computer with one cable.

Chip list comes from the published KiCad schematic. mcu.kicad_sch names the LPC4330FET180 and a W25Q32JVSS SPI flash (32 Mbit, recorded as 4096 KB). fpga.kicad_sch names the ICE40UP5K-SG48ITR. The RF chain uses a MAX2831 transceiver, an RFFC5072 mixer, a MAX5864 ADC/DAC and an Si5351C clock generator fed by a TCXO. Cores counts the M4 and M0 in the LPC4330. Clock speed and SRAM size were not confirmed from a fetched NXP page. No radio vocabulary value fits a general SDR. As a result, radios is empty. The board has an SMA RF port, SMA clock in and out, internal pin headers and an LPC JTAG header. Size was not published. Hacker Warehouse lists 6 x 4 x 1 in and 0.4 lb, which may be the package size.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Explore the airwaves

    Open SDR++ and scroll through the bands on a live waterfall. Spot FM stations, pagers, and weather radio by their shapes.

    Add: wideband antenna, USB-C cable.

  • Listen to FM and AM radio

    Tune local broadcast stations with a free SDR app. See each station as a bright stripe on the screen.

    Add: wideband antenna, USB-C cable.

  • Spot noisy home gadgets

    Sweep the bands near your desk with hackrf_sweep. Switch devices on and off and find which charger leaks radio noise.

    Add: wideband antenna.

Builder ideas

  • Watch airplanes overhead

    Tune to 1090 MHz and decode the public position messages from airliners. Plot nearby flights on a map.

    Add: 1090 MHz antenna.

  • Track ships near the coast

    Decode the public AIS messages that ships broadcast near 162 MHz. Show boat names and routes on a map.

    Add: marine band antenna.

  • Weather satellite pictures

    Catch passing weather satellites near 137 MHz and turn their signals into cloud images. Compare the pictures with the forecast.

    Add: satellite antenna.

  • Read your own weather station

    Pick up the signals from your own outdoor weather sensor. Log the temperature on your computer.

    Add: 433 MHz antenna.

  • Test your own antennas

    Compare homemade antennas on a known local broadcast. Pick the design with the strongest signal.

    Add: homemade antennas, SMA adapters.

  • Learn modulation in class

    Build AM, FM, and digital receivers in GNU Radio from recorded samples. Students see each wave shape on screen.

  • Handheld field receiver

    Add a PortaPack add-on screen and battery. Carry it outside and scan bands with no laptop.

    Add: PortaPack add-on.

  • Python signal explorer

    Grab raw samples with the HackRF library and load them in Python. Plot the spectrum and try your own filters.

Expert ideas

  • Radio astronomy hydrogen hunt

    Listen near 1420 MHz for the faint hiss of hydrogen gas in the Milky Way. A horn or dish antenna points at the sky.

    Add: horn or dish antenna, low noise amplifier.

  • Antenna switching lab

    Add the Opera Cake board to switch between several antennas. Compare signals from different directions in one session.

    Add: Opera Cake add-on, several antennas.

  • Synced receivers experiment

    Link two HackRF units through the clock ports. Record the same signal on both and study timing differences.

    Add: second HackRF, SMA clock cable.

  • Licensed ham radio experiments

    Hold an amateur radio license first. Then send and receive on your licensed bands with low power and your call sign.

    Add: antenna for your band, band filter.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The hardware is fully open. Full KiCad files use the CERN-OHL-P-2.0 license, and OSHWA certified it as US002820. Firmware and host tools use GPL-2.0 and build with free tools. The radio chips stay closed, and open tools for the FPGA design are unconfirmed.
