Hardkernel ODROID-M1S

Hardkernel

A quiet, low-power Linux computer for home servers and smart home hubs.

Overview

The ODROID-M1S is a small Linux computer from Hardkernel in Korea. It runs four Arm cores on a Rockchip RK3566 chip and draws about one watt at idle. Every board has built-in eMMC storage, an NVMe slot, Gigabit Ethernet, and HDMI. It suits people who want a quiet home server or a Home Assistant hub.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running Home Assistant as a dedicated smart home hub
  • Small file servers with a fast NVMe SSD
  • Always-on network services like an ad blocker
  • 4K media playback on a TV
  • Learning Linux on a low-power desk computer

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on the board
  • Quick sensor projects, as the GPIO headers come unsoldered on the base model
  • Tight budgets, now that prices have climbed well above launch
  • Heavy AI or video work, which needs a faster chip

Specs in plain words

  • Four Cortex-A55 cores at 1.8 GHz give it the pace of a modest laptop for light jobs.
  • 4 GB or 8 GB of RAM runs a home server and a few apps at once. The Lite has 2 GB.
  • 64 GB of soldered eMMC holds the operating system with room to spare. You can skip the SD card.
  • The M.2 slot takes one NVMe SSD for big storage. It runs at PCIe 2.1 x1 speed.
  • About 1.1 watts at idle costs very little to leave on all year.
  • The 0.8 TOPS NPU speeds up small AI models such as simple sound or image sorting.

Power5 V (4.9 to 5.3 V) over USB-C, 5 V 3 A adapter recommended
Size90 x 65 mm

Values are for the 4 GB model with 64 GB eMMC. The 8 GB model keeps 64 GB eMMC, and the Lite has 2 GB with 32 GB eMMC. RAM is 32-bit LPDDR4 at 2112 MT/s. The M.2 slot is PCIe 2.1 x1 with no SATA. USB is one USB 3.0 host, one USB 2.0 host, one micro USB OTG and a USB-C port for power only. The board has 16 MB SPI flash, a 3.5 mm headphone jack, a mono speaker output, an RTC battery connector and a debug UART. The 40-pin and 14-pin headers are unpopulated unless you buy the IO Header version, and LinuxGizmos lists PWM and ADC among their functions. Height with heatsink is 16 mm and weight is 52 g.

Hardkernel shares its Linux kernel on GitHub under GPL-2.0, and the Ubuntu image uses the open Panfrost GPU driver. The shop page says schematics are on the wiki, but no hardware license is stated. The Rockchip chip, its boot files, and the NPU runtime stay closed.

Arm Cortex-A
Cortex-A is a family of Arm processor designs for full computers.
eMMC
eMMC is flash storage soldered right onto a board.
Gateway
A gateway is a device that links one network to another.
GPIO
GPIO pins are metal pins you can control from code.
GPU
A GPU is a chip part built to do many small math jobs at once.
HDMI
HDMI is the common plug for TVs and monitors.
Home Assistant
Home Assistant is free open-source software for running a smart home.
NPU
An NPU is a chip part made just for running AI models.
PCIe
PCIe is a very fast link inside computers.
Resolution
Resolution is how many dots make up an image or screen.
Soldering
Soldering joins metal parts with a melted metal called solder.
TOPS
TOPS stands for trillion operations per second.
