Hardkernel ODROID-M2

Hardkernel

A fast, low-profile eight-core Linux board for home servers and edge AI.

Field marks: Spot a flat square board with eight cores, a 6 TOPS NPU, and a USB-C port that powers nothing. A barrel jack does the real work.

Overview

The ODROID-M2 is a flat, square Linux computer from Hardkernel. Its Rockchip RK3588S2 chip has eight Arm cores, a strong GPU, and a 6 TOPS AI engine. It comes with 8 GB or 16 GB of fast memory and 64 GB of built-in storage. It suits makers who want a quiet home server, media box, or edge AI box.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Home servers, media servers, and backup boxes
  • A quiet Linux desktop with two screens
  • Running AI models on the built-in NPU
  • Learning containers and server skills
  • Kernel work, thanks to mainline Linux support

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on the board
  • Fast NVMe storage, as the slot tops out near 400 MB per second
  • Small budgets, now that the price has roughly doubled since launch
  • Battery or portable builds, as it needs a 12 volt supply

Specs in plain words

  • Four fast Cortex-A76 cores plus four efficient cores feel close to a budget laptop.
  • 8 GB of LPDDR5 runs many apps at once. The 16 GB model can hold a small language model.
  • 64 GB of soldered eMMC holds the system and apps. You can skip the SD card.
  • The 6 TOPS NPU runs image and object detection models quickly.
  • About 1 watt at idle and 7.5 watts under load keep power bills low.
  • HDMI and DisplayPort over USB-C let you drive two monitors.

RAM is the 8 GB model. A 16 GB model exists, and ameriDroid also lists a 4 GB variant. The A76 clock is about 2.3 GHz plus or minus 0.1 GHz per Hardkernel. Storage is 64 GB soldered eMMC. The USB-C port carries data and DisplayPort but not power. Interfaces on the 40-pin and 14-pin headers are not fully listed on the shop page. As a result, the I2C, SPI and UART entries follow the Pi-compatible header described by CNX Software. Height is 21 mm. There is no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth onboard. A PCF8563 RTC with a CR2032 holder is included.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot confirmed
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Mainline Linux and U-Boot support the board under GPL-2.0, and Hardkernel shares its kernel on GitHub. A kernel patch links a schematic PDF on the wiki, but no hardware license was found. The Rockchip chip, its boot firmware, and the NPU runtime stay closed.
