Overview

HealthyPi Move is a wristwatch from ProtoCentral that records body signals for research and learning. It measures single-lead ECG, pulse, blood oxygen, skin temperature, skin response, and motion. Data stays on the watch and syncs to a phone app with no cloud account. It suits students, researchers, and developers who want open hardware they can study and change.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Collecting raw ECG and pulse data for school or research
  • Testing your own health algorithms on real signals
  • Learning how wearable sensors work
  • Private health logging with no cloud account
  • Studying open watch hardware down to the KiCad files

Not great for

  • Medical decisions, as it is no certified medical device
  • Tight budgets, at around 300 USD or more
  • Adding your own sensors, as the case is sealed and compact
  • Quick firmware edits, as builds need the nRF Connect SDK

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF5340 chip has two small cores. One runs the watch and one handles Bluetooth.
  • 512 KB of working memory is enough for the watch screens and sensor processing.
  • 128 MB of extra flash stores days of sensor logs on the wrist.
  • The 1.2 inch round AMOLED screen shows readings in sharp color.
  • A 300 mAh battery charges over USB-C.
  • The case is 43 mm across and 13 mm thick, about the size of a regular sports watch.

The clock is the nRF5340 application core at 128 MHz, and a second network core runs at 64 MHz. RAM is the 512 KB on the application core, and flash_kb is the 1 MB inside the chip. The watch adds 128 MB of external QSPI flash for data storage. The README lists a 1.2 inch 390 x 390 round AMOLED, a 300 mAh battery and a 13 mm thickness. The 2024 CNX article described an earlier prototype with a 1.28 inch TFT, a 200 mAh battery and 16 mm thickness. As a result, the design changed before shipping. The size is the 43 mm case diameter and thickness. The finger PPG sensor plugs into the USB-C port through a custom connector board.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Personal heart rate diary

    Wear the watch through your day and sync it to the phone app. You can see how your heart rate shifts with work, meals, and sleep.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Exercise recovery tracker

    Record heart rate variability each morning. Over weeks you learn which workouts leave you rested or worn out.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Stress and calm check

    Watch the skin response readings during a busy day and a quiet one. A breathing break may show up as a change.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Class biosignal lab

    Students take a single-lead ECG with a finger on the bezel. They compare their traces and learn how the heart beats.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Sleep temperature log

    Wear the watch overnight and log skin temperature. The chart shows how your body cools and warms through the night.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Music tempo and pulse study

    Listen to slow and fast songs while the watch records. Then plot your heart rate against each song's tempo.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Your own health algorithm

    Export raw ECG and PPG data from the flash log. Write a script that finds heartbeats and test it against the watch.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Custom phone dashboard

    Fork the open Flutter companion app and design your own charts. Show the readings that matter to you.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Step and motion study

    Use the motion sensor to count steps and spot falls or sudden stops. Log the events for a science fair project.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Custom case and strap

    Edit the Fusion 360 enclosure files and 3D print a new case. Standard 22 mm straps still fit.

    Add: 3D printer filament, 22 mm watch strap.

Expert ideas

  • New watch face and screens

    Change the LVGL screens in the Zephyr firmware. Add a watch face or a new readout on the round AMOLED.

    Uses: Screen. Add: SWD programming adapter.

  • Research data collection rig

    Give several volunteers a watch for a wellness study. Each one logs data offline and syncs it later with no cloud account.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in, Battery ready.

The hardware repo holds KiCad schematics, board layouts, Gerbers, and enclosure CAD under CERN-OHL-P v2. The firmware is MIT licensed and builds on Zephyr. The Nordic chip, some Nordic SDK files, and the sensor hub algorithms stay closed.
