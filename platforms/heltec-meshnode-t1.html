Heltec Mesh Node T1

Heltec Automation

A rugged, card-sized LoRa tracker with GPS, a screen, and a big battery.

Overview

The Heltec Mesh Node T1 is a sealed LoRa tracker about the size of a credit card. It packs a long-range radio, a multi-system GPS, a motion sensor, a buzzer, and a small color screen. A built-in 1850 mAh battery runs it for long days outdoors. It ships with mesh firmware and also runs Meshtastic and MeshCore, which makes it a friendly first mesh radio.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting with friends where phones have no signal
  • Sharing group positions on hikes and trips
  • Tracking gear, boats, or pets
  • Outdoor use, thanks to the IP65 case
  • Learning about mesh radio with ready-made firmware

Not great for

  • Adding your own sensors, as the case is sealed with no header
  • Buying in the US, with no US store found yet
  • Dual-band GPS accuracy, as the stock antenna uses only the L1 band
  • Big programs, with only 256 KB of working memory

Specs in plain words

  • The SX1262 LoRa radio sends short messages across kilometers of open ground.
  • GPS, BeiDou, GLONASS, Galileo, QZSS, and NavIC help it find its spot to within about 1.5 meters.
  • An 1850 mAh battery and an 11 microamp sleep mode stretch runtime for days.
  • The 0.96 inch color screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It shows messages and maps.
  • The 9-axis motion sensor includes a compass. It can tell which way you face.
  • At 9.5 mm thick and 53 grams, it slips into a shirt pocket.

Flash and RAM are the nRF52840 internal values. The device is a sealed product. As a result, no user GPIO or ADC header is exposed. The schematic shows SWD test pads, I2C to the sensors and SPI to the display. Datasheet current draw is 110 mA at 20 dBm LoRa TX, 20 mA LoRa RX, 35 mA for GNSS and 15 to 20 mA for the display. The GNSS hardware supports L1 and L5, but Heltec says the stock antenna is tuned only for L1. The product page gives -10 to 65 C operation and the datasheet gives -20 to 60 C. The product page and datasheet say IP65. A search snippet of the wiki said IP67, but the fetched wiki page said IP65. The buzzer has no matching onboard value in the schema vocabulary. Weight is 53 g. Thread and 802.15.4 come from the nRF52840 silicon, but no firmware for this board uses them. As a result, they are left out of radios.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Battery ready. Add: lanyard.

    Clip the card to a large dog's collar. Your own node shows where the dog wandered.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: collar pouch.

  • Phone-free message reader

    Read incoming mesh messages on the color screen. You can leave your phone in a pocket on the trail.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen.

  • Neighborhood emergency check-in

    Give a card to each home on the block. Neighbors post a status note during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth, Battery ready.

  • School radio range lesson

    Students walk the campus with cards and note where messages still arrive. They map how buildings block radio.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

  • Bike ride track drawing

    Use the stock F&T firmware to draw your ride path. Compare routes with friends after the ride.

    Uses: GPS, Screen, Battery ready. Add: bike mount.

Heltec publishes a schematic PDF, but its documents reserve all rights and forbid commercial use. The Heltec nRF52 Arduino core and Meshtastic are GPL-3.0, and MeshCore is MIT. The Nordic Bluetooth stack and Heltec's LoRaWAN library stay closed.
