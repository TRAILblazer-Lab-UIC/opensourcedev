Heltec Vision Master E290

Heltec Automation

A LoRa radio board with a 2.9 inch e-paper screen for Meshtastic.

Overview

The Heltec Vision Master E290 is a small radio board with a 2.9 inch black and white e-paper screen. It runs on an ESP32-S3 chip with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. A socket holds a LoRa long-range radio. Most people flash it with Meshtastic for off-grid texting. The screen keeps its image with no power, which makes it a thrifty message reader and status sign.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Off-grid Meshtastic texting with a readable screen
  • Battery status signs that update a few times a day
  • Low-power sensor nodes that show their last reading
  • Badges and labels that hold an image with the power off

Not great for

  • Fast animations or games, as each screen refresh takes about 2 seconds
  • Using Wi-Fi and Bluetooth together, which the maker says it cannot do
  • Commercial copies, as Heltec forbids commercial use of its files
  • Arduino LoRaWAN work with Heltec's library, which needs a per-chip key

Specs in plain words

  • The 2.9 inch screen shows 296 by 128 dots in black and white. That fits a few lines of text or a simple icon.
  • The ESP32-S3 has two cores at 240 MHz. Its 8 MB of extra memory holds messages, menus, and sensor logs with room to spare.
  • Deep sleep draws about 18 microamps. A small battery can run a sensor node for a long time.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows, such as 915 MHz in the US or 868 MHz in Europe.
  • The board is about 88 by 37 millimeters. That is about the length of a credit card.
  • A LiPo battery plugs in and charges over USB-C.

The ESP32-S3R8 has 8 MB of PSRAM inside the chip package. The datasheet table says "16M PSRAM", which conflicts with the R8 part number and the product page's 8 MB. Heltec's FAQ says the board has no external PSRAM, which fits in-package PSRAM. Flash is a W25Q128 (16 MB) per the schematic. The GPIO count is the number of GPIO pins on the 2 x 20 header in the datasheet pin table. Many are shared with the e-paper display and LoRa module. ADC is 6 ADC1 channels plus 1 ADC2 channel per the datasheet. Six touch pins are also listed. USB uses the ESP32-S3 native USB (GPIO19 and 20). The schematic shows no USB-serial bridge. The base HT-VME290 model has no LoRa module. The -LF and -HF models include the HT-RA62 SX1262 module. US resellers sell the HF version. The datasheet lists 220 mA at 20 dBm LoRa TX and 18 uA sleep. The product page says Wi-Fi and Bluetooth cannot be used at the same time. Bluetooth Classic is not supported by the ESP32-S3.

Things you could build

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid texting with a screen

    Flash Meshtastic and pair the board with your phone. Messages from friends stay on the e-paper screen with no phone in hand.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen, Bluetooth. Add: LiPo battery.

  • Hiking group check-in

    Give each hiker a node with a battery. The group can text each other in places with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: LiPo battery.

  • Neighborhood outage board

    Keep a node in each home on the block. Neighbors post short status notes during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen. Add: LiPo battery.

  • School radio range lesson

    Students carry nodes across campus and note where messages still arrive. They learn how hills and walls block radio.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: LiPo battery.

  • Name badge that lasts

    Load a CircuitPython example that draws your name on the screen. The image stays put with the power off.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Solar window repeater

    Tape the node to a sunny window with a small solar panel. It relays messages for the neighborhood day and night.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: small solar panel, LiPo battery.

  • Garden soil moisture display

    Plug a soil sensor into the small sensor port. The screen shows how dry each bed is and the radio sends it to the house.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LiPo battery, weatherproof box.

  • Kitchen weather card

    Pull the forecast over Wi-Fi each morning and draw it on the screen. The board sleeps between updates and sips power.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen, Sips power. Add: LiPo battery.

  • Classroom shelf labels

    Use a few boards as reusable labels for science supplies. Update the text over Wi-Fi as bins change.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Bus and train times sign

    Fetch arrival times over Wi-Fi and show the next three departures by the door. The crisp screen reads well in daylight.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Greenhouse temperature logger

    Add a temperature and humidity sensor and send readings across the yard by LoRa. The screen keeps the last reading on view.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, LiPo battery.

Expert ideas

  • Trail visitor counter

    Wire a motion sensor beside a park path. The board counts passersby and radios a daily total to the ranger station.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, LiPo battery, weatherproof box.

  • Community air quality map

    Hang several nodes with air sensors around town. Each one shares readings over the mesh to a central screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: air quality sensor, small solar panel, LiPo battery.

  • Bluetooth sensor gateway

    Collect readings from nearby Bluetooth tags and forward them across town over LoRa. Write the bridge in Arduino code.

    Uses: Bluetooth, LoRa long range radio. Add: Bluetooth temperature tags.
