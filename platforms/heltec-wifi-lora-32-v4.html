Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V4

Heltec Automation

A stronger LoRa radio board with solar input for off-grid mesh texting.

Overview

The Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V4 is the 2025 follow-up to the popular V3 radio board. It pairs an ESP32-S3 chip with a LoRa long-range radio, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a tiny screen. New parts include a power amplifier, a solar panel input, and a plug for a GNSS location module. It suits Meshtastic fans who want a low-cost repeater or a stronger handheld node.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends off the grid with Meshtastic or MeshCore
  • Solar-powered repeaters on a roof or pole
  • Long-range sensor links around a farm or big yard
  • Learning LoRa radio in Arduino or PlatformIO

Not great for

  • Tiny batteries, as full-power sending draws a lot of current
  • Long battery life compared with lower-power radio boards
  • Location sharing out of the box, as the GNSS module costs extra
  • Mixing up firmware, as the R8 version needs its own build

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 has two cores at 240 MHz. It can run the radio, the screen, and Bluetooth together.
  • 16 MB of flash and 2 MB of extra memory give room for firmware, maps of nearby nodes and message history.
  • Heltec rates the high-power version at up to 28 dBm. On paper that is about five times the sending power of the V3.
  • The solar input takes a small 5 to 6 volt panel. Your node can charge its battery in daylight.
  • Full-power sending draws about 750 milliamps. A weak USB port or tiny battery may cut out.
  • The whole board is about 5 cm long and 2.5 cm wide, close to a stick of gum.

Values are for the standard V4 (V4.2 and V4.3.1) with the ESP32-S3R2, 2 MB PSRAM and 16 MB external flash. A V4-R8 variant uses the ESP32-S3R8 with 8 MB PSRAM. Meshtastic says it has a different pinout and firmware. The high-power HTIT-WB32LAF covers 863-928 MHz at 28 +/- 1 dBm. The low-power HTIT-WB32LAF-N covers 433, 470-510 and 863-928 MHz at 21 +/- 1 dBm. GPIO count is the 28 GPIO-numbered header pins. Several are shared with the OLED, LED, GNSS and battery sense. The GNSS port uses an SH1.25 8-pin connector, and the GNSS module is sold separately. Sleep current is 20 uA on battery per datasheet V4.3.1. TX at 27 dBm draws about 750 mA. Heltec's product page lists 22 dBm for the low-power version while the datasheet says 21 dBm.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid chat for camping trips

    Give each camper a board paired with their phone. Everyone can text the group in places with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: small rechargeable battery.

  • Neighborhood emergency mesh

    Keep a node in each home on your street. Neighbors can post a quick check-in during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: USB power adapter.

  • Window repeater

    Park a node high in a window on USB power. It passes along messages for radios across the block.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: USB power adapter.

  • Classroom radio range test

    Students carry nodes across the school grounds and note where messages still arrive. They learn how walls and hills block radio waves.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

  • Pocket message pager

    Add a small battery and a case. Incoming texts pop up on the little screen without your phone.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen, Battery ready. Add: small rechargeable battery, small case.

    Plug a GNSS module into the board's GNSS port. The mesh shares each hiker's position on the phone app map.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: GNSS module, rechargeable battery.

    Wire a soil sensor to the board and send readings across the yard by radio. A second board indoors shows which beds need water.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, small solar panel, weatherproof box.

  • Mailbox opened alert

    Fit a magnetic door switch to a far-off mailbox. The board sends a radio ping each time the lid opens.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, small rechargeable battery.

  • Backyard weather station

    Add a temperature and humidity sensor. The screen shows the reading and the radio shares it with the house.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Livestock water tank check

    Put a water level sensor in a far pasture tank. A solar node radios a warning once the level drops low.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: water level sensor, small solar panel, weatherproof box.

  • Wildlife trail visit counter

    Point a motion sensor at an animal path. The board counts visits and radios a daily total back home.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, small solar panel, weatherproof box.

  • Community garden gate bell

    Press a button at the far gate and a buzzer sounds in the tool shed. Nobody needs a phone signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: push button, small buzzer.

Expert ideas

Heltec shares schematic PDFs, pin maps, and 3D files for the board and its expansion kit. No board layout files are posted, and Heltec allows only personal, non-commercial use of its documents. The Meshtastic firmware is open under GPL-3.0, and the Arduino tools are open. The ESP32-S3 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers stay closed.
