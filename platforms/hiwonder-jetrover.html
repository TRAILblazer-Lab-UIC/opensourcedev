Overview

The Hiwonder JetRover is a ready-made robot car kit for learning ROS, the popular robot software framework. It carries a six-joint arm with a depth camera at the wrist and a spinning lidar sensor on top. Buyers pick a chassis style and a main computer, such as an NVIDIA Jetson or a Raspberry Pi 5. A long tutorial series walks through mapping, navigation, arm control, and computer vision.

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as kits start near 780 US dollars
  • First-time coders, as real projects need Python or C++ in ROS
  • Outdoor rough ground, as it is an indoor education robot
  • Building your own copies, as only a controller schematic is shared

Specs in plain words

  • The lidar spins and measures distance in every direction. That lets the robot draw a map of a room.
  • The depth camera sees how far away each object is. The arm uses that to reach for things.
  • A small STM32 controller runs the motors, wheel sensors, and tilt sensor. The main computer handles the thinking.
  • The 11.1 volt 6000 mAh battery pack comes with a charger. It powers the motors, arm, and computer together.
  • The Mecanum chassis has special wheels that slide sideways. The Ackermann chassis steers like a car. The tank version rolls on treads.
  • The robot weighs about 4 to 5.5 kilograms. That is about as heavy as a large bag of flour.

Core count, clock, RAM and AI throughput depend on the chosen controller (Jetson Nano 4 GB, Orin Nano Super 4 or 8 GB, Orin NX Super 8 or 16 GB, Raspberry Pi 5 4 or 8 GB). As a result, they are left null. The STM32F407VET6 controller has 512 KB flash and 192 KB SRAM and a built-in IMU. It runs motor PID, encoders, IMU and RGB light control under FreeRTOS. Sizes are 324 x 260 x 659 mm and 4.7 kg (Mecanum M1), 325 x 261 x 657 mm and 5.5 kg (Ackermann A1), 345 x 246 x 660 mm and 4.3 kg (tank T1). Control methods listed are USB serial, CAN, Bluetooth app and remote controller. Communication is USB, Wi-Fi and Ethernet. The 7-inch screen and microphone array are in higher kits. Which exact items each kit tier includes comes from packing-list images not read. Servos are HTS-20H, HTS-21H, HTD-35H or HX-12H serial bus servos.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Drive it around the house

    Steer the robot with the phone app or the remote. It is a gentle first look at how the chassis and arm move.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Map your living room

    Run the lidar mapping tutorial and drive slowly through each room. The robot draws a floor plan you can save.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Self-driving tour guide

    Mark spots on a saved map, such as a classroom door or a lab bench. The robot drives to each spot and avoids people on the way.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

  • Color block sorter

    Teach the arm camera to spot red, green, and blue blocks. The arm picks each one up and drops it in a matching bin.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: colored foam blocks, small bins.

  • Follow me cart

    Use the depth camera to track a person. The robot trails behind with a light bag on its back.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Voice command robot

    Speak commands such as come here or turn left. The microphone array hears you and the speaker answers back.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Runs Linux.

  • Robot soccer match

    Use the camera to find a small ball. Two classes write code to push it into a goal with their robots.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small foam ball.

  • Hand gesture driving

    Run a hand tracking example on the camera feed. A wave makes the robot move forward or stop.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Room status dashboard

    Show the robot's map, battery, and camera view on its 7-inch screen. Visitors can watch what the robot sees.

    Uses: Screen, Camera, Runs Linux.

  • Practice in simulation first

    Load the robot model in the Gazebo simulator on a laptop. Test new code on a virtual robot before the real one moves.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • Toy cleanup helper

    Train an object finder on a few toys. The robot drives to each toy and lifts it into a basket.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small basket.

  • Chatty robot greeter

    Try the large language model demo on the higher kits. The robot greets visitors and answers simple questions out loud.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

  • Garden row patrol

    Map the paths between raised beds. The robot drives the rows and takes photos of each plant for you to check later.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux, Battery ready.

  • Science lab sample fetcher

    Program the arm to pick up a light sample tube from one bench. The car carries it to another bench across the room.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: light sample tubes, tube rack.

  • Lost item search party

    Teach the robot to recognize keys or a water bottle. It roams the map and reports where it spotted them.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

  • Robot learning research

    Collect arm motion data and train a grasping model on it. Researchers have shared projects like this on GitHub.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.
