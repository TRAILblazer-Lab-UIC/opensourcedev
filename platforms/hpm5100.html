Overview

The HPM5100 is a microcontroller chip from HPMicro that runs a single RISC-V core at 400 MHz. Its standout is analog sensing, with four 16-bit converters, four amplifiers, and four comparators inside the chip. HPMicro aims it at motor control, power conversion, and precise sensors. It launched in October 2026 and suits experienced embedded developers.

Not great for

  • Buyers in the US, as boards are sold mainly in China
  • Arduino or MicroPython fans, as neither supports it yet

Specs in plain words

  • The core runs at 400 MHz with math helpers for audio and control. That is many times faster than a classic Arduino.
  • 416 KB of working memory and 1 MB of flash hold a large program and lots of sensor data.
  • Each 16-bit ADC splits a voltage into about 65,000 steps. It can take 2 million readings per second.
  • The four op-amps boost tiny signals from sensors such as scales and current sensors.
  • High-speed USB lets the chip move data to a computer quickly or act as a USB host.
  • The chip comes in packages as small as 5 by 5 millimeters, about the size of a pencil eraser end.

clock_mhz is the recommended 400 MHz. The 480 MHz extreme mode needs an external supply and ambient temperature at or below 70 C. ram_kb is 256 KB ILM, 128 KB DLM and 32 KB AHB SRAM. flash_kb is the 1 MB SiP flash. gpio is the maximum of 79 in the LQFP100 package. adc_channels counts the up to 48 analog input pins across four ADCs. Connectors, onboard parts and buttons describe the HPM5100EVK, which has two USB-C ports, a 40-pin Raspberry Pi style header and wake, user and reset buttons. The EVK also has an ES8389 audio codec, a CAN transceiver, RS485/422, motor and op-amp connectors and an onboard HPM5301-based debugger. No sleep current figure was published on fetched pages.

Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Lab bench power monitor

    Measure voltage and current from a bench supply with the fast ADCs. Send the readings to a laptop over USB for graphing.

    Add: current sense resistor.

  • Precision scale

    Amplify a load cell signal with the on-chip op-amps. The ADC turns tiny changes into a steady weight reading.

    Add: load cell, small screen.

  • Heart rate sensor experiment

    Read a pulse sensor through an op-amp stage and filter the signal in code. Students see their own heartbeat as a wave.

    Add: pulse sensor.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the host port. Turn key presses into commands for a robot or a light show.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB adapter cable.

  • Solar panel tracker logger

    Measure panel voltage and current all day with the ADCs. Log the data and compare sunny and cloudy days.

    Add: small solar panel, current sense resistor, microSD card module.

Expert ideas

  • Quiet fan motor driver

    Use the PWM outputs and current sensing to spin a small brushless fan smoothly. The chip measures motor current and adjusts power many times per second.

    Add: brushless motor driver board, small brushless fan.

  • Home energy meter study

    Sample a safe low-voltage transformer output with the 16-bit ADCs. Plot the waveform and estimate power use in a science class.

    Add: low-voltage AC transformer.

  • USB audio interface

    Pair the I2S ports with an audio codec and the high-speed USB port. A laptop sees the board as a sound card for recording.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, microphone, headphones.

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Feed a guitar signal through the codec and run echo or distortion math on the DSP instructions. Play the result through an amplifier.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, guitar jack, foot switch.

  • Robot arm joint controller

    Drive a small motor and read its position sensor. Share commands with other joints over the CAN FD bus.

    Add: small DC motor with encoder, motor driver board, CAN transceiver.
