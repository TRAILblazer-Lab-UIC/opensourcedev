HX6538 (WiseEye2)

Himax Technologies

A low-power AI chip for spotting people, objects, and gestures with a camera.

Overview

The HX6538, also called WiseEye2, is an always-on AI chip from Himax for cameras and microphones. It pairs two Arm Cortex-M55 cores with a small neural processor that speeds up AI models. Most makers meet it on the Seeed Grove Vision AI Module V2, a board that costs about 16 dollars. You can load ready models from a web browser and get person or object detection with no code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Camera projects for spotting people, objects, or gestures
  • Low-power AI that runs all day on a small battery
  • Classroom demos of AI with no coding
  • Adding vision to an Arduino, ESP32, or Raspberry Pi project

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on its own, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Big AI tasks such as chatbots or voice assistants
  • Users who want the full chip datasheet, as Himax gates the latest one

Specs in plain words

  • A 400 MHz main core plus a 150 MHz helper core share the work of running and managing models.
  • The Ethos-U55 neural processor does about 50 billion small math steps each second. That is enough for live person detection on tiny images.
  • About 2.4 MB of fast memory holds a small AI model and one camera frame at a time.
  • 16 MB of flash on the Grove board stores your firmware and models.
  • A camera connector, a microphone input, and a JPEG encoder handle images and sound on the chip.
  • Himax says the chip plus a camera sips only a few thousandths of a watt.

clock_mhz is the big core. ram_kb is the configurable system memory from the datasheet. flash_kb is the external 16 MB QSPI flash used on the Grove Vision AI V2. The chip itself has no internal flash. ai_tops is 0.05 from Himax's up to 50 GOPS figure, which matches 64 MACs at 400 MHz. adc_channels is the up-to-4-channel differential ADC. gpio is the datasheet maximum of 37. Chip packages are LQFP128, QFN88 and a 2.3 x 5.6 mm WLCSP65. Connectors, onboard parts and size describe the Grove Vision AI Module V2. The HX6538 has no USB controller. Boards use a CH343 USB-to-UART bridge. Himax shows power by mode only in an image. No sleep current figure was found in text.

Starter ideas

  • Person counter for a doorway

    Load the ready person detection model and point the camera at a door. It counts how many people walk through each hour.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module.

  • Classroom AI demo station

    Flash a few pretrained models from the browser and swap between them. Students see a chip find faces, people, and objects live.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module.

  • Garden visitor log

    Watch a garden bed and save a photo to the memory card each time a cat or bird shows up. Check the card later to see who visited.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module, microSD card.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Plug-in connectors. Add: camera module, small host board, relay module.

  • Parking spot watcher

    Aim the camera at a parking space and detect cars. A host board with Wi-Fi sends a note to your phone once the spot opens.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Plug-in connectors. Add: camera module, small Wi-Fi board.

  • Smart pet feeder trigger

    Detect your pet at the bowl and log each meal time. A host board can run a small motor to drop food.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Plug-in connectors. Add: camera module, small host board, servo motor.

  • Wake word listener

    Use the onboard microphone to listen for one spoken word. A connected board lights an LED strip once it hears the word.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in, AI chip. Add: small host board, LED strip.

  • Bird feeder species camera

    Train a custom model on photos of local birds with Edge Impulse. Log which kinds visit the feeder each day.

  • Robot that follows a person

    Feed person positions over I2C to a robot car board. The car turns and drives to keep the person in view.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Plug-in connectors. Add: camera module, small robot car kit.

    Count items on a shelf in a school pantry or shop. Flag a shelf that runs low.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module, small host board.

Expert ideas

  • Fall alert for a care room

    Train a model to spot a person lying on the floor. A host board with a buzzer sounds an alarm for help.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Plug-in connectors. Add: camera module, small host board, buzzer.

  • Sound event detector

    Train a model on clapping, glass breaking, or a dog bark. The chip listens all day on very little power.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip. Add: microphone board.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Seeed publishes the Grove Vision AI Module V2 as a KiCad project, but the download names no hardware license. The Himax SDK is MIT licensed and builds with the open Arm compiler, along with open TensorFlow Lite Micro and Vela tools. The chip is closed, and the firmware packing tool ships only as a prebuilt program.

Hardware license: Board-dependent. Grove Vision AI V2 files have no stated license. SenseCAP Watcher repo is Apache-2.0. · Software license: MIT (Himax Seeed_Grove_Vision_AI_Module_V2 SDK)
