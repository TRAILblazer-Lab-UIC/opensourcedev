InMoov

Gael Langevin (InMoov, France)

A life-size humanoid robot you 3D print and build from free plans.

Field marks: A life-size printed humanoid assembled from 37 sections and about thirty servos, each part printed on a bed just 12 centimeters wide.

Overview

InMoov is a life-size humanoid robot that you print at home from free files. French sculptor Gael Langevin started it in 2012 as a robot hand. It grew into a full head, arms, torso, and legs. Arduino boards move about thirty hobby servos, and a PC runs the MyRobotLab software for speech, vision and gestures. It suits patient makers, school labs, and research groups with time and a 3D printer.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Long maker projects that grow one body part at a time
  • Learning about robot hands and prosthetics
  • University and school labs that study humanoid robots
  • Showing off at maker fairs and museums

Not great for

  • Quick builds, as the full robot takes many months
  • Tight budgets, as servos and power supplies cost a lot
  • Selling printed parts or kits, as the license forbids commercial use
  • Walking, as the legs have no motors

Specs in plain words

  • Parts fit a printer bed of 12 by 12 by 12 centimeters. That is about the size of a large coffee mug.
  • An Arduino Mega has 8 KB of working memory. That is plenty for moving servos. A PC does the heavy thinking.
  • The full build uses about thirty servos. Each one moves a finger, a wrist, the jaw, or the eyes.
  • The servos run from a 6 volt supply that must deliver a lot of current. A big 6 volt battery is a common choice.
  • The cost figure of about 1,635 US dollars is a rough 2016 estimate. It converts Langevin's figure of under 1,500 euros into dollars. Prices have likely risen since.

Specs describe the Arduino Mega 2560, the main servo controller in the default hardware map (2 Megas recommended for the full robot. The default setup is one Uno and one Mega, later configurations add a Nano). The Mega uses a USB-B connector, which is not in the vocabulary. Usb_a stands in for the PC-side cable. The author's current PC is an Intel i7-8550U mini PC with 32 GB RAM running Windows 10 Pro and Ubuntu, with a Lenovo tablet as screen. Onboard items are what the full build carries: webcam or OAK-D-Lite cameras, two 10 W speakers with a 6 V amplifier and NeoPixel rings. It also uses HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors, a PIR sensor and finger pressure sensors. Height is life size (adult human). No exact dimension was found.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Finger starter project

    Print one finger and move it with a single servo and an Arduino. It is the classic first InMoov build.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printer, servo motor, Arduino board, fishing line.

  • Virtual robot sandbox

    Test gesture scripts on the virtual InMoov inside MyRobotLab. Try ideas before any part is printed.

    Add: computer.

Builder ideas

  • Rock paper scissors hand

    Build the hand and forearm. Program the fingers to throw rock, paper, or scissors against a friend.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printer, servo motors, Arduino board, 6 V power supply.

  • Prosthetic hand study

    Print the hand and test how tendons pull each finger. Students compare grip shapes for real objects.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printer, servo motors, Arduino board, fishing line.

  • Talking robot head

    Build the head with jaw, eyes, and speakers. The jaw moves in time as the robot speaks.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: 3D printer, servo motors, Arduino board, speakers, computer.

  • Robot art installation

    Pose the robot and light it with color LED rings. Change the pose and colors on a timer.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: LED rings, computer.

Expert ideas

  • Face-tracking eyes

    A camera in the eye follows faces in the room. The head and eyes turn to look at people.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: webcam, servo motors, Arduino board, computer.

  • Museum greeter robot

    Stand the robot near a doorway with a motion sensor. It waves and says hello to each visitor.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound, Sensors built in. Add: motion sensor, speakers, computer, 6 V power supply.

  • Gesture storytelling

    Script arm and head gestures to match a spoken story. Perform it for a class or library event.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound. Add: computer, speakers.

  • Sign language practice

    Program the hands to form simple signs. Learners can study each hand shape up close.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo motors, Arduino board.

  • Rolling robot base

    Mount the upper body on a wheeled base. The robot can roll down a hall at a maker fair.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: wheeled base, 6 V battery.

  • Touch-sensing grip

    Add pressure sensors to the fingertips. The hand stops squeezing once it feels an object.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: finger pressure sensors.

Every printed part is a free STL download under the CC BY-NC 3.0 license. That license forbids commercial use. The MyRobotLab software and InMoov scripts are open under Apache 2.0. The original CAD files and Nervo Board schematics are not published.
