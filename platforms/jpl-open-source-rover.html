JPL Open Source Rover

NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) and volunteer maintainers

A free plan from NASA JPL for building your own six-wheel Mars-style rover.

Overview

The JPL Open Source Rover is a free build-it-yourself plan for a six-wheel rover modeled on NASA's Mars rovers. JPL first shared the plans in 2018. Volunteers now keep up the current off-the-shelf edition. It suits schools, clubs, and patient hobbyists who want a long team project in mechanics, electronics and robot code.

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as parts cost about 1,600 US dollars
  • Total beginners working alone
  • Anyone wanting a boxed kit, as no official kit is sold

Specs in plain words

  • Six wheels on a rocker-bogie suspension let the rover climb over bumps like the Mars rovers do.
  • Ten motors drive and steer it, with six for the wheels and four for turning the corner wheels.
  • A top speed of about 1.6 meters per second is a brisk walking pace.
  • One battery charge gives at least 3 hours of rough-ground driving.
  • A Raspberry Pi 3, 4, or 5 is the brain, and the Pi 5 is the best pick.
  • A 4S LiPo battery above 14 volts powers it all, and it needs careful charging.

The brain is a builder-supplied Raspberry Pi with any RAM size, booting from microSD. As a result, clock and memory depend on the Pi chosen. Only the Pi 5 has two full serial interfaces. As a result, it is the only model that can talk to the RoboClaws and use Bluetooth at the same time. The control board carries three RoboClaw 2x7A motor controllers, a PCA9685 servo driver, an INA260 power monitor and regulators. Drive motors are six goBILDA 5203 Yellow Jacket planetary gear motors with encoders. Steering uses four goBILDA 2000 series servos. Wireless radios come from the Raspberry Pi. Rover dimensions and weight were not found on a fetched page.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Drive it around the schoolyard

    Steer the rover over grass, gravel, and curbs with a game controller. It shows how the rocker-bogie keeps all six wheels on the ground.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: game controller.

  • Mars mission day

    Build a red rock field in the gym and send the rover on a pretend mission. Students take turns as drivers and mission control.

    Uses: Drives motors, Wi-Fi. Add: cardboard rocks.

  • Suspension science lab

    Drive over ramps and blocks of different heights. Record which obstacles the rover climbs and which ones stop it.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: wooden blocks, ramp boards.

  • Parade and community float

    Dress the rover up for a local parade or science fair. Its Mars look draws a crowd of kids.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: decorations.

Builder ideas

  • Battery life test

    Log the battery voltage from the power monitor on a long drive. Graph how far the rover goes on one charge.

    Uses: Battery ready, Runs Linux.

  • Phone or tablet remote control

    Drive the rover over Wi-Fi from a simple web page. Anyone with a phone can take the wheel.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Bluetooth gamepad driving

    Pair a Bluetooth controller with a Raspberry Pi 5 brain. The rover drives with no cables or laptop nearby.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors. Add: Bluetooth game controller.

  • Rover webcam scout

    Mount a USB webcam on the mast and stream live video to a laptop. Drivers can explore a room they cannot see.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: USB webcam.

  • Garden patrol

    Send the rover down the garden rows with a webcam. Check on plants without walking through the mud.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Wi-Fi, Drives motors. Add: USB webcam.

  • Nature trail sampler

    Carry a small sensor pack along a trail and log temperature and humidity as you drive. Map how the air changes from sun to shade.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Practice in simulation first

    Run the rover in the Gazebo simulator before the real build is done. New team members learn to drive without risk.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Custom rover head with a face

    Add the optional head with a small screen. Make the rover show moods and greet visitors at school events.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: small screen, extra servo motor.

Expert ideas

  • Robot arm add-on

    Bolt a small arm to the deck for picking up rocks. Use the spare servo outputs to move it.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small robot arm, servo motors.

  • Self-driving waypoint rover

    Write a ROS 2 node that drives the rover through a list of points. Add a USB GPS for outdoor routes.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: USB GPS receiver.

  • Obstacle avoiding explorer

    Add a USB depth camera or lidar and teach the rover to steer around rocks. It becomes a true little explorer.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: USB depth camera or lidar.

  • Mapping a room

    Use a lidar and ROS 2 mapping tools to draw a map of a hallway. Compare the map to a real floor plan.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB lidar.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The whole project is under the Apache-2.0 license and is OSHWA certified as US002551. The KiCad control board files, wiring guides, parts lists, Onshape CAD model, and ROS 2 Python code are all public. The Raspberry Pi and the RoboClaw motor controllers are closed commercial parts.
