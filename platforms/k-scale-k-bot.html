Overview

K-Bot is an open-source humanoid robot from K-Scale Labs, built at adult size with 20 motorized joints. A Raspberry Pi 5 runs the robot, and strong actuators move each joint on a 48 V power system. K-Scale closed in November 2025 and refunded all pre-orders. The full design is now public, and anyone with the money and skills can build one from the plans.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Robotics labs that want an open full-size humanoid design
  • Learning reinforcement learning in the free K-Sim simulator
  • Studying how a real humanoid is designed, wired, and assembled
  • Research on VR teleoperation and walking control

Not great for

  • Buying ready-made, as the company closed and no store sells it
  • Beginners, as it needs machining, high-power wiring, and Rust or Python skills
  • Small budgets, as the actuators and metal frame cost a great deal
  • Home use without safety gear, as a heavy robot on 48 V can cause harm

Specs in plain words

  • 20 degrees of freedom means 20 joints that bend or turn, enough for walking and arm moves.
  • Robstride actuators push up to 120 newton meters at peak. That is enough to lift and swing heavy limbs.
  • A 48 V battery pack with 12 amp hours powers the whole robot for a work session.
  • A Raspberry Pi 5 with four 2.4 GHz cores runs the control software on Linux.
  • Seven CAN buses carry commands to each limb on its own line.

Clock and core count are the Raspberry Pi 5 values. Pi RAM size used by K-Scale was not stated, and it boots from a 32 to 64 GB microSD card. Actuator mix per the docs: 2 RS00, 6 RS02, 8 RS03 and 4 RS04 (20 total). Rated loads range from 5 to 40 Nm, peaks to 120 Nm. Battery pack space is 135 x 180 x 65 mm. The BMS current limit is 50 A. The power board feeds 48 V plus CAN to each limb and 24 V to two extra CAN lines and the Pi buck converter. The docs describe streaming two camera feeds from the head. Camera model was not stated. Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Study the open CAD in class

    Open the Onshape model and explore each joint and part. Students learn how a full-size robot is put together.

Builder ideas

  • Train a walking policy in simulation

    Use K-Sim Gym to teach a virtual K-Bot to walk in MuJoCo. You can do this on a computer with no robot at all.

Expert ideas

  • Build a single robot arm

    Machine one arm from the parts files and wire its actuators to a CAN bus. Practice joint control before a full build.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: arm actuators, CNC-machined arm parts, 48 V power supply, CAN adapter.

  • Full humanoid build

    Order the frame from a CNC shop and assemble all 20 actuators. Wire the power board and boot the Raspberry Pi 5.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready, Runs Linux. Add: CNC-machined frame, 20 actuators, 48 V battery pack, microSD card.

  • VR puppet control

    Run the VR teleop web app on a headset. The robot copies your arm and head moves in real time.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: VR headset.

  • Robot camera stream

    Stream the two head camera feeds to a laptop over Wi-Fi. Use them for remote viewing or vision research.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

  • Wave and greet demo

    Script a friendly wave and bow in Python. Show it at a school open house or maker fair.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Swap in a new hand

    Design a custom hand that fits the quick-swap mount. It shares power and CAN with the arm.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printed parts, small actuators.

  • Send a learned policy to the robot

    Export a trained walking policy with kinfer. Run it on the real robot with a safety tether in place.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux, Battery ready. Add: safety gantry or tether.

  • Joint health monitor

    Read temperature and position from each actuator over CAN. Plot them on a laptop to catch a failing joint early.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux, Wi-Fi.
