Kendryte K210

Canaan (Kendryte)

A low-cost RISC-V chip with a built-in helper for camera AI projects.

Field marks: A small board with a camera on one side and a screen on the other, finding faces in 6 MB of memory with a pin map that changes per board.

Overview

The Kendryte K210 is a dual-core RISC-V chip from Canaan. It has a special helper for running small AI models on camera images. Boards like the Sipeed Maix Bit made it the first cheap AI camera for hobbyists. It suits makers who want to try face finding or object spotting with short Python programs.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Face and object spotting with ready-made models
  • Learning how AI vision works in a classroom
  • Robot projects that need simple eyes
  • Sound projects with several microphones

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on most boards. Only the Maixduino adds Wi-Fi.
  • Large or modern AI models, with only about 6 MB of free memory
  • Analog sensors. The chip has no analog inputs.
  • Long-term projects. Its software stopped growing years ago.

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 400 MHz handle the camera and your program at the same time.
  • 8 MB of memory sits inside the chip. Small models and images must fit in about 6 MB of it.
  • The AI helper can find faces in real time at small picture sizes.
  • The audio helper can listen through up to eight microphones and tell where a sound comes from.
  • The Maix Bit is about the size of two sticks of gum side by side.
  • 48 pins can each take on many jobs. Pin maps change between boards.

The datasheet lists 400 MHz as nominal. Sipeed says 600 MHz overclock works, and Seeed lists up to 800 MHz. ram_kb is the 8 MB on-chip SRAM. Linux and U-Boot see about 6 MB free. Flash is external SPI NOR, and 16 MB is the common board value (Maix Bit). The chip has no ADC and no USB. Boards add a USB-to-UART bridge. ai_tops is null because Canaan gives no TOPS figure. Seeed lists 0.5 TOPS at an 800 MHz overclock and 230 GMULps for the KPU, and DFRobot says hundreds of GOPS. lcd means the SPI 8080-style LCD port used for the 24-pin LCD connector on Maix boards. JTAG is the debug port. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Sipeed Maix Bit. The Maixduino adds an ESP32 for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Face finder camera

    Draw a box around every face the camera sees on the small screen. It is a fun first taste of machine vision.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Object naming game

    Point the camera at everyday things and let a ready-made model guess their names. Kids can test how well it does.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Color sorter helper

    Spot red, green, and blue blocks with the camera. Show the color name on the screen for a sorting game.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Snapshot photo booth

    Press a button to save a picture to the microSD card. Add a frame or sticker drawn on top.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen, microSD card, push button.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder watcher

    Watch a feeder and save a photo each time something moves in front of it. Check your visitors at the end of the day.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, microSD card, 5 V power bank.

  • Line following robot eyes

    Use the camera to see a dark line on the floor. Send steering hints to a robot car over a serial wire.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, robot car kit.

  • QR code door sign

    Read QR codes held up to the camera and show a welcome message. Use it at a club check-in table.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Clap counter

    Listen with the onboard microphone and count claps or loud sounds. Show the total on the screen.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in, Screen. Add: small screen.

  • Talking greeter

    Spot a face and play a short hello sound through a speaker. Place it by a classroom door.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound. Add: camera module, small amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Sound spectrum art

    Turn music from the microphone into moving bars of color on the screen. It shows how sounds split into pitches.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Sound direction finder

    Use a ring of microphones to point toward the loudest voice in a room. Light an LED on that side.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone array board, LED ring.

  • Train your own sorter

    Teach a small model to tell apart a few objects you choose. Load it onto the board and test it live.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Mask or hat detector

    Train a model to notice hats or masks on people in view. Use it for a science fair on how AI learns.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Pet door spotter

    Spot your cat on the camera and flash a light to tell you it is home. Keep the board near the pet door.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, LED, 5 V power supply.
