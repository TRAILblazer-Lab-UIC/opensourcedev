Kendryte K230

A RISC-V AI vision chip for smart cameras, robots, and classroom projects.

Overview

The Kendryte K230 is an AI vision chip from Canaan with two 64-bit RISC-V cores. Its AI helper runs camera models much faster than the older K210. Boards like the CanMV-K230 let you program it in MicroPython with ready-made demos. It suits makers, robot builders, and teachers who want smart camera projects.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Face, object, and gesture spotting in real time
  • Robot eyes for line following and object tracking
  • Classroom lessons on how AI vision works
  • Recording and playing back video with its video helper

Not great for

  • Quick plug and play setup on day one
  • Analog sensors. The chip has no general analog inputs.
  • Projects that need lots of English guides and forum help
  • Tiny budgets. Kits with a camera and screen cost more.

Specs in plain words

  • The main core runs at 1.6 GHz, fast enough for a light Linux system.
  • Most boards carry 1 GB of memory, room for a camera stream and an AI model at once.
  • Sellers rate the AI helper at about 6 TOPS. Canaan says it runs common models up to 13.7 times faster than the K210.
  • Up to three camera inputs let you build depth and multi-camera projects.
  • The video helper can record and play compressed video, like a small security camera.
  • The CanMV-K230 board is about the size of a credit card.

clock_mhz is the big core. The second core runs at 800 MHz. ram_kb is 1 GB, the common value on current boards such as the 01Studio CanMV K230, the Lushan Pi K230 1G and the Yahboom module. The original CanMV-K230 V1.0 has 512 MB LPDDR3, and the K230D has 128 MB in-package LPDDR4. flash_kb is null because most boards boot from microSD. ai_tops of 6 is a vendor "equivalent" figure from youyeetoo and Yahboom listings. Canaan publishes only frame rates. The datasheet lists 64 GPIO plus 8 PMU GPIO (50 plus 6 on the K230D). It describes the audio codec's two ADC channels but no general-purpose ADC. As a result, adc_channels is null. The chip has no radios. The CanMV-K230 board adds Wi-Fi 4 and Bluetooth 4.0 (AP6212), 10/100 Ethernet and HDMI through a bridge chip. Connectors, onboard parts and size are for the Canaan CanMV-K230.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Face finder camera

    Draw a box around every face on a small screen in real time. It is a quick first look at AI vision.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: camera module, small screen, microSD card.

  • What is this object

    Point the camera at things around the house and let a ready-made model name them. Kids can test where it gets confused.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: camera module, small screen, microSD card.

  • Color block hunter

    Find colored blocks in the camera view and show their names. Use it for a sorting game at school.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: camera module, small screen, microSD card.

  • Clap and voice meter

    Listen through a microphone and show sound levels as moving bars. Use it to explore loud and quiet in science class.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: microphone, small screen.

Builder ideas

  • Hand gesture counter

    Hold up fingers and let the camera count them on screen. It makes a fun math helper for young kids.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: camera module, small screen, microSD card.

  • Line following robot

    Let the camera spot a tape line on the floor. Steer a small robot car along the track.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, robot car kit, motor driver board.

  • Object tracking pan camera

    Follow a ball or a face with two servo motors. The camera turns to keep it in the middle.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module, two servo motors, pan and tilt bracket.

  • Bird feeder visitor log

    Spot birds at a feeder and save a photo of each visit. Count your visitors by species over a season.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module, microSD card, 5 V power supply.

  • Talking object reader

    Name objects the camera sees and play a short spoken word through a speaker. It can help young readers learn words.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound, AI chip. Add: camera module, small speaker.

  • Photo copier to USB stick

    Plug in a USB storage stick and copy saved photos onto it. Share a day of pictures with the class.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Camera. Add: USB flash drive, camera module.

Expert ideas

  • Garden pest spotter

    Train a model to notice slugs or rabbits near your plants. Save a picture and flash a light to warn you.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: camera module, LED, 5 V power supply.

  • Classroom attendance helper

    Recognize faces of students who agreed to take part. Show a friendly hello with their name on screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Video doorbell recorder

    Record short video clips to the microSD card each time someone comes to the door. Watch them later on a monitor.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, microSD card, 5 V power supply.

  • Depth sensing distance check

    Use two cameras and the depth engine to guess how far away objects are. Warn a robot before it bumps into walls.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: two camera modules.

  • Sign language letter trainer

    Train a model to recognize a few hand signs. Show the matching letter on screen for practice.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip. Add: camera module, small screen.

  • Tiny Linux vision lab

    Run the Linux system and write C programs that use the AI helper. Learn how real smart cameras are built.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip, Camera. Add: camera module, microSD card.

How open is it?

Open Board

The SDKs are on GitHub under the BSD-2-Clause license, and the nncase model compiler uses Apache-2.0. Canaan shares full design files for its demo boards. Their hardware license is unclear. About 73 media libraries ship as closed files. The chip and its cores stay closed.
