Kitronik Autonomous Robotics Platform for Pico

Kitronik

A no-solder two-wheel robot buggy kit that a Raspberry Pi Pico drives.

Field marks: Look for a green board on two yellow-hubbed wheels, often seen tracing black lines across classroom floors.

Overview

This kit turns a Raspberry Pi Pico into a small two-wheel robot. The chassis has motors, color LEDs, a buzzer, and sockets for sensors already built in. It suits classrooms, clubs, and families who want a first robot with no soldering. Its pen hole and line sensors make it stand out from plain buggy kits.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First robotics lessons in school or at home
  • Learning MicroPython with something that moves
  • Line following and obstacle dodging projects
  • Drawing shapes with a pen on paper

Not great for

  • Remote control from a phone, as the kit code has no ready-made app
  • Hardware hackers who want the board schematic
  • Buyers in the US, as stock there is hard to find
  • Rough outdoor ground, as it has small wheels and no suspension

Specs in plain words

  • Two geared motors let the robot drive, turn, and spin in place.
  • Three line sensors look at the floor and tell dark tape from light ground.
  • The ultrasonic sensor measures distance by bouncing sound off objects, like a bat.
  • Four servo sockets let you add moving arms, claws or flags.
  • The board is about 126 by 80 millimeters, close to the size of a postcard.
  • Four AA batteries power the motors, and the Pico takes code over a micro USB cable.

Specs describe the Raspberry Pi Pico that plugs into the kit. The chassis has no processor of its own. adc_channels counts the three Pico ADC inputs used by the left, centre and right line sensors. Free GPIO count is not published. The speaker entry stands for the PWM-driven buzzer. The motor driver chip is not named by Kitronik. Size is the chassis PCB. Wheel diameter with tyre is 67.5 mm. A Pico W adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, which the kit software does not use.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First drive around the room

    Make the buggy drive forward, turn, and stop with a few lines of code. It is a quick win for a first coding lesson.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Light show buggy

    Set the four color LEDs to flash and fade as the robot moves. Each direction can get its own color.

  • Beep and go button

    Press the button to start a short drive and play a beep. Students learn how inputs trigger actions.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

  • Drawing robot artist

    Drop a pen in the center hole and program shapes like squares and stars. Let the robot draw on big paper.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: felt tip pen, large sheet of paper.

Builder ideas

  • Line following racer

    Use the three line sensors to follow a black tape track on the floor. Tune the speed for a class race.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: black electrical tape.

  • Wall dodging explorer

    Read the ultrasonic sensor and turn away from walls before a bump. The robot can wander a room alone.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Parking sensor beeper

    Make the buzzer beep faster as an object gets closer. It works like the parking sensor in a car.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Robot music box

    Play a short tune on the buzzer and flash the LEDs to the beat. The robot can even dance in time.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Flag waving parade robot

    Add a servo with a paper flag that waves as the buggy rolls. It is a fun piece for a school show.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo motor, paper flag.

  • Garden path patrol

    Follow a tape line along a patio and stop at obstacles. Pretend it is a tiny delivery robot.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: black electrical tape.

Expert ideas

  • Maze solving challenge

    Combine the ultrasonic sensor and turning rules to find a way out of a cardboard maze. Teams can compare strategies.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: cardboard boxes.

  • Grabber arm buggy

    Mount a small servo with a claw on the front. The robot can drive to a block and pick it up.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: servo motor, small gripper claw.

  • Rear guard reversing

    Fit a second ultrasonic sensor on the back. The robot can back up safely without hitting things.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor.

How open is it?

Open Code

The code side is open. Kitronik's MicroPython module uses the MIT license and runs on stock MicroPython. The chassis has no published schematic or hardware license, and the Pico chip itself is closed.

Hardware license: None published for the kit · Software license: MIT (Kitronik MicroPython module)
