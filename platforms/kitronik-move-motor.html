Kitronik Move Motor

Kitronik

A ready-built robot buggy that a BBC micro:bit drives and controls.

Overview

The Kitronik :MOVE Motor is a ready-built two-wheel robot buggy for the BBC micro:bit. The micro:bit slides into the buggy and acts as its brain. It comes with motors, a distance sensor, line sensors, color lights, a sounder and a pen holder. It suits classrooms and kids who want a robot that moves on day one.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First robotics lessons with MakeCode blocks
  • Line following and obstacle avoiding projects
  • Drawing robot art with a pen
  • Remote control with a second micro:bit

Not great for

  • Rough outdoor ground, as it is a small indoor buggy
  • Adding many extra sensors, as it has few spare connectors
  • People without a micro:bit, as it needs one to run
  • Perfectly straight driving out of the box, as the motors may need balancing

Specs in plain words

  • It is about 11 centimeters long, roughly the size of a large smartphone.
  • Two motors with speed control let it drive, spin and turn smoothly.
  • The ultrasonic sensor measures distance with sound, much like a bat.
  • Line sensors under the nose see dark tape on a light floor.
  • Four AA batteries power the motors and the micro:bit together.
  • Two servo outputs let you add moving parts such as a claw.

Processor numbers are left null because the brain is the separate micro:bit. See the bbc-microbit-v2 card for its specs. The buggy itself has no programmable processor. The MakeCode extension shows early PCB versions (V1.0, V2.0) use a PCA9632 I2C PWM chip to drive the motor driver. By contrast, board V3.1 has no PCA9632. Bluetooth and the micro:bit radio come from the micro:bit. As a result, radios is left empty for the buggy. The onboard ultrasonic sensor and line-following sensors have no matching value in the onboard vocabulary. Size is 111 mm long, 90 mm wide with wheels and 67 mm high.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First drive forward and back

    Use MakeCode blocks to drive forward, stop and reverse. Kids see their code move a real robot in minutes.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Line following race

    Tape a black line on the floor and let the buggy follow it. Hold a race to see whose code finishes first.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: black electrical tape.

  • Robot that never bumps

    Read the ultrasonic sensor and turn away before hitting a wall. The buggy can roam a room on its own.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Drawing robot art

    Put a felt pen in the pen holder and program spirals, stars and squares. Students make art with geometry.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: felt tip pen, large paper.

  • Light show buggy

    Make the four color LEDs flash like a police car or fade like a rainbow. Mix it with motor moves for a dance.

    Uses: Battery ready.

  • Musical robot

    Play a tune on the sounder as the buggy drives. Make it beep a warning as it backs up.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Speed and distance science

    Time the buggy over a measured track at different speeds. Graph the results and calculate its speed.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: tape measure, stopwatch.

  • Waving flag mascot

    Fix a servo with a flag to the buggy and wave it on cue. Use it to cheer at a school sports day.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo motor, paper flag.

Builder ideas

  • Radio remote control

    Tilt a second micro:bit to steer the buggy over the micro:bit radio. It works like a homemade remote car.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: second micro:bit.

  • Maze solver challenge

    Build a cardboard maze and code the buggy to find its way out with the distance sensor. Teams compare strategies.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: cardboard boxes.

  • Parking sensor demo

    Beep faster as the buggy gets closer to a wall, like a car parking sensor. It teaches how sensors guide drivers.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Grabber robot

    Add a claw on the servo outputs and pick up small blocks. Build a clean-up robot for a toy floor.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: robot claw add-on, servo motor.

  • Robot sumo

    Code two buggies to push each other out of a taped ring. The distance sensor helps each one find its rival.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: second buggy, tape.

  • Python patrol robot

    Write a MicroPython program that patrols a square and flashes the LEDs at each corner. It is a gentle step from blocks to text code.

    Uses: Drives motors.
