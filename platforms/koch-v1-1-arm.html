Koch v1.1 Arm

Alexander Koch (original design), Jess Moss (v1.1) and Hugging Face LeRobot

An open 3D-printed robot arm pair for teaching robots by example.

Field marks: Two printed Koch arms share one desk, and the follower copies every move of the hand-steered leader for about 477 dollars in Dynamixel servos.

Overview

The Koch v1.1 is an open-source pair of 3D-printed robot arms built from Robotis Dynamixel servos. You move the leader arm by hand and the follower arm copies it. Hugging Face used it as the first reference arm for its LeRobot project. It suits makers, students, and researchers who want to record demos and train robots to learn by example.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Recording teleoperation demos for robot learning
  • Training imitation learning policies with LeRobot
  • University and club labs on AI and robotics
  • Builders who like sturdy smart servos with good tools

Not great for

  • Total beginners, as it needs printing, assembly, and Python setup
  • Tight budgets, as a pair costs about twice the cheaper SO-101 arm
  • Heavy lifting, as it is a small desktop arm
  • Projects needing the latest LeRobot tutorials, as those now focus on the SO-101

Specs in plain words

  • Each arm has six smart servos, which report their own position and settings.
  • The follower uses two stronger XL430 servos for the first joints and four XL330 servos for the rest.
  • The leader uses six XL330 servos geared to move easily by hand.
  • A computer runs all the code and talks to each arm over a USB cable.
  • The follower needs a 12 volt power supply and the leader needs a 5 volt supply.
Full spec sheet

MemoryUnknown

Ways to connect partsSerial (UART), USB device
ConnectorsUSB-C, Servo ports
Built inmotor driver
Power12 V supply for the follower (through a voltage reducer) and 5 V supply for the leader

The follower has two Dynamixel XL430-W250-T servos for the first two joints and four XL330-M288-T servos. The leader has six XL330-M077-T servos, which are geared to be easy to move by hand. In the DIY build each arm uses a Waveshare Serial Bus Servo Driver Board over USB-C. Robotis kits use an OpenRB-150 board instead. The Waveshare board is run at 5 V on the leader even though it is rated 9 to 12.6 V. The README says this is intended. Arm reach, payload and dimensions were not found on a fetched page.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Copycat arm demo

    Move the leader arm by hand and watch the follower copy every motion. It is a great first show at a science fair.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Robot drawing assistant

    Tape a marker to the gripper and draw shapes with the leader arm. The follower copies your drawing on paper.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: marker, paper.

  • Chess move helper

    Move chess pieces with the leader arm while a friend plays from afar. The follower moves the real pieces on the board.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small chess set.

  • Share a dataset with others

    Upload your recorded demos to the Hugging Face Hub. Other builders can train on your data and share theirs.

    Add: USB webcam.

  • Servo science

    Read joint angles from the smart servos as you move the arm. Graph how each joint turns during a task.

    Uses: Drives motors.

Expert ideas

  • Teach it to stack blocks

    Record a few dozen demos of stacking blocks with the leader arm. Train a LeRobot policy and let the follower try alone.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: wooden blocks, USB webcam.

  • Pick and place sorter

    Teach the arm to drop red blocks in one cup and blue blocks in another. It shows how robots learn from examples.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: colored blocks, small cups, USB webcam.

  • Tidy the desk robot

    Train the arm to pick up pens and drop them in a cup. Your desk tidies itself after enough demos.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: pen cup, USB webcam.

  • Garden seed planter

    Teach the arm to drop seeds into a row of small pots. It is a fun way to test repeat tasks.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: seed tray, small pots, USB webcam.

  • Robot learning class lab

    Students record demos, train a policy, and measure how often it succeeds. It turns machine learning into a hands-on lesson.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: USB webcam.

  • Musical chime robot

    Teach the arm to tap a row of chimes or bells in order. It plays a simple tune by repeating your demo.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small chimes or bells, USB webcam.

  • Accessible reach helper

    Practice using the arm to fetch light objects across a table. It is a starting point for assistive robot research.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: USB webcam.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The koch-v1-1 repo is under the Apache-2.0 license and holds STL files, SolidWorks models, a wiring diagram, and a parts list. The LeRobot library and the Dynamixel SDK are open source too. The Dynamixel servo firmware is closed, and the arm has no OSHWA entry.
