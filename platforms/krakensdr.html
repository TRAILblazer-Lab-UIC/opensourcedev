Overview

KrakenSDR is a radio receiver with five tuners that share one clock. The software compares the five signals and shows which way a transmitter lies. It suits radio hobbyists, search teams, and researchers who want to find signals. The open software runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 or Pi 5 and shows results in a web page.

Specs in plain words

  • A range of 24 to 1766 MHz covers FM radio, aircraft, ham bands, and many sensor gadgets.
  • Each channel hears a slice of the dial about 2.5 MHz wide at a time.
  • Five tuners share one clock. The software can compare tiny timing gaps to work out direction.
  • A built-in noise source recalibrates the channels on its own.
  • It draws about 11 watts and runs a fan to stay cool.
  • The box is about the size of a hardcover book and weighs about 670 grams.

The adc_channels value counts the five 8-bit RTL2832U receive channels. Maximum input is +10 dBm per port. The radio is a receive-only VHF and UHF SDR, which does not match any radios vocabulary value. As a result, radios is empty. The five antenna ports are SMA, which has no connectors vocabulary value. The case adds 4.7 mm for the fan guard. Weight is 670 g. Rated for ambient temperatures up to 50 C. The data cable does not carry power. Each tuner has a DIP switch to turn it off.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open

The direction-finding app, the data capture software, and the GNU Radio block are open source under GPL-3.0. No schematics or board files are published, and the tuner chips are closed. The Android app is closed and free only for non-commercial use.
