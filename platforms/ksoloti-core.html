Overview

Ksoloti Core is a small board made for building digital synths and sound effects. You connect boxes on screen in the free Ksoloti Patcher app and send the patch to the board. It suits musicians and makers who like to solder and tinker. It carries on the work of the older Axoloti board and runs most of its patches.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building your own synth, effects box, or drum machine
  • Visual sound design with drag-and-drop blocks
  • Playing patches standalone from a memory card with no computer attached
  • Adding sound to Eurorack and desktop instrument builds

Not great for

  • Plug-and-play use, with no audio or MIDI jacks on the board itself
  • Newcomers who have never soldered
  • Projects that need wireless, a camera, or a full computer

Specs in plain words

  • The 168 MHz processor is quick enough to run many sound voices and effects at once.
  • 32 MB of extra memory holds several seconds of recorded audio for loopers and samplers.
  • The stereo sound chip gives line in, line out, and a headphone signal at studio-like quality.
  • The USB host port lets you plug in a USB music keyboard or controller directly.
  • At 59 by 90 millimeters, the board is about the size of a playing card.

Storage1 MB
Pins42 pins
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), USB device, USB host, Digital microphone (PDM), SD card bus

Built inbuttons
Power5 V over USB-C or pin header (no on-board 5 V regulator)
Size59 x 90 mm

The v0.7 schematic lists an STM32F42xZGT (1 MB flash, 144 pin), an ADAU1961 codec, W9825G6KH or IS42S16160J SDRAM and an 8 MHz crystal. psram_kb holds the 32 MB external SDRAM. ram_kb is the MCU's internal 192 KB SRAM plus 64 KB CCM, as mapped in the firmware linker script. The firmware reserves the last 128 KB of flash for patch storage. The codec is 24-bit 96 kHz capable but the editor and firmware run at 24-bit 48 kHz. In-to-out latency is about 2 ms per the comparison sheet. GPIO count (42) is the sum of pins listed in the comparison sheet for PCB v0.7 and later, including pins shared with the two buttons and two LEDs. Thickness is under 5 mm excluding pin headers. The board has four M3 mounting holes and was designed to fit behind a 12HP Eurorack panel.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Play with a USB keyboard

    Plug a USB music keyboard into the host port. Each key plays a note on a synth you patched yourself.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: USB music keyboard, headphones.

  • Classroom sound lab

    Students drag oscillators and filters together in the Patcher and hear the result at once. It turns sound science into a hands-on lesson.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • Talking gallery box

    Wire a button to the board and play a recorded story with each press. Visitors hear about the art in front of them.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: buttons, small speaker, amplifier board.

Builder ideas

  • Desktop synth from a kit

    Build the Gills kit around the board and get a box with knobs, a screen and buttons. Patch your own synth sounds and play them right away.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: desktop synth kit, headphones.

  • Guitar effects box

    Run a guitar through the board and add echo, distortion or a wobbly chorus. Save each effect to the memory card for gigs.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio jacks, knobs, foot switch.

  • Drum machine with knobs

    Patch a kick, snare, and hi-hat pattern that loops on its own. Turn knobs to change the beat while it plays.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, buttons, audio jacks.

  • Looper for practice

    Record a short phrase and let it repeat. Play along on top to practice solos or build layered songs.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio jacks, foot switch.

  • Bird song echo sculpture

    Add a tiny digital microphone and let the board pick up garden sounds. It plays them back slowed down and echoing through a speaker.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: digital microphone, small speaker, amplifier board.

  • MIDI note sorter

    Place the board between a keyboard and other gear. It can split, transpose, or arpeggiate the notes it passes along.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: MIDI jack board, USB music keyboard.

Expert ideas

  • Eurorack module build

    Mount the board in the Big Genes kit and add it to a modular synth case. It can become a new oscillator, filter, or sequencer with each patch.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: Eurorack module kit, Eurorack case, patch cables.

  • Granular sound sampler

    Load sound clips from the memory card and chop them into tiny grains. Smear them into clouds of new texture for art pieces.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: memory card, knobs.

  • Custom C sound object

    Write your own effect in C and drop it into the Patcher as a new block. Share it with other players on the forum.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Polysynth with a screen

    Patch a synth that plays several notes at once and show settings on a small screen. It works like a homemade keyboard synth.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: small OLED screen, knobs, USB music keyboard.
