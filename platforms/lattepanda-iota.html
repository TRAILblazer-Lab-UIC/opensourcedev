Overview

The LattePanda Iota is a tiny Intel computer about the size of a palm. It runs Windows or Ubuntu like a regular PC. A Raspberry Pi RP2040 helper chip sits on board for sensors, LEDs and motors. It suits makers who want PC software and real pins on one board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Windows or Linux projects that need a full PC
  • Home servers and media players
  • Kiosks and signs with a screen
  • Robots that need PC power plus simple motor control

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as the board-only price now sits near 339 US dollars
  • Battery projects, as it needs a 15 volt supply
  • Open hardware fans, as no schematic is published
  • Old 5 volt add-ons, as the pins now run at 3.3 volts

Specs in plain words

  • The four-core Intel N150 is about as quick as a modest laptop for everyday tasks.
  • 8 or 16 GB of memory holds a browser, a few apps and a small server at once.
  • 64 or 128 GB of built-in storage is room for an operating system and plenty of files.
  • The RP2040 helper chip gives 16 pins for buttons, sensors and LEDs, with 3 that read changing voltages.
  • The PCIe connector matches the Raspberry Pi 5, which lets many Pi 5 drive and AI boards plug in.
  • The USB-C port takes power only, and it needs a 15 volt charger with Power Delivery.

RAM and eMMC values are the base 8 GB / 64 GB model. A 16 GB / 128 GB model also exists. Clock is the 3.6 GHz max turbo. GPIO and ADC counts are the RP2040 pins on the 36-pin header (GP0 to 13 and GP26 to 28, the last three can be ADC). The header also carries a PC UART, a native USB 2.0 port, speaker output, power-state pins and RP2040 SWD pins. I2C refers to the touch FPC connector. eDP has no matching interface tag. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth need an M.2 2230 card, which kits include. Board height is 19 mm. CNX Software listed 88 x 77 mm, but the official spec says 88 x 70 mm.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: HDMI monitor, USB keyboard, USB mouse.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: HDMI cable, USB remote.

  • Room climate logger

    Wire a temperature and humidity sensor to the helper chip. The PC side saves the readings and draws charts.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, jumper wires.

  • Classroom coding station

    Set up one board with Thonny and a big screen. Students write MicroPython for the helper chip and watch LEDs react.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: HDMI monitor, LEDs, breadboard, jumper wires.

Builder ideas

  • Quiet home server

    Run file sharing, backups, and a photo library on one small box. It sips power compared with an old desktop.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe SSD with adapter board, network cable.

  • Shop or library kiosk

    Show a touch screen menu or a sign-up page at a front desk. The board starts the page on its own at power on.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: touch screen.

  • Garden watering station

    Read a soil sensor with the helper chip and switch a small pump on dry days. Log each watering on the PC side.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Retro game console

    Load an emulator front end and plug in two USB gamepads. Play classic games on the family TV.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB gamepads, HDMI cable.

  • Network ad blocker

    Run a filter for your home network on the wired port. Fewer ads reach every device in the house.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: network cable.

  • Science data station

    Read light and air sensors with the helper chip and crunch the numbers in Python on the PC. Share results with the class.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: light sensor, air quality sensor, jumper wires.

  • Art light show

    Drive an LED strip from the helper chip and sync it to music played by the PC. Hang it in a hallway or gallery.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply for the strip.

  • Community bulletin screen

    Show events, weather, and bus times on a screen at a club or library. Update it from any laptop on the network.

    Uses: Screen, Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: HDMI monitor.

Expert ideas

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The x86 side runs Ubuntu with open drivers, and the RP2040 uses the open arduino-pico core or MicroPython. LattePanda shares only mechanical 3D files, with no schematic and no stated license. The BIOS, the Intel firmware, and the Intel chip stay closed.

LED

MicroPython
MicroPython is a small version of Python made for microcontrollers.
Open hardware
Open hardware is a physical device whose design files are public.
Operating system
An operating system is the main software that runs a computer.
PCIe
PCIe is a very fast link inside computers.
Pin header
A pin header is a row of metal pins or sockets on a board.

Raspberry Pi is a line of low-cost single-board computers from a UK charity and company.
Raspberry Pi Pico
The Raspberry Pi Pico is a small microcontroller board.

USB Power Delivery is a way for a charger and device to agree on extra power.
