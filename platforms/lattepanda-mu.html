LattePanda Mu

DFRobot (LattePanda)

A palm-sized Intel PC module that plugs into a carrier board.

Overview

The LattePanda Mu is a small Intel computer module from DFRobot. It has an N100 or Core i3-N305 chip, memory, and storage on one card. The card has no ports and plugs into a carrier board. It suits tinkerers who want a full PC for servers or custom gadgets. Its open carrier board designs set it apart.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Home servers, network storage, and media boxes
  • Running Windows or regular Linux on x86
  • Driving up to three 4K screens
  • Designing your own custom PC board

Not great for

  • First-time builders, with a carrier, cooling, and power to sort out
  • Battery projects, with a draw of 6 to 35 watts
  • Reading sensors directly, with only UART and I2C headers on the Lite carrier
  • Tight budgets, with the kit now listed at 410 USD by DFRobot

Specs in plain words

  • The N100 has four cores up to 3.4 GHz. It feels like a budget laptop.
  • 8 GB of memory handles a desktop, a browser, and a few servers at once.
  • 64 GB of built-in storage holds an operating system and some apps.
  • Up to nine PCIe lanes let a carrier add fast drives, network cards, or a graphics card.
  • The card is a bit smaller than a playing card at about 70 by 60 millimeters.
  • It reached about 91 degrees Celsius on a passive heatsink. Use the active cooler for long jobs.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorIntel N100 (Alder Lake-N, x86-64), 4 cores at 3.4 GHz
Memory8 GB
Storage64 GB
AI chipIntel UHD Graphics (24 EU on N100, 32 EU on N305)
Pins64 pins
Ways to connect partsI2C, Serial (UART), USB host, PCIe, HDMI
ConnectorsUSB-C, USB-A, M.2 slot
Power9 to 20 V DC to the module (Lite carrier takes USB-C PD or a 12 to 20 V jack)
Size69.6 x 60 mm

Values are for the common N100 8 GB module (DFR1146). The N305 module (DFR1149) has 8 cores, 3.8 GHz turbo and 16 GB RAM. The clock is the max turbo. GPIO is the expandable maximum of 64 per the spec page. The module has no ports of its own and plugs into a carrier through an edge connector. Connectors listed are those of the Lite carrier in the official kit, which also has Gigabit Ethernet, a PCIe x4 slot and Gravity UART and I2C headers. The module has no radios. The PCIe x4 slot on the Lite carrier needs 12 V power, per CNX Software.

Key terms on this page

Short definitions of words used above and below. Select a term for the full glossary entry.

Carrier board
A carrier board holds a compute module and adds ports.
Compute module
A compute module is the brain of a computer on a small card.
I2C
I2C is a simple way for chips to talk over two wires.
KiCad
KiCad is a free and open-source app for designing circuit boards.
Module
A module is a small ready-made board that does one job.
Operating system
An operating system is the main software that runs a computer.
PCIe
PCIe is a very fast link inside computers.
Pin header
A pin header is a row of metal pins or sockets on a board.
Resolution
Resolution is how many dots make up an image or screen.
UART
UART is a simple way for two devices to send text and data over two wires.
USB Power Delivery
USB Power Delivery is a way for a charger and device to agree on extra power.
Voltage
Voltage is the push that moves electricity through a circuit.
