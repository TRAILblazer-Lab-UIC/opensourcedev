Overview

LeKiwi is an open-source mobile robot you build yourself. It puts an SO-101 robot arm on a round three-wheel base that can roll in any direction. A Raspberry Pi 5 on board streams camera views to a laptop running the Hugging Face LeRobot software. It suits makers, clubs, and teachers who want to explore robot learning on a small budget.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how robots copy human demos and learn new tasks
  • Recording datasets and sharing them with the LeRobot community
  • University and high school robotics clubs with a 3D printer
  • Builders who like a big weekend project

Not great for

  • People who want a robot that works straight out of the box
  • Homes without a 3D printer or a budget for a kit
  • Rough ground or outdoor trails
  • Lifting heavy things with small hobby servos

Specs in plain words

  • The Raspberry Pi 5 with 4 GB of memory is a small full computer riding on the robot.
  • Three omni wheels let the base slide sideways, spin in place and drive forward all at once.
  • Top speed is about a third of a meter each second, which is a slow walking pace.
  • Two cameras give a room view and a close view from the wrist.
  • A full 5 V build costs about $499 in parts before printing, according to the parts list.
  • You can pick a power bank build, a 12 V battery build, or a cheaper build wired to a laptop.

Compute values are for the Raspberry Pi 5 4GB listed in the BOM. The docs say any 5 V computer with two or more USB ports works, and Seeed also lists Jetson support. The Pi boots from microSD. As a result, flash_kb is null. Three STS3215 servos (IDs 7, 8 and 9) drive the wheels and six more drive the arm, all on one bus servo adapter over USB serial. Cameras are two USB webcams (front and wrist). Arducam 5MP wide-angle USB is a listed alternative. The wired build drops the Pi and battery and tethers the base to a laptop through a USB hub and extension cable. LeRobot speed modes reach 0.3 m/s and 90 deg/s. Overall dimensions and weight were not found on a fetched page.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Drive it around the room

    Steer the base with your laptop keyboard and watch the camera feed. It is the first test after the build.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors, Camera.

  • Copy your arm moves

    Move a leader arm by hand and the robot arm copies you live. It feels like a puppet with a camera eye.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: leader arm.

Builder ideas

  • Classroom robot learning lab

    Students take turns recording demos and compare how many demos a task needs. It turns machine learning into a hands-on experiment.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Share a dataset online

    Record your demos and upload them to the Hugging Face hub. Other builders can train on your data.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera, Runs Linux.

  • Garden patrol rover

    Drive the base along garden paths on a flat surface and stream video of your plants. Check for pests from the couch.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera, Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Hallway greeter

    Park the robot by the door and wave the arm at visitors. A keyboard key can trigger the wave.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Paint with a moving brush

    Tape a brush to the gripper and drive slow loops over big paper. Each run makes a new piece of robot art.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: paint brush, large paper.

  • Science fair sorting demo

    Train the robot to sort red and blue blocks into two bins. Show judges how accuracy grows with more demos.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: colored blocks, two bins.

Expert ideas

  • Tidy up the toy floor

    Record yourself picking up blocks a few dozen times. Train a policy and let the robot try the job alone.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: toy blocks, storage bin.

  • Fetch a snack from the shelf

    Teach the robot to drive to a low shelf and grab a small bag. The wrist camera helps it line up the grip.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Water a potted plant

    Teach the arm to tip a small watering can over a pot. Run the trained policy each morning.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small watering can.

  • Robot chess helper

    Drive up to a board and move a chess piece with the arm. Start with teleoperation and then train the move.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: chess set.

  • Simulate it first

    Load the published robot model into a simulator and test moves on screen. It saves wear on real servos.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Add a second arm

    Follow the XLeRobot project to mount two arms on a bigger base. Two hands can fold a towel or open a box.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: second robot arm, larger base plates.
