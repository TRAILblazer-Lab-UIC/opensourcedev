Libre Computer Le Potato

Libre Computer (Shenzhen Libre Technology)

A budget Linux board shaped like a Raspberry Pi 3 with 4K video.

Field marks: A Raspberry Pi 3 lookalike with four USB ports and HDMI 2.0, minus Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, trailing an Ethernet cable to the nearest router.

Overview

Le Potato is a low-cost Linux board in the Raspberry Pi 3 shape. It has four cores, up to 2 GB of memory, and HDMI with 4K video. Libre Computer puts its effort into mainline Linux support. It suits tinkerers who know a little Linux and want a Pi-style board for media or servers.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Media centers and retro gaming on a TV
  • Small home servers on a wired network
  • Reusing Raspberry Pi cases and many HATs
  • Learning Linux on a budget

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Audio HATs that use the PCM pins
  • Beginners who want step by step official guides
  • Camera projects, as it has no camera connector

Specs in plain words

  • Four Cortex-A53 cores give it speed close to a Raspberry Pi 3.
  • 2 GB of memory runs a light desktop or a few small servers at once.
  • HDMI 2.0 with 4K video makes it a fine TV box.
  • Storage lives on a microSD card or an optional plug-in eMMC module.
  • 100 Mb Ethernet is fine for streaming video but slower than gigabit ports.
  • It runs on 5 volts over micro-USB and needs a solid 2 amp supply.

RAM is the common 2 GB model. A 1 GB model was also sold. Clock is the 1.512 GHz figure from Wikipedia. The Libre hub lists 1.4 GHz. Storage is microSD or an optional eMMC module. As a result, flash_kb is null. The 40-pin header count includes power pins, and the usable GPIO count was not found. There is an ADC (SARADC) header, but the channel count was not found. Size is from the Electromaker board guide. The board has an IR receiver and a composite AV jack. There is no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The board runs upstream Linux and U-Boot under GPL-2.0, and the Mali GPU has the open Lima driver. Libre Computer shares schematics, but no layout files or hardware license were found. Some Amlogic boot firmware ships as closed binaries, and the chip itself is closed.
