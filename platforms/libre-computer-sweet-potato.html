Libre Computer Sweet Potato

Libre Computer (Shenzhen Libre Technology)

A credit-card Linux computer shaped like a Raspberry Pi 3, with a PoE header.

Field marks: Look for a Pi 3 lookalike with no Wi-Fi, a tuber for a name, and a Wi-Fi dongle sticking out of its USB port.

Overview

The Libre Computer Sweet Potato is a small Linux computer the size and shape of a Raspberry Pi 3. It has a four-core chip, 2 GB of memory, and an HDMI port for 4K video. It adds a USB-C power port, a header for Power over Ethernet, and built-in boot firmware. It suits tinkerers who want a Pi-style board for media, networks, and kiosks.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Media players that show 4K video on a TV
  • Wired network jobs such as file sharing and ad blocking
  • Hallway screens and kiosks powered over a network cable
  • Projects that reuse Raspberry Pi add-on boards on the 40-pin header

Not great for

  • Wireless projects out of the box, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • First-time users who need step-by-step guides
  • Heavy desktop use or modern games with 2 GB of memory
  • Battery projects, as it needs a steady 5 V wall supply

Specs in plain words

  • Four cores at 1.5 GHz handle a media center, a small server, or a web browser at a gentle pace.
  • 2 GB of memory is room for a light desktop or a few server apps at once.
  • HDMI 2.0 with 4K decode plays sharp movies on a big TV.
  • 16 MB of boot firmware lets it start many standard Linux installers.
  • The PoE header lets one network cable carry power and data with an add-on board.
  • The board has an internal USB header for a webcam, plus normal USB ports for keyboards and drives.

The retail board ships with 2 GB DDR4. A 1 GB model is OEM only per Libre Computer, though LoverPi lists a 1 GB variant. The 16 MB SPI flash holds boot firmware and is not counted as flash_kb. Storage is microSD or an optional eMMC module. As a result, flash_kb is null. Size is the Raspberry Pi 3 form factor, but no exact figure was found on a fetched page. The 40-pin header includes power pins, and the usable GPIO count was not found. There is an ADC plus I2S header, but the channel count was not found. Other features are an IR receiver, an SPDIF header, a 3-pin UART debug header and a CEC/GPIO jumper. There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, composite video or analog audio jack.
