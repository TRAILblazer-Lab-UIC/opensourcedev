Overview

The LilyGO T-Beam Supreme is the ESP32-S3 successor to the classic T-Beam. It packs a LoRa radio, a GPS receiver, a 1.3 inch screen and an 18650 battery holder. It also adds a motion sensor, a compass, a weather sensor, and a microSD slot. It suits hikers, preppers, and makers who want a well-equipped Meshtastic node or a GPS project board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends off the grid with Meshtastic
  • Sharing GPS positions with a group
  • Long battery life from a big 18650 cell
  • Weather, motion, and compass projects with no extra wiring
  • Logging trips to a memory card

Not great for

  • Folks who want a finished case out of the box
  • Any battery you already own, as only flat-top cells without a protection board fit
  • Plugging in many extra parts, as nearly every pin is already used
  • Pocket carry, as the board is longer than a deck of cards

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It can track GPS, run the radio, and drive the screen together.
  • 8 MB of extra memory gives room for maps of nearby nodes and message history.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Buy a licence-free band your country allows, such as 915 MHz in the US or 868 MHz in Europe.
  • The GPS finds your spot on Earth from satellites. It needs a view of the sky.
  • The weather sensor reads temperature, humidity, and air pressure. The motion sensor and compass know which way the board tilts and faces.
  • The power chip charges the 18650 cell over USB-C. It also reports battery level to the screen.

Values come from the LilyGO hardware doc and wiki. Flash and PSRAM are 8 MB each per the LilyGO store and repo. The ESP32-S3 sits on the T-Beam S3 Core module, which plugs into an M.2-style socket on the carrier per the V3.1 schematic. The LilyGO pin map marks every listed pin as used except UART1 on the QWIIC socket. As a result, the free GPIO count was not confirmed. The BME280 adds temperature, humidity and pressure. The RTC is a PCF8563 per the repo doc and schematic. The wiki spec table says PCF85063ATL. The repo doc says Micro-USB is used for module firmware upgrade, but the V3.1 schematic shows only a Type-C connector on the carrier. The 18650 holder fits only unprotected flat-top cells per Meshtastic. GNSS hot start needs the 18650 installed. The microSD slot shares the SPI bus with the IMU. sdio in interfaces reflects the ESP32-S3 SD host, used here in SPI mode.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid group chat for hikes

    Give each hiker a Supreme paired with their phone. The group can text each other in places with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Friend finder on a map

    Each radio shares its GPS position over the mesh. The phone app shows where everyone in your group stands.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Family emergency check-in

    Keep a node in each home on your street. Neighbors can post a status note during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Window repeater for the block

    Park a node high in a window on USB power. It relays messages for radios across the neighborhood.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: USB power adapter.

  • School radio range lesson

    Students carry nodes across campus and log where messages still arrive. They learn how hills and walls block radio.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS. Add: 18650 battery.

Builder ideas

  • Backpack weather station

    The built-in sensor reads temperature, humidity, and air pressure. The screen shows a falling barometer before a storm.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Digital compass for hikers

    Use the magnetometer and GPS together. The screen points the way back to camp.

    Uses: Sensors built in, GPS, Screen. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Trail log on a memory card

    Record your route, altitude, and weather to the microSD card. Load the file on a computer for a map of the trip.

    Uses: GPS, Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, 18650 battery.

  • Bike crash alert

    The motion sensor notices a sudden fall. The board radios your GPS spot to a friend with a second node.

    Uses: Sensors built in, GPS, LoRa long range radio. Add: 18650 battery, small project box.

  • Lost pet collar tracker

    Put the board in a tough case on a large dog's collar. It radios its GPS spot back to a base node.

    Uses: GPS, LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery, small waterproof case.

  • Garden sensor hub

    Plug a soil sensor into the QWIIC port. The board sends readings to the house by radio.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Plug-in connectors, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor, 18650 battery, weatherproof box.

  • Science class motion lab

    Students toss or swing the board and log the motion sensor data. Graphs show forces and spins.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Wildlife trail visit counter

    Attach a motion sensor near an animal path. The board counts visits and reports totals by radio once a day.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, 18650 battery, weatherproof box.

Expert ideas

  • Glider and drone traffic beacon
