Overview

The LilyGO T-Beam is an ESP32 board with a LoRa long-range radio, a GPS receiver and a holder for one 18650 battery. It first appeared in 2018 and became one of the classic Meshtastic message radios. It suits hikers, preppers, and hobbyists who want texting and location sharing with no cell towers. You can also program it yourself with Arduino tools.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends off the grid with Meshtastic
  • Sharing GPS positions with a group
  • Long battery life from a big 18650 cell
  • Learning LoRa radio and GPS in Arduino

Not great for

  • Folks who want a finished case out of the box
  • Any battery you already own, as only flat-top cells without a protection board fit
  • Plugging in many sensors, as few header pins are free
  • Newer radio features found on the T-Beam Supreme

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It can track GPS, run the radio, and drive the screen together.
  • 8 MB of extra memory gives room for maps of nearby nodes and message history.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Buy the band your country allows, such as 915 MHz in the US or 868 MHz in Europe.
  • The NEO-6M GPS finds your spot on Earth from satellites. It needs a view of the sky.
  • The 0.96 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It shows messages and battery level.

Values are for the current V1.2 board with the AXP2101 PMU. The LoRa radio sits on SPI and the GPS on UART, and the OLED and PMU share the I2C bus on GPIO 21 and 22. The LilyGO pin map marks every listed pin as used. As a result, the count of free header GPIO was not confirmed. Connector type conflicts between sources. The V1.2 schematic, the LilyGO store page and Tinytronics show micro-USB. By contrast, the LilyGO repo quick start text says USB-C. The USB serial chip is listed as CH9102 or CH9102F by the store and Tinytronics, but the schematic shows a CP2104. Revisions likely differ. The 18650 holder fits only unprotected flat-top cells per Meshtastic. The battery is not included. The LoRa antenna uses an SMA connector. Wi-Fi uses a 3D antenna with an optional IPEX path after moving a resistor.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid group chat for hikes

    Give each hiker a T-Beam paired with their phone. The group can text each other in places with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Friend finder on a map

    Each radio shares its GPS position over the mesh. The phone app shows where everyone in your group stands.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Family emergency check-in

    Keep a node in each home on your street. Neighbors can post a quick status note during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: 18650 battery.

  • School radio range lesson

    Students carry nodes across campus and log where messages still arrive. They learn how hills and walls block radio.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

  • Window repeater for the neighborhood

    Park a node high in a window on USB power. It relays messages for radios across the block.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: USB power adapter.

Builder ideas

  • Bike ride tracker

    Strap the board to your bike in a small box. It logs your route and sends your spot to a friend at home.

    Uses: GPS, LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery, small project box.

  • Garden soil moisture alerts

    Wire a soil sensor to the board and send readings across the yard by radio. Your phone gets a note once the beds turn dry.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor, 18650 battery, weatherproof box.

  • Mailbox opened alert

    Fit a magnetic door switch to a far-off mailbox. The board sends a radio message each time the lid opens.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, 18650 battery.

  • Backyard weather beacon

    Add a temperature and humidity sensor. The small screen shows the reading and the radio shares it with the house.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Lost pet collar tracker

    Put the board in a tough case on a large dog's collar. It radios its GPS spot back to a base node.

    Uses: GPS, LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery, small waterproof case.

  • Coverage mapping walks

    Walk around town with a node that logs GPS spots and signal strength. You end up with a map of where the radio reaches.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Wildlife trail visit counter

    Attach a motion sensor near an animal path. The board counts visits and reports totals by radio once a day.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, 18650 battery, weatherproof box.

Expert ideas

  • Glider and drone traffic beacon

    Flash the SoftRF firmware for gliding hobbies. It shares your position with nearby aircraft running the same system.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Battery ready. Add: 18650 battery.

  • Wi-Fi bridge for sensor data

    Let a home node pass LoRa readings onto your Wi-Fi network. You can chart garden or barn data on a computer.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power adapter.

How open is it?

Open Board

LilyGO shares schematic PDFs for the board and the screen add-on. No board layout files are posted, and the repo has no license. The Meshtastic firmware is open under GPL-3.0. The ESP32 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: No license on the LilyGO example repo. Meshtastic firmware is GPL-3.0.
