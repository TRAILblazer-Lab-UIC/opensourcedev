Overview

The LilyGO T-Deck Plus is a handheld gadget with a thumb keyboard, a trackball and a 2.8 inch touchscreen. It packs a LoRa radio, GPS, a 2000 mAh battery, and a case. Most people run Meshtastic or MeshCore on it to text with no cell network. Makers also treat it as a pocket computer for Arduino and LVGL projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sending texts off the grid without a phone
  • Sharing GPS spots with a group
  • Building pocket apps with a real keyboard and screen
  • Audio projects with the built-in speaker and microphones

Not great for

  • Adding your own sensors, as the Grove port is taken by the GPS
  • Tight budgets, as US stores charge much more than LilyGO
  • Brand new coders who want to write their own firmware on day one
  • Long weeks in the field without a recharge

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It handles the screen, radio, and keyboard at once.
  • 16 MB of storage and 8 MB of extra memory leave room for maps, fonts and message history.
  • The 2.8 inch screen is about the size of a large postage stamp block. It shows 320 by 240 dots and responds to touch.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows.
  • The 2000 mAh battery is about the size of a phone battery from years past.
  • The whole device is about 115 by 72 mm, close to the size of a deck of cards.

CPU, memory, display, LoRa and GPS values come from the LilyGO store page and CNX Software. The size is from CNX and covers the cased device. Interfaces are those of the ESP32-S3 used on the board. The LCD uses SPI, audio uses I2S, and the keyboard and touch panel use I2C. The Grove (HY2.0-4P) port exists on the board, but LilyGO says the T-Deck Plus gives its pins to the GPS. As a result, it is not listed as a free connector. CNX reports four Hall effect sensors around the trackball and two MEMS microphones on an ES7210 codec. The schematic text confirms the ES7210, MAX98357A, TP4065 and ESP32-C3. The LoRa module footprint is labeled HPD16A in the schematic. LilyGO and Meshtastic list the radio as an SX1262.

Starter ideas

  • Phone-free trail texting

    Type messages on the keyboard with Meshtastic. Friends with other radios get them with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

  • Group map for a camping trip

    Each device shares its GPS spot over the mesh. You can see where your group is on the screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Screen.

  • Neighborhood emergency messenger

    Keep one in a drawer charged and ready. Send updates to neighbors during a storm or power outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Festival meetup radio

    Hand a few to friends at a big outdoor event. Text the group to meet up even with jammed cell towers.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Classroom radio range lab

    Students carry devices around the school and note where messages arrive. The class maps radio reach on paper.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

Builder ideas

  • Pocket field notebook

    Write a small app for logging bird or plant sightings. Each note saves with a GPS spot to the memory card.

    Uses: GPS, Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Hiking breadcrumb trail

    Log your GPS track to the memory card on a hike. Load it on a computer later to see your route.

    Uses: GPS, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • Handheld text adventure game

    Code a story game where players type commands. The keyboard and screen make it feel like a tiny retro computer.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Two-player radio word game

    Build a guessing game that sends turns over LoRa. Two friends can play across a park.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen.

  • Voice memo recorder

    Use the built-in microphones to save short audio notes to the card. Play them back on the speaker.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microSD card.

  • Simple music sequencer

    Map keys to notes and loop a beat through the small speaker. It makes a pocket sketchpad for tunes.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

  • Wi-Fi weather dashboard

    Pull the local forecast over Wi-Fi and draw it on the screen. Take it along as a desk or bedside display.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Garden sensor reader

    Pair it with a LoRa soil sensor node in the yard. The screen shows moisture levels from the back porch.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: second LoRa board, soil moisture sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Custom LVGL pocket menu

    Design your own touch menus with the LVGL graphics library. It can run timers, notes, and settings screens.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Off-grid chat bridge to Wi-Fi

    Write firmware that passes mesh messages to a home server over Wi-Fi. Family at home can read trail updates.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Wi-Fi.

  • Talking sensor alerts

    Play a spoken clip on the speaker once a radio alert arrives. It helps people who cannot glance at the screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Makes sound. Add: microSD card.
