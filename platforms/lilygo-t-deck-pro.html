LilyGO T-Deck Pro

LilyGO

A pocket LoRa messenger with an e-paper touchscreen, keyboard, GPS, and battery.

Overview

The LilyGO T-Deck Pro is a pocket handheld with a 3.1 inch e-paper touchscreen and a thumb keyboard. Every unit has a LoRa radio, GPS, a motion sensor, and a 1400 mAh battery. You pick a 4G version or an audio version. Most people run Meshtastic on it to text with no cell network, and makers program it with Arduino or PlatformIO.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sending texts off the grid with no phone
  • Reading the screen outdoors in bright sun
  • Sharing GPS spots with a group
  • Building pocket apps with a real keyboard

Not great for

  • Fast animation or games, as e-paper redraws slowly
  • 4G in the US, as the modem bands suit Europe and Asia better
  • Tight budgets, as US stores charge much more than LilyGO
  • Brand new coders who want to write their own firmware on day one

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It handles the screen, radio, and keyboard at once.
  • 16 MB of storage and 8 MB of extra memory leave room for fonts, maps and message history.
  • The e-paper screen looks like printed paper. It holds an image with almost no power.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows.
  • The 1400 mAh battery is a bit smaller than a typical phone battery.
  • The device is about 120 by 66 mm, close to the size of a slim phone.

CPU, memory, display, LoRa, GPS and battery values come from the LilyGO store data, the repo README and CNX Software. Size is from CNX. Cellular applies only to the A7682E version (LTE-FDD B1/B3/B5/B7/B8/B20 and GSM 900/1800. As a result, it suits Europe and Asia better than the US). The PCM5102A version drives audio straight from the ESP32-S3. On the 4G version the modem handles audio. The V1.0 and V1.1 schematics both name the BQ25896 charger and BQ27220 gauge. CNX lists a TP4065B charger, which the schematics do not show. The V1.0 schematic shows the LTR-553ALS light sensor. The V1.1 schematic adds a DRV2605 haptic driver and the text no longer names the light sensor. Free GPIO count, ADC count and sleep current were not found.

Starter ideas

  • Phone-free trail texting

    Type messages on the keyboard with Meshtastic. Friends with their own radios get them with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

  • Group map for a camping trip

    Each device shares its GPS spot over the mesh. You can check where your group is on the screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Screen.

  • Neighborhood emergency messenger

    Keep one charged in a drawer. Send updates to neighbors with their own radios during a storm or power outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Classroom radio range lab

    Students carry devices around the school and note where messages arrive. The class maps radio reach on paper.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

  • Sunlight-friendly hiking companion

    The e-paper screen stays easy to read in bright sun. Check your mesh messages and GPS spot on a summit.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Pocket field notebook

    Write a small app for logging bird or plant sightings. Each note saves with a GPS spot to the memory card.

    Uses: GPS, Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Hiking breadcrumb trail

    Log your GPS track to the memory card on a hike. Load it on a computer later to see your route.

    Uses: GPS, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • E-paper reading slate

    Load short stories or study notes onto the memory card. Page through them on the paper-like screen for hours.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • Step and motion diary

    Use the built-in motion sensor to count steps on walks. The screen shows a daily tally.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Wi-Fi weather and tide board

    Pull the local forecast over Wi-Fi and draw it on the e-paper screen. It can sit on a shelf for days on one charge.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen, Battery ready.

  • Garden sensor reader

    Pair it with a second LoRa board in the yard. The screen shows soil moisture from the back porch.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: second LoRa board, soil moisture sensor.

  • Plug-in air quality checker

    Clip a small sensor onto the Qwiic port. The screen shows temperature, humidity, or air quality readings.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: Qwiic temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Handheld text adventure game

    Code a story game where players type commands. The keyboard and screen make it feel like a tiny retro computer.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Voice memo recorder

    The voice version can record short notes with its microphone. Play them back through headphones.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microSD card, headphones.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only

LilyGO shares schematic PDFs for each hardware version under the GPL-3.0 license. No board layout files are posted. Meshtastic and the LilyGO code are open under GPL-3.0. The ESP32-S3 chip, its wireless drivers and the 4G modem firmware stay closed.
