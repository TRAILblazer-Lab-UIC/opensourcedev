Overview

The LilyGO T-Echo is a small handheld radio with an e-paper screen, GPS and an 850 mAh battery in a case. A low-power Nordic chip and the paper-like screen help the battery last a long time. It is a favorite Meshtastic and SoftRF node for hikers and pilots. Tinkerers can also program it with Arduino tools.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting off the grid with a phone over Bluetooth
  • Long trips on a single charge
  • Reading the screen in bright sunlight
  • Simple weather sensing with the BME280 version

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi
  • Fast animations, as e-paper redraws slowly
  • Adding many extra sensors, as the case leaves few free pins
  • Every USB-C charger, as some refuse to power it

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF52840 chip runs at 64 MHz and sips power. It is built for small battery gadgets.
  • 256 KB of working memory is enough for messaging and simple screens.
  • The 1.54 inch e-paper screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It keeps its picture with no power at all.
  • The L76K GPS reads several satellite systems. It finds your spot faster under open sky.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows.
  • The 850 mAh battery is small, but the low-power parts stretch it a long way.

Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), USB device
RadiosBluetooth Low Energy, Thread or Zigbee, LoRa, GPS, NFC
ConnectorsUSB-C, Battery plug
Built inscreen, GPS, LoRa radio, buttons, temperature sensor, battery charger
Power5 V over USB-C (4.8 to 5.5 V), or the built-in 3.7 V 850 mAh battery

RAM, flash and clock are nRF52840 chip values. The board adds a 2 MB MX25R1635F QSPI flash, confirmed in the schematic text. LilyGO's store lists "RAM 2MB", which appears to confuse it with that flash. Interfaces listed are the chip buses used on the board (SPI for e-paper and LoRa, UART for GNSS, I2C for RTC and sensors, native USB). The chip also has I2S, PDM and QSPI. The temperature entry applies only to the BME280 version. The battery uses an MX 1.25 mm connector. The radio uses an HPD16A module footprint in the schematic, listed as SX1262 by LilyGO and Meshtastic. NFC is supported by the chip and NFC nets appear in the schematic. LilyGO says a USB-A to USB-C cable is needed. Some USB-C to USB-C chargers refuse to power it.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid hiking messenger

    Pair the T-Echo with your phone over Bluetooth. Text your group in the backcountry with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth.

  • Friend finder on a map

    Each node shares its GPS spot over the mesh. The phone app shows where everyone in the group is.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Bluetooth.

  • Family emergency check-in

    Keep a charged node in each home. Everyone can post a quick status during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • School radio range lesson

    Students carry nodes across the playground and record where messages arrive. They learn how radio waves travel.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

  • Backpack weather station

    Get the BME280 version and share temperature, humidity, and air pressure with your group. Watch for a pressure drop before a storm.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Sensors built in.

  • Community event message relay

    Place nodes around a fair or farmers market. Volunteers share updates across a field with no Wi-Fi.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth.

Builder ideas

  • Garden climate log

    Leave the BME280 version on a porch. It sends temperature and humidity readings to a node inside.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: USB power adapter.

  • Glider traffic badge

    Flash the SoftRF Badge firmware. It shows nearby gliders and light aircraft that run the same system.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Screen.

  • Paper-screen trail clock

    Write a sketch that shows time, date, and GPS position on the e-paper screen. It sips power on long trips.

    Uses: GPS, Screen, Battery ready.

  • Bird count tally

    Use the buttons to count birds by type on a walk. The screen keeps the totals visible all day.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Coverage mapping walks

    Log GPS spots and signal strength as you walk a route. You get a map of where your mesh reaches.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS.

  • Custom e-paper name badge

    Draw your name and a pixel art animal on the screen for a meetup. Add a message feed from the mesh below it.

    Uses: Screen, LoRa long range radio.

Expert ideas

  • Smart home button over Thread

    Program the nRF52840 to join a Thread or Zigbee network. The touch button can then switch a lamp.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee.
