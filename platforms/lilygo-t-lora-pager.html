Overview

The LilyGO T-Lora Pager is a landscape handheld with a QWERTY keyboard, a rotary dial and a 2.33 inch color screen. It packs a LoRa radio, GPS, an NFC reader, a motion sensor, a speaker, and a 1500 mAh battery. People run Meshtastic or MeshCore on it to text with no cell network. Makers program it in Arduino or ESP-IDF with the LilyGoLib library.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sending texts off the grid with no phone
  • Sharing GPS spots with a group
  • Audio and buzz feedback projects with the speaker and haptics
  • Building pocket apps with a keyboard and dial

Not great for

  • Adding your own hardware, as only four pins are free
  • Long trips with no charger, as users report about a day of battery life
  • Tight budgets, as US stores charge much more than LilyGO
  • Brand new coders who want to write their own firmware on day one

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It handles the screen, radio, sound, and keyboard at once.
  • 16 MB of storage and 8 MB of extra memory leave room for fonts, sounds and message history.
  • The 2.33 inch screen is wide and short, about the shape of a stick of gum laid flat. It shows 480 by 222 dots.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows.
  • The 1500 mAh battery is a bit smaller than a typical phone battery.
  • The device is about 106 by 89 mm, close to the size of a drink coaster.

Memory, chips, display, battery and power figures come from the LilyGoLib hardware page and the schematic. Size is from the LilyGO store and CNX. The gpio value counts the free pins on the 12-pin expansion socket: GPIO9, GPIO43, GPIO44 and XL9555 GPIO11. I2C and SPI also appear on that socket but are shared with onboard parts. Deep sleep draws 530 uA, light sleep about 2.26 mA, and power off 26 uA, per LilyGoLib. The USB-C port can also power external devices (4.55 to 5.55 V, up to 1 A). CNX lists an RC01812 codec and magnetic wireless charging. LilyGoLib and the schematic name the ES8311, and the wireless charging claim was not confirmed elsewhere. The 1500 mAh battery figure is from LilyGoLib. The LilyGO store text does not give a capacity.

Starter ideas

  • Phone-free trail texting

    Type messages on the keyboard with Meshtastic or MeshCore. Friends with their own radios get them with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

  • Group map for a camping trip

    Each device shares its GPS spot over the mesh. You can check where your group is on the screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS, Screen.

  • Neighborhood emergency messenger

    Keep one charged on a shelf. Send updates to neighbors with their own radios during a storm or power outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Festival meetup radio

    Hand a few to friends at a big outdoor event. Text the group to meet up with busy cell towers.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Classroom radio range lab

    Students carry devices around the school and note where messages arrive. The class maps radio reach on paper.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

Builder ideas

  • Pocket field notebook

    Write a small app for logging bird or plant sightings. Each note saves with a GPS spot to the memory card.

    Uses: GPS, Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Hiking breadcrumb trail

    Log your GPS track to the memory card on a hike. Load it on a computer later to see your route.

    Uses: GPS, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • NFC tag scavenger hunt

    Hide your own NFC stickers around a park or classroom. Players tap the pager on each tag to collect a clue.

    Uses: Screen. Add: NFC sticker tags.

  • Voice memo recorder

    Record short notes with the built-in microphone to the memory card. Play them back on the speaker.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microSD card.

  • Pocket music sequencer

    Map keys to notes and use the dial to change the tempo. Loop a beat through the speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

  • Handheld text adventure game

    Code a story game where players type commands. The keyboard and color screen make it feel like a tiny retro computer.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Wi-Fi weather dashboard

    Pull the local forecast over Wi-Fi and draw it on the color screen. It makes a handy desk display.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Shake and tilt game

    Use the built-in motion sensor to steer a ball through a maze. The pager buzzes at each wall.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Garden sensor reader

    Pair it with a second LoRa board in the yard. The screen shows soil moisture from the back porch.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: second LoRa board, soil moisture sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Talking radio alerts

    Play a spoken clip on the speaker as a mesh alert arrives. It helps people who cannot glance at the screen.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Makes sound. Add: microSD card.

How open is it?

Open Board

LilyGO shares the schematic PDF and a full pin map in the LilyGoLib repo under the MIT license. No board layout files are posted. Meshtastic is open under GPL-3.0. The ESP32-S3 chip and its wireless drivers stay closed.

Hardware license: MIT (repo-wide LilyGoLib license covering the schematic PDF) · Software license: MIT (LilyGoLib). Meshtastic firmware is GPL-3.0.
