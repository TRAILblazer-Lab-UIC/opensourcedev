LILYGO T-Watch S3

LILYGO (Xinyuan)

A programmable smartwatch with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a LoRa radio inside.

Overview

The T-Watch S3 is a wrist computer from LILYGO built on the ESP32-S3 chip. It packs Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a LoRa long-range radio into a small case with a touchscreen. Beginners can flash Meshtastic and start texting over LoRa with no code. Makers can also write their own apps in Arduino or ESP-IDF.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Off-grid text messages with Meshtastic
  • Custom watch faces and small touch apps
  • Sound projects with the microphone and speaker
  • Wi-Fi gadgets such as weather displays and home buttons
  • Learning to code a wearable with lots of parts inside

Not great for

  • Adding extra sensors by wire. Every chip pin is already in use.
  • Maps and location. The base model has no GPS, but the newer S3 Plus adds it.
  • Long battery life with Meshtastic. Users report heavy power use and crashes on this model.
  • Fuss-free Arduino setup. Older library versions cause build errors with newer tools.

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 240 MHz give the watch enough speed for a smooth touch screen and a radio at once.
  • 8 MB of extra working memory holds pictures and sound clips for apps.
  • 16 MB of storage fits many programs, fonts, and images.
  • The 1.54 inch screen is about the size of a large postage stamp.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages much farther than Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. Buy the band that matches your country.
  • Deep sleep draws very little power, and the watch wakes with a button press.

All ESP32-S3 pins drive internal parts, and the hardware doc marks none as free. The low_power_ua value is deep sleep with button wake, from the LilyGoLib hardware doc, and power off with only backup power is 50 uA. The LilyGoLib doc lists a 470 mAh battery. By contrast, CNX Software and Meshtastic list 400 mAh. Later batches use a BMA456 because the BMA423 is being discontinued. The micro-USB port charges and programs the watch but cannot power external devices. The size is the watch face size from CNX Software.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wrist messenger off the grid

    Flash Meshtastic and pair the watch with your phone. You can text friends who carry their own radios with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

  • Family hike check-ins

    Give each hiker a Meshtastic radio on the licence-free band for your country. Short messages pop up on everyone's wrist.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen.

  • Storm outage notes for neighbors

    Keep the watch charged for power cuts. Neighbors with their own Meshtastic radios can swap updates on the local mesh.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready.

  • Classroom radio range lab

    Students walk the school grounds with their own radios and note where messages arrive. Check your local radio rules first.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

Builder ideas

  • Custom watch face

    Start from a LilyGoLib example and draw your own clock on the touchscreen. Add the date and battery level.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Step counter for gym class

    Use the accelerometer to count steps during the day. The screen shows a simple goal bar.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Wrist TV remote

    Program the built-in infrared transmitter to send TV codes. Your watch changes channels and volume.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Voice memo recorder

    Record short notes with the microphone and play them back on the speaker. It works for shopping lists or ideas on the go.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Pocket music box

    Tap notes on the touchscreen and play them through the speaker. Save a short tune and loop it.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

  • Weather on your wrist

    Fetch the local forecast over Wi-Fi each hour. The screen shows temperature and an icon for rain or sun.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Smart home buttons

    Put touch buttons on the screen that send Wi-Fi messages to your home lights. A tap turns the lamp on.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Clap counter science lab

    Measure sound levels with the microphone during a class experiment. Students compare loud and quiet spots.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Reaction time game

    Flash a target on the screen at random times. Players tap fast and the watch shows their score.

    Uses: Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Garden sensor alerts

    Pair the watch with your own LoRa soil sensor in a far corner of the garden. The haptic motor buzzes for dry soil.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: LoRa board, soil moisture sensor.

  • Bluetooth game controller

    Tilt the watch to steer a game on a computer. Motion data travels over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Wildlife sighting pager

    Volunteers on a nature count send short sightings to a base radio over LoRa. Use your own radios on a licence-free band.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen.

How open is it?

Open Board

LILYGO publishes the schematic as a PDF and MIT licensed libraries and examples. No board layout files are published, and the MIT license may not cover the hardware design. The ESP32-S3 Wi-Fi and Bluetooth firmware are closed binary blobs.
