Overview

The LilyGO T3-S3 is a small ESP32-S3 board with a LoRa radio, a 0.96 inch screen and a microSD slot. Buyers choose the radio module, from 433 MHz up to 2.4 GHz. It is one of the cheapest ways to build a Meshtastic node. Makers also use it for range tests and sensor gateways.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A low-cost Meshtastic node paired with your phone
  • Home and garden LoRa sensor projects
  • Radio range experiments in class or outdoors
  • Solar-powered nodes on the V1.3 board

Not great for

  • Texting with no phone, as it has no keyboard
  • Big programs, as it has only 4 MB of storage
  • Rough weather out of the box, as it comes without a case
  • Unprotected batteries, as the board has no battery protection circuit

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 runs at 240 MHz with two cores. It can read sensors and run the radio at the same time.
  • 4 MB of storage and 2 MB of extra memory fit Meshtastic and small sensor programs.
  • The 0.96 inch screen is about the size of a fingernail. It shows short lines of text.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles across open ground. Pick the band your country allows.
  • The board is about 66 by 36 mm, close to the size of a pack of gum.

Memory, OLED and connector values come from the LilyGO store data, the LilyGo-LoRa-Series docs and the LilyGO wiki. Size is from the wiki. The radio module varies by order. The SX1262 version covers 400 to 520 MHz and 830 to 945 MHz. The SX1280 versions cover 2.4 GHz. Free pins are the Qwiic UART pins (GPIO43, GPIO44) and GPIO10 and GPIO21, which are tied to radio DIO3 and DIO4 until two resistors are removed. Other pins are on the headers, but the docs give no free-pin count. The V1.3 schematic shows a CN3065 charger, which accepts solar input. V1.2 and V1.3 share the same pin map. The board has no battery protection circuit. As a result, use a protected cell. Sleep current was not found.

  • Soil moisture sender

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor, second LoRa board.

  • Mailbox alert

    Put a board with a door switch in your mailbox. It sends a LoRa ping to a board in the house each time the door opens.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, second LoRa board, LiPo battery with protection.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

LilyGO shares schematic PDFs for several versions and an outline drawing of V1.3. The repo has no license, and no board layout files are posted. The code builds with open tools such as Arduino-ESP32, PlatformIO and RadioLib. The ESP32-S3 chip and its wireless drivers stay closed.
