LilyPad Arduino USB

A round, sewable Arduino board for light-up clothes and soft circuits.

Overview

The LilyPad Arduino USB is a round, flat Arduino board you can sew into fabric. Big petal pads take conductive thread in place of wires. It plugs straight into a computer over micro-USB and can pretend to be a keyboard, mouse or MIDI instrument. It suits students, costume makers and teachers who love soft circuits.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Light-up clothing, badges and costumes
  • Teaching circuits with needle and thread
  • Soft USB keyboards and MIDI controllers
  • Battery-powered wearables with its built-in charger

Not great for

  • Wireless projects. The board has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
  • Big programs. Memory is very small.
  • 5 V sensors. The board runs at 3.3 V and higher voltages can damage it.
  • Long-term projects. Arduino lists the board as retired.

Specs in plain words

  • 32 KB of program space fits a simple sketch with a few lights and sensors.
  • 2.5 KB of working memory is room for a few hundred numbers at once.
  • 8 MHz is plenty fast for blinking lights and reading buttons.
  • Nine sew pads connect to lights, buttons and sensors, and four of them can dim LEDs smoothly.
  • The board is 50 mm across, about the size of a cookie, and very thin.
  • A socket and charger let a small LiPo battery power it and recharge over USB.
Full spec sheet
Processor8-bit AVR (ATmega32U4), 1 core at 8 MHz
Memory2.5 KB
Storage32 KB
Pins9 pins, 4 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), USB device
ConnectorsMicro USB, Battery plug
Built inbattery charger
Power3.8 to 5 V over micro-USB or a 3.7 V LiPo
Size50 diameter mm

Specs are from the SparkFun product page. The bootloader uses 4 KB of the 32 KB flash. Make reported 16 MHz and five digital pins in a 2017 review, but SparkFun lists 8 MHz and nine digital I/O. The board runs at 3.3 V, and 5 V on the pins can damage it. I2C, SPI and UART come from the chip, but only some of those pins reach the sew pads. No sleep current is published for the board.

How open is it?

Wild Open

SparkFun publishes the full design files, Gerbers and bootloader on GitHub. The board is OSHWA certified as US000984 and uses CC BY-SA 3.0 or CERN hardware licenses. The code tools are open, but the ATmega32U4 chip is a closed Microchip design.

Hardware license: CC BY-SA 3.0 (repo README), CERN (OSHWA record) · Software license: MIT (OSHWA record), modified beerware (Caterina bootloader in the repo)
