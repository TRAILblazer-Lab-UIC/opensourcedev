Overview

The LimeSDR Mini 2.0 is a software defined radio from Lime Microsystems. It plugs into a computer over USB 3.0 and turns it into a radio lab. It can listen across a wide range from 10 MHz to 3.5 GHz and can transmit as well. It suits students, radio hobbyists, and teachers who want open board files and an open chip design flow.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Receiving public signals such as FM radio, airplane messages, and weather satellites
  • Teaching radio and signals with GNU Radio over a cable loopback
  • Licensed amateur radio experiments
  • Studying open board files and an open FPGA build flow
  • Python and SoapySDR signal projects

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as the price has climbed well above its 399 USD launch price
  • Two-channel projects, as it has one transmit and one receive path
  • Quick plug and play use, as it needs drivers and SDR apps first
  • Casual transmitting, which needs a license in most bands

Specs in plain words

  • 10 MHz to 3.5 GHz covers FM radio, airplane signals, home weather sensors, and the 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi band. Some gaps exist in the coverage.
  • Up to 40 MHz of bandwidth lets it see a slice of the airwaves about as wide as a few Wi-Fi channels at once.
  • 12-bit samples capture weak and strong signals with good detail.
  • Full duplex means it can transmit and receive at the same moment.
  • The ECP5 chip is a reprogrammable logic chip with 44 thousand building blocks for fast signal math.
  • It draws at most 4.5 watts from the USB port, which can make it warm.

The flash is 128 Mb for FPGA configuration per the myriadrf docs. CNX Software listed 4 MB at launch. As a result, the docs value is used. Also 2 x 128 Kb optional EEPROMs. FPGA block RAM is 1944 Kb plus 351 Kb distributed RAM. No external RAM is listed. As a result, ram_kb is null. The board also supports LFE5U-25F and LFE5U-85F parts. The FPGA GPIO and JTAG headers ship unpopulated. As a result, swd_jtag means FPGA JTAG. RF connectors are SMA female for TX and RX, plus U.FL clock in and out and a fan connector. There are 3 dual-colour LEDs and an LM75 temperature sensor. No radio vocabulary value fits a general SDR. As a result, radios is empty. Frequency range is quoted as 10 MHz to 3.5 GHz on the Lime product page, 10 MHz to 2.6 GHz with non-continuous coverage on the myriadrf v2.4 docs, and up to 3.8 GHz in Lime marketing text. Bandwidth is 40 MHz on the Lime page and 30.72 MHz on the myriadrf docs. Rx gain is 89 dB combined. Weight is about 25 g.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Listen to FM radio on a laptop

    Tune across the FM broadcast band in an SDR app. Hear local stations and see each one as a bright stripe on screen.

    Add: telescopic antenna.

  • See the radio crowd at home

    Sweep the 2.4 GHz band and draw a live picture of how busy it is. Pick a quieter channel for your own Wi-Fi.

    Add: 2.4 GHz antenna.

  • Spot noisy home gadgets

    Scan the bands near your desk as you switch devices on and off. Find the charger or lamp that leaks radio noise.

    Add: telescopic antenna.

  • Eavesdrop on ham radio chats

    Tune to the amateur radio bands and listen to licensed operators talk. Learn how contacts work before you study for a license.

    Add: wideband antenna.

Builder ideas

  • Watch airplanes overhead

    Tune to 1090 MHz and decode the public position messages that airliners send out. Plot nearby flights on a map.

    Add: 1090 MHz antenna.

  • Read your own weather station

    Pick up the 433 MHz signals from your own outdoor weather sensor. Log the temperature and humidity on your computer.

    Add: 433 MHz antenna.

  • Catch weather satellite pictures

    Listen near 137 MHz as a weather satellite passes over. Turn its signal into a cloud picture of your region.

    Add: V-dipole antenna.

  • Safe cable loopback lab

    Join the transmit and receive jacks with a cable and an attenuator. Send a test tone down the wire with nothing going on the air.

    Add: SMA cable, SMA attenuator.

  • Teach modulation in class

    Build AM, FM, and digital signals in GNU Radio over the cable loopback. Students watch each wave shape change on screen.

    Add: SMA cable, SMA attenuator.

  • Python signal explorer

    Pull raw samples into Python through SoapySDR. Plot the spectrum and try your own filters.

  • Test your homemade antennas

    Compare antennas you built by watching the strength of a known local broadcast. Keep the design that hears best.

    Add: homemade antennas, SMA adapters.

  • Keep an eye on board heat

    Read the onboard temperature sensor during long listening sessions. Add a small fan once the numbers climb.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: small 5 volt fan.

Expert ideas

  • Radio astronomy hydrogen hunt

    Listen near 1420 MHz for the faint hiss of hydrogen gas in the Milky Way. A dish or horn antenna points at the sky.

    Add: horn or dish antenna, low noise amplifier.

  • Licensed ham radio station

    Earn an amateur radio license first. Then send and receive on your licensed bands with low power and your call sign.

    Add: antenna for your band, band filter.

  • Custom FPGA signal blocks

    Build your own gateware with the open Yosys and nextpnr tools. Move heavy signal math from the laptop onto the board.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The board files are open under the Solderpad Hardware License v2.1, with schematics, Gerbers, and parts lists. The LimeSuiteNG driver and the gateware use the Apache-2.0 license. The newer gateware builds with open tools such as Yosys and nextpnr. The radio chip and the FPGA chip stay closed.
