Luxonis OAK 4

Luxonis

A rugged AI camera that runs Linux and sees depth on its own.

Field marks: Look for a weatherproof brick with three lenses, a 48 TOPS chip, and an M12 network plug, priced closer to a used car payment than a webcam.

Overview

The OAK 4 is a standalone AI camera line from Luxonis. Each camera is a full Linux computer with a Qualcomm chip. Apps run on the camera with no host PC. The OAK 4 D sees depth with two extra cameras, and the OAK 4 S has the color camera only. It suits robot builders, factory teams, and schools with a big budget.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Robots that need to judge distance
  • Spotting and tracking objects in live video
  • Outdoor and factory use with its IP67 case
  • Python coders who want AI vision without a separate computer
  • Multi-camera rigs built on the open FFC carrier

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as prices start near 849 USD
  • Beginners with no Python experience
  • Battery projects, given its 25 W peak power draw
  • Open hardware fans, as the cameras have no published design files

Specs in plain words

  • 48 TOPS of AI power lets it spot many objects in live video at once.
  • 8 GB of memory and 128 GB of storage make it a real computer inside a camera.
  • The 48 MP color camera captures fine detail, enough to read small labels.
  • Two global-shutter cameras measure depth, much like a pair of eyes judging distance.
  • PoE means one network cable carries both power and data.
  • The OAK 4 D weighs about 674 grams, a bit more than a can of soup.

ai_tops is the 48 INT8 TOPS figure from the Luxonis RVC4 docs. CNX Software quotes 52 TOPS combined. The GPU adds 4 FP16 TOPS. CPU core layout, RAM, flash, IP67 rating and size come from CNX Software. The Luxonis RVC4 page gives 6 ARMv8 cores, which conflicts with the 8-core QCS8550 layout in CNX. Size and weight (674 g) are for the OAK 4 D and D Pro. The OAK 4 S is 71 x 58 x 58 mm and 325 g. The M8 aux connector carries USB 2.0 host, 5 V at up to 5 W, and pins for GPIO, UART and FSYNC. The M12 Ethernet and M8 aux connectors do not map to the allowed connector list. mipi_csi applies to the OAK 4 SoM and FFC carrier, which expose five camera ports. Power figures from Luxonis docs: 1.6 W idle, 10 to 15 W average, 25 W peak.

The DepthAI core library is open under the MIT license. Luxonis also shares full Altium files for two OAK 4 carrier boards under MIT. The cameras, the SoM, Luxonis OS, and the Qualcomm AI stack stay closed.
