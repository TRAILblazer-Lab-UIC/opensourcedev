Overview

The AtomS3R-CAM is a camera module from M5Stack about the size of a sugar cube. It runs on an ESP32-S3 chip with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth LE, and 8 MB of extra memory. It also packs a motion sensor, a compass, and an infrared LED. Out of the box it works as a plug-in USB webcam.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Tiny Wi-Fi cameras for pets, plants, and bird feeders
  • QR code readers and simple robot eyes
  • Voice and vision chatbots with the Atomic Echo Base
  • Makers who like snap-together M5Stack parts

Not great for

  • Sharp photos, as the camera is only 0.3 megapixels
  • Projects that need many wires, with only six pins plus a Grove port
  • Recording sound on its own, as the board has no microphone
  • Saving lots of photos, with no microSD slot listed

Specs in plain words

  • The 240 MHz dual-core chip is quick for a camera this small.
  • 8 MB of PSRAM holds a few camera frames and a small vision model.
  • The VGA camera captures 640 by 480 pictures at up to 30 frames per second.
  • The 57.6 degree view is a bit narrower than a phone camera.
  • Deep sleep draws about 11 microamps, which lets it nap for a long time on a battery.
  • At 7.4 grams it weighs about as much as two quarters.

gpio counts the six bottom header pins (G5, G6, G7, G8, G38, G39) plus G1 and G2 on the Grove port. low_power_ua is the 11.63 uA sleep current measured with 5 V on the GPIO header. Sleep current is 92.5 uA when powered over USB. The onboard list has no magnetometer or IR value. The board has a BMM150 magnetometer and an IR LED on G47. CNX Software lists a user LED on GPIO18. The docs page does not say whether it is RGB. As a result, rgb_led may be wrong. No microphone is on the board. The AI Chatbot kit adds one through the Atomic Echo Base.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Instant desk webcam

    Plug it into a laptop with a USB-C cable. The factory firmware makes it show up as a webcam for video calls.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Wi-Fi peek camera

    Stream live video to a phone over Wi-Fi. Check on a pet or a sleeping baby from the next room.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi.

  • Tilt-triggered photo

    Use the motion sensor to snap a photo each time the cube is shaken or flipped. It makes a fun party photo booth.

    Uses: Camera, Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • QR code door reader

    Point the camera at a QR code and read it. Use it to check in club members at the door.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Bird feeder snapshot

    Take a photo every few minutes and send it over Wi-Fi. Build a picture log of feeder visitors.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: weatherproof box, USB power bank.

  • Robot eyes

    Mount the cube on a small robot and send what it sees over Wi-Fi. Drive the robot from a laptop by watching the video.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: small robot car kit.

  • TV remote helper

    Send TV remote codes from the IR LED. Turn the TV off with a button press or on a timer.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Talking vision chatbot

    Add the Atomic Echo Base and flash the XiaoZhi firmware. Ask questions out loud and hear spoken answers.

    Uses: Camera, Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi. Add: Atomic Echo Base.

  • Plant growth time-lapse

    Snap a photo of a seedling every hour and save it to a computer. Stitch the photos into a growth video for class.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power supply.

  • Color sorting game

    Have the camera name the color of a card held in front of it. Kids race to show the right color first.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Compass heading logger

    Read the compass and motion sensor together. Track which way a model boat or kite points over time.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Grove sensor station

    Plug a temperature sensor into the Grove port. Send readings and a photo to a phone over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Tiny object spotter

    Run a small vision model on the 8 MB of extra memory. Light an LED when it spots a chosen object.

    Uses: Camera. Add: LED.

  • Battery trail camera

    Put the cube in deep sleep and wake it on a timer to take a picture. A small power bank lasts much longer this way.

    Uses: Camera, Sips power, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power bank, weatherproof box.

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

M5Stack posts schematic PDFs for the main board, but they carry no license. The enclosure STL file sits in an MIT-licensed repo. The coding tools are open, including MIT-licensed M5Unified and Apache-2.0 ESP-IDF. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth stacks inside the ESP32-S3 stay closed.
