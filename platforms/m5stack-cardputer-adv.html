Overview

The Cardputer-Adv is a credit-card sized pocket computer from M5Stack. It packs a 56-key keyboard, a small color screen, a speaker, a microphone, and a rechargeable battery around an ESP32-S3 chip. Beginners can code it with blocks in a web browser. Tinkerers love it for games, notes, radio messaging, and swappable community firmware.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A first gadget that needs no wiring at all
  • Typing, note taking, and text games on the go
  • Off-grid messaging with an add-on LoRa radio cap
  • Sound projects with the speaker, microphone, and headphone jack
  • Trying many community firmware apps from one microSD card

Not great for

  • Big graphics or long audio clips. The chip has no extra PSRAM memory.
  • Camera projects. There is no camera connector.
  • Fast typing of long documents on the tiny keys
  • Projects that need lots of free pins for wiring

Specs in plain words

  • The two-core 240 MHz processor runs small games and apps smoothly.
  • 512 KB of working memory is enough for simple apps and sensor readings. Heavier firmware may not fit.
  • 8 MB of storage holds your programs, and a microSD card adds room for files.
  • The 1.14 inch screen shows about eight short lines of text in color.
  • The 1500 mAh battery runs the unit for several hours of use.
  • The case is 84 by 54 millimeters and weighs 81 grams, about the size of a thick credit card.

Values come from the official docs page. The ESP32-S3FN8 has 8 MB in-package flash and no PSRAM. The docs list no PSRAM. The 512 KB SRAM figure is the ESP32-S3 chip value (CNX Software also lists it). The keyboard counts as buttons here. There is also one IR emitter on GPIO44. Expansion is a HY2.0-4P Grove port and a 2.54 mm 14-pin EXT header. The docs do not give a free GPIO count. Measured draw at 4.2 V is 120 mA idle, 132 mA with Wi-Fi and 155 mA with BLE. Standby with the switch off is 0.23 uA. Weight is 81 g.

How open is it?

Open Board

M5Stack publishes schematic PDFs for the Cardputer-Adv and its Stamp-S3A module, but no board layout or license comes with them. The M5Unified library, the factory demo, and the UiFlow2 MicroPython firmware are open under the MIT license. The ESP32-S3 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code stay closed.

Hardware license: No license stated on the schematic PDFs, the M5_Hardware repo is MIT but holds only an enclosure STL for this product · Software license: MIT (M5Unified, M5Cardputer-UserDemo, uiflow-micropython), Apache-2.0 ESP-IDF
