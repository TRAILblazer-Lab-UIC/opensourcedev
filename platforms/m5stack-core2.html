Overview

The Core2 is a 54 millimeter square computer from M5Stack with a 2 inch touchscreen. A battery, speaker, microphone, motion sensor, clock chip and vibration motor all sit inside the case. Teachers like it for UiFlow block coding, and makers use it for home dashboards. Add-on modules stack under it like building blocks.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classroom coding with blocks in a web browser
  • Home control panels and desk dashboards
  • Sensor projects with plug-in Grove parts
  • Simple sound and touch games
  • Stacking extra modules with no soldering

Not great for

  • Camera or vision projects. There is no camera.
  • Battery gadgets that must run for weeks
  • Beginners following old tutorials. A newer screen chip needs updated libraries.
  • Projects that need many bare pins on a breadboard

Specs in plain words

  • The two-core 240 MHz processor handles a touchscreen app and Wi-Fi together.
  • 8 MB of extra PSRAM memory holds screen images and longer sound clips.
  • 16 MB of storage holds your programs, and a microSD card adds room for data.
  • The 2 inch touchscreen has 320 by 240 dots, about the size of a postage stamp pair.
  • The 500 mAh battery keeps it running for a short while away from a cable.
  • The case is 54 millimeters on each side, about the size of a matchbox.

Size54 x 54 x 16.5 mm

Values are for the current Core2 v1.3 (K010-V13) from the official docs. USB-C goes through a CH9102F USB-serial bridge (CP2104 on early batches). As a result, there is no native USB device mode. Headers means the 30-pin M5-Bus socket on the back. The docs list PORT.A (I2C), PORT.B (DAC/ADC) and PORT.C (UART) pinouts. The touch panel is an FT6336U. The LCD controller changed from ILI9342C to ILI9342E on 2026-08-07. Onboard extras outside the vocab are a vibration motor, a BM8563 RTC and a green power LED. v1.3 weighs 58.8 g. The original Core2 weighs 54.9 g.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Desk clock with weather

    Show the time and the local forecast on the touchscreen. Wi-Fi keeps both up to date.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Shake counter game

    Shake the Core2 as fast as you can for ten seconds. The motion sensor counts each shake and shows a score.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Tilt maze game

    Roll a ball through a maze on the screen by tilting the case. Students learn how motion sensors work.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Study timer with a buzz

    Tap the screen to start a focus timer. The case buzzes gently at break time.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Plant moisture monitor

    Plug a soil moisture sensor into the Grove port. A happy or thirsty face appears on the screen.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Smart home touch panel

    Mount the Core2 on a wall as a touch panel. Its buttons switch lights and plugs over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Classroom noise meter

    The built-in microphone measures how loud the room is. The screen turns from green to red as noise rises.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Touch drum pad

    Draw four drum pads on the touchscreen. Each tap plays a different beat through the speaker.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Doorbell chime and alert

    Wire a button at the door to a second board on Wi-Fi. The Core2 plays a chime and shows who rang.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound. Add: push button, second Wi-Fi board.

  • Greenhouse data logger

    Read temperature and humidity every few minutes. Save the readings to a microSD card and graph them on the screen.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, microSD card.

  • Pet feeder with a schedule

    Connect a servo motor to open a food flap at set times. The clock chip keeps the schedule on track.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: servo motor, food container.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Point a motion sensor at a feeder. The Core2 counts each visit and shows the busiest hour.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: motion sensor.

  • Air quality badge

    Add an air quality sensor to a Grove port and carry it around on battery. The screen shows how fresh the air is in each room.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen, Battery ready. Add: air quality sensor.

  • Community event check-in

    Guests tap their name on the touchscreen to sign in. The list goes to a shared sheet over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi.

Expert ideas

  • Tilt game controller

    Pair the Core2 with a computer over Bluetooth. Tilting the case steers a car or plane in a game.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Cloud weather station

    Send sensor readings to a cloud service over Wi-Fi. Friends can check the garden weather from anywhere.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

How open is it?

Open Board

M5Stack publishes schematic PDFs for the Core2, but no board layout or license comes with them. The M5Unified and M5Core2 libraries and the UiFlow MicroPython firmware are open under the MIT license. The ESP32 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code stay closed.

Hardware license: No license stated on the schematic PDFs, the M5_Hardware repo is MIT but holds only enclosure files for the Core2 · Software license: MIT (M5Unified, M5Core2, uiflow-micropython), Apache-2.0 ESP-IDF
