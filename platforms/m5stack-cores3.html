Overview

The CoreS3 is a 54 millimeter square computer from M5Stack and the newer sibling of the Core2. It adds a small camera, two microphones, a compass sensor, and a proximity and light sensor to a 2 inch touchscreen. Beginners can code it with UiFlow2 blocks in a web browser. Makers use it for camera demos, voice tricks, and home dashboards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Simple camera and AI demos in a ready-made case
  • Classroom coding with blocks
  • Sensor experiments with no wiring
  • Smart home panels and workshop displays
  • Stacking add-on modules under the case

Not great for

  • Sharp photos. The camera has only 0.3 megapixels.
  • Big AI models such as chatbots
  • Compass projects near magnets or stacked bases
  • Long battery life. The base holds a 500 mAh battery.

Specs in plain words

  • The two-core 240 MHz processor has extra math tricks for small AI models.
  • 8 MB of extra PSRAM memory holds camera frames and sound buffers.
  • 16 MB of storage holds programs, and a microSD card up to 16 GB adds room for files.
  • The 0.3 megapixel camera takes small pictures, about the size of an old phone photo.
  • The 2 inch touchscreen has 320 by 240 dots for buttons, charts and faces.
  • The DinBase accepts 9 to 24 volts. It can run from a workshop power supply.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorXtensa LX7 (ESP32-S3), 2 cores at 240 MHz
Memory512 KB plus 8 MB extra (PSRAM)
Storage16 MB
AI chipESP32-S3 vector instructions for DSP and small neural networks
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), Digital audio (I2S), USB device, USB host, Camera port (parallel)
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsUSB-C, Grove, Pin headers, microSD slot
Built inscreen, camera, microphone, speaker, motion sensor, light sensor, buttons, battery charger
Power5 V over USB-C, or 9 to 24 V DC through the DinBase DC5521 jack
Size54 x 54 x 15.5 mm

Values are for the full CoreS3 kit (K128) from the official docs. The 512 KB SRAM is the ESP32-S3 chip value from CNX Software. The docs call the ports PORT.A, PORT.B and PORT.C (HY2.0-4P), which are Grove-compatible. Headers means the 30-pin M5-Bus. The microSD slot supports cards up to 16 GB per the docs. The 500 mAh battery sits in the DinBase. With the DinBase the set measures 69 x 54 x 31.5 mm and weighs 72.7 g. The CoreS3 Lite has the same sensors with a 200 mAh battery and a cover. The CoreS3 SE drops the camera, IMU, magnetometer, proximity sensor and battery. The LCD controller changed from ILI9342C to ILI9342E on 2026-08-07.

How open is it?

Open Board

M5Stack publishes schematic PDFs for the CoreS3 and its DinBase, but no board layout or license comes with them. The M5Unified and M5CoreS3 libraries and the UiFlow2 MicroPython firmware are open under the MIT license. The ESP32-S3 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code stay closed.

Hardware license: No license stated on the schematic PDFs, the M5_Hardware repo is MIT but holds only enclosure files and an M5-Bus module footprint for the CoreS3 · Software license: MIT (M5Unified, M5CoreS3, uiflow-micropython), Apache-2.0 ESP-IDF
