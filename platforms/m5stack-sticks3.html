Overview

The StickS3 is a pocket gadget from M5Stack built around an ESP32-S3 chip. It packs a small color screen, a speaker, a microphone, a motion sensor, infrared, and a battery into a case about the size of a matchbox. Beginners can code it with blocks in a web browser. It replaces the older M5StickC Plus2 and adds real sound and more memory.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A first gadget with no wiring needed
  • Voice helpers and sound projects with the speaker and microphone
  • Infrared remotes for TVs, fans, and projectors
  • Wearable and pocket projects on battery power
  • Classroom coding with blocks in UiFlow2

Not great for

  • Long battery life. The 250 mAh battery is small.
  • Big wiring projects. Only a handful of pins reach the outside.
  • Camera projects. The stick has no camera connector.
  • Some older Stick hats. A few of them no longer fit.

Specs in plain words

  • The two-core 240 MHz processor runs small apps, games and voice firmware smoothly.
  • 8 MB of extra PSRAM memory gives room for sound clips and screen graphics.
  • 8 MB of storage holds your programs and a few small files.
  • The 1.14 inch screen shows about six short lines of text in color.
  • The one watt speaker is loud enough for alarms, voices and short tunes.
  • The case is 48 by 24 by 15 millimeters and weighs 20 grams, about the weight of four sugar cubes.

ConnectorsUSB-C, Grove, Pin headers

Values come from the official docs page and store page for SKU K150. RAM is the 512 KB on-chip SRAM of the ESP32-S3, per CNX Software. Low power is the 14.02 uA power-off draw at 4.2 V on the store page. L1 is 52.47 uA and L2 is 102.40 uA. Headers means the 16-pin Hat2 bus with G1 to G8, G43, G44, BAT, EXT_5V, 5V_IN and 3V3. The Grove port carries G9 and G10 and can supply up to 0.38 A at 4.88 V in output mode. Onboard extras outside the vocab are an IR transmitter (G46), an IR receiver (G42), a green status LED and the M5PM1 PMIC at I2C 0x6e. The docs list no RTC. USB host refers to the USB-C OTG support named by CNX Software and the Zephyr board page. Weight is 20 g. Operating temperature is 0 to 40 C.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Hears sound, Makes sound.

    Fetch the local forecast over Wi-Fi. The screen shows the temperature and the chance of rain.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Study timer with chimes

    Set a work timer on the screen. The speaker plays a chime at the end of each session.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

How open is it?

Open Board

M5Stack publishes a schematic PDF for the StickS3, but no board layout or license comes with it. The case model sits in an MIT-licensed hardware repo. The M5Unified and M5GFX libraries are open under the MIT license, and the ESP32-S3 chip with its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth code stays closed.

Hardware license: No license stated for the schematic, enclosure STL in the MIT-licensed M5_Hardware repo · Software license: MIT (M5Unified, M5GFX), Apache-2.0 ESP-IDF and Zephyr
