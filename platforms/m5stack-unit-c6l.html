Overview

The Unit C6L is a small boxed LoRa radio from M5Stack. It pairs an ESP32-C6 chip with a Semtech SX1262 long-range radio. A tiny screen, a button, a buzzer, and an RGB LED sit on the case. Meshtastic supports it, which makes it a cheap way to join an off-grid text mesh.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Off-grid text messaging with Meshtastic
  • Testing LoRa range for a school or club project
  • Grove sensor nodes that report over long distances
  • Learning Zephyr or Arduino on a ready-made radio

Not great for

  • Carrying around all day, with no battery or charger inside
  • Location tracking, as it has no GPS
  • US use out of the box, as the included LoRa antenna is labeled 868 MHz
  • Projects that need many wires, with only two free pins on the Grove port

Specs in plain words

  • The SX1262 radio can send small messages over long distances.
  • Up to 22 dBm of power is the most a small LoRa radio usually sends.
  • The ESP32-C6 adds Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth LE, Zigbee, and Thread.
  • 16 MB of flash is plenty of room for Meshtastic and your own code.
  • The 0.66 inch screen shows a few short lines of text.
  • The 62 by 24 mm case is about the size of a pack of gum.

Values come from the M5Stack docs page and the Zephyr board page. ram_kb is the HP-core RAM from the Zephyr board page. The LP core has 15 KB more. gpio counts the two user pins on the Grove port (GPIO4 and GPIO5). The buzzer is listed as speaker because the vocabulary has no buzzer item. low_power_ua is M5Stack's measured whole-unit sleep current of about 697 uA when powered from the Grove port. It is 866 uA from USB-C. LoRa RX standby draws about 85 mA. Weight is 13.6 g without antennas. The unit has no battery or battery charger. The OLED shares SPI with the SX1262, and an I/O expander sits on the only hardware I2C bus.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid text messenger

    Flash Meshtastic and pair the unit with a phone over Bluetooth. Send short texts to friends with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: USB power bank.

  • Camping group check-in

    Give each family member a node on a hike. Everyone can send a quick message across the campsite.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: USB power bank.

  • Neighborhood mesh relay

    Leave one unit powered in a high window. It passes messages between other nodes nearby.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: USB power supply, 915 MHz antenna.

  • Event volunteer pager

    Hand nodes to volunteers at a fair or race. The buzzer and LED signal each new message.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Makes sound, Bluetooth. Add: USB power bank.

Builder ideas

  • School radio science lab

    Place nodes around a school field and test how far messages reach. Students map the range and note what blocks the signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen. Add: USB power bank.

  • Garden soil alert

    Plug a soil sensor into the Grove port. Send a LoRa message to the house when the beds get dry.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Plug-in connectors. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Mailbox ding

    Add a door switch to the Grove port at a far-off mailbox. The node sends a message and beeps the buzzer on the other end.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Plug-in connectors, Makes sound. Add: magnetic door switch, USB power bank.

  • Weather station node

    Read temperature and humidity from a Grove sensor. Show it on the tiny screen and share it over the mesh.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Mesh to Wi-Fi bridge

    Connect one node to home Wi-Fi. Messages from the mesh can reach a computer on the home network.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Wi-Fi. Add: USB power supply.

  • Wildlife gate counter

    Wire a motion sensor to the Grove port at a trail gate. Count passing animals and send a daily total over LoRa.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Plug-in connectors. Add: motion sensor, USB power bank, weatherproof box.

Expert ideas

  • Zigbee smart home bridge

    Write custom firmware to talk to Zigbee or Thread smart plugs. Trigger them from LoRa messages sent across a farm.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, LoRa long range radio. Add: Zigbee smart plug.

  • Zephyr radio playground

    Build your own LoRa firmware with Zephyr. Learn how packets, sleep, and radios work at a low level.

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

M5Stack posts schematic PDFs for the unit and its radio module, but they carry no license. Meshtastic is open under GPL-3.0, and ESP-IDF and Zephyr use Apache-2.0. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth stacks for the ESP32-C6 ship as closed binary files.
