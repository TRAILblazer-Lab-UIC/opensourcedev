Overview

The M5StickC Plus2 is a tiny computer in a plastic case about the size of a lighter. It packs a color screen, a motion sensor, a microphone, a buzzer, an infrared light, and a small battery. Makers like it for wearable gadgets, remotes, and little dashboards. M5Stack has stopped making it and now points buyers to the newer M5StickS3.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wearable gadgets such as watches and step counters
  • Wi-Fi sensor projects with a screen built in
  • Classroom coding with UiFlow blocks
  • Infrared remote tricks for TVs and air conditioners
  • Small projects that run on battery

Not great for

  • Projects with many wires, as it has only a few free pins
  • Long battery life, as the battery holds just 200 mAh
  • Buying new in large numbers, as stock is running out
  • Reusing old StickC Plus code, which does not run on this model

Specs in plain words

  • The dual-core 240 MHz chip can read sensors and talk over Wi-Fi at the same time.
  • 8 MB of storage holds your programs plus pictures and sounds for the screen.
  • The 1.14 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp and shows text, icons and simple charts.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth let it send data to a phone or the internet.
  • The 200 mAh battery runs a simple project for a few hours on one charge.
  • The side port takes plug-in sensors with no soldering.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorXtensa LX6 (ESP32-PICO-V3-02), 2 cores at 240 MHz
Memory520 KB plus 2 MB extra (PSRAM)
Storage8 MB
Ways to connect partsI2C, Serial (UART), Digital audio (I2S), Digital microphone (PDM)
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy, Bluetooth Classic
ConnectorsUSB-C, Grove, Pin headers
Built inscreen, motion sensor, microphone, buttons, battery charger
Power5 V at 500 mA over USB-C, or the internal 200 mAh battery
Size48 x 24 x 13.5 mm

Values are from the official docs page for SKU K016-P2. RAM is the 520 KB on-chip SRAM of the ESP32 die in the PICO-V3-02. Headers means the 8-pin top header with G0, G25/G36, G26, G32 and G33 for HAT modules. The Grove port carries G32 and G33. Onboard extras outside the vocab are a passive buzzer (G2), an IR emitter sharing G19 with a red LED, a green sleep-state LED and a BM8563 RTC. USB-C goes through a CH9102 USB-serial bridge. As a result, there is no native USB device mode. Weight is 16.7 g. Operating temperature is 0 to 40 C per the docs. CNX lists 0 to 60 C. The docs give no sleep current.

Starter ideas

  • Wrist step counter

    Strap the stick to your wrist like a watch. The motion sensor counts your steps and the screen shows the total.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen, Battery ready. Add: watch strap.

  • Name badge with a wave

    Show your name and a small picture on the screen. Tilt the stick and the picture slides around.

    Uses: Screen, Sensors built in.

  • Pocket reaction game

    Wait for the screen to flash and press the big button fast. The stick beeps and shows your time.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Classroom noise meter

    The built-in microphone listens to the room. A bar on the screen turns red once the class gets too loud.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Simple music metronome

    Set a tempo with the buttons. The buzzer clicks and the screen flashes on every beat.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • TV remote on your wrist

    Use the infrared light to send codes to a TV. Change channels with the side buttons.

    Uses: Battery ready.

  • Weather on a stick

    Fetch the local forecast over Wi-Fi. The screen shows the temperature and a tiny cloud or sun.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Plant thirst reporter

    Plug a soil moisture sensor into the side port. The stick shows a happy or thirsty face and sends readings over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Wi-Fi, Screen. Add: plug-in soil moisture sensor.

    Add a temperature and humidity sensor and log readings to an online chart. Check your bedroom from your phone.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Wi-Fi. Add: plug-in temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Motion tilt game controller

    Tilt the stick to steer a game on a computer. It sends the moves over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Doorbell buzzer pager

    Press a button at the front door. The stick in your pocket beeps and shows who is at the door.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Makes sound, Battery ready. Add: second board with a push button.

    Mount the stick near a bird box with a motion sensor. It counts visits and keeps the time with its clock.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Battery ready, Screen. Add: plug-in motion sensor, weatherproof box.

    Clip the stick to your bike. It records how much you lean in each turn and shows the biggest lean.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready, Screen. Add: bike mount.
