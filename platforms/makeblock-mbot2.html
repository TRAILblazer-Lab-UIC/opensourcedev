Overview

The mBot2 is a classroom coding robot from Makeblock. Its brain is the CyberPi, a small board with a color screen, a speaker, a microphone, and motion sensors. Kids start with drag and drop blocks and move up to Python in the same app. The metal frame and smooth motors make it tough enough for busy classrooms.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding lessons with a robot that moves
  • Classrooms that want to go from blocks to Python
  • Lessons on sound, light, motion, and simple AI
  • Clubs and competitions that need a sturdy robot

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as the full robot costs about 150 US dollars
  • Fully open projects, as only the CyberPi board design is shared
  • Rooms full of robots uploading over Bluetooth, as the links get mixed up
  • Rough handling of screws, as the metal threads can strip

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 240 MHz give it room to do a few jobs at once. It can drive, sense, and play sounds together.
  • 8 MB of extra memory helps with screen graphics and Wi-Fi lessons.
  • The CyberPi can store up to eight programs. Kids switch between them on the robot.
  • The motors track each turn of the wheel to about one degree. The robot drives and turns with good accuracy.
  • The 2500 mAh battery gives about five hours of play. It charges in about an hour over USB-C.
  • The robot is about as long as a dollar bill and as tall as a coffee mug. It weighs about as much as a small water bottle.

CPU, memory and sensor specs come from Makeblock's mBot2 spec sheet and the mBot2 introduction PDF. The ESP32 also supports Bluetooth Classic. Makeblock lists Bluetooth without naming the mode. The shield has 2 encoder motor ports, 2 DC motor ports and 4 servo ports, and 2 servo ports also accept LED strips or Arduino analog and digital sensors. Size is the spec sheet product dimension, and net weight is 570 g. Stemfinity lists about 5 hours of play per charge and about 1 hour to charge. CyberPi also has a joystick, per the Arduino library examples. The exact GPIO count was not found. Core Electronics says sensors daisy-chain over I2C. Makeblock calls the system mBuild.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: black electrical tape.

    The ultrasonic sensor spots walls ahead. The robot stops and turns before it hits them.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Talking name tag

    Show your name and a smiley face on the color screen. Play a happy sound at the press of a button.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Clap to go

    The microphone hears a loud clap. The robot rolls forward and stops at the next clap.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Color sorting game

    Place colored cards on the floor. The robot plays a different tune for each color it drives over.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound, Drives motors. Add: colored paper cards.

  • Classroom noise meter

    The robot listens to the room. Its screen and lights turn red as the class gets loud.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

How open is it?

Open Board

The CyberPi controller is certified open hardware as CN000007. Makeblock shares its schematic, board drawings, and Gerber files under the CERN-OHL-P v2 license. The CyberPi Arduino library uses GPL-3.0, but it includes one closed font file. The mBot2 shield, motors, sensors, the mBlock app, the stock firmware, and the ESP32 chip stay closed.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-P-2.0 (CyberPi) · Software license: GPL-3.0 (CyberPi Arduino library and stated CyberPi software license), mBlock is free but closed
