MangDang Mini Pupper 2

MangDang Technology

A desktop robot dog that runs Linux and ROS 2 for learning real robotics.

Overview

Mini Pupper 2 is a small four-legged robot dog from MangDang. A Raspberry Pi CM4 inside runs Ubuntu Linux and ROS 2, the software many real robots use. A helper chip runs the twelve leg servos, the motion sensor, and the touch sensor. It suits students, teachers, and hobbyists who want a real robotics stack on a desk-sized robot.

  • Anyone who needs open hardware design files for this model

Specs in plain words

  • The CM4 with 2 GB of memory is a small Linux computer, much like a Raspberry Pi 4.
  • Twelve servos report their position, which lets the code know where each leg is.
  • The 320 by 240 pixel screen is small but has room for a cartoon face.
  • It weighs about 465 grams, a bit more than a can of beans, and can carry about 200 grams.
  • The body is about 21 cm long, close to the length of a pencil.
  • The 1000 mAh battery is a small pack for short play sessions.

The upgrade kit SKU includes a CM4102000 (2 GB RAM, wireless, Lite with no eMMC). RAM shown is for that module, and the full kits' CM4 variant was not confirmed. The BSP flashes Ubuntu to a microSD card. As a result, flash_kb is null. MCU is an ESP32-S3 per the official spec table. The 2022 CNX article only said ESP32. The spec table lists one USB 2.0 port and one HDMI output. CNX Software listed a micro USB port in 2022. As a result, the connector type is from that article. Servos are serial bus servos with position feedback. The store sells a 3.5 kg.cm TTL serial bus servo as a spare. Touch sensor is onboard but has no matching enum value. The battery_charger entry assumes in-robot charging through the custom adapter. This was not confirmed on a fetched spec page. Weight 465 g and payload 200 g are from the official spec table. CNX listed 450 g.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Change its face

    Draw your own eyes and moods for the face screen. Swap them to match how the dog moves.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Drive it from a laptop

    Steer the dog over Wi-Fi with keyboard keys. Try trot, turn, and sit commands.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

Builder ideas

  • Teach the pup a dance

    String together steps and poses, then play music through the speaker. It is a fun show for friends.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

  • Follow a line on the floor

    Tape a dark line on the floor and use camera code to follow it. It makes a neat classroom race track.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi camera module, colored tape.

  • Voice command pet

    Speak simple words into the microphone and have the dog sit or wave. Pair it with a cloud voice service for chat.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

  • Pet it to say hello

    Tap the touch sensor on its head and have it wag and bark. A small script links the touch to a sound.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound.

  • Balance test lab

    Read the motion sensor while the dog walks on a gentle slope. Students can graph how it tilts.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Runs Linux.

  • Ball chaser

    Use OpenCV to spot a bright ball with the camera. The dog turns and trots after it.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi camera module, bright ball.

  • ROS 2 class kit

    Teach a robotics course with real ROS 2 topics and nodes. Each student writes one new behavior.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi.

  • Story time robot

    Have the dog read a short story through its speaker. Its face can change with each scene.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen, Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • Map your living room

    Add the lidar and run the SLAM package to draw a map of the room. Watch the map grow as the dog walks.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: small lidar sensor.

  • Walk to a spot on the map

    Pick a point on a saved map and let Nav2 plan the path. The dog walks there and avoids furniture.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: small lidar sensor.

  • Teach a new gait

    Try reinforcement learning in simulation and test the new walk on the real dog. Compare the speed of each gait.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Night patrol camera

    Send the dog on a short loop and stream its camera to your phone. Check the kitchen from bed.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: Raspberry Pi camera module.

  • Depth camera explorer

    Mount a depth camera and spot objects by distance. The dog can stop before it bumps a wall.

    Uses: Camera, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: depth camera.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published

  • Coding toolsMostly open

The software is open under Apache-2.0, including the board support package, helper chip firmware, and ROS 2 packages. Design files for Mini Pupper 2 hardware were not found, and the Mini Pupper 1 files carry no license. The Raspberry Pi firmware and ESP32 radio code stay closed.

Software license: Apache-2.0 (BSP, ROS packages), GPL for the FuelGauge driver, MIT for the StanfordQuadruped fork
