MAX78000

Analog Devices (formerly Maxim Integrated)

A low power chip with a built in brain for tiny vision and sound AI.

Overview

The MAX78000 is a low power chip from Analog Devices with a special engine for small neural networks. It pairs an Arm Cortex-M4F with a RISC-V helper core. It can spot faces, objects, or spoken words while running on a battery. It suits curious programmers who want to try AI on a tiny device.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Keyword spotting and simple voice commands
  • Small image tasks such as spotting a face or an object
  • Battery powered AI gadgets
  • Learning how AI models move from a computer onto a chip

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Beginners, as the tools need C and some AI training know-how
  • Large AI models or chat style voice assistants
  • Quick plug and play projects with lots of tutorials

Specs in plain words

  • The AI engine has 64 tiny processors working side by side on each image or sound.
  • Room for about 442,000 model weights fits a small vision or keyword model.
  • 128 KB of working memory holds your main program and a little data.
  • The Feather board adds 1 MB of extra memory for pictures and sound.
  • The onboard camera sees in VGA, which is about the sharpness of an old webcam.
  • The Feather board is about 6.6 by 2.3 centimeters, a bit longer than a stick of gum.

Chip values come from CNX Software, the Zephyr board page and a DigiKey product highlight. The analog.com datasheet would not load. The psram_kb value is the 1 MB QSPI SRAM on the MAX78000FTHR. Connectors, onboard parts, power and size are for the MAX78000FTHR Feather board (0.9 x 2.6 in). The FTHR has an OVM7692 VGA camera, a digital microphone, a MAX9867 audio codec, a MAX20303 PMIC with fuel gauge, microSD and an RGB LED. Zephyr lists a 10-bit ADC but the channel count was not confirmed. As a result, adc_channels is null. No TOPS figure is published. As a result, ai_tops is null.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Spoken number demo

    Power up the Feather board and try the keyword demo it ships with. Say a number from one to ten and watch the board respond.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip.

Builder ideas

  • Voice controlled night light

    Train the board to hear Go and Stop. It turns an LED strip on and off at your command.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip. Add: LED strip.

  • Classroom image sorting lab

    Students train a small model to tell apart a few objects. The board names each object held up to the camera.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Sound logger for nature walks

    Record short clips through the microphone to the memory card. Play them back later to learn the sounds of your park.

    Uses: Hears sound, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, small rechargeable battery.

  • Talking reminder box

    Store short voice clips on the memory card. A button press plays the right reminder through a speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker, push buttons, microSD card.

Expert ideas

  • Bird feeder visitor sorter

    Point the camera at a feeder and run a small image model. The board logs each time a bird lands and saves a picture to the memory card.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, small rechargeable battery, weatherproof box.

  • Face check door greeter

    Teach the board to know a few family faces. It plays a friendly sound for each person at the door.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Snore and cough counter

    Train a sound model on a few nights of recordings. The board counts snores or coughs and saves a nightly total.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, small rechargeable battery.

  • Battery powered wildlife camera

    Run the board on a battery in the woods. It saves only the frames that show an animal.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Battery ready. Add: small rechargeable battery, microSD card, weatherproof box.

  • Hand sign game

    Train a model on a few hand shapes. Play rock paper scissors against the board through its camera.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: small display.

  • Package on the porch alert

    Aim the camera at your doorstep. The board flashes a light when a box shows up.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: LEDs.

  • Machine sound watcher

    Train a model on the normal hum of a fan or pump. The board flags strange noises that may mean trouble.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip.

How open is it?

Open Board

The MSDK and the ai8x training and conversion tools are open under the Apache-2.0 license. The Feather schematic appears in the board datasheet, but no editable layout files were found. The chip, its RISC-V helper core and the AI engine design stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Apache-2.0 (MSDK, ai8x-training, ai8x-synthesis)
