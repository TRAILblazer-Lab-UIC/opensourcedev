MCX N947

NXP Semiconductors

A dual-core NXP microcontroller with a small AI engine, Ethernet, and fast USB.

Overview

The MCX N947 is a microcontroller chip from NXP with two Arm Cortex-M33 cores. It also has a small AI engine for tasks like hearing a keyword or sorting pictures. Most people buy it on the FRDM-MCXN947 board for about $22 to $25. It suits makers and students who already know some C and want Ethernet, fast USB, and AI on one board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Small AI demos like keyword spotting and image sorting
  • Wired network projects over Ethernet
  • Motor control and factory-style projects with CAN buses
  • Learning professional embedded tools on a cheap board

Not great for

  • First-time coders, with examples only in C
  • Wireless projects with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth radio on the chip
  • MicroPython or Arduino fans, with no support found
  • Quick buying, with stock and lead times that vary

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores at 150 MHz let one core read sensors while the other runs a model.
  • 512 KB of memory holds a small AI model plus plenty of sensor readings.
  • 2 MB of flash is room for a large program and a small trained model.
  • The AI engine is rated at 4.8 GOPS. That is enough for keywords and tiny pictures. Chat-style AI needs far more.
  • Deep sleep draws about 2 microamps. A battery can last for months on a sleepy project.
  • 124 GPIO pins on the largest package leave room for many buttons, lights, and sensors.

Chip values come from the NXP MCX N94X/N54X product page and the MCXNx4x data sheet Rev. 4 (January 2024). The NPU is rated at 4.8 GOPS, entered here as 0.0048 TOPS. GPIO is 124 on the VFBGA184 package and 78 on the HLQFP100 package. The product page lists four 16-bit ADCs. By contrast, the data sheet feature table lists two ADC instances. As a result, the input count is left null. Low power is the 2 uA deep power-down figure with RTC and 32 KB SRAM retained. Power-down with full 512 KB retention is 6 uA. Connectors and onboard parts describe the FRDM-MCXN947, which also has a P3T1755 temperature sensor and a touch pad. Camera and display are through FlexIO and SmartDMA. As a result, camera_dvp and lcd here mean parallel interfaces.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink the RGB LED

    Run the blink example from the SDK on the onboard RGB LED. It proves your tools, cable, and board all work.

  • Desk temperature logger

    Read the onboard temperature sensor every minute and print the values to your computer. Watch how your room warms up through the day.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • Touch pad light switch

    Use the onboard touch pad to turn an LED strip on and off. It makes a simple lamp with no moving button.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: LED strip, 5 V power supply.

  • Classroom sensor dashboard

    Plug the board into the school network over Ethernet. Serve a small web page with live readings from a few sensors.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: network cable, temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Weather station screen

    Show temperature, humidity, and pressure on a small color screen. Hang it by the back door to check before you head out.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small color screen, weather sensor.

  • USB keyboard game controller

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board and use it to steer a simple game on a small screen. Kids can design their own levels.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, small color screen.

  • Smart fan controller

    Drive a small motor driver from the board to set fan speed by room temperature. It keeps a workshop comfortable.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: motor driver board, small DC fan.

  • Battery soil monitor

    Wake the chip once an hour to read a soil moisture sensor, then sleep at very low power. A battery pack lasts a long time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • USB flash drive data logger

    Save sensor readings straight to a USB flash drive plugged into the board. Pull the drive out and open the file in a spreadsheet.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB flash drive, light sensor.

  • Click board sensor lab

    Add a mikroBUS shield and swap in different sensor boards. Each week the class can test a new kind of sensor.

    Add: mikroBUS shield, mikroBUS sensor boards.

Expert ideas

  • Spoken word light control

    Train a small keyword spotting model and run it on the AI engine. Say a word into a microphone and a light turns on.

    Uses: AI chip, Hears sound. Add: digital microphone, LED strip.

  • Garden pest camera

    Connect a small camera and run an image classifier on the AI engine. It flags birds or squirrels near your vegetable bed.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: parallel camera module.

  • Tiny synth with knobs

    Read knobs with the precise analog inputs and play tones through a speaker. The math engine helps shape the sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, small speaker, small amplifier board.

  • Gesture sorter for science class

    Record motion from a small motion sensor and train a model to tell gestures apart. Students learn how AI finds patterns.

    Uses: AI chip. Add: motion sensor.

NXP posts a schematic and design files for the FRDM board behind a free account, but no hardware license is stated. We did not see what those files contain. The MCUXpresso SDK core is open under BSD-3-Clause, and Zephyr support is open under Apache-2.0. The chip, the default LinkServer flash tool, and the AI model tools stay closed.

Bus
A bus is a shared path that carries data between parts.
CAN bus
CAN bus is a sturdy wired network made for cars.
Deep sleep
Deep sleep is a mode where a chip shuts down almost everything.
GPIO
GPIO pins are metal pins you can control from code.
Keyword spotting
Keyword spotting is AI that listens for one short word or phrase.
Microamp
A microamp is one millionth of an amp.
MicroPython
MicroPython is a small version of Python made for microcontrollers.
Toolchain
A toolchain is the set of programs that turn your code into firmware.
Training
Training is how an AI model learns from examples.
