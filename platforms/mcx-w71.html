Overview

The MCX W71 is a wireless microcontroller chip from NXP. One Arm Cortex-M33 core runs your code, and a second core runs the radio. The radio speaks Bluetooth LE and the 802.15.4 standard behind Thread, Zigbee, and Matter. It suits makers with some C experience who want to build smart home devices and long-lasting battery sensors.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Matter, Thread, and Zigbee smart home gadgets
  • Battery sensors that sleep most of the time
  • Bluetooth links to a phone
  • Small mesh networks of sensor nodes

Not great for

  • First-time coders, with programs only in C
  • Wi-Fi projects with no Wi-Fi on the chip
  • Screens, cameras, or AI, given its small memory
  • Fully open setups with a closed radio firmware

Specs in plain words

  • A 96 MHz main core is plenty for reading sensors and sending small messages.
  • 128 KB of memory for your code holds a sensor app, but nothing like a photo.
  • 1 MB of flash fits a full Matter or Thread program.
  • Deep sleep uses under 1.5 microamps. A coin-sized battery can run a sleepy sensor for a long time.
  • The FRDM board adds a motion sensor, a light sensor, an RGB LED, and 8 MB of extra flash.

Chip values come from the NXP product page and the MCXW71 data sheet Rev. 3 (29 September 2026). The radio core adds 256 KB flash and 88 KB SRAM that user code does not share. GPIO is 29 on the 48-pin HVQFN and 22 on the 40-pin HVQFN. The product page says Bluetooth LE 5.3 (upgradeable). By contrast, data sheet Rev. 3 calls it a Bluetooth LE 6.3 radio. NXP points users who want Bluetooth 6 features to the MCX W72. CAN FD is only on the MCXW716C parts. The MCXW716A parts lack it. The ADC input count was not read from the data sheet. As a result, it is null. Low power is the under 1.5 uA deep power-down figure with RTC. NXP also quotes 300 nA modes and a smart power switch under 100 nA. Onboard parts describe the FRDM-MCXW71, which has an accelerometer, a light sensor, an RGB LED, 8 MB SPI flash and a CAN FD transceiver. The FRDM-MCXW71 USB connector type was not confirmed.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and button test

    Build the Zephyr blinky sample and light the onboard RGB LED. Then make a button change its color.

Builder ideas

  • Room light meter

    Read the onboard light sensor and send the value to a phone over Bluetooth. Check which room gets the most sun.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Door open alarm

    Use the onboard motion sensor to notice a door or drawer moving. Send an alert to your phone over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Coin cell plant monitor

    Read a soil moisture sensor once an hour and sleep the rest of the time. A small battery can last for many months.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery holder.

  • Bluetooth step counter

    Wear the board on a shoe and count steps with the onboard motion sensor. Read the daily total on a phone app.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in. Add: battery pack.

  • Classroom quiz buzzers

    Give each team a board with a big button. The first press sends a Bluetooth signal to the teacher's laptop.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: large arcade buttons, battery packs.

Expert ideas

  • Matter smart light switch

    Follow NXP's Matter demo to build a light switch for your smart home. It works with Matter-ready home hubs.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: Matter home hub, LED strip.

  • Thread sensor network

    Spread several boards around a house to report temperature over Thread. Each board passes messages along to reach the far rooms.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: temperature sensors.

  • Zigbee bird feeder counter

    Count visits at a bird feeder with a motion sensor and report the total over Zigbee. Track the busiest time of day.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack.

  • Greenhouse mesh of sensors

    Place low-power sensor nodes along a greenhouse and link them in a mesh. Each node reports humidity and light to one hub.

    Uses: Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: humidity sensors, battery packs.

  • Smart lock prototype

    Drive a small servo to slide a latch on command from a Matter hub. It teaches how secure smart home gadgets work.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: servo motor, Matter home hub.

  • CAN bus data tap

    Use the onboard CAN FD transceiver to read messages on a hobby CAN network. Forward the readings to a phone over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: CAN cable.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot confirmed
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The app code tools are mostly open, with the MCUXpresso SDK core under BSD-3-Clause and Zephyr under Apache-2.0. The radio core runs a closed firmware image, and Bluetooth needs binary blobs. NXP posts board design files behind a free account with no stated license, and the chip itself is closed.
