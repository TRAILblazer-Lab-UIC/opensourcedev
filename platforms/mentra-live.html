Mentra Live

Mentra Labs

Camera glasses with speakers and microphones that run apps from your phone.

Field marks: These black glasses carry a 119 degree camera, three microphones, and no display at all, plus a charging case with eight times the battery of the frames.

Overview

Mentra Live is a pair of camera glasses from Mentra Labs. It has a wide camera, three microphones, and stereo speakers, but no display in the lenses. Apps run on your phone through the open MentraOS platform. It suits developers who want to build their own camera, voice and streaming apps for glasses.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Hands-free photos and point-of-view video
  • Livestreaming and video calls over Wi-Fi
  • Voice apps such as captions and spoken assistants
  • Developers who know TypeScript or phone app coding

Not great for

  • Beginners on a small budget. The glasses cost about 449 US dollars.
  • Seeing text or pictures in front of your eyes. The lenses have no display.
  • Tinkering with the hardware. No design files are public.
  • Long video sessions. The battery lasts about 45 minutes of video calling.

Specs in plain words

  • The 119 degree camera sees about as wide as your own side-to-side view.
  • 1080p video is the same sharpness as a regular HD TV.
  • Three microphones help pick out your voice from room noise.
  • The 260 mAh battery runs about eight hours of light use, and the 2200 mAh case refills it on the go.
  • The frame weighs 43 grams, about the same as a pair of regular sunglasses.

Getting started checklist

Asking before you record

Camera glasses can record people who never notice. Tell people nearby before you start a photo, video, or audio app. Ask for a clear yes and stop at once for anyone who says no. Point out the white privacy light and explain what it means. Keep recording off in bathrooms, locker rooms, schools, and other private places. Many places have laws about recording conversations. Check your local rules first.
