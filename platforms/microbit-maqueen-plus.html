micro:bit Maqueen Plus

DFRobot

A two-wheel classroom robot car that a BBC micro:bit drives.

Overview

Maqueen Plus is a small robot car from DFRobot. A BBC micro:bit plugs into its slot and acts as the brain. Kids and teachers program it with drag-and-drop blocks in a web browser. The newest V3 model adds a laser distance sensor and wheel sensors for very exact moves.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First robot lessons in school or at home
  • Line following and robot competition practice
  • Learning loops, sensors, and simple decisions with blocks
  • Clubs that already own a set of micro:bits

Not great for

  • Buyers who lack a micro:bit, which costs extra
  • Rough outdoor ground or thick carpet
  • People who want to study or change the robot circuit board

Specs in plain words

  • The micro:bit V2 brain has 128 KB of working memory. That is plenty for robot programs made of blocks.
  • The V3 laser sensor sees from about two centimeters up to four meters. It splits its view into 64 small zones like a tiny checkerboard.
  • Five line sensors sit under the car. They can follow a strip of black tape on a light floor.
  • The body is about 14 by 6.5 centimeters. That is close to the size of a TV remote.
  • Power comes from four AA batteries or one rechargeable cell. The rechargeable version tops up over a USB cable.

The robot has no user-programmable processor of its own. As a result, CPU, RAM, flash and radio values are for the BBC micro:bit V2 that plugs into it. GPIO is the 14 extended GPIOs listed for V3. V2 lists 3 x 5 V IO, 8 x 3.3 V IO, two 3.3 V I2C and one 5 V I2C port. The V3 laser sensor connects by I2C, UART or USB on a Gravity PH2.0-4P port and its own USB-C port. The light sensors, encoders and laser sensor are V3 only. V2 has an SR04 ultrasonic sensor. Size is the V2 figure from the DFRobot wiki. V3 dimensions were not published on the pages read. Battery charging applies to the 18650 versions. The micro:bit adds its own LED matrix, buttons, IMU, microphone and speaker.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Line-following racer

    Draw a black track on white paper. The robot reads the line and races around the loop.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: black electrical tape, white poster board.

  • Party light show

    Make the four color lights under the car blink and fade in patterns. It becomes a tiny disco on wheels.

  • Square dance robot

    Program the car to drive forward and turn four times. Students learn loops and angles by watching it trace a square.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Remote control car

    Use a second micro:bit as a hand controller. Tilt it to steer the robot across the floor.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: second micro:bit, battery pack.

  • Obstacle dodger

    The distance sensor spots walls and chair legs ahead. The robot stops and turns away on its own.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Robot drawing artist

    Tape a marker to the back of the car. Program shapes and spirals and let it draw on big paper.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: marker, large paper.

Builder ideas

  • Phone-controlled rover

    Pair the micro:bit with a phone app over Bluetooth. Drive the robot with buttons on the screen.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors.

  • Maze solver

    Build a maze from tape or cardboard walls. The robot checks each crossing and finds its way to the exit.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: cardboard, black electrical tape.

  • Light-seeking bug

    The light sensors compare brightness on each side. The robot creeps toward a flashlight like a moth.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: flashlight.

  • Garden row inspector

    Drive the car slowly along a raised bed path. A clip-on soil sensor takes readings at each stop.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Delivery bot for the classroom

    Follow a taped path from desk to desk. The car stops at each crossing to hand out pencils or notes.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: black electrical tape, small basket.

  • Gripper arm pickup

    Add a gripper kit to the front. The robot drives to a small block, grabs it, and carries it home.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: gripper arm kit, servo motor.

  • Exact distance driver

    The V3 wheel sensors count each turn of the wheels. Ask the robot to drive exactly one meter and measure how close it gets.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: measuring tape.

  • Robot soccer match

    Two teams each program a car to push a light ball. The first goal wins the round.

    Uses: Drives motors, Bluetooth. Add: ping pong ball, second robot.

Expert ideas

  • Room mapper

    The V3 laser sensor sees a grid of 64 distance zones. Turn the robot in place and sketch a rough map of the walls.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • AI camera follower

    Mount an AI vision camera on top. The robot spots a colored card and follows it around the room.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: AI vision camera module.

How open is it?

Open Code

The MakeCode extension that drives the robot is open code under the MIT license. MakeCode itself is open source too. The robot circuit board and its firmware stay closed, with no published schematics or hardware license.
