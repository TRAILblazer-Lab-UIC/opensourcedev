Milk-V Duo S

Milk-V (Shenzhen MilkV Technology)

A tiny Linux board with camera inputs, Ethernet and a small AI chip.

Field marks: Look for a square board about the size of a sticky note with a chunky network jack perched on one edge.

Overview

The Milk-V Duo S is a square Linux board about four centimeters on each side. Its main chip can boot as either an Arm or a RISC-V processor, picked with a small switch. It adds a network jack, two camera connectors, a small AI chip and optional Wi-Fi. It suits tinkerers who already know some Linux and want a very cheap camera or network gadget.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Low-cost camera and vision projects
  • Wired network gadgets and tiny servers
  • Trying RISC-V and Arm on the same board
  • Learning embedded Linux on a budget

Not great for

  • Complete beginners who have never used Linux
  • Projects that need the newest Linux kernel
  • Mixing 3.3 V parts on the 1.8 V header
  • People who want fully open AI and camera software

Specs in plain words

  • 512 MB of memory is enough for a small Linux system and a light web page.
  • The 0.5 TOPS AI chip can run small models that spot simple objects in a camera feed.
  • Two camera connectors let you plug in one or two small cameras.
  • 100 Mbps Ethernet is fast enough to stream a camera feed around your home.
  • Up to 39 GPIO pins give you plenty of spots to wire sensors, buttons and lights.
  • A USB-A port lets you plug in a keyboard, a flash drive or a USB speaker.

Power5 V 1 A over USB-C
Size43 x 43 mm

The base board boots from microSD. An 8 GB eMMC is an option. As a result, flash_kb is null. Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5 are only on the WIFI variants. USB-C device mode and the USB-A host port share one controller and are switched in software. Header J3 is 3.3 V with I2C, UART, PWM, SPI and JTAG. Header J4 is 1.8 V with PWM, I2C, UART, MIPI DSI and audio lines. The Arm and RISC-V main cores cannot run at the same time. ADC count was not confirmed.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Hello Linux over the network

    Plug the board into your router and log in from your laptop. You get a tiny Linux computer to explore with commands.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: network cable, microSD card.

  • Blinking LED with Python

    Wire an LED to the 3.3 V header and blink it from a short Python script. It is a first taste of controlling hardware from Linux.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: LED, resistor, jumper wires.

  • Room temperature logger

    Read a temperature sensor over I2C and save a reading every minute. Open the file later to see how your room warms and cools.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

Builder ideas

  • Garden camera timelapse

    Snap a photo of your plants every hour with a small camera. Stitch the photos into a video of your garden growing.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box, microSD card.

  • Tiny home web dashboard

    Run a small web server that shows sensor readings on a page. Any phone on your home network can open it.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Wireless plant watering helper

    Check soil moisture and switch a small pump on through a relay. Watch the readings from your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Bluetooth step counter receiver

    Collect data from a Bluetooth fitness tag and log it on the board. Students can chart how many steps they took in a week.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Runs Linux. Add: Bluetooth sensor tag.

  • Classroom network sensor station

    Put the board in a classroom and read light and air sensors. Each student can view the data from a shared web page.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: light sensor, air quality sensor.

  • USB keyboard music box

    Plug a USB keyboard into the USB-A port and map keys to sound clips. Send the sound out through a small USB speaker.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB keyboard, USB speaker.

  • Network ad blocker

    Run a lightweight DNS filter for your home network. Pages load with fewer ads on every device.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: network cable.

Expert ideas

  • Bird feeder visitor counter

    Point a camera at a feeder and run a small object detection model on the AI chip. Count how many birds stop by each day.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Doorbell camera that spots people

    Watch the front step with a camera and flag frames that show a person. Get a message on your network when someone arrives.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi. Add: camera module, 5 V power supply.

  • Stereo camera depth test

    Use both camera connectors at once to capture two views. Compare the images to guess how far away objects are.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: two camera modules.

  • Small status screen

    Drive a small display from the display header and show the time and weather. It makes a neat desk gadget.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: small MIPI display.

  • Real-time motor helper

    Run a small real-time program on the second core next to Linux. It can spin a servo on time while Linux handles the network.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

Getting started checklist

How open is it?

Open Board

Milk-V posts the schematic, board outlines and a 3D model on GitHub. The repo has no license file. Reuse rights are unclear. The Linux, U-Boot and OpenSBI code builds from source, but the AI, camera and video libraries come as closed files. The SG2000 chip itself is closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Mixed. Linux GPL, U-Boot GPL, OpenSBI BSD. No top-level SDK license.
