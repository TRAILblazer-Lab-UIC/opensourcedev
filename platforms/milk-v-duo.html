Overview

Milk-V Duo is a tiny RISC-V Linux board about 2 by 5 centimeters. One core runs Linux and the other can run Arduino sketches. It has a camera connector and a small AI helper. It suits tinkerers who want a cheap Linux brain for small projects, though it is now out of production.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning RISC-V and small Linux systems
  • Tiny camera gadgets with simple motion or object spotting
  • Mixing Linux scripts with Arduino timing on one board
  • Building Linux into your own circuit board

Not great for

  • Desktop use or anything with a screen and windows
  • Big AI models or voice assistants
  • Wireless projects. It has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
  • Buyers who need steady stock. Production has ended.

Specs in plain words

  • Two RISC-V cores run at 1 GHz and 700 MHz, enough for light scripts and sensor jobs.
  • 64 MB of memory is tight. It fits a few small programs, and the camera reserves part of it.
  • 0.5 TOPS means the AI helper does about half a trillion small math steps each second for small vision models.
  • The camera connector takes sensors up to 4 megapixels at 25 frames each second.
  • Up to 26 pins and two analog inputs let you hook up sensors, LEDs and servos.
  • Ethernet needs an add-on board with a network jack.
Full spec sheet

Memory64 MB
StoragemicroSD card
AI chipCVITEK TPU 0.5 TOPS
Pins26 pins, 2 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), SD card bus, Camera port (MIPI CSI), Ethernet, USB device, Debug port
ConnectorsUSB-C, Camera connector, microSD slot, Pin headers
Power5 V 1 A over USB-C
Size21 x 51 mm

The board boots from microSD. An SD NAND footprint exists but is not usually fitted. As a result, flash_kb is null. Ethernet comes from an on-chip 100 Mbps PHY on pads and needs an external transformer and RJ45 jack. LinuxGizmos lists 3 I2C, 5 UART, 1 SPI, 2 ADC, 7 PWM, 1 SDIO and 1 JTAG. The USB pads may support host mode, but this was not confirmed. Headers are castellated pads or pins on the with-header version. Some RAM is reserved for ION camera buffers.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Arduino blink on the small core

    Load an Arduino sketch onto the second core and blink an LED. Linux keeps running on the main core beside it.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: LED, resistor, jumper wires.

  • Python sensor reader

    Read a temperature sensor over the pins with a short Python script. Print the values in a terminal window.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

  • Light level logger

    Read a light sensor on one of the two analog inputs. Save a reading every minute to the memory card.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: light sensor, microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder motion camera

    Attach a small camera and save a photo once something moves at the feeder. Review the visitors each evening.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box, power bank.

  • Wired plant monitor

    Add an Ethernet add-on and log soil moisture to a small web page. Check your plant from any computer at home.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: Ethernet add-on board, soil moisture sensor, network cable.

  • Network status light

    Ping your home router every minute over the wired port. An RGB LED turns red once the internet drops.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: Ethernet add-on board, RGB LED.

  • Servo pointer toy

    Drive a servo from a PWM pin to point at a printed dial. The dial can show weather, mood, or class votes.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: servo motor, cardboard dial.

  • Time-lapse of a sprouting seed

    Snap one camera photo each hour onto the memory card. Join the pictures into a short growth movie later.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, microSD card.

Expert ideas

  • Person detector for a doorway

    Run a tiny vision model on the AI helper to spot people. It counts visitors at a classroom or library door.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module.

  • Hidden helper inside a gadget

    Solder the board flat onto your own circuit board with its edge pads. It adds Linux to a homemade device.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: custom circuit board.

  • RISC-V learning kit

    Build the Linux image from source and boot it on the board. It is a hands-on way to study a new chip family.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: microSD card.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Milk-V publishes the schematic PDF, a schematic library, and layout files in its duo-files repo. The repo has no license file. Reuse rights are unclear. Linux, U-Boot, OpenSBI, and FreeRTOS build from source. The chip is closed, and the camera, video, and AI parts ship as prebuilt closed files.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Mixed. Linux GPL, U-Boot GPL, OpenSBI BSD. No top-level SDK license.
