Overview

The Milk-V Mars is a small Linux computer the size of a credit card. Its JH7110 chip has four RISC-V cores, a newer open chip design family. The board copies the Raspberry Pi 3 Model B shape. Many Pi 3 cases fit it. It suits Linux fans who want to learn RISC-V or port software to it.

Specs in plain words

  • Four cores at up to 1.5 GHz give it the feel of an older, slower desktop.
  • Memory comes in 1 to 8 GB, enough for a light desktop or a few small servers.
  • Gigabit Ethernet moves files across your home network at full speed.
  • Three USB 3.0 ports let you plug in fast drives, a keyboard, and a webcam.
  • HDMI 2.0 can drive a 4K screen.
  • The 40-pin header offers up to 28 pins at 3.3 V for sensors, buttons, and lights.

RAM comes in 1, 2, 4 and 8 GB versions. The 4 GB middle option is shown. Arace currently lists only 2, 4 and 8 GB. The board boots from microSD or an optional eMMC module, and SPI flash holds the bootloader. As a result, flash_kb is null. Wi-Fi is not on the board and needs an M.2 E-Key card. The GPU model and clock come from the JH7110 datasheet and the bret.dk launch post. The 3.5 mm audio jack is from the bret.dk spec list. The official page is inconsistent about 2-lane and 4-lane CSI. The hardware table lists one 2-lane CSI port. Interfaces on the 40-pin header follow the JH7110 pin mux and were not listed pin by pin on a page I read.

Builder ideas

  • First steps with RISC-V Linux

    Flash Debian to a microSD card and log in from your laptop. You get a small RISC-V computer to explore with commands.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: microSD card, network cable, 5 V 3 A USB-C power supply.

  • Tiny home file server

    Plug a USB drive into a fast USB 3.0 port and share it on your home network. Every computer in the house can save files to it.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Wired network. Add: USB hard drive, network cable.

  • Network ad blocker

    Run a DNS filter on the wired network port. Pages load with fewer ads on every device in your home.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: network cable.

  • Kitchen recipe screen

    Hook up an HDMI monitor and open a recipe site in a light web browser. Keep a USB keyboard nearby to scroll.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: HDMI monitor, USB keyboard.

  • Classroom weather station

    Read a temperature and humidity sensor from the 40-pin header with Python. Show the numbers on a web page the class can open.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, jumper wires.

  • Retro music jukebox

    Load a music player and send sound to speakers through the headphone jack. A USB keypad can pick songs.

    Uses: Makes sound, Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: small powered speakers, USB keypad.

  • Voice memo recorder

    Record short notes through a USB microphone and save them with the date. Play them back later through the audio jack.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB microphone, headphones.

  • Community notice board

    Run a small screen in a library or club room that cycles through events. Update it from any computer on the network.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: HDMI monitor.

Expert ideas

  • Garden timelapse camera

    Connect a camera to the camera connector and snap a photo of your plants every hour. Stitch the photos into a video of the garden growing.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Port your favorite program to RISC-V

    Compile an open-source tool from source on the board. Report what works and what breaks to help the RISC-V community.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Bird feeder visitor log

    Point a camera at a feeder and save a photo whenever motion shows up. Look back at the day's visitors each evening.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Plant watering helper

    Read a soil moisture sensor through a small converter board and switch a pump with a relay. Water your plants on a schedule.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: soil moisture sensor, analog-to-digital converter board, small water pump, relay module.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Milk-V posts schematics and a size drawing on GitHub. No board layout files are shared, and the repo has no license file. The Linux, U-Boot, and OpenSBI code builds from source under GPL-2.0 and BSD-2-Clause. The GPU needs closed libraries, and the JH7110 chip itself is closed.

Hardware license: None stated. The mars-files repo has no license file · Software license: No top-level license on the Milk-V SDK repo. Bundled Linux and U-Boot are GPL-2.0, OpenSBI is BSD-2-Clause
