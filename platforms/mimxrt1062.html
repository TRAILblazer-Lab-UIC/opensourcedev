MIMXRT1062

A very fast NXP chip that powers Teensy 4 boards for audio and music gadgets.

Overview

The MIMXRT1062 is a fast microcontroller chip from NXP that runs at 600 MHz. Makers know it best as the brain of the Teensy 4.0 and Teensy 4.1 boards. It suits musicians, tinkerers, and anyone who needs a lot of speed in a small board. A strong audio library makes it a favorite for synths, effects and MIDI gear.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Music gear such as synths, effects pedals, and MIDI controllers
  • Fast data logging to a microSD card
  • Big LED art and light shows
  • Robots and motor projects that need quick reactions
  • Wired network projects on the Teensy 4.1

Not great for

  • Battery projects that must run for months
  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Running Linux or desktop programs
  • Total beginners who want a no-setup first board

Specs in plain words

  • One core at 600 MHz is among the fastest you can get in a microcontroller.
  • 1 MB of working memory holds long audio buffers and big LED patterns.
  • 8 MB of storage on the Teensy 4.1 holds large programs and sound clips.
  • A built-in math unit handles precise decimal numbers quickly for audio and science.
  • The Teensy 4.1 adds a microSD slot, a USB host port, and Ethernet with an add-on kit.
  • It draws about 100 milliamps at full speed. Plan on wall or USB power.

Chip values come from the NXP product page. Board values are for the Teensy 4.1 from the PJRC page. The chip has no internal flash. Teensy 4.1 has 8 MB QSPI flash and Teensy 4.0 has 2 MB. PSRAM is 0 by default. Teensy 4.1 has pads for one or two 8 MB QSPI PSRAM chips. The Teensy 4.1 has 55 digital pins with 42 on the breadboard, and 18 analog inputs. Teensy 4.0 has 40 pins and 14 analog inputs. Ethernet needs the Teensy 4.1 version with the DP83825 PHY and an RJ45 kit. CAN needs an external transceiver. SWD/JTAG exists on the chip but Teensy boards do not expose it. CNX reports about 100 mA at 600 MHz on Teensy 4.0. No sleep current was found. As a result, low_power_ua is null. Size 61 x 18 mm is the commonly cited Teensy 4.1 size and was not confirmed in a fetched source. CNX gives 36 x 18 mm for Teensy 4.0.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • USB MIDI controller

    Make a box of knobs and pads that your computer sees as a music keyboard. Control music apps with real hands-on knobs.

    Add: knobs, arcade buttons, enclosure.

  • Talking science display

    Play recorded facts through a speaker each time a visitor presses a button. It brings a school museum table to life.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio adapter board, small speaker, push buttons, microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Run a guitar signal through echo, chorus, and distortion effects. Knobs tweak each effect live on stage.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio adapter board, guitar jacks, knobs, enclosure.

  • Pocket synthesizer

    Build a small synth with keys and knobs that makes its own sounds. Plug in headphones and play.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio adapter board, push buttons, knobs.

  • Drum pad trainer

    Turn tap sensors into drum pads that send MIDI notes. Practice beats with a music app on the computer.

    Add: piezo tap sensors, resistors.

  • Classroom sound visualizer

    Show the pitch and loudness of a voice or instrument on a small screen. Students can see sound waves change as they sing.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: audio adapter board, microphone, small color screen.

  • Fast science data logger

    Record many sensor readings each second to a microSD card. It suits rocket, drop, or pendulum experiments.

    Add: microSD card, motion sensor.

  • Giant LED art wall

    Drive thousands of color LEDs with smooth animations. Hang it in a hallway or on a stage.

    Add: LED strips, large 5 volt power supply.

  • Retro game console

    Write simple arcade games and draw them on a small color screen. Use buttons as the controls.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small color screen, push buttons, small speaker.

  • Bird song recorder

    Record bird songs at dawn to a microSD card. Listen back later and count the different species.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: audio adapter board, microphone, microSD card, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Network audio player

    Stream sound to the board over a wired network and play it on speakers. Put one in each room for whole-house music.

    Uses: Wired network, Makes sound. Add: Ethernet kit, audio adapter board, speakers.

  • Wired home sensor hub

    Gather readings from sensors and share them on a web page over a network cable. It stays steady with no Wi-Fi drop-outs.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet kit, temperature sensors.

  • USB game controller adapter

    Plug a USB game pad into the board and remap its buttons. Your own games can then use it.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB host cable.

  • Car data reader

    Listen to the data line inside a car and log speed and engine readings. Study the trip later on a computer.

    Add: CAN transceiver board, microSD card.

  • Robot motor controller

    Read many sensors and drive several motors at once with quick reactions. It suits balancing robots and robot arms.

    Add: motor driver board, motors, motion sensor.

  • Simple camera snapshot box

    Hook up a basic camera and save small snapshots to a memory card. Expect plenty of low-level coding.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, microSD card.

How open is it?

Wild Open

PJRC shares Teensy 4 schematics as pictures with no license. SparkFun publishes full design files for its MicroMod Teensy board under CC BY-SA 4.0. The Teensy code library uses an MIT-style license and the uploader is GPL-3.0. The chip design and the Teensy bootloader firmware stay closed.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA-4.0 (SparkFun MicroMod Teensy Processor), none stated (Teensy 4.0 and 4.1 schematic images) · Software license: MIT with a PJRC listing clause (Teensyduino cores), GPL-3.0 (teensy_loader_cli), BSD-3-Clause and mixed (MCUXpresso SDK)
