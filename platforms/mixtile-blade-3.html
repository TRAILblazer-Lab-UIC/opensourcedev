Mixtile Blade 3

Mixtile

A palm-sized RK3588 server board built to stack into clusters.

Field marks: Look for a fanless Pico-ITX board with a server drive connector on its edge, two 2.5 gig ports, and a habit of linking up with copies of itself.

Overview

The Mixtile Blade 3 is a small server board about the size of a paperback cover. It runs on the Rockchip RK3588 chip with eight cores, a strong GPU, and a 6 TOPS AI engine. An edge connector carries fast PCIe, SATA, and power. Several boards can link into a cluster this way. It suits home lab fans, cluster learners, and AI tinkerers with Linux skills.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Small clusters for learning Kubernetes
  • Fast home routers and network gear
  • AI vision with the built-in NPU
  • Media servers and recording from HDMI input

Not great for

  • Beginners who have never used Linux
  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi
  • Tight budgets, with prices starting near 229 US dollars
  • People who want fully open AI software

Specs in plain words

  • Eight cores at up to 2.4 GHz make it about as quick as a budget laptop.
  • The 6 TOPS NPU can spot objects in a camera feed in real time.
  • Two 2.5 Gigabit ports move data more than twice as fast as normal Ethernet.
  • Up to 32 GB of memory holds several servers or a mid-size AI model.
  • HDMI in lets the board record or stream video from another device.
  • The fanless body draws at most 10 W, about as much as a small night light bulb.

RAM and eMMC come in 4/32, 8/64, 16/128 and 32/256 GB pairs. Values shown are for the 16 GB / 128 GB model. The 30-pin header carries GPIO, I2C, I2S, SPI, CAN, ADC, PWM and USB 2.0, but no pin count for GPIO was given. A mini PCIe socket carries PCIe 2.0 and USB 2.0. Maximum power is 10 W. Operating range is 0 to 80 C. The A76 clock of 2.4 GHz comes from CNX Software.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Home media server

    Store movies on an SSD and stream them to TVs around the house. The eight-core chip handles video with ease.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: SSD, U.2 to M.2 adapter case, USB-C PD charger.

  • Home router with OpenWrt

    Use the two 2.5 Gigabit ports to build a fast router and firewall. Block ads and watch traffic on your network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: two network cables.

  • Two-screen info board

    Drive one screen over HDMI and another over USB-C DisplayPort. Show a school schedule on one and news on the other.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: two monitors, USB-C to DisplayPort cable.

  • Garden camera timelapse

    Snap a photo of the garden every hour with a camera module. Turn a season of photos into a short video.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Camera. Add: camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Classroom coding server

    Host Jupyter notebooks or a small web app for a class. Students log in from any laptop on the network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: network cable.

  • Music streamer for the living room

    Play internet radio or your music library through speakers. Control it from a phone on the home network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Makes sound, Wired network. Add: powered speakers.

  • Sensor hub for a science fair

    Read sensors from the 30-pin header and log the data. Chart it later to show your results.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: temperature sensor, light sensor, jumper wires.

  • Network storage for a club

    Attach a drive through the U.2 edge for shared files. Members back up their work every week.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: SATA drive, breakout board.

Expert ideas

  • Small Kubernetes cluster

    Link several boards with breakout boards and cables. Learn how cloud apps spread work across many computers.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: extra Blade 3 boards, breakout boards, SFF-8643 cables, 12 V power supply.

  • Webcam object counter

    Run a detection model on the 6 TOPS NPU. Count passing cars, bikes, or birds on a camera feed.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip, Camera. Add: camera module.

  • HDMI capture recorder

    Feed a game console or laptop into the HDMI input. Record lessons or gameplay to storage.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: HDMI cable, SSD.

  • Private photo tagger

    Use the NPU to tag family photos by what they show. Your pictures stay on your own board.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip. Add: SSD.

  • Voice note transcriber

    Record notes through a USB microphone and turn them into text with a small speech model. Read them back later.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Hears sound, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB microphone.

  • Robot brain with CAN bus

    Talk to motor controllers over the CAN pins on the header. A camera adds sight for a rolling robot.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Camera. Add: CAN transceiver board, motor controllers, camera module, battery pack.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Mixtile posts schematic PDFs for the board and the breakout board. No layout or Gerber files are shared, and no hardware license is named. The OS builds live on GitHub, and Linux and U-Boot are GPL-2.0. The RK3588 chip, its memory start-up code, and the NPU runtime stay closed.
