Overview

The norns shield is an open-source add-on board that turns a Raspberry Pi into a norns sound computer. It adds stereo audio jacks, a small screen, three keys, and three knobs. It runs the same software as monome's own norns, with a big library of community instruments. monome stopped selling it in 2022 and now shares the design files for people to build their own.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Musicians who want a norns at do-it-yourself cost
  • Playing ready-made community scripts with no coding
  • Looping, sampling, and granular sound experiments
  • Learning Lua through music
  • Pairing with a light-up button grid

Not great for

  • Buying off the shelf, with no new units sold by monome
  • First-time builders who have not soldered or ordered circuit boards
  • Headphone listening at high volume, with no headphone amp on board
  • Battery-powered use away from a wall outlet

Specs in plain words

  • The Raspberry Pi 3B+ host has four cores at 1.4 GHz, plenty for live sound engines.
  • 1 GB of memory gives room for samples, loops and several scripts.
  • The 128 by 64 pixel screen is about the size of a sticky note strip and shows simple shapes and text.
  • Line-level jacks in and out connect to mixers, synths and powered speakers.
  • Wi-Fi lets you edit scripts from a laptop browser and download new ones.

Host specs are for the Raspberry Pi 3B+ that monome recommends with the 210330 shield (BCM2837B0 quad Cortex-A53 at 1.4 GHz, 1 GB LPDDR2, boots from microSD). A Pi 4B also works but its Wi-Fi is noisier and its port layout fits the case less well. Shield parts per the repo README are a CS4270 codec (CS4271 on 211028), NHD-2.7-12864WDW3 OLED, 3 pushbuttons and 3 rotary encoders. The shield has no headphone amp and no battery. Headphones work on the main output. The bus list (i2s, spi, i2c) is inferred from the codec and OLED parts and was not stated on a fetched page.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Runs Linux. Add: microphone or instrument cable.

  • Granular field recordings

    Feed in recordings from a walk in the woods. A granular script stretches them into slow, dreamy sound clouds.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Runs Linux. Add: portable recorder.

  • Play with a MIDI keyboard

    Plug a USB keyboard into the Pi. Use it to play the synth engines in many norns scripts.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: USB music keyboard.

  • Classroom music lab

    Students take turns with different scripts and compare sounds. It shows how loops, echoes, and filters work.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen. Add: small powered speaker.

  • Effects box for vocals

    Route a microphone through delay and pitch scripts. Sing into strange echoes during a live set.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone, small mixer.

The shield's EAGLE schematics, board files, parts lists, and case files are published under GPL-3.0. The norns software is also GPL-3.0. The Raspberry Pi host and its chips stay closed. monome once listed about $390 as the total project cost for its old ready-to-assemble shield. That figure predates the build-your-own path.
