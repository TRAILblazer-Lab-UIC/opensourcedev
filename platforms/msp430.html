Overview

The MSP430 is a family of 16-bit microcontrollers from Texas Instruments. These chips are famous for using very little power. The low-cost LaunchPad boards made them a staple in college and high school electronics labs. They suit students who want to learn how a chip works at a deep level.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning low-level C programming in a lab course
  • Battery and coin cell gadgets that run for months or years
  • Measuring and lowering power use with the LaunchPad power tool
  • Simple sensor and LED projects

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth projects, as it has no radio
  • Big programs or Arduino libraries, given its tiny memory
  • Sound, screens or camera projects
  • Total beginners who want a quick start with examples

Specs in plain words

  • One 16-bit core at 16 MHz handles simple tasks such as reading a sensor and blinking a light.
  • 512 bytes of working memory is about the length of a short paragraph of text.
  • 16 KB of storage holds a small program and nothing much else.
  • Eight analog inputs can read sensors such as light, temperature or a knob.
  • Sleep with memory kept draws about 0.1 microamps. A coin cell can last for years.

Values are for the MSP430G2553 on the MSP-EXP430G2ET LaunchPad, from the TI product page. It has 16 KB flash, 512 B SRAM, 24 GPIO and an 8-channel 10-bit ADC. Active current is 230 uA at 1 MHz and 2.2 V. Standby is 0.5 uA and off with RAM retention is 0.1 uA. The family spans much more. The MSP430FR2433 has 16 KB FRAM and 4 KB SRAM. Wikipedia gives family maximums of 25 MHz, 512 KB flash or 256 KB FRAM, and 66 KB RAM. Some parts such as the MSP430F5529 add USB. swd_jtag here means JTAG and TI's two-wire Spy-Bi-Wire debug. Builder ideas

  • Coin cell night light

    Turn on a soft LED once the room gets dark. A coin cell can keep it going for a very long time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: light sensor, LED, coin cell holder.

  • Power use lesson

    Measure how much power a blinking LED program uses with the LaunchPad power tool. Compare tricks that make it last longer.

    Uses: Sips power.

  • Reaction time game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a player presses the button. Show the score with LEDs.

    Add: LEDs, push button.

  • Touch button lamp

    Use a copper pad as a touch button to switch a small light. No moving parts wear out.

    Add: copper tape, LED.

  • Plant thirst blinker

    Check soil moisture once an hour and blink a red LED once the pot is dry. It runs for months on batteries.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LED, battery pack.

  • Simple electronic dice

    Press a button to roll a number and show it on a group of LEDs. Use it for board game night.

    Add: LEDs, push button.

  • Morse code beeper

    Type a message on a computer and hear it beeped out in Morse code. It makes a fun coding lesson.

    Add: small buzzer.

  • Door open counter

    Count how many times a door opens each day with a magnetic sensor. Send the total to a computer over USB.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: magnetic door sensor.

  • Traffic light model

    Program red, yellow and green LEDs to act like a real crossing. Add a button for people waiting to cross.

    Add: red yellow and green LEDs, push button.

Expert ideas

  • Garden temperature logger

    Read a temperature sensor every few minutes and store the readings. Copy them to a computer at the end of the week.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Battery-free sensor node

    Run a FRAM LaunchPad from a supercapacitor and a small solar cell. Learn how gadgets can live on harvested energy.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: small solar cell, supercapacitor.

How open is it?

Open Board

Texas Instruments shares full design files for the MSP-EXP430G2ET LaunchPad, but they carry no license. The MSP430 GCC compiler is free and open source, and the mspdebug programmer is open under GPL-2.0. Code Composer Studio is free to use but closed. The chip design also stays closed.

Hardware license: none stated (TI LaunchPad design files) · Software license: not named (TI MSP430-GCC, described as free and open source), GPL-2.0 (mspdebug)
