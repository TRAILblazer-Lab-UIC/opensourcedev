Overview

The MSPM0G3507 is an 80 MHz Arm Cortex-M0+ chip from Texas Instruments. It stands out for its analog parts, with fast inputs, an output, amplifiers, and comparators built in. It became the official board for a big Chinese student electronics contest. It suits students and makers who want to build robot cars and sensor gadgets in C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Robot cars and motor control projects
  • Reading sensors with high speed and accuracy
  • Low power gadgets that sleep most of the time
  • Learning C on a low cost board with a built in debugger

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Arduino or Python fans, as current sources confirm no Arduino core
  • Projects with screens full of images or big data logs
  • Beginners who want lots of English beginner tutorials

Specs in plain words

  • The 80 MHz processor handles sensors and motor timing with ease.
  • 32 KB of memory holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 128 KB of flash storage fits a robot car program with room to spare.
  • The fast analog inputs take four million readings each second.
  • Standby draws 1.5 microamps, which lets small batteries last a long time.
  • The WeAct core board is about 4.6 by 1.8 centimeters, about the size of a stick of gum.

Chip values come from the TI product page. GPIO count is 60 for the 64-pin LQFP and drops to 24, 28 or 44 on smaller packages. Connectors and onboard parts are for the LP-MSPM0G3507 LaunchPad, which has a micro-B USB port, 40-pin BoosterPack headers, an XDS110 debug probe with EnergyTrace, two buttons, an LED, an RGB LED, a temperature sensor circuit and a light sensor circuit. The WeAct core board uses USB-C for power and serial through a CH340X and measures 46.2 x 18.3 mm. Low-power value is STANDBY mode. SHUTDOWN is 80 nA.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Light level meter

    Read the light sensor on the LaunchPad. The board prints how bright the room is to your computer.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Color mood lamp

    Change the color of the onboard RGB light with the two buttons. Pick a calm color for reading time.

  • Desk temperature checker

    Read the temperature circuit on the LaunchPad. The RGB light turns blue for cool and red for warm.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • Line following robot car

    Mount the board on a small car with line sensors. It steers along a black tape track on the floor.

    Add: robot car chassis, line sensors, motor driver board, battery pack.

  • Plant soil watcher

    Read a soil moisture sensor every few minutes. A light warns you when the pot needs water.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, LEDs.

  • Heartbeat signal lab

    Feed a pulse sensor into the built in amplifier and fast analog inputs. Students see the heartbeat as a wave on a computer.

    Add: pulse sensor.

  • Simple tone maker

    Send a wave through the analog output to a small speaker. Buttons change the pitch for a tiny music toy.

    Add: small speaker, small amplifier board, push buttons.

  • Solar panel power tracker

    Measure the voltage and current from a small solar panel. The board shows how much power you collect each hour.

    Add: small solar panel, current sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Self balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor and adjust two wheels many times each second. The robot stands upright on its own.

    Add: motion sensor, motors with encoders, motor driver board, battery pack.

  • Battery saver sensor node

    Let the chip sleep between readings at just over a microamp. A pair of AA batteries can run a weather logger for a long time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, battery pack.

  • Motor speed controller

    Run a small motor with a PID loop. The motor holds a steady speed as the load changes.

    Add: motor with encoder, motor driver board.

  • Science class data logger

    Sample a sensor thousands of times each second with the fast analog inputs. Students graph quick events like a dropped ball bouncing.

    Add: vibration sensor.

How open is it?

Open Board

Most of the MSPM0 SDK uses the BSD-3-Clause and MIT licenses, and it builds with free Arm GCC. TI shares LaunchPad schematics and layout plots as a PDF, and WeAct shares a schematic for its core board. The chip and some TI tools stay closed, and no board lists an open hardware license.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: BSD-3-Clause and MIT for most MSPM0 SDK files, a few TI Commercial parts
