Mutable Instruments

Overview

Plaits is a Eurorack synth module that makes 24 kinds of sound from one compact panel. Mutable Instruments made it from 2018 to 2022, and the original is now discontinued. Its open design lives on in clones such as Tall Dog's µPlaits SE. Used originals also turn up on music gear marketplaces.

Specs in plain words

  • 24 synthesis models give you tones, chords, speech, noise, and drums in one place.
  • 12HP is about two and a half inches wide on a modular panel.
  • Eight control inputs let other modules move pitch, tone and shape on their own.
  • Updating firmware means playing a sound file into the module from a phone or computer.
  • The 72 MHz chip runs all the sound math inside the module with no computer.

MCU and DAC come from the official documentation. RAM and flash come from the stmlib linker script for the STM32F373 (32 KB RAM, 256 KB flash). The module has 8 CV and gate inputs (V/OCT, FM, HARMO, TIMBRE, MORPH, MODEL, LEVEL, TRIG) with 100k impedance and 16-bit CV capture, plus OUT and AUX outputs. It has 2 buttons for model selection and an LED array. The swd_jtag entry reflects the makefile's JTAG upload target used in development. End users update over audio. Clone sizes differ: µPlaits SE and Beehive are 8HP.

Starter ideas

  • Main voice for a modular synth

    Patch a sequencer into the pitch input and play melodies. Flip through models to find a bell, a string, or a buzzy lead.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Eurorack case, patch cables, sequencer module.

  • Drum sounds from one module

    Send trigger pulses into the TRIG input. The percussion models turn each pulse into a kick, snare or hi-hat.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: clock or trigger module, patch cables.

  • Chord pads for ambient music

    Pick the chord model and slowly turn the knobs. Long pads drift by for relaxing background sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Eurorack case, patch cables, reverb module.

  • Speech-like robot voices

    Choose the speech model and play with the timbre and morph knobs. The module babbles words and vowel sounds for game or film effects.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: patch cables.

  • Classroom synthesis demo

    Show students how one knob changes a sound's color. Compare the waveform models side by side.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Eurorack case, small powered speaker.

Builder ideas

  • Rain and wind textures

    Use the noise models to make rain, wind, or crackle. Layer them under nature recordings for a sound walk.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: patch cables, mixer module.

  • Self-playing patch

    Patch slow random voltages into the model and timbre inputs. The module changes its own sound for hours.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: random voltage module, patch cables.

  • Audio-rate FM experiments

    Plug another oscillator into the FM input. Fast wobbles turn simple tones into metallic clangs.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: second oscillator module, patch cables.

  • Gallery sound sculpture

    Build a small case with a clock and Plaits playing slow plucks. Leave it running as a quiet installation.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small Eurorack case, clock module, powered speakers.

Expert ideas

  • Hack the open firmware

    Read the open C++ source and add your own synthesis model. Flash it to a clone through the debug port.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: debug programmer.
