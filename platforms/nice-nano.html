nice!nano

Nice Keyboards (Nice Technologies LLC)

A thin Bluetooth brain for building your own wireless keyboard.

Field marks: Barely thicker than two stacked coins, this board hides under a split keyboard and lets each half run on a tiny battery with no cable between them.

Overview

The nice!nano is a tiny controller board for wireless keyboards. It fits in the same spot as the popular Pro Micro board. Most hobby keyboard kits accept it. It adds Bluetooth and a battery charger. Keyboard builders love it for split keyboards that run a long time on a small battery.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building wireless split keyboards with ZMK firmware
  • Turning an old wired Pro Micro keyboard into a wireless one
  • Macro pads, foot pedals, and other button gadgets
  • Battery projects that need to sleep most of the time

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as it has Bluetooth only
  • Total beginners, as a full keyboard needs soldering and assembly
  • Projects with a camera, sound, or a large screen
  • People who want fully open board files

Specs in plain words

  • 1 MB of storage holds keyboard firmware with lots of room for layers and macros.
  • 256 KB of working memory is plenty for reading keys and talking over Bluetooth.
  • 21 input and output pins can read the rows and columns of a full keyboard half.
  • About 20 microamps of idle power lets a small battery last a long time.
  • The board is 3.2 mm thick, about as thin as two stacked coins.
  • The built-in charger fills a 3.7 V lithium battery from the USB-C port.

Power5 V over USB-C, or a 3.7 V lithium battery on the B+ and B- pads
Deep sleep draw20 microamps

Clock and core type come from the nRF52840 chip. The docs list 21 GPIO. By contrast, circuitpython.org lists 23 GPIO total. P0.04 (AIN2) is reserved for the battery voltage reader. P0.13 switches the VCC output that feeds LEDs. I2C, SPI and UART come from the chip's pin-mappable peripherals. The chip also has an 802.15.4 radio, which keyboard firmware does not use. The pins follow the Pro Micro footprint and are normally socketed. Exact board dimensions were not stated on the pages read. There is a 32.768 kHz crystal, a blue user LED and an orange charge LED. Recommended battery is 100 to 2000 mAh.

Starter ideas

  • Custom key layout

    Edit a ZMK keymap file to move keys where your fingers want them. Add layers for numbers, symbols, and arrows.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Wireless macro pad

    Build a small pad of buttons that fire shortcuts for video editing or drawing apps. Keep it beside your mouse with no cable.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: macro pad circuit board, key switches, keycaps, small lithium battery.

  • Classroom slide clicker

    Wire a few big buttons to send next and back keys. Teachers can walk the room while the slides follow along.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: arcade buttons, small lithium battery, project box.

  • Music app foot pedal

    Put a button in a sturdy box on the floor. Tap it to turn pages in sheet music apps or start a recording.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: foot switch, small lithium battery, project box.

Builder ideas

  • Wireless split keyboard

    Build a two-half keyboard like the Corne or Lily58 with one board in each half. Type on your computer with no cable between the halves.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: split keyboard circuit board, key switches, keycaps, small lithium battery, second nice!nano.

  • Swap an old wired controller

    Replace the Pro Micro in a keyboard you already built. Your old wired board becomes a wireless one.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: small lithium battery, socket headers.

  • One-handed gaming pad

    Build a compact pad with the keys your favorite game needs. It pairs with a laptop over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: key switches, keycaps, small lithium battery, hand-wired frame.

  • Accessible big-button keyboard

    Wire large, easy-to-press buttons for a person with limited hand movement. Each button types a word or a shortcut.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: large push buttons, small lithium battery, project box.

  • Rotary knob volume control

    Add a turning knob to a keyboard or a small box. Spin it to change the volume on your computer.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: rotary encoder, knob.

  • Keyboard with glowing keys

    Add an LED strip under the keys for a soft glow. The board cuts power to the lights to save battery.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: LED strip, small lithium battery.

  • Tiny OLED status screen

    Add a small screen to show the battery level and active layer. Glance down to see which half is low.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: small OLED screen.

Expert ideas

  • CircuitPython Bluetooth gadget

    Load CircuitPython and write short Python code to send keypresses or sensor readings over Bluetooth. Try a button that types the temperature.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, push button.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The maker shares schematic and pinout images for both versions. The circuit board layout files are unpublished, and no hardware license is stated. The ZMK firmware and the bootloader are open under the MIT license. The Bluetooth stack inside the bootloader and the Nordic chip itself stay closed.
