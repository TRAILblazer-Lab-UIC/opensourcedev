Overview

The nRF52832 is a small Bluetooth Low Energy chip from Nordic Semiconductor. It came out around 2015 as the first chip in the nRF52 family. It sleeps on almost no power, and it became a staple of wearables, beacons, and smartwatches. Hobbyists use it on boards like the Adafruit Feather nRF52 and the official nRF52 DK.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Bluetooth gadgets that talk to a phone
  • Coin cell sensors that run for a very long time
  • Wearables such as step counters and watches
  • Learning Arduino or MicroPython on a wireless board

Not great for

  • Projects that need Wi-Fi or Thread
  • Plugging straight into USB without a helper chip
  • CircuitPython fans, as current sources show no support
  • Big programs or large data logs

Specs in plain words

  • The 64 MHz processor is quick for sensors, buttons and radio messages.
  • 64 KB of memory holds a small program and a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • 512 KB of flash storage fits your program with room to spare.
  • The deep sleep draw of 0.3 microamps lets a coin cell last a very long time.
  • The built-in NFC tag lets a phone pair with a simple tap.
  • The Adafruit Feather version is about 5 by 2 centimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.

Chip values come from the Nordic product page and the product brief v2.4. RAM and flash are for the common QFAA variant. The QFAB variant has 256 KB flash and 32 KB RAM. The ADC channel count comes from the product specification via search snippets and matches the 8 analog pins on the Adafruit Feather. Board values are for the Adafruit Feather nRF52832, which has a USB serial chip, 19 GPIO, a red LED, a reset button and LiPo charging. low_power_ua is System OFF at 3 V with no RAM retention. The chip has no USB, QSPI or high-speed SPI.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

Two nRF52832 boards are certified open hardware. SparkFun shares its breakout files under CC-BY-SA-4.0, and Adafruit shares its Feather files under CC BY-SA. Zephyr is Apache-2.0, but Nordic's own SDK code uses a license that limits it to Nordic chips. The Bluetooth stack ships as a closed binary, and the chip itself is proprietary.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA-4.0 (SparkFun nRF52832 Breakout, OSHWA US000896), CC BY-SA (Adafruit Feather nRF52832, OSHWA US000238), none stated (Nordic nRF52 DK) · Software license: LicenseRef-Nordic-5-Clause (nRF Connect SDK), Apache-2.0 (Zephyr)
