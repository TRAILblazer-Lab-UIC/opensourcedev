Overview

The nRF52833 is a wireless chip from Nordic Semiconductor. It talks Bluetooth Low Energy, Bluetooth mesh, Thread, and Zigbee from one radio. Most people meet it inside the BBC micro:bit V2, where kids program it with blocks in a web browser. Makers also use it for wireless keyboards, smart home nodes, and asset tags.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classrooms using the micro:bit V2
  • Battery gadgets that link to a phone
  • Smart home sensors on Thread, Matter, or Zigbee
  • DIY Bluetooth keyboards
  • Rugged builds that face heat up to 105 degrees Celsius

Not great for

  • Projects that need Wi-Fi to reach the internet
  • Cameras, video, or big AI models
  • Finding plain breakout boards from big maker shops
  • Beginners who skip the micro:bit and start in Nordic's own SDK

Specs in plain words

  • The 64 MHz processor is quick for sensors, buttons and radio messages.
  • 128 KB of memory holds a small program and a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 512 KB of flash storage fits your program with room to spare.
  • Built-in USB lets the chip talk straight to a computer.
  • The deep sleep draw of 0.6 microamps lets a coin cell last a very long time.
  • The chip runs on 1.7 to 5.5 volts and handles a wide range of batteries.

Chip values come from the Nordic product page and product specification key features. The 42 GPIO are for the aQFN73 and WLCSP packages. The QFN40 package has 18 GPIO. Board values describe the Nordic nRF52833 DK, which has 4 LEDs, 4 buttons, Arduino Uno Rev3 headers and an onboard J-Link. The DK size was not stated on fetched pages. low_power_ua is System OFF at 3 V with no RAM retention.

Starter ideas

    Press the buttons on a micro:bit V2 to play notes from its speaker. Kids can compose a short tune with blocks.

  • Radio message relay

    Send short messages between two micro:bit boards. Each team passes secret codes across the classroom.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: battery pack.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil sensor into a pot and wire it to the board. A beep warns you that the plant needs water.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor.

Builder ideas

    Wire buttons and a joystick to the board and pair it with a computer. It acts like a small wireless gamepad.

  • Bird feeder visit logger

    Place a motion sensor near a feeder and count each visit. The board sleeps between birds and reports totals to a phone.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, small rechargeable battery, weatherproof box.

  • Science fair weather station

    Read temperature and humidity every few minutes. The board sends the data to a laptop for a class graph.

Expert ideas

  • Wireless DIY keyboard

    Build a custom keyboard on a small nRF52833 breakout board. It types over Bluetooth and charges over USB.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready, Sips power. Add: key switches, keyboard circuit board, small rechargeable battery.

  • Thread door sensor

    Pair a magnetic switch with the board and join a Thread or Matter home network. Your home app knows the moment a door opens.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, coin cell holder.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: push button, relay module, small power supply.

How open is it?

Open Board

The BlueMicro833 keyboard breakout shares its files under CERN-OHL-W-2.0. The micro:bit V2 shares its schematics under CC BY-SA 4.0, and its CODAL runtime is MIT. Zephyr is Apache-2.0, but Nordic's own SDK code uses a license that limits it to Nordic chips. The Bluetooth stack ships as a closed binary, and the chip itself is proprietary.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-W-2.0 (BlueMicro833), CC-BY-SA-4.0 schematics (micro:bit V2), none stated (Nordic nRF52833 DK) · Software license: LicenseRef-Nordic-5-Clause (nRF Connect SDK), Apache-2.0 (Zephyr)
