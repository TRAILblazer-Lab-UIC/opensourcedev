Overview

The nRF5340 is a wireless chip from Nordic Semiconductor with two processors. One core runs your program and the other runs the radio. It supports Bluetooth LE Audio, Bluetooth mesh, Thread, Zigbee and Matter. It suits experienced makers who want to build earbuds, smart home gear or advanced wearables.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Bluetooth LE Audio projects such as earbuds and shared audio
  • Matter and Thread smart home devices
  • Gadgets that need extra security features
  • Learning Zephyr on a capable wireless chip

Not great for

  • Absolute beginners who want a quick first project
  • Projects that need Wi-Fi to reach the internet
  • Cheap builds, as the DK costs about 46 to 54 US dollars
  • Finding small maker boards from big hobby shops

Specs in plain words

  • The main core runs up to 128 MHz, which is fast for a low-power chip.
  • 512 KB of memory on the main core holds a large program and plenty of sensor data.
  • 1 MB of flash storage fits a big program with room for audio code.
  • A second 64 MHz core handles the radio by itself.
  • The deep sleep draw of 0.9 microamps lets a battery last a long time.
  • Arm TrustZone and a crypto block help keep keys and data safe.

Chip values come from the Nordic product page, product specification key features and product brief v2.2. RAM and flash are the sums of both cores. The application core has 1024 KB flash and 512 KB RAM. The network core has 256 KB flash and 64 KB RAM. clock_mhz is the application core peak. The network core runs at 64 MHz. low_power_ua is System OFF at 3 V from product brief v2.2. CNX Software quoted 1.1 uA at launch. Board values describe the Nordic nRF5340 DK, which has 4 LEDs, 4 buttons, Arduino Uno Rev3 headers, external QSPI memory and an onboard J-Link. Its USB connector type and size were not stated on fetched pages.

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Room temperature beacon

    Read a small temperature sensor and broadcast the value over Bluetooth. Any phone nearby can check how warm the room is.

    Load CircuitPython onto the DK and edit a short script. The board blinks its LEDs in any pattern you write.

Builder ideas

  • Bluetooth plant monitor

    Push a soil sensor into a pot and read it with the board. Your phone shows how dry the soil is.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: soil moisture sensor, small rechargeable battery.

  • Wireless slide clicker

    Wire two buttons to the board and make it act like a Bluetooth keyboard. One press moves your slides forward.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: push buttons, small rechargeable battery.

  • Classroom heart rate monitor

    Clip a pulse sensor to a fingertip and send the beats to a tablet. Students graph their heart rate after exercise.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: pulse sensor.

  • Bird box visit counter

    Mount a motion sensor near the entry hole of a bird box. The board sleeps between visits and reports a count over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, small rechargeable battery, weatherproof box.

  • Clap counting toy

    Listen for claps with a digital microphone and count them. Each clap adds a beat to a simple rhythm game.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone, small speaker.

Expert ideas

  • LE Audio wireless speaker

    Stream music from a phone to the board over Bluetooth LE Audio. A small amplifier and speaker play the sound.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier, small speaker.

  • Shared audio for a museum tour

    Broadcast one audio stream to many LE Audio earbuds at once. A whole group hears the guide clearly.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, microphone.

  • Wireless microphone for class

    Capture a teacher's voice with a digital microphone and send it over LE Audio. A receiver plays it through room speakers.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Hears sound, Battery ready. Add: digital microphone, small rechargeable battery.

  • Matter smart plug

    Add a relay to the board and join a Matter home network over Thread. Any Matter home app can switch a lamp on and off.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: relay module, small power supply.

  • Thread door sensor

    Pair a magnetic switch with the board and join a Thread network. Your home app knows the moment a door opens.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, coin cell holder.

  • Zigbee weather node

    Send temperature and humidity readings into a Zigbee home hub. The hub logs the weather on your porch.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small rechargeable battery.

  • Bluetooth mesh garden lights

    Put a board in each garden lamp. One tap on your phone passes the message from lamp to lamp across the yard.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Mesh messaging. Add: LED strip, small power supply.

How open is it?

Open Board

The Hurley Research NRFICE board is certified open hardware under CERN-OHL-P-2.0. Nordic shares DK layout and schematic files, but no license is stated. Zephyr is Apache-2.0, but Nordic's own SDK code uses a license that limits it to Nordic chips. The Bluetooth controller ships as a closed binary, and the chip itself is proprietary.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-P-2.0 (Hurley Research NRFICE, OSHWA US002181), none stated (Nordic nRF5340 DK) · Software license: LicenseRef-Nordic-5-Clause (nRF Connect SDK), Apache-2.0 (Zephyr)
