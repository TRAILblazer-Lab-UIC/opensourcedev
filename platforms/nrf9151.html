Overview

The nRF9151 is a cellular module from Nordic Semiconductor. It packs an LTE-M and NB-IoT modem, GPS, and a small processor into a package about 11 by 12 millimeters. It suits people who build trackers and remote sensors far from Wi-Fi. Newer modem firmware also adds satellite links.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Asset and vehicle trackers with GPS
  • Remote sensors on farms, rivers, and trails
  • Battery gadgets that check in a few times a day
  • Learning how cellular IoT works

Not great for

  • Beginners, as it needs C code and an SDK
  • Cheap projects, as boards cost about 70 to 100 US dollars plus a data plan
  • Phone calls or fast internet, as the modem handles small data
  • Short-range Bluetooth projects, as it has no Bluetooth radio

Specs in plain words

  • A 64 MHz processor is plenty for reading sensors and packing up messages.
  • 256 KB of working memory holds a few thousand sensor readings before upload.
  • 1 MB of storage fits your program next to the cellular code.
  • LTE-M and NB-IoT are slow cell networks made for small messages over long distances.
  • GPS lets the board find its place on Earth with no phone nearby.
  • The whole module is about the size of a fingernail.

Chip values come from the Nordic product page. DECT NR+ is supported on 915 MHz and 1.9 GHz but has no matching radio tag. Board values are for the nRF9151 DK, which has Arduino Uno Rev3 headers, four buttons, four LEDs, a nano SIM slot and an onboard J-Link. The Circuit Dojo Feather adds an accelerometer, a temperature sensor and an nPM1300 PMIC. Nordic does not list a sleep current on the product page. As a result, low_power_ua is null. size_mm is the SiP package.

Things you could build

Builder ideas

  • Bike or car tracker

    Hide a board in a bag on your bike. It sends its GPS location over the cell network each hour.

    Uses: Cellular, GPS. Add: LiPo battery, small enclosure.

  • Remote weather station

    Put a weather sensor on a fence post far from any Wi-Fi. Readings reach a web dashboard over LTE-M.

    Uses: Cellular. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, solar panel, LiPo battery.

  • Farm water tank monitor

    Measure the water level in a stock tank. Get a text alert before it runs dry.

    Uses: Cellular. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor, weatherproof box, LiPo battery.

  • Hiking breadcrumb logger

    Record your trail with GPS and upload the path at the end of each day. Share the map with friends.

    Uses: GPS, Cellular. Add: LiPo battery.

  • Mailbox alert

    Put a switch on a rural mailbox door. The board sends a message to your phone each time mail arrives.

    Uses: Cellular. Add: magnetic reed switch, LiPo battery.

  • Shipping box journey log

    Drop a board into a package and track its trip. Later you can map where it went and how long it waited.

    Uses: GPS, Cellular. Add: LiPo battery.

Expert ideas

  • Community air quality node

    Place air sensors around a neighborhood and send the readings over the cell network. A shared map shows hot spots.

    Uses: Cellular, GPS. Add: air quality sensor, 5 V power supply.

  • Wildlife collar test rig

    Build a test tracker that logs GPS points and uploads them in batches. It teaches how real animal trackers save power.

    Uses: GPS, Cellular. Add: LiPo battery, small enclosure.

  • River level warning

    Watch a creek level from a bridge and post updates online. Neighbors get a warning as the water rises.

    Uses: Cellular. Add: ultrasonic distance sensor, solar panel, LiPo battery.

  • Talking GPS clock

    Read exact time from GPS satellites and speak it through a small speaker. It never needs setting.

    Uses: GPS, Makes sound. Add: small speaker, small amplifier board.

  • Cabin noise monitor

    Listen through a microphone at a vacation cabin. Get a message after loud noises.

    Uses: Hears sound, Cellular. Add: digital microphone, 5 V power supply.

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

The Circuit Dojo nRF9151 Feather publishes KiCad files under CERN-OHL-S-2.0 and is certified open hardware. The software builds on Zephyr under Apache-2.0, and Nordic's SDK code uses the Nordic 5-Clause license. The modem firmware and the chip stay closed, and no hardware files were found for Nordic's own DK.
