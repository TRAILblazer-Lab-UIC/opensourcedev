Overview

The A20-OLinuXino-LIME2 is a Linux board from Olimex in Bulgaria. It has been on sale for about ten years, and Olimex says it will stay available. It has Gigabit Ethernet, a SATA hard drive plug, and a battery charger. It suits people who want a small home server built on fully open hardware.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Home servers such as FreedomBox
  • Small network storage with a SATA drive
  • Battery backed controllers that ride out power cuts
  • Studying a fully open and certified board design

Not great for

  • Fast desktop use or modern games
  • AI or camera projects
  • Quick wiring, as the pins use a very fine spacing
  • Buyers who need Wi-Fi on the board

Specs in plain words

  • Two 1 GHz cores are slow by today's standards but fine for a small server.
  • 1 GB of memory runs a light Linux system with a few services.
  • Gigabit Ethernet moves files across your home network quickly.
  • Native SATA lets you plug in a real hard drive for lots of storage.
  • The LiPo charger keeps the board running from a battery in a power cut.
  • 160 GPIO pins give room for many sensors and switches, but they need fine pitch cables.

The base LIME2 has no eMMC, NAND or SPI flash and boots from microSD. As a result, flash_kb is null. The e16Gs16M variant adds 16 GB eMMC and 16 MB SPI flash. SATA is native but not in the interface list. The LCD row uses a 0.05 inch pitch. The USB OTG connector type was not confirmed. ADC count was not confirmed. A 2 KB EEPROM stores the MAC address.

How open is it?

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files

Olimex publishes full Eagle schematics, board layouts, and parts lists on GitHub. OSHWA certified the board with UID BG000039, with documentation under CC BY-SA and software under GPL. The board runs mainline Linux with no closed drivers needed, but the Allwinner A20 chip itself is closed.
