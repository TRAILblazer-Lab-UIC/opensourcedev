Overview

The iCE40HX8K-EVB is an open-source hardware FPGA board from Olimex. It uses a Lattice iCE40HX8K chip, which you rewire with code to act like almost any digital circuit. The board adds 512 KB of fast memory and a 34-pin bus for add-on modules. It suits students and hobbyists who want to learn Verilog with fully open tools.

  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio

Specs in plain words

  • 7,680 logic cells is room for a small processor plus a few extra circuits.
  • 512 KB of fast SRAM can hold a full screen image or a long burst of captured signals.
  • 2 MB of flash stores your design for loading at power-up.
  • A 100 MHz clock ticks one hundred million times each second.
  • The board is about 65 by 67 millimeters, about the size of a drink coaster.
  • Four 40-pin headers break out pins for your own wiring.

clock_mhz is the onboard 100 MHz oscillator. ram_kb is the external 512 KB SRAM. The FPGA also has 128 Kbit (16 KB) of block RAM. flash_kb is the 2 MB SPI configuration flash. Rev B1 swapped the W25Q16 for an EN25Q16B, which broke an older Olimex flash library. The FPGA is configured over SPI through the 10-pin PGM connector at 3.3 V. The FAQ says there is no JTAG. The number of I/O pins on the four 40-pin headers and 34-pin bus was not found. As a result, gpio is null. All other interfaces are soft logic.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Blink the two LEDs

    Write your first Verilog design and blink both user LEDs at different speeds. It shows how hardware runs many jobs at once.

    Add: SPI programmer, 5 V power supply.

  • Button logic lab

    Connect the two buttons to the LEDs through logic gates you design. Try AND, OR, and XOR by hand.

    Add: SPI programmer, 5 V power supply.

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time the button press. Show the best score on a number display.

    Add: seven segment display, jumper wires.

  • Frequency counter

    Measure how fast a signal flips using the 100 MHz clock. Test a fan sensor, a buzzer, or a signal generator.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a strip of color LEDs with patterns made in logic. Light up a bookshelf or a stage.

    Add: LED strip, level shifter module, 5 V power supply.

Expert ideas

  • VGA video demo

    Draw color patterns on a monitor with the VGA add-on module. The fast SRAM can hold a full picture.

    Add: VGA and PS/2 add-on module, VGA monitor.

  • Keyboard and screen typewriter

    Type on a PS/2 keyboard and see the letters on a VGA monitor. It is a classic first step toward a homemade computer.

    Add: VGA and PS/2 add-on module, VGA monitor, PS/2 keyboard.

  • Homemade logic analyzer

    Capture fast digital signals into the 512 KB SRAM. View them later as waveforms on your computer.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Fast sound sampler

    Record a signal with the 100 MHz ADC module and store it in memory. Science classes can study sound waves up close.

    Add: FPGA ADC add-on module, microphone module.

  • Signal generator

    Make sine, square, and sawtooth waves with the DAC module. Use them to test audio gear or science experiments.

    Add: FPGA DAC add-on module.

  • Soft RISC-V computer

    Load a small RISC-V processor design into the FPGA. Run tiny C programs from the external SRAM.

    Add: SPI programmer.

Olimex publishes KiCad files, a BOM, and Gerbers for Rev B and B1. The board is OSHWA certified as BG000132 under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and the repo itself uses Apache-2.0. The open IceStorm tools are ISC licensed, but the iCE40 chip itself is closed.
