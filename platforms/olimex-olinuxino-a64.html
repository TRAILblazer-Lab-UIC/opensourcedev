Overview

The A64-OLinuXino is a Linux computer on one board from Olimex in Bulgaria. It has a four-core 64-bit processor, 1 or 2 GB of memory, Gigabit Ethernet, and HDMI. A LiPo battery charger is built in. Its full KiCad design files are public, and it is OSHWA certified.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Small servers that keep running through short power cuts
  • Learning embedded Linux on fully documented hardware
  • Kiosks and info screens with HDMI or an LCD panel
  • Wired network projects with fast Ethernet
  • Studying a real open Linux board design in KiCad

Not great for

  • People who have never set up Linux
  • Projects that need Wi-Fi on the base model
  • Heavy graphics or AI work on an older chip
  • Quick plug-and-play results in a few minutes

Specs in plain words

  • Four Cortex-A53 cores at 1.2 GHz give it enough speed for a desktop web browser or a small server.
  • 1 GB of memory runs a light Linux desktop or several small background programs.
  • Gigabit Ethernet moves files over a wired network about as fast as a home router allows.
  • The LiPo charger lets a battery take over during a power cut.
  • The base model boots from a microSD card and has no built-in storage chip.
  • The board is 90 by 62.5 mm, a bit bigger than a deck of cards.

Values are for the base A64-OLinuXino-1G, which has 1 GB RAM, no eMMC and no wireless. As a result, flash_kb is null and it boots from microSD. The 1Ge16GW version adds 16 GB eMMC plus Wi-Fi and BLE 4.0. The IND versions have 2 GB RAM. The camera interface sits on the 40-pin GPIO header, and its exact type (parallel or CSI) was not confirmed. GPIO count was not confirmed. The LCD and Ethernet share pins. As a result, a jumper change is needed for LCD use.

Olimex publishes the full KiCad schematics, board layout, Gerbers, and parts lists on GitHub. OSHWA certified it as BG000042, with documentation under CC BY-SA and software under GPL. The Allwinner A64 chip stays closed, and the Wi-Fi version likely needs vendor firmware.
