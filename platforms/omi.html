Overview

Omi is a small AI pendant from Based Hardware. It records speech with two microphones and sends the audio to a phone app for transcripts and summaries. The board designs, firmware, apps and backend are all open under the MIT license. It suits tinkerers who want to study or change a real AI wearable.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Meeting notes, lecture notes and voice diaries
  • Searching past conversations by key word
  • Writing app integrations for the Omi marketplace
  • Studying a fully open wearable design
  • Running your own private backend

Not great for

  • Beginners who want to change the firmware. It needs Zephyr tools and a debugger.
  • Screens, lights or sound output. The pendant has a vibration motor for feedback.
  • Heavy free cloud use. Big users need a paid plan.
  • Recording others without asking. Many places treat that as illegal.

Specs in plain words

  • The Nordic nRF5340 chip has two small cores for app work and radio work.
  • 512 KB of working memory handles audio streaming and Bluetooth at once.
  • 512 MB of extra storage keeps hours of audio offline until your phone is back.
  • Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth move recordings to the phone or the internet.
  • The pendant is 25.5 millimeters across, about the size of a large coin.
  • The 150 mAh battery charges on a small magnetic base.

The nRF5340 application core runs at up to 128 MHz with 1 MB flash and 512 KB RAM. The network core runs at 64 MHz with 256 KB flash and 64 KB RAM. Values shown are for the application core, from the Nordic product page. Offline audio goes to a separate CSNP4GCR01 4 Gbit SD NAND chip, which is not counted in flash_kb. Charging uses a BQ25101 charger IC. A 3 V vibration motor gives haptic feedback. Interfaces list the buses used on the board and the SWD pads that the Firmware Developer Kit reaches. The pendant has no user connector. The board GPIO count is not stated. Mainboard is 21 x 21 mm, charger board 13 x 20 mm and FPC 29 x 13 mm.

Getting started checklist

Asking before you record

Omi listens to everyone near it. Tell people you are wearing a recorder before a conversation starts. Ask for a clear yes and switch it off for anyone who says no. Leave it off in bathrooms, doctor visits and other private moments. Audio goes to the Omi cloud by default. Self-host the backend to keep it on your own computer. Many places have laws about recording conversations. Check your local rules first.

How open is it?

Open Board

Omi publishes the full board sources, Gerbers, parts list and case files under the MIT license. The Zephyr firmware, phone app, desktop app and backend are MIT too. The Nordic chips stay closed, and the Wi-Fi chip needs a closed firmware patch from Nordic.
