Overview

Open Smartwatch is a community smartwatch with open hardware and its own firmware called OSW-OS. The Light Edition has an ESP32 chip with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. It also has a round color screen and a step counter. It suits makers who want to write their own watch faces, apps, and games.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Writing your own watch faces and apps in C++
  • Learning how a real wearable is built
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth gadgets you wear on your wrist
  • Testing apps on a computer with the desktop emulator

Not great for

  • All-week battery life, with about one day per charge
  • Heart rate or health tracking with no pulse sensor
  • Total beginners new to code and build tools
  • Heavy Wi-Fi use on rev 3.2 and 3.3 boards

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32 runs at up to 240 MHz on two cores, which is plenty for smooth watch faces.
  • 4 MB of flash holds the firmware, several apps, and a few games.
  • The 240 by 240 round screen shows color pictures about the size of a large coin.
  • The accelerometer senses tilt and steps without any extra parts.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth let the watch talk to phones, laptops, and the internet.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorEspressif ESP32-PICO-D4 (Xtensa LX6), 2 cores at 240 MHz
Memory520 KB
Storage4 MB
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART)
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy, Bluetooth Classic
ConnectorsMicro USB, Battery plug
Built inscreen, motion sensor, battery charger
Power5 V over USB, or a 3.7 V LiPo on solder pads

Values are for the Light Edition. The ESP32-PICO-D4 has 4 MB of in-package flash and 520 KB of SRAM per the ESP32 datasheet. The project page quotes 320 KB of usable RAM. The Light Edition has no PSRAM per the project page and no microSD. The GPS Edition adds GPS and microSD. CNX Software reported a Micro USB port at launch. A Banggood buyer review of a V3.3plus variant reports USB-C. As a result, the port may vary by seller. Battery capacity depends on what you fit. The project suggests a 450 mAh round or 350 mAh rectangular LiPo. Rev 3.2 and 3.3 have a known power delivery limit of about 300 mA that can crash the watch during Wi-Fi use. Size is not stated on the pages read.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Daily step tracker

    Use the built-in step counter to log your walks. Set a daily goal and watch the count climb.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Study timer watch

    Build a focus timer with short breaks. The round screen fills up as each session goes by.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Design your own watch face

    Draw a new face in C++ and test it in the desktop emulator. Then flash it to your wrist.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Wi-Fi weather glance

    Fetch the local forecast over Wi-Fi. A quick glance at your wrist shows the day's weather.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Wrist game arcade

    Write a tiny game that you play by tilting your wrist. The accelerometer steers the player.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Bike ride motion log

    Record motion data during a ride. Upload it over Wi-Fi at home to see how bumpy the route was.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi, Battery ready.

  • Custom 3D-printed case

    Edit the case files and print a new shell in your favorite color. Make the watch truly yours.

    Add: 3D printer, watch strap.

Expert ideas

  • Phone notification buzz

    Send text from your phone over Bluetooth to the screen. Short messages show up on your wrist.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Classroom quiz clicker

    Each student taps an answer on their watch. The answers go to a teacher's laptop over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Smart home remote

    Tap a button on the watch to switch a Wi-Fi smart plug. Turn on a lamp from across the room.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: Wi-Fi smart plug.

  • Fall alert for a relative

    Watch the accelerometer for a sudden fall. The watch sends an alert over Wi-Fi to family.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi, Battery ready.

  • Lua app playground

    Build the Lua edition of the firmware. Try small scripts on the watch without a full C++ rebuild.

    Uses: Screen.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The KiCad schematic, board layout, Gerbers, and parts list are on GitHub under GPL-3.0. The case files and the OSW-OS firmware are GPL-3.0 too. The ESP32 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers stay closed, and the watch has no OSHWA certificate.
