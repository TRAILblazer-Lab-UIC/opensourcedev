Overview

OpenEarable 2 is a pair of open source research earbuds from the TECO lab at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology. Each earbud holds microphones, a pulse sensor, a motion sensor, a temperature sensor, and a pressure sensor. It streams audio over Bluetooth LE Audio. It suits university labs that study health and motion from the ear.

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF5340 chip has two cores at up to 128 MHz for sensors and Bluetooth.
  • 512 KB of memory holds sensor readings for a short stretch before saving.
  • The audio DSP chip processes sound inside the earbud.
  • The microphones hear sounds too high and too low for human ears.
  • The battery lasts up to 8 hours and charges in about 45 minutes.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorNordic nRF5340 (Arm Cortex-M33 application core plus Cortex-M33 network core), 2 cores at 128 MHz
Memory512 KB
Storage1 MB
AI chipAnalog Devices ADAU1860 audio DSP (FastDSP plus Tensilica HiFi 3z)
Ways to connect partsI2C, Digital microphone (PDM), Serial (UART), Debug port, USB device
RadiosBluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsUSB-C, microSD slot, Pin headers
Built inmicrophone, speaker, motion sensor, temperature sensor, buttons, color LED, battery charger
Power5 V over USB-C, internal rechargeable cell

Values are per earbud. RAM and flash are nRF5340 application core figures from Nordic's spec (512 KB RAM, 1 MB flash). The board adds internal SPI flash of unstated size and a microSD slot. The application core runs up to 128 MHz and the network core up to 64 MHz. The 14-pin header carries 1.8 V, 3.3 V, GPIO, I2C, PDM and serial lines. Sensors are Knowles SPH0641LU4H-1 microphones, Bosch BMA580 bone-conduction accelerometer, ADI MAXM86161 PPG, Bosch BMX160 IMU, Melexis MLX90632 temperature and Bosch BMP388 pressure. The speaker is a Huayunxin RE8 dynamic driver. KIT's datasheet names a Varta CP1454 cell. Battery life is up to 8 hours and a full charge takes about 45 minutes. The pulse oximeter (PPG) does not fit the onboard vocabulary. Size and weight were not stated on the pages read.

Builder ideas

  • Heart rate from the ear

    Read the PPG sensor while someone rests or walks. Plot their heart rate on a laptop with the Python library.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Body temperature tracker

    Log skin temperature through the day to the microSD card. Graph the results in Python.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • Noise exposure meter

    The outer microphone measures sound around the wearer. A class can map loud spots around the school.

    Uses: Hears sound, Battery ready. Add: microSD card.

  • LE Audio streaming test

    Stream music from a recent Android phone over Bluetooth LE Audio. Compare sound quality and delay.

    Uses: Makes sound, Bluetooth. Add: Android phone.

  • Lab teaching dataset

    Record a class session of motion and sound data to microSD. Students practice signal analysis on real data.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Hears sound, Battery ready. Add: microSD card reader.

Expert ideas

  • Breathing rate study

    Record the bone-conduction accelerometer and inner microphone. Analyze the data to count breaths per minute.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Hears sound.

  • Chewing and eating log

    Detect jaw motion while someone eats. Researchers can study eating habits without a camera.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Hears sound.

  • Head nod controls

    Use the motion sensor to spot a nod or a shake. Answer a phone prompt with a head gesture.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth. Add: Android phone.

  • Ear pressure sensing

    Read the ear-canal pressure sensor while the wearer yawns or swallows. Study how the ear responds.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Posture reminder

    Track head angle with the motion sensor. Play a soft tone in the ear after long slouching.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound.
