Overview

The OpenMV AE3 is a tiny camera board that runs AI right on the board. You program it in MicroPython from the free OpenMV IDE. It suits makers, students, and teachers who want a smart camera without a full computer. Its standout trick is fast object detection on very little power.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Counting people, faces, or objects in a small space
  • Battery-powered wildlife or garden cameras
  • Learning computer vision with short Python scripts
  • Small robots that need to see where they are going

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, with the board at about 150 dollars and add-ons near 100 dollars
  • Battery use out of the box, with no battery charger on the base board
  • Projects with 5 volt parts, with pins that only handle 3.3 volts or less
  • Buyers in the US, with no US store found

Specs in plain words

  • Two AI chips rated at 204 and 46 GOPS can find objects in video about 30 times a second.
  • A 1 megapixel global shutter camera takes sharp pictures of fast motion.
  • 13.5 MB of working memory holds a small AI model and a few camera frames.
  • An 8 by 8 distance sensor sees objects up to about 4 meters away.
  • Deep sleep draws about 80 microamps. A small battery can last for months on light jobs.
  • The board is about 30 millimeters on each side, close to a postage stamp.

ram_kb is the on-chip SRAM. The chip also has 5.5 MB MRAM for program code. flash_kb is the 32 MB octal storage flash. The second core runs at 160 MHz. ai_tops is the combined 204 plus 46 GOPS. There are 10 user I/O pins. P0 to P5 are 3.3 V and P6 to P9 sit on the back B2B header at 1.8 V. Pins are not 5 V tolerant. The two 12-bit ADC inputs are 1.8 V referenced. The product page also lists an I3C bus. Bluetooth is listed as v5.1 BR/EDR/BLE. Deep sleep is about 80 uA with the IMU asleep and about 500 uA with it running (docs). CNX quoted under 500 uA at launch. The board has no LiPo charger. OpenMV sells a separate AE3 Battery Shield. Size conflicts. The docs say 30 x 30 mm. CNX and the Alif E3 dossier say 25.4 x 25.4 mm.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Hello camera snapshot

    Run the first IDE example and watch live video on your computer. Then save a photo to the board's drive.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Color tracker toy

    Teach the board to follow a red ball across the table. It draws a box around the ball in the live view.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Face counter for a doorway

    Run the face detection example and count people who look at the camera. Students can graph visitors during a school open house.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Distance alarm for a desk

    Use the built-in distance sensor to notice someone walking up behind you. Light the RGB LED as a warning.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Tilt and shake logger

    Record motion from the built-in IMU during a skateboard ride or a bike trip. Plot the bumps afterward.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: small LiPo battery.

Builder ideas

  • Birdfeeder snapshot camera

    Detect birds at a feeder and save a photo of each visit. Review your guests at the end of the day.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: weatherproof case, microSD card.

  • Garden pest watch

    Check a garden bed for squirrels or rabbits with an object detection model. Send an alert to your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi. Add: weatherproof case.

  • Parking spot helper

    Mount it in a garage and measure how close the car gets to the wall. Flash a light at the stopping point.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: LED strip.

  • Clap or knock detector

    Listen through the microphone for a loud clap. Trigger a photo or a light each time it hears one.

    Uses: Hears sound.

  • Hand gesture light switch

    Wave a hand over the distance sensor grid to turn a lamp on or off. The 8 by 8 zones can tell left from right.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth. Add: smart plug or relay board.

  • Science lab motion tracker

    Film a rolling ball at high frame rates with the global shutter camera. Measure its speed for a physics class.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Phone viewer over Bluetooth

    Send object counts to a phone app over Bluetooth. A teacher can check a classroom experiment from across the room.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Camera.

  • Air quality add-on station

    Plug a sensor into the Qwiic port and log readings next to a photo of the sky. Upload both to a web dashboard over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Battery trail camera

    Sleep most of the day and wake up to check for animals. A deep sleep mode lets it run on a battery for a long time.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Sips power. Add: battery shield, small LiPo battery, weatherproof case.

  • Robot that follows a person

    Put the camera on a small robot car and track a person ahead. The robot steers to keep them in view.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: small robot car chassis, motor driver board.

  • Sorting machine eye

    Spot colored blocks on a moving belt and tell a servo which bin to push them into. It makes a fun art or factory demo.

    Uses: Camera, Plug-in connectors. Add: servo motor, small conveyor belt.
