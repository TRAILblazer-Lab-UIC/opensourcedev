OpenMV N6

A small smart camera board you program in Python to see and recognize things.

Overview

The OpenMV Cam is a small camera board with its own brain that you program in MicroPython. The current flagship is the OpenMV N6, which has a neural processing chip for running object detection right on the board. It suits makers, robot builders, and teachers who want vision projects without a full computer. The free OpenMV IDE shows the live camera view next to your code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Robots that follow lines, colors, or tags
  • Counting and spotting objects with small AI models
  • Barcode, QR code, and AprilTag reading
  • Battery-powered smart sensors that watch a scene
  • Teaching how computer vision works

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, with the N6 at about 215 dollars
  • Running large AI models like chatbots
  • Projects that need 5 volt signals, which the N6 pins cannot take
  • Plug-and-go wiring, with headers that come unsoldered

Specs in plain words

  • An 800 MHz main chip handles the Python code with speed to spare for a microcontroller.
  • The neural chip is rated at 600 billion operations per second, enough for live object detection.
  • 64 MB of extra memory holds many full camera frames at once.
  • The global-shutter camera grabs sharp pictures of fast-moving things at up to 120 frames per second.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, a microphone, and a motion sensor are built right onto the board.
  • A battery plug with charging lets it run away from a wall outlet.

ram_kb is the 4.2 MB on-chip SRAM. psram_kb is the 64 MB external SDRAM. The ADC count is one 12-bit ADC pin. The Ethernet PHY is onboard but the RJ45 jack and PoE come from an external shield (CNX Software). CNX Software reports under 0.75 W for the N6. No deep-sleep current figure was found for the N6. The RT1062 sibling draws under 30 uA in low-power mode per its product page.

Things you could build

18 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Color tracking robot buddy

    Mount the camera on a small rover. It follows a red ball around the room by spotting its color.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small robot chassis, motor driver board, two hobby motors.

  • Barcode pantry scanner

    Hold groceries up to the lens. The camera reads each barcode and keeps a pantry list on the memory card.

    Uses: Camera. Add: microSD card.

  • AprilTag treasure hunt

    Print a set of AprilTag markers and hide them. The camera reads each tag and reports which clue was found.

    Uses: Camera. Add: printed AprilTag sheets.

  • Classroom face finding demo

    Students watch the camera find faces live in the IDE. It is a gentle way to talk about how computer vision works.

    Uses: Camera.

Builder ideas

  • Line following race car

    Point the camera at the floor. The car steers itself along a taped track.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small robot chassis, motor driver board, servo motor.

  • Garden visitor counter

    Run a ready-made object detection model on the camera. It counts birds or squirrels at a feeder and logs the times.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: weatherproof box, rechargeable battery.

  • Wi-Fi plant growth time-lapse

    Snap a photo of a seedling every hour. The board sends each picture to your computer over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi. Add: microSD card.

  • Parking spot watcher

    Aim the camera at a driveway or bike rack. It notes whether each spot is empty or full.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: weatherproof box.

  • Hand-clap and motion trigger

    Use the onboard microphone and motion sensor. The camera takes a photo only after a clap or a bump.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in, Camera. Add: microSD card.

  • Battery trail camera

    Run the board from a small battery in a weatherproof box. It records short clips of animals passing by.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready. Add: rechargeable battery, weatherproof box, microSD card.

  • Science lab colony counter

    Place a petri dish under the lens. The camera counts spots and saves the totals for each day.

    Uses: Camera. Add: small lamp.

  • Sorting machine for colored beads

    Pair the camera with a servo arm. It sorts beads or candies into cups by color.

    Uses: Camera. Add: servo motor, small funnel, cups.

Expert ideas

  • Package on the porch alert

    Train a small model to spot boxes at your door. The camera sends a Bluetooth or Wi-Fi alert to your phone.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Gesture controlled music player

    Teach the camera a few hand shapes. Each shape plays, pauses, or skips a sound clip.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Wired security camera node

    Add an Ethernet shield for a steady network link. The camera streams compressed video to a home server.

    Uses: Camera, Wired network. Add: Ethernet shield, network cable.

  • Smart art installation eye

    Let the camera watch visitors in a gallery. Lights or sounds change as people move closer.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: LED strip, small speaker.

  • Robot arm pick and place

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: small robot arm kit.

  • Posture reminder desk buddy

    Point the camera at your chair. It nudges you over Bluetooth after a long stretch of slouching.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Bluetooth.

How open is it?

Open Board

Most of the firmware is MIT licensed, and the OpenMV IDE is MIT too. Some image code is GPL, and a few modules are closed and free only for non-commercial use. The N6 schematics are viewable in an online viewer only, with no download or stated license. Older boards up to the H7 Plus have full design files under CC BY-SA 3.0.

Hardware license: unknown for N6 (legacy OpenMV boards are CC-BY-SA-3.0) · Software license: MIT (most firmware), some GPL image code, some proprietary non-commercial modules
