Orange Pi 6 Plus

Shenzhen Xunlong Software (Orange Pi)

A powerful 12-core Arm board built for local AI and desktop work.

Field marks: A hefty Arm board with 12 cores, two NVMe slots, two 5 GbE ports, and a 100-watt charger, roughly the point where a single-board computer becomes furniture.

Overview

The Orange Pi 6 Plus is a high-end Arm board with 12 cores and lots of fast memory. It has an NPU, which is a chip that speeds up AI models. Two NVMe slots and two fast network ports make it feel close to a small desktop PC. It suits experienced makers who want local AI or a quiet Arm server.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running AI models on your own desk
  • Arm desktop use with a big monitor
  • Home servers with fast storage
  • Robots and vision projects that need two cameras

Not great for

  • Beginners, as setup takes firmware updates and Linux skills
  • Tight budgets, as US prices have climbed a lot
  • People who want fully open GPU and AI drivers
  • Battery or low power projects, as it draws over 30 watts under load

Specs in plain words

  • 12 cores let it juggle many programs at once like a desktop PC.
  • 16 to 64 GB of memory is enough to load sizable AI language models.
  • The NPU is rated near 30 TOPS, fast enough to spot objects in live video.
  • Two NVMe slots hold fast solid state drives for apps and big files.
  • Two 5 GbE ports move data about five times faster than common home Ethernet.
  • It needs a 100 W USB-C charger and a fan to stay cool.

RAM is 16, 32 or 64 GB. The 16 GB base value is shown. Storage is 64 Mbit SPI flash, microSD and two NVMe slots, with no eMMC. As a result, flash_kb is null. ai_tops is the official NPU figure of 28.8. CNX quotes up to 30 TOPS for the NPU, and Orange Pi quotes 45 TOPS combined. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth need an M.2 E-key module. The 40-pin header carries GPIO, UART, I2C, SPI and PWM, and the exact GPIO count was not found. The battery connector is board-to-board. The board weighs 132 g and needs active cooling. The display list includes DisplayPort and eDP, mapped here to lcd.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen, Makes sound. Add: HDMI cable, NVMe SSD.

  • Classroom coding station

    Set up one board as a shared coding computer for a class. Students write Python and see results on a big screen.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: HDMI monitor, USB keyboard, USB mouse.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen, Makes sound. Add: HDMI monitor, powered speakers.

Builder ideas

  • Home file and media server

    Fit two NVMe drives and share files over the fast network ports. The whole family can stream from one box.

  • Small home lab server

    Run several services such as a wiki, a backup tool, and a photo library. The 12 cores share the work.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe SSD, network cable.

  • Science image lab

    Capture photos through a microscope camera and measure the cells with Python. It speeds up a biology class project.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: microscope camera module.

  • Multi screen art installation

    Drive several displays at once with moving visuals. Hang the screens in a gallery or school hall.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: two monitors, display cables.

  • Sensor dashboard on the 40-pin header

    Wire a temperature sensor to the header and show live charts on screen. It is a good first hardware test.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.
