Orange Pi RV2

Shenzhen Xunlong Software (Orange Pi)

A low-cost Linux computer built on an eight-core RISC-V chip.

Field marks: An eight-core RISC-V board with two Ethernet jacks and two M.2 slots, rated at 2 TOPS of AI that the CPU cores must supply themselves.

Overview

Orange Pi RV2 is a small Linux computer built on the Ky X1, an eight-core RISC-V chip. It has two Gigabit network ports and two M.2 slots for fast NVMe drives. Orange Pi rates its AI speed at 2 TOPS, and that work runs on the main cores. It suits tinkerers who want a cheap RISC-V machine for servers, routers or software tests.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Trying out RISC-V Linux on real hardware
  • Small home servers and file storage
  • Routers with two wired network ports
  • Media servers with a fast NVMe drive
  • Camera projects with two camera inputs

Not great for

  • Absolute beginners who want plug-and-play ease
  • Speed fans. Reviews put it at about half a Raspberry Pi 4.
  • Heavy AI work. It has no separate AI chip.
  • Booting from the top M.2 slot, which does not work

Specs in plain words

  • Eight processor cores share big jobs well, but each core is fairly slow.
  • 2, 4, or 8 GB of memory runs a desktop, a browser, and a few servers at once.
  • 2 TOPS means the cores can do about 2 trillion small AI math steps each second.
  • Two Gigabit network ports let it sit between your modem and your home network.
  • Two M.2 slots take fast SSD drives for storage.
  • The board is about 9 by 5.6 centimeters, close to a playing card.

RAM comes in 2, 4 and 8 GB versions. Ram_kb shows the 4 GB board. flash_kb is null because there is no soldered eMMC. Storage is an optional eMMC module (16 to 128 GB), microSD or NVMe. The board also has 16 MB (128 Mbit) of SPI NOR flash by default, with 32 MB optional. The official page does not give a clock speed. The 1.6 GHz value comes from search snippets of a Linux.org post and a Boiling Steam review. ai_tops is the vendor's 2 TOPS INT8 claim for AI work run on the CPU cores. The 26-pin header carries GPIO, UART, I2C, SPI and PWM. The GPIO count was not stated. USB is three USB 3.0 host ports, one USB 2.0 host or device port and one USB 2.0 host on a 4-pin header. The battery connector is a 2-pin RTC backup battery header. Display output supports two screens at up to 1920 x 1440 at 60 fps.
