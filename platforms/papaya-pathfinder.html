Papaya Pathfinder

A 3D-printed Mars-style rover you build yourself around an ESP32.

Overview

Papaya Pathfinder is a small open-source rover you print and build yourself. Its six-wheel rocker-bogie legs copy the design of NASA's Mars rovers. An ESP32 runs the motors and listens for commands over Wi-Fi or a hobby RC radio. It suits makers with a 3D printer who want a rugged robot project.

  • Budget planning, with no official parts cost list
  • Long-term support, with one maker behind the project

Specs in plain words

  • A dual-core ESP32 chip at 240 MHz drives the motors and runs a small web server at the same time.
  • About 512 KB of working memory is enough for drive control and a simple video stream.
  • The standard rover runs on a 3S LiPo battery. Two power converters feed the logic and the servos.
  • The Mini uses an ESP32 camera board to send live video over Wi-Fi.
  • Four steering servos let the standard rover turn in tight spaces.

The README says only "ESP32". The standard firmware uses GPIO 38, 41, 42 and 47 and an RGB LED on GPIO 48, which matches an ESP32-S3 devkit. As a result, chip specs shown are ESP32-S3 values. Flash and PSRAM depend on the builder's devkit. The camera applies to the Mini only, and the RGB LED is on the devkit. ExpressLRS control needs a separate ELRS receiver on a UART (CRSF). Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Paint the wheels art bot

    Dip the TPU tires in washable paint and drive over big paper. Each run leaves a six-track drawing.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: washable paint, large paper roll.

  • Rover color lights

    Change the color of the devkit's RGB LED to show drive mode or battery state. It is a gentle first code tweak.

  • Obstacle course race

    Set up ramps and rocks in the yard and time each driver. The suspension makes rough tracks fun to tackle.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Backyard Mars rover drive

    Build the standard rover and drive it over rocks and roots with a gamepad. The rocker-bogie legs keep all six wheels on the ground.

    Uses: Drives motors, Wi-Fi. Add: six gear motors, four steering servos, two motor driver boards, 3S LiPo battery.

  • Living room camera explorer

    Build the Mini and stream live video to your laptop. Drive it under the couch to look for lost toys.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Drives motors. Add: ESP32 camera board, six small gear motors, 2S LiPo battery.

  • Classroom space mission

    Hide objects around a room and have students steer the Mini by video only. They practice working like a real rover team.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Garden patrol cam

    Drive slowly between garden rows and check plants through the camera. Look for pests without bending down.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Custom controller app

    Use the Python desktop app as a starting point and add new buttons. You could add a horn or a lights switch.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: small buzzer.

  • Night drive with headlights

    Mount a small LED strip on the front and switch it from the app. Explore a dark garage by camera.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Camera, Battery ready. Add: LED strip.

  • Snapshot nature survey

    Drive the Mini along a path and grab still photos of bugs and plants. Students can label them later.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi.

Expert ideas

  • Long-range RC rover

    Swap in the ExpressLRS firmware and drive with a hobby RC transmitter. It reaches far past normal Wi-Fi range.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: ExpressLRS receiver, RC transmitter.

  • Soil sensor sampler

    Add a soil moisture probe on an arm and stop at plants to take readings. Log the results to your laptop over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors. Add: soil moisture sensor, small servo motor.
