Overview

Bittle X is a small robot dog from Petoi. It has nine moving joints, a balance sensor, a voice command module, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth. Kids can control it with an app or their voice. Older students and researchers can program it with blocks, Arduino code or Python.

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32 brain has two cores running at 240 MHz. It can walk, listen, and talk to your phone at the same time.
  • Nine servo motors move the eight leg joints and the head. Each one turns to an exact angle.
  • A six-way motion sensor feels tilt and turns. It lets Bittle keep its balance and stand back up.
  • Four Grove plugs accept add-on sensors with no soldering.
  • The 1000 mAh battery gives about an hour of walking. It recharges in about an hour and a half over USB-C.
  • Standing up, Bittle is about 19 centimeters long. That is about the size of a small loaf of bread.

Bittle X V2 ships with BiBoard V1, which uses an ESP32-MINI-1 module (ESP32-U4WDH) with 4 MB flash. Values shown are for V1. The original Bittle X used BiBoard V0 with an ESP32-WROOM-32D module, 16 MB flash and a 64 Kbit EEPROM, which is what the Petoi spec page still lists. ADC count is the four analog inputs on Grove ports G3 and G4. The Grove ports also give UART2 and I2C. The Raspberry Pi socket needs a 5-pin header and supports Pi 3A+, 4 and 5. The IMU is an MPU6050 or ICM42670 depending on chip supply. The microphone belongs to the voice command module. The speaker entry rests on the BiBoard audio circuit and the Digital Trends review of its speech output. Size is standing size. Weight is 269 to 353 g depending on servos and arm. Battery runtime is about 1 hour and charging takes about 1.5 hours. The USB charger is not included.
