Overview

Nybble Q is a palm-sized robot cat from Petoi with eleven moving joints, including its head and tail. It arrives pre-assembled and runs the open OpenCat firmware on an ESP32 board. Kids can code it with blocks, and older makers can use Arduino, Python, or ROS 2. It suits classrooms and families who want a lively robot pet to program.

  • Long play sessions, with about one hour per charge
  • Rough handling near the delicate ribbon cable under the back shell
  • Kids under ten working alone

Specs in plain words

  • Eleven servo joints let it walk, look around, and wag its tail.
  • The ESP32 chip has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth for wireless control from a phone or computer.
  • The balance sensor helps it notice a fall and stand back up.
  • The battery runs about an hour and recharges in about an hour and a half over USB-C.
  • It weighs about 403 to 433 grams, close to a can of soup.
  • Four Grove ports take plug-in sensors with no soldering.

The store page says BiBoard V1 (ESP32), whose guide lists the ESP32-U4WDH with 520 KB SRAM and 4 MB flash. Values shown are for V1. The Nybble Q spec page instead lists an ESP32-WROOM-32D with 4 MB flash and a 64 Kbit EEPROM, which matches the older BiBoard V0. ADC count is the four analog inputs on Grove ports G3 and G4. The Grove ports also give UART2 and I2C. The IMU is an MPU6050 or ICM42670 depending on chip supply. The pack holds 12 servos (P1S alloy or P1L plastic, coreless, max 8.4 V). 11 are used as joints. Runtime is about 1 hour and charging takes about 1.5 hours. The USB charger is not included. Standing size is from the Petoi spec page. Weight is 403 to 433 g. Make reported distance sensors and color-changing eyes in its Petoi review, but they were not confirmed as standard Nybble Q parts on the spec page.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Try the built-in tricks

    Pick a skill in the Petoi app and watch the cat walk, sit or stretch. It is the quickest way to meet your new pet.

    Uses: Drives motors, Bluetooth.

  • Talk to your cat

    Say a voice command and the cat performs a trick. No coding is needed for the built-in commands.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors.

  • Block code a routine

    Drag Petoi Coding Blocks to chain walks, turns, and poses. Kids can make the cat do a short show.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Pose it to teach a skill

    Move the legs by hand in the Skill Composer and save each pose. Play the poses back as a new move.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Self-righting test

    Gently tip the cat over and watch the balance sensor help it stand. Students can time how fast it recovers.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

Builder ideas

  • Custom voice words

    Add up to ten of your own voice commands in C++. Teach it to answer to your family's words.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors.

  • Cat that avoids walls

    Plug a distance sensor into a Grove port and have the cat turn before it bumps a wall. It makes a fun hallway explorer.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Drives motors. Add: Grove distance sensor.

  • Light-seeking kitty

    Add a light sensor and have the cat walk toward a sunny spot. It acts a lot like a real cat on a nap hunt.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Drives motors. Add: Grove light sensor.

  • Drive it from Python

    Send commands from a Python script over a cable or Bluetooth. Make the cat react to events on your computer.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors.

  • Plant check reminder

    Plug a soil moisture sensor into a Grove port near a pot. Dry soil makes the cat meow and wave its tail.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Makes sound, Drives motors. Add: Grove soil moisture sensor.

  • Robot cat dance party

    Program a dance with head bobs and tail wags. Play a tune on the speaker to match the moves.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

  • Wi-Fi remote pet

    Send moves to the cat over Wi-Fi from another room. Surprise your family with a wave.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

Expert ideas

  • Gait science lab

    Change step timing in the Arduino firmware and measure walking speed. Graph which gait covers the most ground.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: tape measure.

  • ROS 2 robot lessons

    Connect the cat to ROS 2 through the OpenCat wrapper and send it commands as topics. It is a small step into pro robot tools.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Add a Pi brain

    Mount a Raspberry Pi in the socket for extra computing power. Run bigger programs that tell the cat what to do.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi board.
