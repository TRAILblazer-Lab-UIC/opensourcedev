Overview

pib is a humanoid robot torso you print on a 3D printer and fill with ready-made electronics. It has a head, arms, five-finger hands, and a touchscreen face, and it sits on a table. A Raspberry Pi 5 runs the robot, and a depth camera lets it see. It suits schools and makers who want one robot that grows from block coding to Python and ROS2.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classrooms that want a long robotics project with a friendly face
  • Learning block coding first and moving on to Python and ROS2
  • Exploring robot hands, vision, and speech in one build
  • Makers who enjoy big 3D printing jobs

Not great for

  • Small budgets, with parts boxes from about 3,145 euros
  • Buyers outside the EU, with the shop shipping only inside the EU
  • Walking or moving around, with no legs or wheels
  • Quick projects, with a long print and assembly time

Specs in plain words

  • A Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB of memory works like a small desktop computer inside the robot.
  • An OAK-D Lite depth camera sees how far away things are and runs its own AI chip.
  • A 7-inch touchscreen acts as the robot's face and control panel.
  • A microphone array and a speaker let pib listen and talk.
  • Tinkerforge servo boards drive the hobby servos in the arms, hands, and head.

Specs describe the Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB named in the pib#5 release notes and shop box contents. It boots from a microSD card. Interfaces and radios are those of the Raspberry Pi 5. It is not all used by pib. The OAK-D Lite TOPS figure was not checked. The RGB buttons are the Tinkerforge RGB LED Button Bricklets on the torso. Earlier versions (pib#4 and before) used a Raspberry Pi 4. The setup script keeps a legacy mode for it. Height and joint count for pib#5 were not stated. Humanoid.guide lists pib.Pro at 80 cm with 20 degrees of freedom.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wave hello with blocks

    Drag Blockly blocks to make pib raise an arm and wave. It is a first robot program students can finish in one class.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Talking greeter

    Have pib say hello through its speaker and show a smile on its screen face. Place it at a school entrance for visitors.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

  • Mood faces on the screen

    Draw a set of cartoon faces and switch them with block code. Students can link each face to a pose.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Dance routine to music

    Time arm and head moves to a song played through the speaker. Students choreograph the robot in Blockly.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Web dashboard control

    Open the Cerebra web page from a laptop over Wi-Fi and move pib with sliders. Classmates can take turns driving.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

Builder ideas

  • Rock paper scissors game

    Let pib play rock paper scissors with its five-finger hand. The camera reads your hand to keep score.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Drives motors.

  • Voice command robot

    Speak a word into the microphone array and have pib nod or point. It is a friendly start to speech control.

    Uses: Hears sound, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Robot storyteller

    Program pib to read a short story aloud with hand gestures. Students write the script and the moves.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Museum guide bot

    Have pib turn toward visitors it sees and explain an exhibit. A touchscreen menu lets guests pick a topic.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, Makes sound.

  • Python arm control lab

    Move each servo by angle in Python and log the positions. It bridges block coding and real programming.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Printing and assembly project

    Print the 114 parts as a class and build the robot step by step. Students learn 3D printing, wiring, and teamwork.

    Add: 3D printer, filament.

Expert ideas

  • Copy my hand

    Use the depth camera's hand tracking to make pib mirror your fingers. It shows how robots turn sight into motion.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Drives motors.

  • Sign language practice partner

    Program simple hand signs and let learners copy them. A camera check can tell them how close they got.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Drives motors.

  • ROS2 robotics course

    Explore ROS2 topics and nodes on a real humanoid. The digital twin lets students test code before the arms move.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Object hand-off helper

    Spot a cup with the depth camera and reach out to grab it. It is a hard but rewarding grasping challenge.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Drives motors.

The 3D print files for the standard robot are open under GPL-3.0. The software, web interface, Blockly tools, and SDK are open under AGPL-3.0. The Raspberry Pi and camera chips and their firmware stay closed, and the Advanced print files show no license.
