PIC32MX

Microchip Technology

A 32-bit microcontroller family that powers retro BASIC computers and USB gadgets.

Overview

PIC32MX is a family of 32-bit microcontroller chips from Microchip Technology. It first came out in 2007. Some parts come in a 28-pin package that fits straight into a breadboard. Hobbyists know it best for the Maximite and Micromite BASIC computers and the Arduino-style chipKIT boards.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Retro computing fans who want a BASIC computer they can build
  • Breadboard projects with a chip you can hold in your fingers
  • USB gadgets such as custom keyboards and game controllers
  • Learning how a microcontroller works at the chip level

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth projects with no extra radio board
  • Newcomers who want fresh tutorials, as the chipKIT tools have been quiet since 2022
  • Anyone using Arm debug tools, which do not work with its MIPS debug port

Specs in plain words

  • A 50 MHz MIPS core runs simple programs fast enough for sensors, lights, and small games.
  • 256 KB of flash holds your program with plenty of space for a few thousand lines of code.
  • 64 KB of memory is room for a few hundred sensor readings at a time.
  • USB On-The-Go on the PIC32MX2XX parts lets the chip act as a USB device or talk to a USB keyboard.
  • The 28-pin DIP package is about the length of a thumbnail and plugs into a breadboard.
  • Larger parts reach 80 MHz with 512 KB of flash, and some add Ethernet.

Chip values are for the PIC32MX270F256B from the PIC32MX1XX/2XX datasheet DS60001168F. The 50 MHz rating applies from -40 to 85 C. The -40 to 105 C grade runs at 40 MHz. The 21 GPIO and 9 ADC channels are for the 28-pin part. The 44-pin PIC32MX270F256D has 34 I/O and 13 ADC channels. PIC32MX1XX parts such as the PIC32MX170F256B used in Micromites lack USB. Larger PIC32MX5XX, 6XX and 7XX parts run at 80 MHz with up to 512 KB flash and 128 KB RAM, and some add Ethernet and CAN. low_power_ua is the typical power-down current at 25 C from table 29-7, marked preliminary for 256 KB parts. The cover page quotes 20 uA typical. swd_jtag reflects the chip's 4-wire MIPS EJTAG port. It is not Arm SWD. Connectors and onboard parts describe Olimex boards such as the PIC32-RETROBSD, which has headers, mini-USB, microSD, LEDs and a button. usb_micro is the closest allowed value for its mini-USB.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and beep starter

    Load a short Arduino sketch on a Fubarino Mini. An LED blinks and a buzzer beeps on a timer.

    Add: LED, small buzzer, breadboard.

  • Retro BASIC computer

    Plug a VGA screen and a keyboard into a DuinoMite board. Type BASIC programs like a home computer from the 1980s.

    Add: VGA monitor, PS/2 keyboard, microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Micromite thermostat

    Turn a cheap chip into a BASIC controller. It reads a temperature sensor and switches a heater relay.

    Add: temperature sensor, relay module, breadboard.

  • Garden soil logger

    Read a soil sensor every hour and save the numbers to a microSD card. Graph how fast your garden dries out.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, microSD card, battery pack.

  • Touch-pad light switch

    Use the chip's capacitive touch feature on a copper pad. A tap turns a lamp on or off.

    Add: copper tape, relay module.

  • Custom USB game controller

    Wire buttons to a USB-capable PIC32MX board. Your computer sees it as a gamepad for classic games.

    Add: arcade buttons, joystick.

  • Simple sound player

    Send audio from the chip through an I2S audio board. Play short sound effects for a doorbell or a game.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: I2S audio board, small speaker, microSD card.

  • Clap counter for class

    Read a microphone module and count claps or loud sounds. A display shows the total for a science lesson.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone module, small display.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    An infrared sensor counts each visit to a feeder. The chip sleeps between visits to save battery.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: infrared motion sensor, battery pack.

  • Community weather board

    Read temperature, humidity, and pressure sensors. Show the readings on a screen in a library or club room.

    Add: weather sensor board, small display.

Expert ideas

  • USB keyboard reader

    Use the USB host port to read a normal USB keyboard. Each keypress can trigger a light or a sound in a project.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB adapter cable.

  • Tiny Unix machine

    Boot RetroBSD on the Olimex PIC32-RETROBSD board. Explore a small Unix system over a serial terminal.

    Add: microSD card, USB serial adapter.
