Overview

Badger 2040 W is a programmable name badge with a 2.9 inch E Ink screen. A Raspberry Pi Pico W on the back adds Wi-Fi. It suits makers, teachers, and event-goers who want a low power info display. The screen keeps its picture with no power, and a clock wakes it on a timer.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Name badges for meetups and conferences
  • Small dashboards for weather, transit, or news
  • Sensor readouts that run for weeks on batteries

Not great for

  • Video or animation, as E Ink refreshes slowly
  • Color images, as the screen is black and white
  • New buyers, as stores show it out of stock or discontinued

Specs in plain words

  • The 296 by 128 pixel screen fits a name, a few lines of text or a simple picture.
  • E Ink holds its image with the power off, much like printed paper.
  • 264 KB of memory is room for small apps and a few screen images.
  • 2 MB of flash stores your programs, fonts, and a handful of pictures.
  • A deep sleep draw of about 20 microamps lets a small battery pack last a long time.
  • The plug-in sensor port takes Qwiic and STEMMA QT sensors with no soldering.

Display is 2.9 inch E Ink, 296 x 128 pixels, dot pitch 0.227 x 0.226 mm. Thickness is 10 mm including connectors. The Pico W radio supports Wi-Fi, and its CYW43439 also supports Bluetooth, which Pimoroni does not advertise for the badge. low_power_ua is the roughly 20 uA deep-sleep figure with the Pico W powered down, from the Raspberry Pi Official Magazine review. User-accessible GPIO and ADC counts are not published. Breakouts connect over I2C through the Qw/ST port.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pimoroni publishes a PDF schematic, but no board layout or hardware license. The firmware, BadgerOS, and graphics libraries use the MIT license. The chip is closed, and the Wi-Fi chip needs a closed firmware file.
