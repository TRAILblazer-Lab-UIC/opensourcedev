Overview

Badgeware Badger is a wearable e-paper badge from Pimoroni. People also call it the Badger 2350. It comes in a sturdy case with a battery, a lanyard, and MicroPython already loaded. It suits event goers, teachers, and anyone who wants a friendly first coding project.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Name badges for events, clubs, and conferences
  • Low-power desk displays for weather, charts, or reminders
  • First MicroPython projects with a ready-made screen and buttons
  • Adding sensors through the plug-in Qwiic port

Not great for

  • Fast moving pictures on a slow e-paper screen
  • Color graphics, as the screen shows four shades of grey
  • Wiring lots of parts beyond the single Qwiic port

Specs in plain words

  • The 2.7 inch screen is about the size of a large sticky note.
  • E-paper keeps its picture with no power, much like a printed page.
  • 16 MB of flash holds many small badge apps and pictures.
  • 8 MB of extra memory gives room for bigger images and data.
  • The 1000 mAh battery and a sleep clock let the badge idle for up to 100 days.
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth let it pull data from the internet or talk to other gadgets.

The Badgeware docs give 200 MHz. By contrast, the Pimoroni product page says 250 MHz. The battery charger is an MCP73831 at 455 mA, with an XB6096I2S protector. A PCF85063A real-time clock wakes the badge from sleep. Size includes the case. An SWD debug connector is on the board. The docs say Badger idles for up to 100 days. A forum user measured about 167 uA at 3.6 V in sleep, but this is not an official figure. No user GPIO header is described beyond the Qw/ST port. As a result, gpio is null.
