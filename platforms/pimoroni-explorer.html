Overview

Pimoroni Explorer is a learning board with a color screen, buttons, a speaker and a mini breadboard on one base. It runs on the Raspberry Pi RP2350 chip. It suits students, teachers and curious beginners who want to wire parts with no soldering. Four servo sockets make simple robots and moving art easy.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Classroom electronics with no soldering
  • Learning MicroPython or CircuitPython with a screen
  • Small games, graphs and sensor dashboards
  • Simple robots with the Starter Kit servos and wheels

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Projects that need many pins, as it has six easy-access GPIO pins
  • Tiny or wearable builds, as the board is about the size of a paperback

Specs in plain words

  • The 2.8 inch 320 by 240 color screen is big enough for graphs, games and menus.
  • 520 KB of memory holds a decent game or a long list of sensor readings.
  • 16 MB of flash stores many programs, images and fonts.
  • Six GPIO pins and four analog pins connect LEDs, buttons and sensors.
  • Two plug-in sensor ports take Qwiic and STEMMA QT sensors with one cable each.
  • Four servo outputs let you move arms, wheels and pointers.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorRaspberry Pi RP2350B, dual Arm Cortex-M33 or dual Hazard3 RISC-V, 2 cores at 150 MHz
Memory520 KB
Storage16 MB
Pins6 pins, 4 analog inputs
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART)
ConnectorsUSB-C, Pin headers, Qwiic or STEMMA QT, Servo ports, Battery plug
Built inscreen, buttons, speaker
Power5 V over USB-C, or a battery on the 2-pin JST-PH connector
Size107 x 85 mm

Display is a 2.8 inch IPS LCD, 320 x 240, ST7789V driver, 250 cd/m2. Assembled depth is 16 mm. gpio is the six easy-access GPIO headers. adc_channels is the four ADC headers listed by CNX Software, Core Electronics and PiShop. The Explorer MicroPython library defines NUM_ADCS = 6, which may count shared pins. The schematic shows a W25Q128JV flash chip and no PSRAM. Battery voltage range is not published.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Hello screen and buttons

    Draw your name on the color screen and change colors with the buttons. It makes a quick first program.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Breadboard traffic light

    Wire red, yellow and green LEDs on the breadboard and cycle them. Show the light state on the screen.

    Uses: Screen. Add: red, yellow and green LEDs, jumper wires.

  • Button piano

    Play a different note on the speaker for each of the six buttons. Kids can compose short tunes.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Reaction time game

    Flash a shape on the screen and time the player's button press. Keep a high score table.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Room weather station

    Plug in a temperature, humidity and pressure sensor and draw live graphs on the screen. Leave it on a desk all day.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: plug-in weather sensor.

  • Plant watering helper

    Read a soil moisture sensor on an analog pin and show a happy or sad plant face. Add a beep for thirsty plants.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, jumper wires.

  • Two-wheel robot

    Attach two continuous rotation servos with wheels and drive the board around. Steer with the buttons or a simple program.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: two continuous rotation servos, wheels, battery pack.

  • Pan and tilt pointer

    Use two servos to aim a laser-free pointer or a paper arrow. Control it with a knob on the breadboard.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: two servo motors, potentiometer.

  • Retro arcade game

    Write a simple snake or brick breaker game for the screen. Play it with the six chunky buttons.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Science class light meter

    Read a light sensor and plot brightness over time on the screen. Compare sunny and shady spots.

    Uses: Screen. Add: light sensor, jumper wires.

  • Fruit circuit drum kit

    Clip the crocodile terminals to fruit or foil pads and play drum sounds on touch. It makes a fun music club project.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: crocodile clip leads, foil or fruit.

  • Step counter for PE class

    Read a motion sensor and count steps or jumps on the screen. Students can race to a target.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen, Battery ready. Add: plug-in motion sensor, battery pack.

  • Kinetic art mobile

    Swing paper shapes slowly with servos and show a pattern on the screen. Hang it in a classroom window.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: servo motors, paper shapes, string.

Expert ideas

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Count visits with a motion sensor near a feeder and log them on screen. Check the tally after school.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, jumper wires.

  • Oscilloscope style graph

    Sample an analog pin fast and draw a live wave on the screen. It helps explain signals in science lessons.

    Uses: Screen. Add: jumper wires.

  • Custom C program

    Build a program with the Pico SDK in C for the RP2350. It is a step up for students ready for real firmware.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pimoroni publishes a PDF schematic, but no board layout or hardware license. The pimoroni-pico libraries use the MIT license, and the board needs no closed firmware files. The Explorer examples repo has no license file, and the RP2350 chip is closed.

Hardware license: None stated · Software license: MIT (pimoroni-pico libraries), the pimoroni/explorer repo has no license file
