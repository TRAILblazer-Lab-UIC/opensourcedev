Overview

The Pico Audio Pack is a stereo sound board for the Raspberry Pi Pico from Pimoroni. It has one jack for line-out and a second jack with a headphone amp. A Pico plugs into the back with its male headers. It suits makers who want good sound for synths, players, or games.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Adding clean stereo sound to a Pico project
  • Music players, synths, and sound effect boxes
  • Learning how digital audio works on a small board
  • Projects that need both headphones and a line-out to a speaker

Not great for

  • Recording sound with no microphone input
  • Driving a bare speaker, which needs a powered speaker or headphones
  • Use on its own with no Pico attached

Specs in plain words

  • The DAC turns numbers into sound at up to 32-bit and 384 kHz, beyond CD quality.
  • The headphone amp has a low and a high gain switch for quiet or loud headphones.
  • Two 3.5 mm jacks fit the same plugs as normal headphones and speaker cables.
  • The board is about 53 by 29 mm, close to the size of a Pico itself.
  • Female headers come soldered on. A Pico with male headers plugs right in.

This is an add-on with no processor. As a result, CPU and memory fields are null. The Pimoroni C++ example defines I2S data on GPIO 9 and bit clock on GPIO 10, with the LR clock on GPIO 11. The store page CircuitPython snippet uses I2SOut(GP10, GP11, GP9). A 2021 forum thread says an early version of the store page listed wrong pins. The RP2040 has no I2S block. As a result, I2S runs in PIO.
