Overview

Tufty 2040 is a programmable name badge from Pimoroni with a Raspberry Pi RP2040 chip inside. It has a 2.4 inch color screen, five front buttons, a light sensor and a plug-in sensor port. You program it in MicroPython with simple drawing tools or in C++. Pimoroni has retired it, and a newer Badgeware Tufty has replaced it.

Specs in plain words

  • Two 133 MHz cores are fast enough to draw menus, pictures and simple games.
  • 264 KB of working memory holds one screen of graphics and a small program.
  • 8 MB of storage has room for your code plus a few photos.
  • The 2.4 inch screen is about the size of a matchbox top, with 320 by 240 dots.
  • The badge draws about 100 mA, and most of that powers the screen.
  • The badge is a bit smaller than a playing card and thinner than a pencil.

Flash is a W25Q64JV (8 MB) per the schematic. No general GPIO header is exposed. The schematic shows a 10-way pad set with I2C, UART and SWD lines plus the Qw/ST port. As a result, gpio and adc_channels are null. The RP2040 ADC pins are used internally for the light sensor, a 1.24 V reference and battery voltage. Pimoroni gives about 100 mA total draw, 80 mA for the screen at full brightness and 20 mA for the RP2040. There is a white user LED but no RGB LED. There is no battery charger or battery protection. Size is 65.2 x 52.7 x 9.6 mm including connectors.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pimoroni publishes a schematic PDF and a size drawing with no license. The C++ and MicroPython libraries are open source under the MIT license, and the Pico SDK uses BSD-3-Clause. The badge has no radio and needs no closed firmware. The RP2040 chip design itself is closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: MIT (pimoroni-pico libraries and MicroPython build), BSD-3-Clause (Pico SDK)
