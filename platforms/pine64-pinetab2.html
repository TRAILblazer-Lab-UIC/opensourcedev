Overview

The PineTab2 is a 10.1-inch tablet from Pine64 that runs Linux. It comes with a detachable backlit keyboard that also works as a cover. It ships with DanctNix Arch Linux, and Pine64 calls that software beta. It suits tinkerers who want a repairable tablet and like helping fix software.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A hackable Linux tablet with a real keyboard
  • Testing mainline Linux kernels on ARM hardware
  • Learning Linux and Python on a portable device
  • Repair practice, with a battery and cameras that swap in about five minutes

Not great for

  • Everyday use as a polished tablet. The software is still beta.
  • Wireless projects. The built-in Wi-Fi driver often crashes and Bluetooth does not work yet.
  • Photos or video calls. The cameras have no working drivers yet.
  • Long standby. Suspend is turned off in the factory image.

Specs in plain words

  • The four-core 1.8 GHz processor handles web pages, text editing, and light apps.
  • 4 GB of memory fits a browser with a few tabs. The 8 GB model handles more at once.
  • 64 GB of built-in storage holds the system plus thousands of songs or books. A microSD slot adds more.
  • The 1280 by 800 screen is about the size of a sheet of paper folded in half.
  • The 6000 mAh battery is about two phone batteries.
  • At 538 grams, it weighs about as much as a hardcover book.

RAM and eMMC values are for the 4 GB / 64 GB base model, which was the one in stock. The 8 GB / 128 GB model doubles both. The BES2600 Bluetooth driver is not implemented yet, and the Wi-Fi driver often crashes. As a result, Pine64 suggests a USB Wi-Fi dongle. The PCIe flat flex connector is inside the case and there is no room for an M.2 drive. Weight is 538 g.

    Uses: Makes sound, Battery ready. Add: microSD card, wired headphones.

    Install an e-book reader app and fill a microSD card with books. The 10-inch screen fits a full comic page.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: microSD card.

  • Family photo frame

    Run a slideshow app from a folder of photos on a microSD card. Plug the micro HDMI port into a TV for a bigger show.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card, micro HDMI cable.

  • Community repair cafe demo

    Show visitors how the battery and keyboard connector swap out in minutes. It makes a good lesson on repairable gadgets.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Pine64 publishes the mainboard schematic and part placement drawings as PDFs. No layout files or hardware license are offered. The tablet runs mainline Linux under GPL-2.0, and the graphics use the open Panfrost driver. The Rockchip chip design is closed, and the Wi-Fi chip needs closed firmware.
