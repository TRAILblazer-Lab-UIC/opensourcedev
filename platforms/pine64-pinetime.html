Overview

The PineTime is an open smartwatch from Pine64. It ships with InfiniTime, a free community firmware, and pairs with open phone apps. It suits people who want a private watch and learners who want to write firmware for a real gadget. It costs about 27 dollars.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A low-cost watch with no company account needed
  • Step counting, heart rate, and phone notifications
  • Learning embedded C++, Rust, Zephyr, or MicroPython
  • Custom watch faces and small wrist apps

Not great for

  • Projects that need Wi-Fi or GPS
  • Wiring extra sensors, with no pins open to the user
  • Low-level hacking on the sealed watch. A bad firmware can brick it.
  • Buying from a local shop. Only the Pine64 store sells it.

Specs in plain words

  • The 64 MHz processor is quick enough for watch faces, menus and simple apps.
  • 64 KB of working memory is small. Apps must stay lean and simple.
  • The extra 4 MB of storage holds fonts, pictures and app files.
  • The 1.3 inch touch screen is about the size of a large postage stamp.
  • Bluetooth Low Energy links the watch to your phone and lets you update it over the air.
  • The 180 mAh battery lasts about a week between charges.

flash_kb is the 512 KB internal flash of the nRF52832. A 4 MB XTX XT25F32B SPI NOR flash sits beside it. The display uses an ST7789V2 controller on units built before May 2026 and an ST7789P3 controller from May 2026, per Pine64's datasheet page. The touch controller is a Hynitron CST816S. The accelerometer is a Bosch BMA425 (BMA421 on early units). The heart-rate sensor is a TianYiHeXin HRS3300 PPG sensor, and there is a vibration motor. Neither fits the onboard vocabulary. The watch has one side button. No GPIO pins are exposed to the user. SWD debug access needs the dev kit, whose case does not snap shut. The sealed watch can only be updated over the air. SPI and I2C are internal buses for the flash, display and sensors. The case is zinc alloy and plastic, weighs 38 g and is rated IP67.

Starter ideas

  • Your own private step counter

    Wear the watch with InfiniTime and count your daily steps. The data stays with you and your chosen phone app.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Phone notifications on your wrist

    Pair the watch with Gadgetbridge on Android. Messages and calls buzz on your wrist without a big company account.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Heart rate check during exercise

    Use the heart rate app during a walk or a gym class. Compare your resting and active numbers over a week.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Music remote for a phone

    Use the music app to pause and skip songs on your phone. It is handy while cooking or gardening with dirty hands.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Class science on movement

    Have students wear the watch for a day of school. Compare step counts from recess, gym, and quiet reading time.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Design a custom watch face

    Change the colors and layout of a watch face in the InfiniTime code. Flash it over the air and show it off.

    Uses: Screen, Bluetooth.

  • Garden watering reminder

    Add a simple app that buzzes at set times to water the plants. A short note on the screen says which bed is next.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Medicine reminder watch

    Build a gentle reminder app for daily pills. The watch buzzes and asks you to tap once each dose is taken.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Wrist quiz game

    Write a tiny trivia or math quiz game for the touch screen. Kids can tap answers and earn points on a bus ride.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Try a Python watch system

    Flash wasp-os onto the dev kit and write watch apps in MicroPython. It is a gentle way into embedded code.

    Uses: Screen. Add: debug probe for flashing.

  • Hiking interval timer

    Make an app that buzzes every set number of minutes. It reminds hikers to drink water or check the map.

    Uses: Battery ready, Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Gesture remote for slides

    Use the accelerometer to spot a wrist flick. Send the flick to a computer over Bluetooth to change slides.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Sleep motion journal

    Log wrist movement through the night. Sync the log to a laptop and chart how restless each night was.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth, Battery ready.

  • Learn Rust or Zephyr on a real gadget

    Build your own firmware with Rust or Zephyr on the dev kit. Debug it step by step over the SWD pins.

    Add: debug probe for flashing.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pine64 publishes the schematic as a PDF, but no board layout or hardware license. InfiniTime is GPL-3.0, and fully open systems such as Zephyr, RIOT, and TinyGo also run on it. The Nordic chip design and parts of its SDK stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: GPL-3.0 (InfiniTime), FreeRTOS, LVGL and NimBLE under MIT or Apache-2.0
