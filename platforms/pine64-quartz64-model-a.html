Overview

The Quartz64 Model A is a single-board computer from Pine64 built on the Rockchip RK3566 chip. It comes with 4 or 8 GB of memory and runs Linux. Its standout part is an open-ended PCIe slot for storage or network cards. It suits makers with some Linux skills who want a small server or a display project.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Small home servers and network storage
  • Projects with E Ink, eDP, or MIPI touch screens
  • Arm Linux development on a low-cost board
  • Builds that need a fast SSD over PCIe

Not great for

  • First-time users. The software is still maturing.
  • Wireless projects out of the box. Wi-Fi comes only on an optional module.
  • Projects with many GPIO pins. The header has only 12 signal pins.
  • Pi add-on boards. The header layout is different.

Specs in plain words

  • The four-core processor handles a home server, a light desktop, or video at 4K on HDMI.
  • 4 GB of memory runs a small web app, a file server, and a few services at once.
  • The PCIe slot takes one lane of speed. It is plenty for an SSD or a SATA card.
  • Gigabit Ethernet moves a large movie file across the house in under a minute.
  • The 0.8 TOPS NPU can speed up small image models.
  • The board is a little smaller than a postcard.

RAM is the 4 GB model. An 8 GB model is also sold. The device page lists 2.0 GHz. By contrast, the specifications page lists 1.8 GHz. As a result, the lower figure is shown. The GPIO header is 2x10. The device page calls it 40-pin, but the specifications page lists 20 pins with 12 signal pins. Storage is a 128 Mbit SPI flash plus microSD or an optional eMMC module. As a result, flash_kb is null. A native SATA port shares lanes with USB 3.0 and was removed in newer revisions. Size 133 x 80 x 19 mm is from the Pine64 schematics page. Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.0 come only through an optional module. As a result, they are not listed as radios.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Pine64 publishes the schematic and a connector drawing as PDFs. No layout files are offered and no license is stated. Linux and U-Boot are open under GPL-2.0, and the graphics use the open Panfrost driver. The Rockchip chip design is closed, and the NPU tools are Rockchip releases.
