Pixy2

Charmed Labs

A smart little camera that finds colors and lines for your robot.

Field marks: Press one button to teach this small camera a color, and it reports position updates 60 times a second under its own 20 lumen lamp.

Overview

Pixy2 is a small vision sensor from Charmed Labs. It does the hard picture work on its own chip and sends only simple answers to your main board. Your Arduino learns where a colored object sits, how big it is, and which color it matched. It suits robot builders, teachers, and FIRST teams who want vision without heavy code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Line-following and object-chasing robots
  • Pan-tilt trackers that follow a colored target
  • Classroom robotics with Arduino or LEGO Mindstorms
  • Teams that want a simple vision tool with good examples

Not great for

  • Recognizing faces, people, or animals by shape
  • Rooms with changing light, as color tracking gets confused
  • Taking photos or streaming video to a screen
  • Buyers outside big maker stores, as some shops list it as discontinued

Specs in plain words

  • 60 frames per second means it checks the scene about twice as fast as a movie shows frames.
  • 264 KB of working memory is enough to track many colored blobs but far too little for AI models.
  • The lens sees 60 degrees across, about the view of a narrow doorway from a few steps back.
  • It draws about 140 mA, a light load that a normal Arduino can supply over USB.
  • The board is about 42 by 38 millimeters and weighs 10 grams, about the weight of two coins.
  • Two white LEDs give a little light for dim corners.

Image sensor is an Aptina (now onsemi) MT9M114 at 1296 x 976 with an integrated image flow processor. Lens field of view is 60 degrees horizontal and 40 degrees vertical. Typical draw is 140 mA. Weight is 10 g. Outputs listed are UART, SPI, I2C, USB, digital and analog. Two white LEDs give about 20 lumens. The GPIO count is not published as a number.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Follow the colored ball

    Teach Pixy2 a bright ball with one button press. Your robot then rolls toward the ball wherever it goes.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small robot car kit, colored ball.

  • Line-following race car

    Use the line tracking mode to steer a small car along a tape track. Race friends on the classroom floor.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small robot car kit, electrical tape.

  • Pan-tilt face of a pet toy

    Mount Pixy2 on a pan-tilt kit and let it follow a colored toy. It looks like a curious little head turning to watch.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: pan-tilt kit with two servo motors.

  • Music by color cards

    Hold up colored cards in front of the lens. Each color plays a different note on a buzzer or speaker.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Arduino board, buzzer or small speaker, colored cards.

  • LEGO robot with eyes

    Use the LEGO version with a Mindstorms brick. Students build a robot that hunts for a colored target.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: LEGO Mindstorms set.

Builder ideas

  • Color sorting station

    Spot the color of each block on a ramp and flip a servo gate. Red blocks go left and blue blocks go right.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: servo motor, cardboard ramp, colored blocks.

  • Barcode treasure hunt

    Print the small Pixy barcodes and tape them around a room. A robot reads each code and turns toward the next clue.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small robot car kit, printed barcodes.

  • Track crossing maze runner

    Use the intersection tracking to find turns in a tape maze. Your robot picks a path at each crossing.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small robot car kit, electrical tape.

  • Bird feeder color counter

    Teach Pixy2 the color of a bright bird species. Count each visit at the feeder and log it on a small computer.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi or Arduino, weatherproof box.

  • Science class motion tracker

    Track a colored cart rolling down a ramp at 60 frames a second. Plot its position to study speed and slope.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Arduino board, toy cart with a colored sticker.

  • Light show that follows you

    Wear a colored wristband and wave your arm. An LED strip lights up under the spot where Pixy2 sees it.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Arduino board, LED strip.

Expert ideas

  • Competition robot targeting

    Connect Pixy2 to a roboRIO and aim a robot at colored game pieces. It is a common trick in FIRST robotics.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: competition robot controller.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The host software, Arduino library, and firmware source are open under the GPL version 2 license. The repo shares the schematic, Gerber board files, a parts list, and pan-tilt models, but it names no hardware license. Rebuilding the camera firmware needs the closed Keil tools, and the NXP chip itself is closed.

Hardware license: Not stated. Wiki says hardware is open. Repo LICENSE.md is GPL-2.0 for source code. · Software license: GPL-2.0
