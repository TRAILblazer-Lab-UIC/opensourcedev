Overview

The Teensy Audio Adaptor is a small add-on board for the Teensy microcontroller. It adds a headphone jack, stereo line in and out, a microphone input, and a microSD card slot. It suits makers and musicians who want to build synths, effects and audio players. Its biggest strength is the free Teensy Audio Library with a drag and drop design tool.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Synthesizers and drum machines
  • Guitar and voice effects
  • Playing and recording WAV files on a microSD card
  • Sound analysis projects such as tuners and light shows

Not great for

  • Working alone, as it needs a Teensy board to run
  • Complete beginners, as it needs basic soldering
  • Wireless audio, as it has no radio
  • Buyers in a hurry, as revisions are easy to mix up

Specs in plain words

  • 16-bit sound at 44.1 kHz is the same quality as a music CD.
  • The headphone jack has its own small amplifier for wired headphones.
  • The microphone input is ready, but you add the microphone yourself.
  • The microSD slot holds hours of songs and recordings.
  • The board is about 38 mm square, about the size of a postage stamp.
  • Rev D fits Teensy 4.0 and 4.1. The older Rev C fits Teensy 3 boards only.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorNone onboard. NXP SGTL5000 audio codec controlled by a Teensy 4.x (or Teensy 3.x for Rev C)

Ways to connect partsI2C, Digital audio (I2S), SPI

Power3.3 V from the host Teensy
Size38.0 x 36.8 mm

The board has no processor of its own. The codec takes control over I2C and audio over I2S from the Teensy. The SD card and the optional memory chips use SPI. Optional parts are a W25Q128JV or FV 16 MB flash chip and a 23LC1024 1 Mbit RAM chip for delays up to 1.5 seconds. They are not fitted on the standard board. As a result, flash_kb is null. The mic input is present, but a microphone is not fitted by default. ProtoSupplies sells a variant with a mic added. Size is from Adafruit (38.0 x 36.8 x 6.7 mm with the jack, 5.1 g). ProtoSupplies gives 35 x 37 mm. ProtoSupplies lists typical current of 16 mA and up to 50 to 60 mA with an SD card at full headphone volume.

Starter ideas

  • Play your first tone

    Load the tutorial example and hear a clean tone in your headphones. It shows how the audio library connects blocks together.

  • Pocket music player

    Copy songs as WAV files onto a microSD card. Press buttons to play, skip, and pause.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, microSD card, push buttons, headphones.

  • Sound effects box for game night

    Store buzzers, cheers, and drum rolls on a microSD card. Each button plays a different sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, microSD card, push buttons, small powered speaker.

  • Talking museum exhibit

    Play a short spoken story at the press of a button. Use it for a school science fair or a community display.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, microSD card, push button, small powered speaker.

Builder ideas

  • Simple synthesizer

    Draw a synth in the browser design tool with oscillators and filters. Turn knobs to shape the sound as you play.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, knobs, push buttons, headphones.

  • Field recorder for bird songs

    Record sound from a microphone straight to a microSD card. Take it outside at dawn and catch the morning chorus.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, small microphone, microSD card, battery pack.

  • Sound level meter for class

    Measure how loud the room gets and show it on a row of lights. Students can test which spots are quiet.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, small microphone, LED strip.

  • Music light show

    Split music into bass, middle, and treble bands. Make an LED strip dance to each band.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, LED strip, audio cable.

  • Guitar tuner

    Detect the pitch of a plucked string. Show on a small screen whether the note is sharp or flat.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, small screen, audio cable.

  • Drum machine

    Play drum samples in a loop and tap pads to add beats. Save patterns and jam along.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, push buttons, small powered speaker.

Expert ideas

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Feed a guitar signal into the line input and add echo, chorus or distortion. A foot switch turns each effect on and off.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, guitar preamp board, foot switch, project box.

  • Long echo with extra memory

    Solder the optional memory chip onto the board. It stores up to about one and a half seconds of sound for big echoes.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, small memory chip.

  • Four channel audio rig

    Stack two boards for four channels of sound in and out. Record a small band or test surround sound.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: Teensy 4 board, second audio board.
