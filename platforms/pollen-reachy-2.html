Pollen Reachy 2

Pollen Robotics (Hugging Face)

A two-armed humanoid research robot with open Python and ROS 2 software.

Overview

Reachy 2 is a humanoid research robot from Pollen Robotics, a French company Hugging Face bought in 2025. It has two human-sized arms, a moving head with cameras and an optional wheeled base. Universities use it to study robot hands-on skills and to collect training data for AI models. It is sold by quote, and the 2025 announced price was about 70,000 US dollars.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Research labs studying robot arms and grasping
  • Collecting demonstration data for imitation learning
  • Teleoperation with a VR headset
  • Teams that already write Python or use ROS 2

Not great for

  • Hobbyists, schools or home makers on a normal budget
  • Anyone looking for open hardware to build or copy
  • Beginners who have never written a program
  • Heavy AI work on the robot itself, which Pollen sends to outside computers

Specs in plain words

  • Each arm has seven joints, about the same as a human arm from shoulder to wrist.
  • Each arm can lift about 3 kg, roughly a full gallon jug of milk.
  • The mobile base runs about 8 hours on its battery, which covers a full workday.
  • The robot stands 136 to 166 cm tall, about the height of a teenager or a short adult.
  • The head has stereo cameras and a depth sensor, which let it judge how far away things are.
  • An industrial PC inside runs Linux, and your own computer talks to it over the network.

Pollen docs name the onboard computer as a fanless SolidRun Bedrock V3000 and give no CPU model, RAM or storage. The Bedrock V3000 line uses AMD Ryzen Embedded V3000 CPUs with up to 8 cores and up to 96 GB DDR5. The Reachy 2 configuration is unknown. Pollen says heavy AI processing runs on the customer's own computers. Interfaces, radios and connectors reflect the Bedrock V3000 product page, where Wi-Fi is optional, and are not confirmed for Reachy 2. Head cameras are Arducam 1.58 MP IMX296 global shutter modules with an OAK-FFC 4P encoder and an OAK-FFC ToF 33D depth module. The mobile base is 50 cm across, 25 cm tall and weighs 25 kg. The full robot weighs up to 50 kg. It comes in four configurations: one or two arms on a fixed or mobile base.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Wave hello to lab visitors

    Write a short Python script that lifts an arm and waves. It makes a friendly first test of the SDK.

    Uses: Drives motors.

Builder ideas

  • Practice moves in simulation

    Run the MuJoCo digital twin on your computer and test arm motions there first. Mistakes cost nothing in the simulator.

  • Drive it with a VR headset

    Use the teleoperation app to see through the head cameras and move the arms with your own hands. It feels like stepping into the robot.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: VR headset.

  • Expressive emotions show

    Play back the emotion demos with head tilts and arm gestures. It works well for school visits and science fairs.

    Uses: Drives motors.

Expert ideas

  • Record demos for robot learning

    Guide the robot through a task by VR many times and save each run. Those recordings become training data for an AI model.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: VR headset, computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Teach it to fold a towel

    Train an imitation learning model on your recorded folding demos. Then let the robot try the folding on its own.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Sort objects on a table

    Use the torso depth camera to find cups, blocks or fruit. Then pick each one up and place it in the right bin.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small bins.

  • Fetch items across the room

    Drive the mobile base to a shelf, grab a light object and bring it back. The LiDAR helps it avoid bumping into things.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Hand tools to a person

    Detect an open hand with the cameras and place a light object in it. Labs use this to study safe teamwork with people.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors.

  • Watering helper for indoor plants

    Roll to each potted plant and tip a small watering can over the soil. Keep the can light and well under three kilograms.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready, Camera. Add: small watering can.

  • Two-handed teamwork tasks

    Coordinate both arms to open a jar or hold a box steady. It is a classic test for humanoid research.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Connect it to your ROS 2 projects

    Link the robot to other ROS 2 tools such as mapping and planning packages. Your lab can reuse code from the wider ROS world.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network.

How open is it?

Open Code

The software is open under the Apache 2.0 license, including the Python SDK, the ROS 2 stack, the simulation files and the VR app. Most of the hardware is closed, with no full CAD, schematics or board files published for the whole robot. The one hardware release is the Orbita 3D joint boards, and those use a non-commercial CC BY-NC-SA license on a patented mechanism.

Hardware license: CC BY-NC-SA (Orbita 3D electronics) · Software license: Apache-2.0
