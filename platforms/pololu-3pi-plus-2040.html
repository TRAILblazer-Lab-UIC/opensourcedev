Overview

The 3pi+ 2040 is a small round robot from Pololu, about the size of a coaster. It runs on the same RP2040 chip as the Raspberry Pi Pico and comes with MicroPython already loaded. It packs line sensors, bump sensors, a screen, and motion sensors onto one tidy body. It suits classrooms, robotics clubs, and anyone who wants a ready robot to program.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Line following and maze solving
  • Learning MicroPython on a real moving robot
  • Robotics classes and contests
  • Lessons on motion, measuring, and control

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on board
  • Carrying heavy loads or driving outdoors on rough ground
  • Camera or voice projects
  • Very new drivers on the Hyper edition, which is fast enough to damage itself in a crash

Specs in plain words

  • The robot is 9.7 cm across, about the size of a drink coaster.
  • 264 KB of memory holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 16 MB of storage fits many program files and small screen images.
  • Four AAA batteries power the motors, and a regulator keeps the speed steady as they drain.
  • Three editions trade speed for control, from about 0.4 to about 4 meters per second.
Full spec sheet

Memory264 KB
Storage16 MB
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), USB device, Debug port
ConnectorsUSB-C, Pin headers, Battery plug
Built inmotion sensor, screen, buttons, color LED, motor driver
Power4 AAA cells (NiMH recommended), or 5 V over USB-C for logic only
Size97 diameter mm

Default clock is 125 MHz, rated to 133 MHz. RAM is the RP2040 internal SRAM. The motor regulator gives 8 V to the DRV8838 drivers and a buck regulator gives 3.3 V logic with up to 1.5 A spare. USB powers the logic but not the motors. Expansion is through 0.1 inch headers with power rails and I/O. The free GPIO count was not stated. The interface list reflects RP2040 peripherals reachable through the expansion headers and is not confirmed pin by pin. RobotShop lists the Hyper edition at 100 g.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Follow a black tape line

    Lay a loop of electrical tape on the floor and run the line following example. Then tweak the speed and watch how it handles curves.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: black electrical tape.

  • Light show with six LEDs

    Make the six color LEDs flash, fade, and chase each other. It is a gentle first MicroPython program.

  • Bump and turn explorer

    Drive forward until a front bump sensor gets pressed. Then back up, turn, and try a new direction.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Name badge on the screen

    Show your name or a small drawing on the OLED screen. Add a button press to switch between pictures.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Play a tune on the buzzer

    Program a short song that plays at the start of each run. Students can compose their own tunes.

  • Tilt alarm guard

    Park the robot on a desk and use the accelerometer to sense a nudge. The buzzer sounds and the LEDs flash red.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

Builder ideas

  • Solve a tape maze

    Build a maze of tape lines with dead ends and let the robot learn the route. On the second run it takes the shortest path.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: black electrical tape, large poster board.

  • Draw shapes with a marker

    Tape a marker to the robot and use the wheel encoders to drive exact squares and stars. Hang the art on the wall.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: washable marker, large paper.

  • Spin to a compass heading

    Use the magnetometer and gyro to turn and face north. Show the heading in degrees on the screen.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Measure the hallway

    Count wheel turns to measure how far the robot drives. Compare its answer with a tape measure for a math lesson.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: tape measure.

  • Line racing contest

    Tune your line follower for the fastest lap and race classmates on the same track. The screen can show lap times.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors, Screen. Add: black electrical tape.

  • Garden path patrol

    Mount a small light sensor on the expansion header and drive along an indoor seed tray row. Log light levels at each stop.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: light sensor.

Expert ideas

  • Smooth driving with PID control

    Write your own steering math to keep the robot dead center on the line. It is a classic control lesson for older students.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Swarm of follow-the-leader bots

    Add a distance sensor to each robot and have them trail one another in a line. A class of robots becomes a little parade.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: distance sensor.

  • Faster code in C

    Rewrite a program with the Pico SDK in C to squeeze out extra speed. Compare lap times against the MicroPython version.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pololu publishes a PDF schematic, a pinout, dimension drawings, and 3D models of the board and chassis. The example code is open under the MIT license, and the tools are open MicroPython, the Pico SDK, and Arduino. No board layout files or hardware license are published, and the RP2040 chip itself is closed.
