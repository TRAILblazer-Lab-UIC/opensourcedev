Overview

The Zumo 32U4 is a small tracked robot from Pololu made for mini-sumo contests. It runs an Arduino-compatible chip and carries line sensors, proximity sensors, motion sensors, and a small screen. A steel blade on the front helps it push rivals around. It suits robotics clubs, classes, and anyone learning Arduino on a sturdy robot.

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on board
  • Camera, voice, or AI projects
  • Total beginners who want a no-setup start

Specs in plain words

  • The robot is under 10 cm on each side, about the size of a small sandwich.
  • 2.5 KB of working memory holds a few hundred sensor numbers at once.
  • 32 KB of program space fits a solid sumo or line following program.
  • The 16 MHz chip is the same one inside the Arduino Leonardo.
  • Four AA batteries power it, and the full robot weighs about 275 g with them.

Specs are for the Zumo 32U4 OLED. RAM is 2560 bytes. The bootloader uses 4 KB of the 32 KB flash. Weight is 160 g without batteries and about 275 g with them. USB is a Micro-B port, inferred from the required USB A to Micro-B cable. Expansion is through 0.1 inch through-holes on the sides and front. The free GPIO count was not stated. The interface list reflects ATmega32U4 peripherals and is not confirmed pin by pin. The original LCD version uses the same MCU with an 8x2 character LCD.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Stay inside the ring

    Use the downward sensors to spot a white edge and back away from it. It is the first skill every sumo bot needs.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: black poster board, white tape.

  • Follow a tape line

    Run the line following example on a loop of black tape. Then adjust the speed to handle tighter turns.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: black electrical tape.

  • Buzzer songs and beeps

    Play a short tune at startup and a victory beep after each win. Students can write their own melodies.

  • Show stats on the screen

    Print battery level and sensor readings on the OLED screen. It helps you see what the robot senses.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Mini-sumo wrestling match

    Program the robot to find an opponent and push it out of a ring. The proximity sensors spot the rival and the blade does the shoving.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: mini-sumo ring or black circle with white edge.

  • Solve a tape maze

    Lay out a maze of tape lines and let the robot explore it. Teach it to remember the shortest route home.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: black electrical tape, large poster board.

  • Drive exact squares

    Use the wheel encoders to drive straight lines and sharp corners. Compare the result to a drawn square on the floor.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Hand follower pet

    Hold your hand in front and the proximity sensors steer the robot toward it. It acts like a curious little pet.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Ramp and tilt explorer

    Read the accelerometer and gyro to sense climbs and tilts. The robot backs off a slope that gets too steep.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: small wooden ramp.

  • Obstacle course runner

    Build a course from books and boxes and use the side sensors to dodge them. Time each run on the screen.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors, Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Smooth steering with PID

    Write your own control math to follow a line fast and smooth. It is a classic lesson for older students.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Sumo strategy tournament

    Code several attack styles and test them in a class bracket. Teams compare which plan wins most often.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: mini-sumo ring.

How open is it?

Open Board

Pololu publishes a PDF schematic, a pinout, drawings, and 3D models of the robot. The Arduino library is open under the MIT license, and the Arduino tools are open too. No board layout files or hardware license are published, and the ATmega32U4 chip is closed.
