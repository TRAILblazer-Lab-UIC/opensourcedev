Overview

Poppy Ergo Jr is a small robot arm with six joints. Its body is made of simple 3D-printed parts held together with plastic rivets. A Raspberry Pi runs the arm, and kids can program it with Snap! blocks in a web browser. It was popular in French schools, but the project has been quiet for a few years.

  • Lifting heavy things, as the small servos suit light loads
  • Finding fresh help, as the official websites no longer load

Specs in plain words

  • Six smart servos act as joints. Each one reports its own position back to the Pi.
  • A Raspberry Pi 2 or 3 is the brain. It runs Linux and serves the coding tools to your browser.
  • The Pixl board sits on the Pi. It powers everything from a 7.5 V supply and talks to the servos.
  • A Raspberry Pi camera lets the arm see colors and objects.
  • Three swappable tools come in the design: a lampshade, a gripper, and a pen holder.

Specs describe the Raspberry Pi 3 Model B, which the README says works alongside the original Raspberry Pi 2. The Pi boots from an 8 GB microSD card with the Poppy image. Generation Robots says the Pixl board is not compatible with the Raspberry Pi 3B+. As a result, newer Pi models may not work without changes. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth exist only on the Pi 3. The Pi 2 has none. The Pixl board converts the 5 V TTL half-duplex Dynamixel bus to the Pi's 3.3 V UART and is designed for six motors.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Robot desk lamp

    Snap on the lampshade tool and program the arm to aim light at your page. Move it with blocks in Snap!.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: small LED light.

  • Drawing robot

    Clip a pen into the pen holder. The arm draws shapes and letters on paper.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: felt-tip pen, paper.

  • Record and replay a dance

    Move the arm by hand while it records each servo position. Play the motion back to music.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Process control for young kids

    Children plan a step-by-step task in blocks and watch the arm carry it out. CERN ran workshops like this for 7 year olds.

    Uses: Drives motors.

Builder ideas

  • Color sorting helper

    The camera spots red, green, and blue blocks. The gripper drops each one into its own cup.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small colored blocks, cups.

  • Friendly greeter

    The camera notices motion near the door. The arm waves hello to visitors.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors.

  • Phone remote control

    Send commands to the robot's REST API over Wi-Fi. Build a web page with buttons for each move.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Python lessons with Jupyter

    Open a Jupyter notebook in the browser and move each servo with pypot. Graph joint angles as the arm moves.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Practice in a simulator

    Run a virtual Ergo Jr in CoppeliaSim. Test a program on screen before the real arm moves.

  • Seed planting arm

    Use the gripper to drop seeds into a tray of soil cells. The arm repeats the pattern for each row.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: seed tray, seeds.

  • Science time-lapse camera

    The arm points the camera at a plant or crystal at set times. Stitch the photos into a growth video.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • Tic-tac-toe opponent

    The camera reads the board and the arm places its pieces. Play against the robot at school.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: game board, game pieces.
