Overview

The PortaPack H4M is an add-on board for the HackRF One radio. It adds a 3.2 inch screen, a click-wheel, a speaker, a microphone, and a battery. Together they make a handheld radio lab that works with no computer. It suits curious tinkerers, radio fans, and RF security learners.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Exploring the radio spectrum from 1 MHz to 6 GHz on the go
  • Tracking planes and catching weather satellite signals
  • Teaching how invisible radio waves fill the air
  • Learning radio with a big open firmware community

Not great for

  • Casual transmitting, as most bands need a license and local rules apply
  • Buyers who want genuine HackRF hardware, as most kits use clones
  • Total beginners who want a plug-and-play radio with one simple menu
  • Projects that need many extra pins, as the GPIO port is small

Specs in plain words

  • It hears from 1 MHz to 6 GHz. That spans AM radio up past Wi-Fi.
  • The HackRF can listen or send, but only one at a time.
  • The 3.2 inch screen is about the size of a playing card corner. It shows menus and a live signal waterfall.
  • The battery chip reports charge, voltage, and current. You always know how much power is left.
  • The microSD slot stores apps, maps, and signal recordings.
  • The brain is a 204 MHz chip on the HackRF with 200 KB of working memory. That is enough for its radio apps but small for anything heavy.

The H4M has no main processor of its own. Mayhem runs on the HackRF One MCU, an NXP LPC43xx (HackRF docs link the LPC4330. NXP lists the LPC4320 at 204 MHz with 200 KB SRAM). Flash is the HackRF W25Q80BV 8 Mbit SPI flash. The HackRF covers 1 MHz to 6 GHz, half duplex, 2 to 20 Msps, 8-bit samples. radios lists sub_ghz only because the vocab has no general SDR value. The real coverage is 1 MHz to 6 GHz receive and transmit. The GPIO port carries I2C. The number of pins was not confirmed. A store link cited by the Mayhem wiki gives the battery as 2500 mAh. Size and weight were not found on a fetched page.

Starter ideas

  • Pocket FM and AM radio

    Tune local broadcast stations and listen through the built-in speaker or headphones. It makes a fun first test of the antennas.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen, Battery ready. Add: headphones.

  • Plane spotter in your hand

    Run the ADS-B receiver app to list nearby aircraft. Match the flight codes to the planes passing overhead.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Signal rainbow explorer

    Open the spectrum view and walk around your home. Watch your own Wi-Fi router and remotes light up the waterfall.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Find your remote's frequency

    Press your own car key fob or doorbell button near the receiver. The waterfall shows the exact band it uses.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Ham radio listening post

    Listen to amateur radio operators on local bands. You can learn the lingo before you study for a license.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen.

  • Classroom radio wave lesson

    Show students how phones, radios, and Wi-Fi sit at different spots on the spectrum. The live waterfall makes invisible waves visible.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Nature night radio walk

    Carry it on a night hike and listen to distant stations bounce in after dark. Note how far signals travel at night.

    Uses: Makes sound, Battery ready. Add: headphones.

Builder ideas

  • Weather satellite pictures

    Listen for passing weather satellites with a suitable outdoor antenna. Save the signal and turn it into a cloud picture.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: satellite antenna, microSD card.

  • Quiet channel finder for a mesh

    Scan the licence-free LoRa band before setting up your own Meshtastic nodes. Pick the calmest channel for your group.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Signal recordings for study

    Record a slice of the spectrum to the microSD card. Open it later on a computer with free radio software.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Garden weather sensor check

    Watch the signal from your own wireless garden weather station. See how often it reports and how strong it is across the yard.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Battery life experiment

    Read the charge, voltage, and current from the battery chip as you use different apps. Graph which apps drain it fastest.

    Uses: Battery ready, Sensors built in.

  • Antenna comparison day

    Try several receive antennas on the same station. Compare the signal strength on the screen and pick a winner.

    Uses: Screen. Add: telescopic antenna, small outdoor antenna.

Expert ideas

  • I2C add-on module

    Wire a small sensor to the GPIO port and read it from your own firmware build. It is a deep dive into embedded C.

    Uses: Screen. Add: I2C sensor module, jumper wires.

  • Write your own Mayhem app

    Build the open Mayhem firmware on a computer and add a simple receive-only app. Share it back with the community.

    Uses: Screen.
