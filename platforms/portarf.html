Overview

PortaRF is a handheld software-defined radio from OpenSourceSDRLab. It puts a HackRF One radio and a PortaPack screen on one board with a battery. It can receive and transmit from 1 MHz to 6 GHz with no computer. Radio hobbyists, students, and security researchers use it to explore signals in the air.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Tracking planes and ships from their radio beacons
  • Seeing which radio bands are busy around you
  • Taking radio experiments outdoors on battery power
  • Learning radio with ready-made apps and no coding

Not great for

  • Transmitting without a license, which is illegal in most countries
  • Sending and receiving at the same moment, as it is half duplex
  • Buyers in a hurry, as it ships from China with no US store found
  • Running your own code without C skills

Specs in plain words

  • A range of 1 MHz to 6 GHz covers AM radio, FM radio, aircraft, ships, Wi-Fi bands, and more.
  • Half duplex means it either listens or talks at one time, like a walkie-talkie.
  • A 4 inch touch screen is about the size of an old smartphone screen.
  • A 3000 mAh battery lets it work away from a wall outlet for field trips.
  • 2 MB of firmware storage holds the many Mayhem apps in one image.

Radio covers 1 MHz to 6 GHz half duplex, the same as the HackRF One. Flash is the 2 MB W25Q16 SPI flash. RAM is the LPC4320 on-chip SRAM, the same figure used for the PortaPack H4M card. The 204 MHz clock is the LPC43xx rated maximum and was not stated on the fetched PortaRF pages. A CR2032 cell keeps the real-time clock running. Dimensions were listed as TBD in the CNX article. The radios list uses sub_ghz to flag the wideband SDR. It also transmits and receives up to 6 GHz. The I2C entry reflects the expansion slot used by the AI MDK board.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Track planes overhead

    Open the ADS-B app and watch nearby aircraft appear with their flight codes. Take it outside on battery power.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: ADS-B antenna.

  • Watch ships near the coast

    Tune the AIS app at a harbor or beach. See ship names and positions on the screen.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: marine VHF antenna.

  • Listen to local radio

    Tune FM stations and other broadcasts with the knob. Hear them through the speaker or headphones.

    Uses: Makes sound, Screen. Add: headphones.

  • Map the airwaves at home

    Use the spectrum view to see which bands are busy. Learn how Wi-Fi, phones, and remotes fill the air.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Pocket music player

    Load songs onto the microSD card. Play them with the built-in MP3 player app.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Catch a weather balloon

    Receive radiosonde signals from weather balloons. Read their height, temperature, and position as they rise.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: better antenna.

  • Hunt down interference

    Walk around with the spectrum display to find a noisy gadget. Fix the device that spoils your radio reception.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready. Add: directional antenna.

  • Computer radio lab

    Plug it into a computer over USB-C and use it as a HackRF. Run desktop radio software for bigger screens and recordings.

    Add: USB-C cable.

  • Voice-controlled radio

    Add the AI MDK board and speak commands to change apps. The voice feature is still in beta.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in. Add: AI MDK extension board.

  • Classroom radio science

    Show students how sound turns into radio waves on the screen. Compare AM and FM signals side by side.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

Expert ideas

  • Ham radio experiments

    Licensed hams can try transmit modes on their bands. Beginners should stay with receive-only apps.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Screen. Add: band antenna, amateur radio license.

  • Custom firmware hacking

    Build the open Mayhem firmware with GCC and add your own app. Flash it from the microSD card.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

The schematic, PCB layout, and Gerber files are open under Apache-2.0. The design comes from the HackRF One, which is open under CERN-OHL-P-2.0. The Mayhem and HackRF firmware is GPL, and the chips themselves are closed.
