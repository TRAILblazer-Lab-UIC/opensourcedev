PSoC 5LP

Overview

PSoC 5LP is a microcontroller from Infineon, once made by Cypress. It pairs an Arm processor with analog parts and logic blocks you can rewire in software. The CY8CKIT-059 kit is a cheap stick-shaped board that teachers like for mixed-signal lessons. It suits students and engineers who want precise sensor readings and custom hardware in one chip.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Very precise sensor readings with the 20-bit analog reader
  • Building custom timers, counters, and serial ports from logic blocks
  • Touch buttons and sliders with no extra chips
  • Low-power projects that sleep most of the time

Not great for

  • Mac or Linux users, as PSoC Creator runs only on Windows
  • Wireless projects, as it has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Quick beginner wins, as the tools take time to learn
  • Fully open workflows, as the design tool is closed

Specs in plain words

  • An 80 MHz Arm Cortex-M3 runs your program at a steady pace for sensor and control jobs.
  • 256 KB of flash holds a program of several thousand lines.
  • 64 KB of memory stores thousands of sensor readings at once.
  • A 20-bit analog reader can sense changes of about one millionth of its full range.
  • Up to 24 logic blocks work like tiny custom chips for timers, PWM, and serial ports.
  • Sleep mode draws about 2 microamps, a tiny trickle for batteries.

Values are for the CY8C5888LTI-LP097 on the CY8CKIT-059, from the CY8C58LP datasheet Rev. *L (April 2017). That 68-QFN part has 48 I/O, of which 38 are GPIO, 8 are SIO and 2 are USB I/O. The 100-pin CY8C5888AXI-LP096 has 72 I/O. CY8C52LP, 54LP and 56LP parts have less flash, fewer UDBs or slower clocks. adc_channels is null because analog inputs route from almost any GPIO through the analog mux. UART, SPI, I2S and LCD are built from UDBs or GPIO drive. I2C, USB and CAN are fixed blocks. low_power_ua is sleep mode. Hibernate with RAM retention is 300 nA. size_mm is the kit's 4.3 x 0.9 inch board including the snap-away KitProg. Connectors are the kit's target micro-USB, KitProg USB edge plug and headers.

Things you could build

Builder ideas

  • Kitchen scale from scratch

    Wire a load cell to the 20-bit analog reader. Weigh flour and sugar down to a gram.

    Add: load cell, small platform.

  • Touch slider lamp

    Make a strip of copper tape into a touch slider. Slide a finger to dim an LED lamp.

    Add: copper tape, LED strip.

  • Keyboard synthesizer

    Build tone makers out of the built-in logic blocks. Play notes from a row of buttons through a speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: push buttons, small speaker, amplifier board.

  • Classroom voltmeter

    Measure batteries and circuits with the precise analog reader. Show the result on a small display.

    Uses: Screen. Add: segment LCD, test leads.

  • Sound level meter

    Feed a microphone through the built-in opamps. Light up bars as the room gets louder.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone, LEDs.

  • Battery garden logger

    Read soil moisture once an hour and sleep in between. The board sips power on batteries for weeks.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • Motor speed counter

    Use a built-in quadrature decoder to count turns of a wheel. Show how fast a fan or robot wheel spins.

    Add: rotary encoder.

  • Home weather station

    Read temperature, humidity, and pressure sensors over I2C. Show the readings on a segment display.

    Uses: Screen. Add: weather sensor board, segment LCD.

Expert ideas

  • Pulse sensor front end

    Clean up a finger pulse sensor signal with the built-in opamps and filter. Count heartbeats per minute.

    Add: optical pulse sensor.

  • Custom USB game controller

    Turn buttons and a joystick into a USB device for your computer. Play games with a homemade controller.

    Add: arcade buttons, joystick.

  • Pocket signal generator

    Make waves with the built-in DACs. Test audio circuits in a school electronics lab.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio jack, potentiometer.

  • Mixed-signal design class

    Draw a circuit in PSoC Creator and watch it come alive inside one chip. Students learn analog and digital design together.

How open is it?

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

SparkFun's FreeSoC2 board is OSHWA certified with hardware under a CERN license and code under MIT. Its Eagle files remain on GitHub, though the board is retired. The Infineon kit guide includes schematics, but PSoC Creator is closed freeware. The chip and its logic fabric are proprietary.
