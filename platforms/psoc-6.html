PSoC 6

Infineon

A dual-core Infineon chip family known for touch sensing and secure IoT.

Overview

PSoC 6 is a family of dual-core chips from Infineon. Cypress Semiconductor first made it before Infineon bought the company in 2020. It suits makers who want touch controls, secure IoT nodes, or small edge AI demos. Its standout traits are CAPSENSE touch sensing and built-in security hardware.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Touch buttons and sliders with no moving parts
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth sensor nodes on the prototyping kits
  • Small edge AI demos on the AI kit with radar, motion and sound sensors
  • Projects that need USB device or host support
  • Learning about secure boot and hardware security

Not great for

  • Total beginners, as the main ModusToolbox tools are heavy
  • Open hardware fans, as kit design files have no open license
  • Cheap experiments, as most kits cost $20 to $130
  • Large community help, as most answers live on Infineon's own forum

Specs in plain words

  • Two cores share the work. The fast 150 MHz core runs your program and the small core handles side jobs.
  • 2 MB of storage holds a big program plus a small trained model.
  • 1 MB of working memory is roomy for sound samples and sensor data.
  • A 12-bit ADC reads analog sensors two million times each second.
  • About 7 microamps in deep sleep lets a battery node rest for long stretches.
  • CAPSENSE turns a plain copper pad into a touch button that works even with a wet finger.

Chip values come from the PSoC 62 CY8C62x8 and CY8C62xA datasheet, Rev. *U dated 2026-09-08. The 102 GPIO are the maximum for that line. The 16 ADC channels are the SAR sequencer channels. Flash and SRAM vary across PSoC 61, 62, 63 and 64 parts. The values shown are for the CY8C624ABZI-S2D44 used on the CY8CPROTO-062-4343W. The chip itself has no Wi-Fi. Radios shown come from the Murata CYW4343W module on the CY8CPROTO-062-4343W. PSoC 63 parts have built-in Bluetooth LE only. Connectors and onboard parts are for the CY8CPROTO-062-4343W, which adds CAPSENSE buttons and slider, a PDM microphone, a thermistor, 512 Mb QSPI flash and microSD. low_power_ua is Deep Sleep with 64 KB SRAM retained.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Touch slider lamp dimmer

    Slide a finger along the touch strip to dim an LED. No moving parts can wear out.

    Add: LED strip.

  • Touch button piano

    Turn touch pads into piano keys and play notes through a small speaker. A fun start for music class.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: copper tape, small speaker, amplifier board.

  • Classroom noise meter

    Listen with the onboard microphone and light a green, yellow or red LED. The room gets a quiet reminder.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in. Add: three LEDs.

Builder ideas

  • Wi-Fi room thermometer

    Read the onboard thermistor and post the temperature to a web page on your home network. Check the nursery from the kitchen.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sensors built in.

  • Bluetooth step counter

    Count steps with a motion sensor and send totals to a phone. The low sleep power stretches a small battery.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, LiPo battery.

  • Weather station with pressure sensor

    Log air pressure and temperature every few minutes. Watch for falling pressure before a storm.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi.

  • Segment display clock

    Drive a simple segment LCD straight from the chip. Show the time synced over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi. Add: segment LCD panel.

  • USB keyboard shortcut pad

    Wire a few keys and have the board act as a USB keyboard. Each key types a handy shortcut.

    Add: push buttons.

  • Garden data logger

    Save soil moisture readings to a microSD card for weeks. Graph the data on a computer later.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, microSD card, battery pack.

  • Touch-proof kitchen timer

    Use liquid-tolerant touch buttons to set a countdown. Beep a buzzer at the end with wet hands still welcome.

    Add: buzzer, small display.

Expert ideas

  • Gesture control for music

    Train a small model on motion data to spot gestures. Wave to skip a song on a Wi-Fi speaker.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sensors built in.

  • Spoken word detector

    Train a tiny model to hear a few spoken words with the microphone. Light a different LED for each word.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sensors built in. Add: LEDs.

  • Presence sensor with radar

    Use the radar on the AI kit to sense a person in the room. Turn the lights off once the room is empty.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi. Add: relay module.

  • USB stick data reader

    Read files from a USB flash drive plugged into the board. Show sensor settings stored on the stick.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB adapter cable, USB flash drive.

  • Machine sound monitor

    Listen to a fan or pump with the microphone and learn its normal hum. Send an alert over Wi-Fi once the sound changes.

    Uses: Hears sound, Wi-Fi, Sensors built in.

How open is it?

Open Code

The PSoC 6 peripheral driver library is open under Apache-2.0. Infineon's Arduino core and MicroPython port are both MIT licensed. ModusToolbox, the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radio firmware, the kit design files, and the chip itself stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Apache-2.0 (PSoC 6 peripheral driver library), MIT (Arduino core), MIT (MicroPython), proprietary (ModusToolbox tools and AIROC radio firmware)
