PSoC Edge E84

Infineon Technologies

An Infineon microcontroller with a built-in AI engine for voice, sound, and gestures.

Overview

The PSOC Edge E84 is a microcontroller from Infineon built to run small AI models on the device. It pairs a fast main core and an AI engine with a second low-power core that listens all the time. The E84 is the top chip of its family and adds a graphics engine for touch screens. It suits makers and engineers who want voice control, sound detection, or gesture sensing without a cloud service.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wake words and voice commands that run on the board
  • Sound detection with up to six digital microphones
  • Gesture sensing with the radar on the AI kit
  • Touch screen panels with smooth graphics
  • Battery projects that need to listen for long hours

Not great for

  • Complete beginners, as the main tools take time to learn
  • Running Linux or full desktop software
  • Quick buys in the US, as stock is often thin
  • Big community help, as the platform is new

Specs in plain words

  • A 400 MHz main core with an Arm AI engine can run small speech, sound, and vision models in real time.
  • A second 200 MHz core with its own tiny AI helper listens for a wake word while using little power.
  • About 6.5 MB of memory on the chip, plus 16 MB of extra memory on the AI kit, holds small models and screen images.
  • 64 MB of flash on the AI kit stores your programs, models, and sound clips.
  • The AI kit adds Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, radar, a motion sensor, a pressure sensor, a humidity sensor, and four microphones.
  • A graphics engine and a MIPI DSI port drive a touch screen.

ram_kb adds the 5120 KB system SRAM, the 1 MB M33 SRAM and the 512 KB M55 TCM from the kit guide block diagram and CNX Software. The chip also has 512 KB of RRAM for code. psram_kb and flash_kb are the KIT_PSE84_AI's 128 Mbit Octal HyperRAM and 512 Mbit QSPI NOR flash. gpio is 96 1.8 V pins plus 16 1.8 to 3.3 V pins from the block diagram. The chip has no radio. The AI kit adds a Murata Type 2FY module with an AIROC CYW55513 for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The AI kit also has a BGT60TR13C 60 GHz radar, BMI270 IMU, BMM350 magnetometer, DPS368 pressure sensor and SHT40 humidity sensor. It carries two digital and two analog microphones and a speaker connector. Camera DVP is listed because earlier AI kits shipped an OV7675 DVP camera. Rev *A and later ship a USB camera. The board size was not found.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Blink and read the sensors

    Run the MicroPython port and print readings from the motion, pressure, and humidity sensors. It is a quick tour of the AI kit.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Room comfort monitor

    Log temperature, humidity, and air pressure every minute. Send the readings to a phone over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Classroom noise meter

    Measure sound level with the microphones and show it as a color bar. Students see when the room gets loud.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen. Add: small screen.

  • Step and posture coach

    Clip the board to a bag strap and track movement with the motion sensor. A buzz reminds you to stand up and walk.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: small battery pack, small vibration motor.

Expert ideas

  • Wake word lamp switch

    Train a small wake word model and let the low-power core listen for it. The board then turns a lamp on or off.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip. Add: relay module, lamp.

  • Voice commands for a desk robot

    Recognize a handful of spoken words such as left, right, and stop. Send each command to a small robot over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Wave to change the song

    Use the 60 GHz radar to spot hand swipes in the air. Each swipe skips or pauses music on a speaker.

    Uses: Sensors built in, AI chip, Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Baby cry alert

    Train a sound model that hears crying in the next room. The board sends an alert to a phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Glass break and smoke alarm listener

    Listen for the sound of breaking glass or a beeping alarm. Log each event and push a notice to the home network.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Bird song counter for the garden

    Record short clips outside and run a small model that tags common bird calls. Count visits through the day.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Battery ready. Add: weatherproof box, battery pack.

  • Touch screen control panel

    Draw buttons, dials, and charts on a touch screen with the graphics engine. Use it as a wall panel for lights or heating.

    Uses: Screen. Add: MIPI DSI touch screen.

  • Smart doorbell with a camera

    Grab frames from the included USB camera and run a tiny person detector. Show who is at the door on a screen.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen. Add: MIPI DSI touch screen.

  • Fall detector for older relatives

    Watch the motion sensor for a sudden drop and stillness. Send a Bluetooth alert to a family phone.

    Uses: Sensors built in, AI chip, Bluetooth, Battery ready. Add: small battery pack.

  • Sign language letter trainer

    Teach a small model to read a few hand shapes from the camera. Show the matching letter on a screen for practice.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen. Add: MIPI DSI touch screen.

  • Wired sensor hub for a lab

    Collect readings from several sensors and share them over wired Ethernet. It suits a workshop with no reliable Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wired network, Sensors built in. Add: Ethernet cable.

  • Talking kitchen timer

    Set a timer by voice and hear the countdown through a speaker. It keeps flour off your phone.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, AI chip. Add: small speaker.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Infineon publishes full board design files for both E84 kits, including schematics, layouts, and parts lists. No open hardware license comes with them, and the kits are sold for lab use only. The board support packages are Apache-2.0, and Zephyr and MicroPython give open paths. Parts of ModusToolbox, the debugger firmware, the radio firmware, and the chip stay closed.
