Overview

The PY32F002A is a very cheap 32-bit Arm chip from Puya Semiconductor in Shanghai. It costs about ten to twenty cents in bulk. It suits people who already write C and want a tiny chip for simple jobs. Its standout trait is its price, which sits among the lowest for any Arm chip with flash memory.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Very cheap controllers for blinkers, timers, and small sensors
  • Learning how an Arm chip works at the register level
  • Battery gadgets that run straight from a Li-ion cell or 5 volts
  • Custom boards where every cent counts

Not great for

  • Beginners, as it needs C code and community toolchains
  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio
  • Big programs, as 20 KB of storage fills up fast
  • Easy shopping in the US, as few stores carry the boards

Specs in plain words

  • The 24 MHz speed is plenty for blinking lights and reading a sensor.
  • 20 KB of storage fits a small program of a few hundred lines.
  • 3 KB of working memory holds only a short list of readings.
  • It runs on anything from 1.7 to 5.5 volts. A Li-ion cell or USB power works with no regulator.
  • A 12-bit ADC can read up to nine analog sensors.
  • The smallest version has eight legs and only six usable pins.

Values come from the PY32F002A datasheet Rev 0.1 EN, mirrored in the community PY32_Docs repo, and match the LCSC listing. The 18 GPIO and 9 ADC inputs are for the largest package. The SOP-8 part exposes 6 I/O. Jay Carlson counts 6 external ADC channels. By contrast, the datasheet says up to 9. low_power_ua is the typical Stop mode current with the low-power regulator at 1.0 V, which the datasheet marks as not tested in production. Several makers, including wagiminator and an EEVblog thread, report that the chip unofficially has the PY32F030 die, with 32 KB flash, 4 KB SRAM, a 48 MHz PLL and DMA. Puya does not guarantee this. Board connectors vary. Most PY32F002A boards are plain breakouts with pin headers and no onboard debugger.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files

  • Coding toolsFully open

Several community boards share their design files, and the wagiminator board uses CC-BY-SA-3.0. A fully open path uses GCC and the OpenPuya firmware library under BSD-3-Clause. Puya's official SDK targets closed Keil and IAR tools, and the chip itself is a closed design.
