Overview

The PYNQ-Z2 is a development board from TUL built around an AMD Zynq chip. The Zynq joins a small Linux computer with a large FPGA, a chip you rewire to become custom circuits. You control everything from Python notebooks in a web browser. It suits university students, teachers, and makers who want to learn hardware speedups, video and audio processing.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning FPGAs through Python and Jupyter notebooks
  • Video projects with HDMI input and output
  • Audio projects with microphone, headphone, and line-in jacks
  • College courses, as it is an AMD University Program board

Not great for

  • Complete beginners, as setup needs an OS image and a network link
  • Fully open toolchains, as custom logic usually needs AMD Vivado
  • Battery or wireless projects, as it has no battery support or radio
  • Tight budgets, as most 2026 store prices sit above 200 US dollars

Specs in plain words

  • Two Arm cores at 650 MHz run Linux and Jupyter, about like a very early smartphone.
  • 53,200 logic cells and 220 math blocks give room for video filters and small neural networks.
  • 512 MB of memory holds Linux, your notebooks, and a few video frames at once.
  • HDMI in and out lets the board take video from a laptop or camera and show it on a monitor.
  • Arduino, Raspberry Pi, and Pmod headers accept many common add-on boards.
  • The board is about 87 by 140 millimeters, close to the size of a paperback book cover.

flash_kb is null because the board boots Linux from microSD. It also has 16 MB of QSPI flash. The FPGA has 125 HR I/O pins. The Arduino header, two Pmods and the Raspberry Pi header share these, and 8 Raspberry Pi pins are shared with one Pmod. As a result, a single GPIO count was not set. The ADC on the Arduino analog pins comes from the Zynq XADC. The channel count was not confirmed on a fetched page. User I/O is 4 LEDs, 2 RGB LEDs, 4 push buttons and 2 slide switches. The micro USB port provides JTAG and UART. DFRobot lists 87 x 138 mm. CNX Software and Mouser list 140 x 87 mm.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The PYNQ framework is open under the BSD-3-Clause license, and the board runs open Linux. TUL publishes a schematic, board files, and a constraints file, but no board layout or open hardware license. Building new FPGA logic usually needs AMD Vivado, which is free for this chip but closed, and the Zynq chip itself stays closed.
