Overview

The QMTech Wukong is a square development board built around an AMD Artix-7 FPGA. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. This one adds 256 MB of memory, HDMI, Gigabit Ethernet, and a microSD slot at a low price. It suits FPGA learners who already know the basics and want to run soft processors such as RISC-V with Linux.

Specs in plain words

  • About 101,000 logic cells is room for a soft processor, a video circuit, and a network circuit together.
  • 256 MB of DDR3 memory gives a soft RISC-V processor enough space to boot Linux.
  • 8 MB of flash stores your FPGA design and loads it at power on.
  • A 50 MHz clock is the steady beat that drives your circuits.
  • Four PMOD headers take plug-in add-ons such as displays, buttons, and sensors.
  • Power comes from a 5 V barrel jack that needs a 2 A supply.

Specs from the V3 hardware user manual (January 2024) and the V3 schematic. ram_kb is the 256 MB DDR3. V3 also has 32 MB of W9825G6KH SDRAM. flash_kb is the 8 MB N25Q64A configuration flash on V3 (V2 used an S25FL256L, per the V2 datasheet folder). clock_mhz is the 50 MHz board oscillator. The FPGA resources (101,440 logic cells, 4,860 Kb BRAM) come from the manual. The DSP slice count was not checked. gpio is null because the manual gives no total. It lists 4 PMOD-compatible headers and a 40-pin header from bank 13, The header page shows about 34 bank 13 signals, and four PMODs add up to 32 more. As a result, the total is roughly 66 by our own estimate. The Ethernet PHY (RTL8211EG) and CH340N USB serial bridge appear in the V3 schematic text but not in the V3 manual's feature list. The USB connector type for the CH340N port on V3 was not confirmed. As a result, no USB connector is listed. GTP transceiver pins are routed in the schematic, but whether a connector for them is fitted on V3 was not confirmed. The XC7A100T has an XADC block, but the manual does not route analog inputs. As a result, adc_channels is null.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Blink the user LEDs

    Write a tiny Verilog design that counts and blinks the four LEDs. It is the classic first FPGA test.

    Add: JTAG cable.

  • Button counter

    Count presses of the three user keys and show the number in binary on the LEDs. It teaches clocks and debouncing.

    Add: JTAG cable.

  • Tone and music synth

    Generate square and triangle waves and play notes through a small speaker. Each key can play a different note.

    Add: small speaker, small amplifier board.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a strip of addressable LEDs with patterns made in logic. Hang it behind a desk or on a stage.

    Add: LED strip, 5 V power supply.

  • Frequency counter for science class

    Measure how fast a signal switches on and off. Test a fan sensor, a buzzer, or a signal generator.

    Add: jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • HDMI test pattern

    Draw color bars and a bouncing square on a monitor over HDMI. You learn how a screen is drawn line by line.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI monitor, HDMI cable.

  • Pong in pure logic

    Build the Pong game fully in hardware and play it with the board keys. Show it on a TV over HDMI.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI monitor, HDMI cable.

  • RISC-V computer running Linux

    Build a VexRiscv soft processor with LiteX and boot Linux from the microSD card. The FPGA becomes a small computer you designed.

    Add: microSD card, JTAG cable.

  • Network blinker

    Add Ethernet to a LiteX soft core and answer pings from your home network. A light flashes for each packet.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable.

  • Tiny web server in hardware

    Serve a simple status page from a soft processor over Ethernet. Show board temperature or button states.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable.

  • Retro game console recreation

    Recreate a simple 8-bit computer in the FPGA. Play old style games on an HDMI screen.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI monitor, PMOD joystick or buttons.

  • Logic analyzer for your bench

    Capture fast digital signals on the PMOD pins and store them in DDR3 memory. Look at the results on a computer.

    Add: jumper wires.

QMTech publishes PDF schematics, manuals, and example projects. It shares no layout files and no license. The FPGA chip is closed. The open Yosys, nextpnr-xilinx, and Project X-Ray flow supports this exact FPGA, and LiteX board files are BSD-2-Clause, though the open flow is still experimental.
