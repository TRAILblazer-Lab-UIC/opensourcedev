Overview

The RA4M1 is a Renesas microcontroller with a 48 MHz Arm Cortex-M4 core. Most makers meet it as the brain of the Arduino UNO R4 Minima, UNO R4 WiFi and Nano R4. It runs at 5 volts, which lets the R4 boards keep working with old Arduino shields. It suits beginners and teachers who want the familiar UNO shape with more speed and memory.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First Arduino projects with lots of example sketches
  • Reusing older 5 volt shields and parts
  • USB gadgets that act as a keyboard or mouse
  • Smooth analog tones from the built-in DAC
  • Classroom labs that need a well-known board

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on the Minima or Nano R4, which have no radio
  • Older libraries written only for the UNO R3 chip
  • Heavy math, big screens, or any AI work
  • Tasks that need a lot of memory at once

Specs in plain words

  • The 48 MHz processor is about three times the clock speed of the classic UNO R3.
  • 32 KB of working memory holds a few hundred sensor readings at a time.
  • 256 KB of flash storage fits a long sketch and several libraries.
  • The chip runs at 5 volts, the same level as most classic Arduino shields.
  • The 14-bit analog input reads a sensor in over 16,000 small steps.
  • The UNO R4 is about 7 by 5 centimeters, roughly the size of a credit card.

gpio and adc_channels are chip maximums for the 100-pin package from the Renesas page. The UNO R4 Minima uses the 64-pin R7FA4M1AB3CFM and exposes 14 digital pins and 6 analog inputs. Size is the UNO R4 board as reported by CNX Software. The chip has no radio. The UNO R4 WiFi adds an ESP32-S3-MINI-1 module for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The Minima draws about 33 mA typical running blink, per the Arduino datasheet. No chip standby current was read from a Renesas datasheet. As a result, low_power_ua is null. The XIAO RA4M1 wiki lists about 42.6 uA in its low-power mode at board level.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Scrolling name badge

    Use the UNO R4 WiFi and its grid of tiny red lights. Your name or a short message scrolls across the board.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a friend presses a button. The fastest time wins the round.

    Add: push button, LED.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Push a soil sensor into a pot and wire up a buzzer. The board beeps once the soil gets too dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Point a motion sensor at a bird feeder. The light grid on the UNO R4 WiFi shows the visit count for the day.

    Uses: Screen. Add: motion sensor.

  • Heartbeat on a light grid

    Clip a pulse sensor to a fingertip. A small heart pattern on the UNO R4 WiFi light grid beats along with you.

    Uses: Screen. Add: pulse sensor.

  • Keyboard shortcut pad

    Wire a few buttons and let the board act as a USB keyboard. Each press can mute a call or flip a slide.

    Add: push buttons.

  • Homemade game controller

    Add a joystick and a few buttons. The board shows up as a mouse or keyboard for simple computer games.

    Add: joystick module, push buttons.

  • Old shield robot car

    Stack an older 5 volt motor shield on the UNO R4. Two small motors drive a little car around the floor.

    Add: motor driver shield, small DC motors, wheels, battery pack.

  • Smooth tone maker

    Use the true analog output to make clean musical tones. A small amplifier and speaker play them out loud.

    Add: small amplifier, small speaker.

  • Light theremin

    Wave your hand over a light sensor to change the pitch. The board plays a spooky sliding tone through a speaker.

    Add: light sensor, small amplifier, small speaker.

  • Fine-grained light meter for class

    Read a light sensor with the detailed 14-bit converter. Students graph sunlight across a school day on a laptop.

    Add: light sensor.

  • Rain gauge counter

    Connect a tipping bucket rain gauge to one pin. The board counts each tip and adds up the rain for the week.

    Add: tipping bucket rain gauge.

Expert ideas

  • Two boards on a CAN network

    Add a CAN transceiver to two boards and link them with a pair of wires. One board sends sensor readings and the other reacts.

    Add: CAN transceiver modules, twisted pair wire.

  • Debug with a real probe

    Plug a debug probe into the SWD header on the UNO R4 Minima. You can pause code and peek at each value step by step.

    Add: SWD debug probe.
