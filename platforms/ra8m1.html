Overview

The RA8M1 is a microcontroller from Renesas Electronics built on the Arm Cortex-M85 at up to 480 MHz. CNX Software called it the first Cortex-M85 chip at its 2023 launch. It adds Helium vector math for sound processing and small AI models. The EK-RA8M1 kit suits experienced makers, engineers, and advanced students who write C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Fast sound and signal processing
  • Small AI models for sounds, gestures, or simple images
  • Wired projects with Ethernet, high-speed USB, and CAN-FD
  • Quick sensor hookups through Qwiic, Grove, Pmod, and mikroBUS sockets

Not great for

  • Beginners, with no Arduino core or MicroPython board found
  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on the chip
  • Battery gadgets that must run for months
  • Finding lots of hobby tutorials, with few projects shared so far

Specs in plain words

  • 480 MHz makes it one of the fastest microcontrollers around, with more than 3000 CoreMark.
  • Helium vector math crunches many numbers at once for audio filters and tiny AI models.
  • 2 MB of flash holds large programs, and the kit adds 64 MB more for data.
  • 1 MB of working memory is room for sound buffers or a small camera frame.
  • The Ethernet jack and high-speed USB move data fast over a cable.
  • The kit sockets accept Arduino Uno shields and many plug-in sensor boards.

Chip values are for the top part with 2 MB flash, as used on the EK-RA8M1 (R7FA8M1AHECBD, 224-pin BGA). Smaller parts have 1 MB flash and clocks of 360 or 400 MHz. The 1 MB SRAM figure includes 128 KB of TCM, and Zephyr maps 896 KiB of RAM on the EK-RA8M1. I/O count ranges from 70 to 174 by package, and 174 is the largest package. The product page lists ADC channel counts of 10, 17, 18, 24 and 25 by package, and 25 is the largest. The EK-RA8M1 adds 64 MB of Octo-SPI flash, which is not counted in flash_kb. Connectors and onboard parts are for the EK-RA8M1, which uses micro-USB ports and an RJ45 Ethernet jack. The SSIE serial sound interface is listed as i2s and the Capture Engine Unit as camera_dvp. The datasheet standby table gives Deep Software Standby mode 1 at about 5.21 typ, but the units in the extracted table were unclear. As a result, low_power_ua is null. Board size was not found.

Builder ideas

  • Wired weather station

    Read outdoor sensors and serve a web page over Ethernet. Anyone on the school network can check the weather.

    Uses: Wired network, Plug-in connectors. Add: weather sensor, network cable.

  • Plug-in sensor lab

    Snap Qwiic and Grove sensors onto the kit with no soldering. Compare light, sound, and temperature readings in class.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: light sensor, temperature sensor, sound sensor.

  • Heartbeat signal viewer

    Read a pulse sensor and clean up the signal with fast math. Send the graph to a computer for a health class.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: pulse sensor.

  • Data logger to memory card

    Record sensor readings many times a second to a memory card. Study a science experiment later on a computer.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: microSD card adapter, microSD card, sensor of your choice.

Expert ideas

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Feed a guitar signal in and add echo or distortion in real time. The vector math keeps the sound smooth.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, audio jacks, foot switch.

  • Clap and whistle detector

    Listen through a microphone and recognize simple sounds. Turn a lamp on with a clap.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone, relay board.

  • Simple gesture camera

    Connect a small parallel camera and run a tiny vision model. Wave a hand to change a slide.

    Uses: Camera. Add: parallel camera module.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard or barcode scanner into the kit. Log what it types for a library checkout desk.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard or barcode scanner.

  • Tone and synth box

    Generate tones and simple synth sounds in real time. Play them with buttons or a USB keyboard.

    Uses: Makes sound, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: audio amplifier board, speaker, push buttons.

  • Vibration watch for a pump

    Read a motion sensor on a pump or fan and look for odd patterns. Get an alert over Ethernet before it breaks.

    Uses: Wired network, Plug-in connectors. Add: motion sensor, network cable.

  • Greenhouse controller

    Read soil and air sensors and switch fans or pumps on a schedule. Check the greenhouse from a web page.

    Uses: Wired network, Plug-in connectors. Add: soil moisture sensor, temperature sensor, relay board, small water pump.

  • Machine bus listener

    Use the CAN-FD channels to read messages from robots or machines. Show the traffic on a laptop.

    Add: CAN transceiver board.

Renesas offers a design package for the EK-RA8M1 that it describes as files for building custom hardware. We could not open that package to check for layout files. No license is stated for those board files. The FSP drivers are open under BSD-3-Clause, and Zephyr support is Apache-2.0. The J-Link debug tools, a few FSP add-ons, and the Arm chip design stay closed.
