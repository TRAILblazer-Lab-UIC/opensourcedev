Overview

The Radxa Dragon Q8B is a single-board computer built on the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8cx Gen 3 laptop chip. It has eight Kryo cores, an Adreno 690 graphics chip, and an AI Engine rated at 29 TOPS or more. Reviewers measured about twice the speed of a Raspberry Pi 5. It suits Linux users who want desktop power on a 100 by 75 millimeter board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A fast Arm desktop with up to three 4K screens
  • Home servers with two NVMe drives and dual 2.5 gigabit Ethernet
  • Local AI projects on the AI Engine
  • Testing Linux and Windows 11 on Arm from standard ISO files

Not great for

  • Beginners, with reviewers reporting a choppy setup and an unfinished BIOS
  • Wireless projects out of the box, with Wi-Fi only through an add-on M.2 card
  • Small budgets, with prices from 149 to 569 dollars by memory size
  • Battery projects, with about 25 watts drawn under load

Specs in plain words

  • Eight cores up to 3.0 GHz feel like a thin laptop for everyday work.
  • The 29 TOPS AI Engine runs vision and language models much faster than the main cores.
  • 4 to 32 GB of memory covers anything from light browsing to big AI models.
  • Two NVMe slots hold fast drives for files, movies, and the operating system.
  • HDMI 2.1 and two USB-C ports drive three 4K screens at 120 Hz.
  • The board idles at about 5.9 watts and needs a 65 watt USB-C power adapter.

RAM is shown for the 8 GB model, the size most stores list. Arace also sells 4, 16 and 32 GB. There is no onboard eMMC. Storage is microSD, a UFS module or NVMe. As a result, flash_kb is null. The 40-pin header supports UART, I2C, SPI and GPIO per Radxa docs. A 50-pin B2B connector carries PCIe, MIPI CSI and I2C. Usable GPIO count and ADC were not confirmed. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth need an M.2 E-key module. As a result, they are not listed as radios. Wi-Fi 7 modules need board v1.3 or later. Bret.dk measured 5.9 W idle and about 25 W under load.

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

Radxa publishes the schematic, a parts placement map, 2D drawings, and a 3D model. No board layout files or hardware license are listed. The Radxa OS build repo is GPL-3.0, and the chip has mainline Linux support. Closed firmware still runs the GPU, the DSP, and other blocks, and the Snapdragon design is closed.
