Radxa Orion O6

Radxa

A twelve-core Arm desktop motherboard with a 30 TOPS AI helper.

Field marks: Twelve Arm cores, a 30 TOPS NPU, a full-size PCIe x16 slot, and dual 5 GbE ports fill this Mini-ITX board, which sits in a PC case designed for x86.

Overview

The Radxa Orion O6 is a Mini-ITX motherboard built on the twelve-core CIX P1 Arm chip. It has a 30 TOPS AI helper, up to 64 GB of memory, and a full-size PCIe slot. Standard UEFI firmware boots ordinary Arm Linux and Windows on Arm images. It suits experienced makers who want a powerful Arm desktop, AI box or development workstation.

Not great for

  • Low power projects, as it idles around 16 watts
  • Wireless projects without an add-on M.2 Wi-Fi card
  • Shoppers in the US, as no US store listing was found

Specs in plain words

  • Twelve processor cores at up to 2.6 GHz feel like a midrange laptop.
  • 8 to 64 GB of memory leaves room for big programs and larger AI models.
  • 30 TOPS means the AI helper does about 30 trillion small math steps each second.
  • The PCIe x16 slot takes full-size cards such as some graphics or storage cards.
  • Two 5 gigabit network ports move files about five times faster than common home ports.
  • The board is 17 by 17 centimeters, the standard Mini-ITX size for small PC cases.

RAM is the 8 GB base model. 16, 32 and 64 GB versions are sold, and Radxa docs also list 12 and 24 GB. Big-core clock is 2.6 GHz per Radxa docs. CNX Software and part of the Radxa product page say 2.8 GHz. There is no onboard eMMC. Storage is an NVMe SSD in the M.2 M-key slot. As a result, flash_kb is null. The 40-pin GPIO header pin count is known, but the usable GPIO count and ADC were not confirmed. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth come only through an optional M.2 E-key module. As a result, they are not listed as radios. CNX Software measured about 16 W idle and 26 W under stress at the wall.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Radxa publishes a schematic PDF, parts placement maps, 2D drawings, and a 3D model. No board layout files are listed, and the hardware files have no stated license. The EDK2 firmware and OS build code use GPL-3.0. The NPU tools need an approved sign-up, and the CIX P1 chip design is closed.
