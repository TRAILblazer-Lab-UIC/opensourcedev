Radxa Orion O6N

Radxa

Overview

The Radxa Orion O6N is a square computer board about 12 centimeters on each side. It runs a 12-core Arm chip with a built-in AI engine and a strong graphics chip. It suits people who want an Arm desktop, a local AI box, or a fast development machine. Two fast drive slots, two camera inputs, and support for three screens set it apart from most small boards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running a full Linux desktop on Arm hardware
  • Local AI projects such as object spotting and small chat models
  • Home servers with lots of fast storage
  • Developers who build and test Arm software

Not great for

  • First-time builders, as setup needs an OS install and a strong power supply
  • Wireless projects out of the box, as Wi-Fi needs an add-on module
  • Tight budgets, as prices rose sharply in 2026
  • Fully open AI tools, as the NPU software comes from the chip vendor

Specs in plain words

  • Twelve processor cores can handle many apps at once, close to a modern laptop.
  • 16 GB of memory on the base model keeps a desktop, a browser, and a small AI model running together.
  • The 30 TOPS AI engine runs vision models on live video in real time.
  • Two NVMe slots hold fast drives, each much quicker than a memory card.
  • HDMI, DisplayPort, and USB-C can drive up to three screens.
  • It needs 12 volts at 65 watts or more, about the power of a laptop charger.

RAM is shown for the 16 GB model, the smallest size listed at Arace now. Radxa docs list 8, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 GB. There is no onboard eMMC. Storage is NVMe in two M.2 slots, or a UFS module on v1.11 boards. As a result, flash_kb is null. Board v1.11 has the UFS socket. Board v1.20 drops UFS and adds M.2 AI accelerator expansion and a second fan header. Docs say 30 TOPS for the NPU. The product page says 45 TOPS built in, which matches the combined CPU, GPU and NPU figure. The 40-pin header count is known, but usable GPIO and ADC were not confirmed. Header bus types were not confirmed. As a result, UART, I2C and SPI are not listed. Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.4 need an M.2 E-key module, and 4G needs an M.2 B-key module. As a result, they are not listed as radios. CNX Software's spec list mentioned a PCIe x16 slot, ATX and USB-C PD power, but Radxa's product page says there is no standard PCIe slot and only 12 V DC input. Radxa's page is used here.

Builder ideas

  • Quiet Arm desktop computer

    Put the board in a case and run Debian or Ubuntu as an everyday desktop. Browse, write, and code on up to three screens.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: NVMe drive, 12 V power supply, case, keyboard and mouse, monitor.

  • Home media server

    Store movies and music on an NVMe drive and stream them to every TV in the house over the wired network.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Two-drive family backup box

    Fill both NVMe slots and copy each family laptop to them every night. One drive can mirror the other for safety.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: two NVMe drives.

  • Classroom science data lab

    Run Python notebooks for a whole class from one board. Students graph experiment data from their own laptops.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Three-screen school dashboard

    Show the lunch menu, the weather, and the day's schedule on three hallway screens at once.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: three monitors, display cables.

  • Retro game console

    Run game emulators on a TV and plug in a few USB controllers. Host a game night for friends.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB game controllers.

  • Home music studio

    Record and mix songs with open-source audio apps. Plug in a USB keyboard to play notes and listen through speakers.

    Uses: Makes sound, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB MIDI keyboard, powered speakers.

Expert ideas

  • Offline AI chat helper

    Run a small language model on the board itself. Ask questions at home with nothing sent to the cloud.

    Uses: Runs Linux, AI chip. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Object spotting camera

    Connect a camera and use the NPU to spot people, cars, or packages in live video. Log each sighting to a file.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: MIPI camera module.

  • Garden wildlife counter

    Point a camera at the garden and let an AI model count visiting birds and squirrels. Make a weekly chart for a nature club.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: MIPI camera module, weatherproof box.

  • Private photo search

    Keep family photos on the board and let a local AI model tag them by scene. Find any beach photo in seconds.

    Uses: AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Stereo camera robot brain

    Use both camera inputs to judge distance like a pair of human eyes. Mount it on a rolling robot to steer around chairs.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: two MIPI camera modules, robot chassis, battery pack.

  • Home network firewall

    Use the two 2.5 gigabit network ports to filter traffic between the internet and your home. Block ads for every device.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: NVMe drive.

  • Arm software build server

    Compile big Arm programs on twelve cores for a club or small team. Everyone shares one fast machine over the network.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: NVMe drive.

Radxa publishes the schematic PDF, a parts placement map, a 2D drawing, and a 3D model. No board layout files are offered, and no hardware license is stated. The UEFI firmware and OS build scripts are open under GPL-3.0. The CIX chip, its AI engine software, and parts of the graphics stack stay closed.
