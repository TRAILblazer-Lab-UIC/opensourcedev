Overview

The Radxa ROCK 4D is a small Linux computer about the size of a credit card. It uses a Rockchip RK3576 chip with eight cores and a 6 TOPS AI engine. It looks and feels much like a Raspberry Pi 5, with a matching 40-pin header. It suits hobbyists who want built-in Wi-Fi 6, a 4K screen output, and room for small AI vision projects.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Media players and small desktops with sharp 4K video
  • Simple AI vision projects with a camera
  • Raspberry Pi style projects that use the 40-pin header
  • Home servers with an NVMe drive on a HAT

Not great for

  • Total beginners, as setup needs an OS image and some Linux skill
  • Fully open AI work, as the NPU uses Rockchip's closed RKNN software
  • Cheap buys right now, as most stores are sold out or priced well above launch
  • Plug-in eMMC storage on boards that have SPI flash fitted

Specs in plain words

  • Eight cores split into four fast ones and four efficient ones, plenty for a desktop or a media player.
  • 4 GB of memory on the common model runs a desktop and a few apps at once.
  • The 6 TOPS AI engine can spot objects in camera video and run very small chat models.
  • HDMI 2.1 can show 4K video at a smooth 120 frames per second.
  • 16 MB of SPI flash holds the boot code, with your files on a card or plug-in module.
  • It runs on 5 volts over USB-C from a 10 to 25 watt phone-style charger.

RAM is shown for the 4 GB model. Radxa docs list 2, 4, 8 and 16 GB, and stores also sell 6 and 12 GB versions. flash_kb is the 16 MB SPI NOR boot flash. There is no soldered eMMC. eMMC or UFS comes as a plug-in module, and eMMC works only when SPI flash is absent per the product page. The A72 clock of 2.2 GHz is from CNX Software. The Radxa product page lists the A53 cores at 2 GHz. Size 87 x 58 x 20 mm is from CNX Software. The Radxa docs page did not list dimensions. The 40-pin header carries GPIO, but the usable GPIO count was not confirmed. The SoC has a SARADC but header ADC pins were not confirmed. PoE needs the 25 W PoE HAT. 2.5 GbE, NVMe and the Penta SATA HAT go through the PCIe FFC.

Key terms on this page

Short definitions of words used above and below. Select a term for the full glossary entry.

eMMC
eMMC is flash storage soldered right onto a board.
Flash memory
Flash memory is storage that keeps its data with the power off.
HAT
A HAT is an add-on board that sits on top of a Raspberry Pi.
HDMI
HDMI is the common plug for TVs and monitors.
NPU
An NPU is a chip part made just for running AI models.
Operating system
An operating system is the main software that runs a computer.
Pin header
A pin header is a row of metal pins or sockets on a board.
Raspberry Pi
Raspberry Pi is a line of low-cost single-board computers from a UK charity and company.
Repository
A repository is an online folder that holds a project's files and their full history.
Resolution
Resolution is how many dots make up an image or screen.
SPI
SPI is a fast way for chips to talk over four or more wires.
TOPS
TOPS stands for trillion operations per second.
