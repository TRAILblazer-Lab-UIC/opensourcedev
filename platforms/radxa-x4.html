Overview

Radxa X4 is a small Intel computer about the size of a credit card. It runs Windows, Debian, and Ubuntu like a regular PC. A built-in RP2040 chip drives a Raspberry Pi style 40-pin header for sensors and lights. It suits makers who want PC software and hardware tinkering on one board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A tiny Windows or Linux desktop
  • Home servers with fast NVMe storage and 2.5 gigabit Ethernet
  • Running regular x86 programs that Arm boards cannot run
  • Mixing PC software with sensors, LEDs, and motors on the pin header
  • Driving two screens at once

Not great for

  • Absolute beginners. It needs an operating system install and careful power setup.
  • Camera projects. The board has no camera connector.
  • Silent or fanless builds. It needs a heatsink or fan.
  • Tight budgets. Prices have climbed far above launch prices.

Specs in plain words

  • The Intel N100 has four cores up to 3.4 GHz. It feels like a basic laptop.
  • Memory comes in 4, 8, 12, or 16 GB. The 8 GB board runs a desktop and several programs at once.
  • The M.2 slot takes a short 2230 NVMe drive. That drive holds the operating system and your files.
  • The 2.5 gigabit network port moves files more than twice as fast as common home ports.
  • The RP2040 is the same chip as in a Raspberry Pi Pico. It handles the pins with fast, steady timing.
  • It needs a 12 volt USB-C PD power supply. A phone charger often falls short.

RAM comes in 4, 8, 12 and 16 GB LPDDR5. Ram_kb shows the 8 GB board. eMMC is optional (32, 64 or 128 GB in store listings). As a result, flash_kb is null. There is no microSD slot. The 4 GB model has Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5. 8 GB and larger have Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2. clock_mhz is the N100 turbo clock. The RP2040 is a dual-core Cortex-M0+ with 264 KB SRAM. Radxa docs mark header pins GPIO26 to GPIO29 as user IO or ADC input. As a result, adc_channels is 4. Hackaday reports the RP2040 links to the N100 over USB 2.0 and UART, with BOOTSEL, reset and SWD routed. There are no MIPI CSI or DSI connectors.

Radxa publishes a schematic PDF, a placement map, and a 3D model of the board. No board layout files or hardware license are published. The RP2040 uses the open Pico SDK and MicroPython. The Intel chip, the BIOS, and some network drivers stay closed.
