Overview

The RAK WisBlock Meshtastic Starter Kit is a small LoRa radio that sends text messages with no cell towers or internet. It arrives ready for Meshtastic, a free mesh messaging firmware. You pair it with your phone over Bluetooth and start chatting with other nodes. It suits hikers, preppers, campers and curious beginners who want a long-battery radio.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends in places with no phone signal
  • Building solar-powered repeaters that run for months
  • Snapping on screens, GPS and sensors with no soldering
  • Learning about radio range and mesh networks in class

Not great for

  • Fast data like photos, video or music
  • Projects that need Wi-Fi out of the box
  • Mixing many add-on modules without checking for pin clashes

Specs in plain words

  • The LoRa radio sends short messages over long distances at very low power.
  • A sleep draw of 2 microamps means a small battery can last a very long time.
  • 256 KB of memory is plenty for messages and simple sensor readings.
  • The base board takes a lithium battery and a small solar panel and charges the battery for you.
  • The standard base board is about 3 by 6 centimeters, roughly the size of a matchbox.
  • Radios come in different bands for different countries. Pick the one that matches yours.

Specs describe the RAK4631 core (nRF52840 plus SX1262) on the RAK19007 base board. Size is the RAK19007 base board. The RAK4631 module is 20 x 30 mm and the RAK19003 mini base is smaller. The nRF52840 has 48 GPIO, but the number broken out depends on the base board and was not counted. The nRF52840 also supports Thread, Zigbee and NFC, but the kit and Meshtastic do not use them. The 2 uA figure is the RAK4631 module sleep current from its datasheet. The RAK4631(H) covers EU868, US915, AU915, KR920, AS923, IN865 and RU864. The RAK4631(L) covers EU433 and CN470. OLED display, GPS and accelerometer are optional add-ons depending on the kit variant.

How open is it?

Open Board

The Meshtastic firmware is open source under GPL-3.0, and the RAK example code is MIT licensed. RAK shows the board schematics only as pictures in its datasheets. No editable board files or hardware license were found. The Nordic Bluetooth software inside the chip is a closed binary.

Node
