Overview

The WisMesh 1W Booster Starter Kit is a small radio for sending text messages with no cell service or internet. It comes with Meshtastic firmware ready to go and needs no soldering. Its amplifier sends at 1 watt, several times stronger than most hobby LoRa radios. It suits hikers, campers, neighborhood groups, and anyone building a local mesh network.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Off-grid text messages paired with a phone app
  • Reaching distant nodes and repeaters in a Meshtastic mesh
  • Fixed relays on rooftops or hills
  • Sending simple sensor readings over long distances

Not great for

  • Running with no battery, as the transmit surge needs a battery or a strong 5 V supply
  • Full power everywhere, as 1 watt may break radio rules in some regions such as Europe
  • Voice, photos, or video, as LoRa carries only short messages
  • Fully open hardware, as only schematic images are published

Specs in plain words

  • The 1 watt output is about five to ten times stronger than the usual 100 to 200 mW hobby radios.
  • A receive sensitivity of -140 dBm lets it hear very faint signals from far away.
  • Sending at full power pulls about 1 amp at 5 volts, about what a phone draws while charging fast.
  • 256 KB of memory and 1 MB of flash give the Meshtastic firmware room to store messages and settings.
  • The base board is about 3 by 6 centimeters, close to a large matchbox, with four slots for sensor modules.
  • Bluetooth LE links the radio to your phone.

Core values are Nordic's nRF52840 figures. Meshtastic docs and CNX list 348 KB SRAM for the RAK3401, which conflicts with Nordic's 256 KB. The 2 uA figure is the RAK3401 module sleep current with LoRa and BLE asleep. The 1 W stack draws far more. The RAK13302 draws about 1000 mA peak at 5 V while sending at 1 W and 12 mA on receive. It covers 902 to 928 MHz and measures 25 x 35 mm. Size is the RAK19007 base board. GPIO count depends on the base board and was not counted. The 3.3 V rail cannot supply the 1 W transmit surge. As a result, a battery or external 5 V supply is required. The kit ships with a separate BLE antenna, a 900 MHz LoRa antenna and an IPEX-to-SMA cable.

The Meshtastic firmware is open under GPL-3.0 and has a dedicated build target for this kit. RAK's WisBlock code examples are MIT. The schematics appear only as images in the datasheets, with no board files and no hardware license, and the Nordic Bluetooth stack is a closed binary.
