Overview

The WisMesh Pocket V2 is a handheld Meshtastic radio from RAKwireless. It comes ready to use with a case, screen, GPS, battery, and antenna. It pairs with a phone over Bluetooth and sends text messages across a LoRa mesh with no cell service. It suits hikers, campers, families, and newcomers who want off-grid messaging with no assembly.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Off-grid text messages on hikes, camping trips, and events
  • Sharing your location with friends on a phone map
  • Joining a local Meshtastic community mesh
  • Long battery life of around three days with GPS and screen on
  • Adding sensors through WisBlock slots later on

Not great for

  • Very long range with the stock antenna, which reviewers measured near 500 to 600 meters
  • Loud alerts, as it has no buzzer and the bottom lights are hard to see
  • Typing on the device itself, as it has one button and no keyboard
  • Fast data such as photos or voice, as LoRa sends only small messages

Specs in plain words

  • The LoRa radio sends small messages a long way on low power over licence-free bands.
  • A 3200 mAh battery is about the size of a big phone battery and lasts a few days.
  • The GPS receiver finds your spot from satellites and shares it with your group.
  • The 1.3 inch OLED screen is about the size of a postage stamp and shows messages and status.
  • 256 KB of memory is plenty for Meshtastic and a few sensor readings.
  • The whole case is about the size of a deck of cards, a bit thicker.

The nRF52840 chip values (64 MHz, 256 KB RAM, 1 MB flash) are standard Nordic figures used on the sibling WisBlock card. No fetched Pocket page restates them. The RAK19026 base board is 41.6 x 60 mm. The 94 x 52 x 30 mm enclosure size and 195 g weight come from a Galaxus listing (search snippet). Rokland gives about 103 x 52 x 35 mm. The 2 uA figure is the base board with no modules. Sleep with modules is under 10 uA and LoRa transmit is about 130 mA. LoRa bands are 868, 915, 920 and 923 MHz SKUs. The battery uses a 3-pin JST PHR-3 with NTC on EU and UK units and a 2-pin JST PHR-2 elsewhere. The GNSS supports GPS, GLONASS, QZSS and BeiDou. The LIS3DH is fitted but Meshtastic does not use it, per the Adrelien review. Headers include an OLED I2C header, GPIO headers and a 4-pin SWD debug header.

How open is it?

Open Board

The software is open. Meshtastic firmware uses the GPL-3.0 license, and the RAK WisBlock examples use MIT. RAK shows schematic pictures for the base board but shares no layout files or hardware license. The nRF52840 chip and its Bluetooth code stay closed.

Software license: GPL-3.0 (Meshtastic firmware), MIT (RAKwireless WisBlock examples)
