Raspberry Pi 4 Model B

Raspberry Pi Ltd

A credit-card-sized Linux computer for learning, home servers, games, and robots.

Field marks: A green card computer with two micro-HDMI ports, two USB 3.0 ports, and a USB-C power jack, plus a drawer somewhere full of the wrong HDMI cables.

Overview

Raspberry Pi 4 Model B is a small Linux computer about the size of a credit card. It runs Raspberry Pi OS with a full desktop and a web browser. It suits students, teachers, home tinkerers, and robot builders. It costs less and uses less power than the newer Pi 5, and the maker plans to keep making it until at least 2034.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A low-cost first computer for learning to code
  • Home servers for files, media, and ad blocking
  • Retro gaming and media players on the TV
  • Camera and robot projects with Python
  • Classroom labs that need many matching computers

Not great for

  • Battery gadgets that must run for days
  • Reading analog sensors directly with no add-on board
  • Fully open hardware, as its main chip and boot software are closed
  • Heavy AI work, where the faster Pi 5 does better

Specs in plain words

  • Four cores at 1.8 GHz make it feel like a basic home computer for browsing and coding.
  • Memory comes in 1, 2, 3, 4, or 8 GB, and 4 GB is a comfy middle choice for the desktop.
  • It has no built-in storage, and the operating system lives on a microSD card or a USB drive.
  • Two micro-HDMI ports can drive two 4K screens at once.
  • Gigabit Ethernet and dual-band Wi-Fi make it a steady home server.
  • A 5 volt, 3 amp USB-C power supply keeps it running well.

RAM comes in 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8 GB versions. Ram_kb shows the 4 GB board. There is no onboard eMMC. It boots from microSD or USB. The 40-pin header exposes GPIO2 to GPIO27 for general use at 3.3 V logic, plus two ID pins for HAT EEPROMs. There is no ADC. Clock was 1.5 GHz on early boards and is 1.8 GHz on current ones. The product page lists OpenGL ES 3.1 and Vulkan 1.0. The October 2026 product brief lists OpenGL ES 3.0. Operating temperature is 0 to 50 C. A 2.5 A supply works if USB peripherals draw less than 500 mA in total. The 4-pole jack carries stereo audio and composite video.

Starter ideas

  • Retro game console

    Turn the Pi into a box for classic games on the TV. Plug in two USB gamepads and play with friends.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB gamepads, micro-HDMI cable, case.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Screen. Add: screen, keyboard, mouse.

    Play movies and music on the living room TV. The board can show video in sharp 4K.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: micro-HDMI cable, case.

  • Blinking LED traffic light

    Wire three LEDs to the pin header and run a short Python program. Your desk gets its own tiny traffic light.

    Add: red, yellow and green LEDs, resistors, breadboard, jumper wires.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, push button, case.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen, Runs Linux. Add: Bluetooth soil sensors, small screen.

  • Touchscreen family calendar

    Hang a screen in the kitchen with the week's plans and the weather. The Pi keeps it updated all day.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Linux kernel and Raspberry Pi OS are open source, and the kernel uses GPL-2.0. Raspberry Pi shares a reduced schematic and a mechanical drawing, but no open hardware license. The Broadcom main chip and its startup firmware stay closed.

Hardware license: none (proprietary, copyright Raspberry Pi) · Software license: GPL-2.0 (Linux kernel), proprietary redistributable boot firmware
