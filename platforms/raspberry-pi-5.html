Raspberry Pi 5

Raspberry Pi Ltd

A credit-card-sized Linux computer for desktops, servers, robots, and home AI projects.

Field marks: A credit-card computer that needs a 5-amp power supply, a fan, a microSD card, and two micro-HDMI adapters before it finally counts as a desktop.

Overview

Raspberry Pi 5 is a small Linux computer about the size of a credit card. It runs Raspberry Pi OS with a full desktop, a web browser and coding tools. It suits curious learners, teachers, home tinkerers, and robot builders. It is the fastest full-size Pi so far, with room for fast drives, two cameras and add-on AI boards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • A low-cost desktop computer for learning to code
  • Home servers for files, media, and websites
  • Robot brains that need cameras and heavy thinking
  • Home AI projects with an add-on accelerator board
  • Classroom labs with Python and Jupyter

Not great for

  • Battery gadgets that must run for weeks
  • Reading analog sensors directly with no add-on board
  • Quick setups with only a board and a cable
  • Fully open hardware, as its main chip and schematic are closed

Specs in plain words

  • Four cores at 2.4 GHz make it feel like a basic laptop for browsing and coding.
  • Memory comes in sizes from 1 GB to 16 GB, and 8 GB is plenty for most projects and many browser tabs.
  • It has no built-in storage, and the operating system lives on a microSD card or a fast drive.
  • Two camera or screen ports let it watch with two cameras at once.
  • Two micro-HDMI ports can drive two 4K screens.
  • A 5 volt, 5 amp USB-C power supply keeps everything running at full speed.

RAM comes in 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 GB versions. Ram_kb shows the 8 GB board. There is no onboard eMMC. It boots from microSD, USB or an NVMe drive over PCIe. The 40-pin header exposes GPIO2 to GPIO27 at 3.3 V logic. There is no ADC. Powered off by default it draws about 1 to 1.4 W. With POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1 it draws about 0.01 W. The product brief lists the GPU as Vulkan 1.2 and the product page lists Vulkan 1.3. Operating temperature is 0 to 70 C. It has two USB 3.0 and two USB 2.0 Type-A ports and two micro-HDMI ports. The RTC battery connector needs a separately bought battery.

Starter ideas

  • First home computer for kids

    Plug in a screen, keyboard, and mouse for a full desktop. Kids can browse, write, and learn Python in Thonny.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: screen, keyboard, mouse, microSD card.

  • Blinking light with Python

    Wire an LED to the header pins and blink it with a few lines of gpiozero code. It is a classic first step.

    Add: LED, resistor, breadboard, jumper wires.

  • Retro game station

    Turn the Pi into a living room arcade on the TV. Plug in two USB gamepads and play classic games.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB gamepads, micro-HDMI cable.

  • Home media player

    Play movies and music on the TV in sharp 4K. A small remote or phone app picks the show.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: micro-HDMI cable, case.

  • Uninterrupted clock and alarm

    Keep accurate time with the built-in clock battery even with no internet. Trigger alarms and lights each morning.

    Uses: Battery ready, Runs Linux. Add: clock backup battery, speaker.

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

The Linux kernel and Raspberry Pi OS are open source, and the kernel uses GPL-2.0. The graphics chip has open drivers too. The Broadcom main chip, its startup firmware and the board schematic stay closed.
