Raspberry Pi AI HAT+ 2

Raspberry Pi Ltd

An AI add-on that runs small chatbots and vision models on a Raspberry Pi 5.

Overview

The Raspberry Pi AI HAT+ 2 is an add-on board for the Raspberry Pi 5. It carries a Hailo-10H AI chip with its own 8 GB of memory. That memory lets it run small chatbots and vision-language models with no cloud service. It suits tinkerers and teachers who already own a Pi 5 and want local AI.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running small chat models fully offline
  • Camera projects with object detection through the usual Pi camera apps
  • Classroom demos of how local AI works
  • Keeping the Pi 5 processor free for other jobs

Not great for

  • Raw chatbot speed, as one review found the Pi 5 processor faster on most models
  • Tight budgets, with a price near 200 US dollars plus the Pi 5
  • Running camera detection and a chatbot together, which failed in early tests
  • Projects that also need a fast NVMe drive on the Pi's single PCIe link

Specs in plain words

  • 40 TOPS is a measure of raw math speed for AI work at low precision.
  • 8 GB of its own memory holds the AI model next to the chip.
  • Launch models have 1 to 1.5 billion parameters, which is small enough for short chats and simple tasks.
  • The chip draws about 3 watts, a bit more than a phone charger at rest.
  • The board measures 66 by 56.5 millimeters, a bit smaller than a credit card.

ram_kb is the 8 GB on the HAT. It is not host memory. SparkFun lists LPDDR4 and Rapid and Jeff Geerling list LPDDR4X. TOPS is at INT4. CNX Software reports PCIe Gen3 x1 over an FPC cable. The 40-pin header is passed through and is not used by the Hailo-10H. Jeff Geerling reports the Hailo-10H is capped at about 3 W. Operating temperature is 0 to 50 C. Height is about 14 mm with heatsink push pins. The NPU uses the Pi 5's only PCIe lane. As a result, an NVMe SSD cannot be used at the same time without a PCIe switch HAT.

How open is it?

Open Code

The software stack is open. HailoRT and the hailo-apps examples use the MIT license, and the PCIe driver is GPL-2.0. The Hailo chip firmware is closed, and the model compiler comes from Hailo's Developer Zone. Raspberry Pi shares a mechanical drawing but no schematics or hardware license.

Hardware license: None stated · Software license: MIT (hailo-apps, libhailort), GPL-2.0 (hailort-drivers), LGPL-2.1-or-later (hailonet GStreamer plugin)
