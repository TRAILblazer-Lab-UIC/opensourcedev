Overview

The Raspberry Pi AI HAT+ is an add-on board for the Raspberry Pi 5. It carries a Hailo AI chip that runs vision models far faster than the Pi alone. It comes in a 13 TOPS version and a 26 TOPS version. It suits makers, teachers, and students who want smart camera projects on a Raspberry Pi.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Fast object detection on live camera video
  • Pose tracking and background cutouts
  • Smart cameras for the home, garden, or classroom
  • Learning how AI vision works with ready-made demos

Not great for

  • Older Raspberry Pi models, as it needs the PCIe port on the Pi 5
  • Chatbots and large language models, which the newer AI HAT+ 2 targets
  • Custom models for beginners, as compiling them needs an x86 Linux PC
  • Projects on a tight budget, as it adds 70 to 110 US dollars to the Pi

Specs in plain words

  • 13 TOPS means about 13 trillion simple math steps each second.
  • A small detection model runs at about 30 to 40 frames per second on the 13 TOPS board. The Pi alone manages about 2 to 5.
  • The 26 TOPS version runs twice the math and suits heavier models.
  • The board is about 6.5 by 5.7 centimeters, the same footprint as the Pi 5.
  • It draws about 2 to 3 watts, and the Pi needs a 5 V 5 A supply to keep up.

TOPS value is for the 13 TOPS base model. The 26 TOPS Hailo-8 model doubles it. The board uses the Pi 5 RAM. The NPU package is about 17 x 17 mm. Operating range is 0 to 50 C ambient. The brief says the board uses PCIe Gen 3. An independent guide says Gen 3 is experimental on the Pi 5 and suggests Gen 2 for stability. It reports about 2 to 3 W draw for the Hailo-8L and recommends a 5 V 5 A supply for the Pi.

How open is it?

Open Code

Raspberry Pi publishes a product brief and a mechanical drawing, but no schematics or board layout. The HailoRT runtime and PCIe driver are on GitHub, with the driver under GPL-2.0 and the example apps under MIT. The Hailo chip firmware and the model compiler stay closed.

Hardware license: None. No design files published · Software license: MIT (hailo-rpi5-examples), GPL-2.0 (hailort-drivers), HailoRT license not shown by GitHub API
