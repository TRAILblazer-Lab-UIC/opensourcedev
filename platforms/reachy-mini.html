Overview

Reachy Mini is a small desktop robot kit from Pollen Robotics and Hugging Face. It has an expressive head, a turning body, two antennas, a camera, four microphones, and a speaker. It suits AI fans, teachers, and robot hobbyists who want a friendly face for their code. It stands out for its easy link to shared AI apps on Hugging Face.

Specs in plain words

  • Nine small servo motors move the head in six directions, turn the body and wag the antennas.
  • A 12 megapixel wide camera sees a whole desk and can autofocus on faces.
  • Four microphones pick up voices from around the room, and a 5 watt speaker talks back.
  • The Wireless version has a Raspberry Pi computer module with 4 GB of memory, Wi-Fi and a battery.
  • The Lite version has no computer inside and runs from your Mac, Windows or Linux computer over USB-C.
  • It stands about 30 centimeters tall at full stretch and weighs about as much as a large bottle of water.

Specs describe the Wireless version, which uses a CM4104016 module (Wi-Fi, 4 GB RAM, 16 GB eMMC). The Lite version has no onboard computer, battery or IMU. It connects to a Mac, Windows or Linux PC over USB-C, which runs the code. The Lite weighs 1.35 kg and the Wireless 1.475 kg. Size is the extended height envelope in mm. The USB-C port on the back does not charge the robot. Early press reports said the Wireless used a Raspberry Pi 5. Current docs say CM4.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Chatty desk companion

    Run the conversation app and talk with the robot out loud. It answers by voice and nods along as it speaks.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Dance party performer

    Pick moves from the dances library and play a song. The robot bobs its head and spins its body to the beat.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

  • Emotion acting game

    Program happy, sad, and surprised poses with the head and antennas. Kids guess the feeling the robot shows.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Stretch break coach

    Remind you to stand up every hour and show a short stretch with its head. A gentle nudge for long study sessions.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

Builder ideas

  • Face-following greeter

    Track a face with the camera and turn the head to keep eye contact. Visitors get a friendly look as they walk by.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors.

  • Storytime reader

    Have the robot read a picture book aloud and act out the scenes. Its head and antennas add drama to each page.

    Uses: Makes sound, Drives motors.

  • Language practice partner

    Practice simple phrases in a new language with spoken replies. The robot nods to cheer you on.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Object naming quiz

    Hold up an item and let a vision model guess what it is. The robot announces its guess and looks proud.

    Uses: Camera, Makes sound.

  • Simulator lesson on robot motion

    Try moves in the MuJoCo simulator before running them on the real robot. Students learn how six joints work together.

  • Shared app on Hugging Face

    Build a small app with the Python SDK and publish it as a Space. Other owners can install and try it.

    Uses: Wi-Fi.

  • Plant check-in camera

    Point the camera at a windowsill garden and snap a photo each morning. Compare growth over a month.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • Sound direction turner

    Use the four microphones to sense where a voice comes from. The head turns toward whoever is speaking.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors.

  • Balance and tilt explorer

    Read the motion sensor and make the head stay level as you tilt the base. It shows how robots sense their pose.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • All-local voice assistant

    Chain speech to text, a small language model, and text to speech on your own hardware. Conversations stay private.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Runs Linux.
