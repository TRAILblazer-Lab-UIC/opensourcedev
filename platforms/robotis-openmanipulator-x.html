Overview

OpenManipulator-X is a small robot arm from Robotis. It has four joints plus a gripper, all made from five smart servo motors. The arm reaches about 38 centimeters and lifts about half a kilogram. It suits college classes and labs that teach ROS 2, often alongside the TurtleBot3.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how robot arms move with ROS 2 and MoveIt 2
  • Classes that already use TurtleBot3 or the OpenCR board
  • Practicing in the Gazebo simulator before using the real arm
  • Mobile robots that need a small arm to pick things up
  • Rebuilding the parts into other robot shapes

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, with the kit at about 1,400 to 1,600 US dollars
  • Lifting anything heavier than about half a kilogram
  • Beginners who have never used Linux
  • A quick plug and play start, as the controller and power supply cost extra

Specs in plain words

  • Five servos give the arm four bending joints and one gripper that opens from about 2 to 7.5 centimeters.
  • The reach of 380 millimeters is about the length of a school ruler and a bit more.
  • The arm repeats a spot within less than 0.2 millimeters, about the thickness of two sheets of paper.
  • The arm has no brain of its own. A PC or the OpenCR board tells each servo where to go.
  • The arm weighs about 0.7 kilograms, about the same as a full water bottle.
  • It runs on a 12 volt power supply that you buy separately.

The spec page lists 5 DoF (4 plus a gripper), 500 g payload, 380 mm reach, 0.70 kg weight, 46 RPM joint speed, under 0.2 mm repeatability and a 20 to 75 mm gripper stroke. Communication is a TTL multidrop bus to the Dynamixel servos. The arm has no processor of its own. As a result, compute specs are empty. The kit does not include a controller, U2D2, power supply or base plate.

Builder ideas

  • Pick and place blocks

    Teach the arm to grab a foam block and set it in a box. It is the classic first task for any robot arm.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: foam blocks, small box.

  • Practice arm in a simulator

    Run a virtual copy of the arm in Gazebo on your computer. A class can try moves safely before the real arm touches anything.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Drag the arm to teach it

    Move the arm by hand through a path and record the joint positions. Play the recording back and the arm repeats your motion.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Plant label helper

    Let the arm place plant markers into a tray of seedling pots. It repeats each spot within a fraction of a millimeter.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: seedling tray, plant markers.

  • Drawing arm for art class

    Tape a marker to the gripper and send it a simple shape. The arm sketches the shape on paper.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: marker, paper, tape.

  • Robot arm math lab

    Measure the arm joints and work out where the gripper should land. Then test your answer on the real arm.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: ruler.

  • Gentle grip tester

    Close the gripper on soft fruit at different strengths. Students learn how robots handle fragile things.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: soft fruit.

Expert ideas

  • Plan moves around obstacles

    Use MoveIt 2 to plan a path that avoids a cup on the table. The arm reaches its goal without bumping the cup.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: plastic cup.

  • Sort things by color

    Add a USB webcam above the table and spot red and blue blocks. The arm drops each color into its own bin.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: USB webcam, colored blocks, two small bins.

  • Robot on wheels

    Mount the arm on a TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi. The robot drives to a table and picks up an object.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: wheeled robot base.

  • Chess piece mover

    Have the arm move pieces on a small board on command. You type a move and the arm makes it.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: small chess set.

  • Rebuild it as a new robot

    Rearrange the five servos into a SCARA or delta robot. Each shape teaches a different kind of robot motion.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printed parts.

  • Copycat arm with a leader arm

    Build a second arm and move it by hand. The real arm copies each move and records data for robot learning.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: second set of servos.

How open is it?

Open Board

The ROS 2 software, OpenCR firmware, and Dynamixel SDK are open under the Apache-2.0 license. Robotis shares the plastic parts as STL files for 3D printing, but no hardware license is named. The servo motor firmware and the chips stay closed.
