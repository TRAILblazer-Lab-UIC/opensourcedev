Overview

OMY is a six-joint robot arm from Robotis for research in robot learning. A person moves a light leader arm, and the bigger follower arm copies each motion. The recorded motions train AI models that let the arm repeat tasks alone. It suits university labs and research teams with a large budget and ROS 2 skills.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Imitation learning research with LeRobot tools
  • Teleoperation demos with a leader and follower arm
  • Testing robot skills in simulation and then on real hardware
  • Labs that want open ROS 2 software on a sturdy arm

Not great for

  • Hobbyists, as prices start near 2,900 US dollars for the leader arm alone
  • Beginners, as it needs ROS 2 and Linux skills
  • Quick projects, as each arm is made to order with a 6 to 8 week wait
  • Open hardware fans, as the arm and its actuator firmware are closed

Specs in plain words

  • Six joints let the arm reach and turn much like a human arm.
  • A 3 kg payload means it can lift about three liters of water.
  • A 580 mm reach is about the length of an adult's arm.
  • Repeatability of 0.05 mm is thinner than a human hair.
  • A Raspberry Pi 5 inside the system runs ROS 2 and updates the joints 400 times per second.
  • The follower weighs 13.5 kg and needs a sturdy table and a 24 V supply, which comes in the box.

Power24 V DC for the follower arm (SMPS included). 12 V DC for the OMY-L100 leader

The follower links to the host over Ethernet and runs its actuators on an internal 4 Mbps RS-485 bus with Dynamixel Protocol 2.0. The end-tool port gives 24 V at 1 A and RS-485 with Dynamixel Protocol 2.0 or Modbus-RTU on an M8 8-pin connector. Joints 1 and 2 use YM080-230-A099-RH and joints 3 to 6 use YM070-210-A099-RH, with TCP speed under 900 mm/s. The OMY-L100 leader weighs 1.46 kg, reaches 560 mm and uses XH540-W150, XC330-T288 and XC330-T181 servos. The camera is on the OMY-F3M only. The Pi 5 RAM size was not stated, and whether the Pi sits inside the arm or in a separate box was not confirmed.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Practice in simulation first

    Load the free OMY model into Gazebo or MuJoCo and plan moves on your laptop. Test ideas before the real hardware arrives.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Hand-guided motion demo

    Use gravity compensation to move the arm by hand like a floating object. Students feel how six joints work together.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Remote control show for an open day

    Let visitors move the light leader arm and watch the big follower copy each motion. It makes robotics feel real for families.

    Uses: Drives motors, Wired network. Add: leader arm.

  • Record a robot learning dataset

    Guide the arm through a task many times with Physical AI Tools. Save the motions and camera video as a LeRobot dataset.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: leader arm.

  • Robot drawing artist

    Mount a pen on the end tool and draw pictures from a file. Precise joints make clean lines on paper.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: pen holder tool, drawing board.

Expert ideas

  • Teach a task by demonstration

    Train an imitation learning model on your recorded runs. The arm then repeats the task on its own.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: leader arm, computer with a graphics card.

  • Color sorting station

    Use the wrist camera to spot blocks by color and drop each one into a matching bin. It is a classic lab exercise.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: colored blocks, bins.

  • Lab sample helper

    Move small tubes between racks for a science lab. Steady motion keeps samples safe.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera. Add: test tube racks.

  • Potted plant waterer

    Use the camera to find plants on a shelf and tip a small cup of water into each pot.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: small watering cup.

  • Chess playing partner

    Spot board positions with the camera and move pieces for a chess engine. Play a real game against the arm.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: chess set.

  • Towel folding research

    Teach the arm to fold soft cloth with leader-follower practice. Soft objects are a hard test for robot learning.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: leader arm, small towels.

  • Train in sim, run for real

    Train a skill in Isaac Sim and move it to the real arm. Compare how well the virtual practice carries over.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: computer with a graphics card.

  • Custom tool on the end port

    Build your own gripper or sensor tool for the M8 end-tool port. Control it over the same bus as the joints.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: custom gripper.
