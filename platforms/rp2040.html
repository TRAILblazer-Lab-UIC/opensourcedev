Overview

The RP2040 is the first chip Raspberry Pi designed in house. It launched on the 4 dollar Raspberry Pi Pico in January 2021 and now sits on hundreds of other boards. Its special PIO blocks act like tiny helpers that can speak almost any pin language, from LED strips to video. It suits total beginners, classrooms and seasoned tinkerers alike.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First coding projects in MicroPython or CircuitPython
  • LED strips, light shows and costumes
  • Custom keyboards and other USB gadgets
  • Robots and sensor projects on a tight budget
  • Classes that need many cheap boards

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on the plain Pico, which has no radio
  • Math-heavy audio or AI work, as the chip lacks a floating point unit
  • Big CircuitPython projects on 2 MB boards
  • Projects that need security features such as secure boot

Specs in plain words

  • Two 133 MHz cores let one part of your program read sensors as the other drives lights.
  • 264 KB of working memory holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • The Pico has 2 MB of flash storage for your code and files. Some third-party boards carry 8 or 16 MB.
  • Eight PIO helpers can create extra ports or video signals on almost any pin.
  • The Pico is about 5 by 2 centimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.
  • In deep sleep the chip draws about 0.18 milliamps.

Values are for the Raspberry Pi Pico with 2 MB flash, no PSRAM, micro-USB and a BOOTSEL button. The Pico exposes 26 GPIO with 3 analog inputs. The chip has 30 GPIO and 4 ADC inputs. clock_mhz is the original rating. The chip is certified for 200 MHz at 1.15 V since SDK 2.1.1, and the SDK default stays 125 MHz. Flash varies by board. The Feather RP2040 has 8 MB and the SparkFun Pro Micro RP2040 has 16 MB. low_power_ua is the typical DVDD current in DORMANT mode (0.18 mA) from datasheet table 658. The Pico W adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5.2 through a CYW43439 radio. I2S and other buses are made with PIO in place of fixed peripherals.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blinking light

    Plug in a Pico and type a few lines of MicroPython in Thonny. The tiny green LED starts to blink.

  • Classroom temperature logger

    Read the temperature sensor inside the chip every minute. Students save the readings and graph the room over a day.

  • Live light graph for science

    Wire a light sensor to an analog pin and watch the plotter in Thonny. The line jumps each time a cloud passes.

    Add: light sensor.

  • Arcade reaction game

    Light a big button at random and time the press. Two players can race for the fastest score.

    Add: arcade buttons.

  • Rainbow LED strip lamp

    Drive a strip of color LEDs with the special PIO pins. The lamp fades through a rainbow on your desk.

    Add: LED strip, 5 volt power supply.

Builder ideas

  • Custom macro keyboard

    Wire a grid of key switches to the board. Each key can type a phrase or fire a shortcut on your computer.

    Add: key switches, keycaps.

  • USB drum pads

    Tap pads fitted with knock sensors to play drums. The board shows up as a music device in a computer drum app.

    Add: piezo knock sensors.

  • Plant watering helper

    Check a soil sensor every hour. The board switches on a small pump for a few seconds once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Garden weather screen

    Read temperature and humidity outside the back door. A small screen shows the numbers by the window.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

  • Heart rate display

    Clip a pulse sensor to your finger. The board counts beats and shows your heart rate on a small screen.

    Add: pulse sensor, small screen.

  • Light-up costume

    Sew an LED strip into a jacket or a hat. A battery pack powers patterns that sparkle at a party.

    Add: LED strip, battery pack.

  • Line-following robot

    Two sensors watch a black line on the floor. The board steers two motors to keep the robot on track.

    Add: motor driver board, small DC motors, line sensors, battery pack.

  • Joystick robot arm

    Move a joystick to steer a few servo motors. A small arm picks up paper cups and drops them in a bin.

    Add: servo motors, joystick module, robot arm kit.

Expert ideas

  • Homemade retro game console

    Use the PIO pins to send a picture to a VGA monitor. Play simple games you write with a few buttons.

    Add: VGA connector board, monitor, push buttons.

  • Typing test with a real keyboard

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board through an adapter. A small screen shows your typing speed and mistakes.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB OTG adapter, small screen.

How open is it?

Wild Open

Raspberry Pi shares the Pico design files and grants permission to use, copy and change them. The Pico SDK is BSD-3-Clause and builds with free GCC tools. Several RP2040 boards, such as the Adafruit Feather RP2040, are certified open hardware. The chip design and the Pico W radio firmware stay closed.

Flash memory is storage that keeps its data with the power off.

A microcontroller is a tiny computer on a single chip.

NeoPixel

Permissive license
