Overview

The RTL8720DN is a wireless chip from Realtek with Wi-Fi on both the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands plus Bluetooth LE 5.0. Most hobbyists meet it on the low-cost BW16 module and BW16 dev boards. It also works as the wireless helper inside the Seeed Wio Terminal. It suits makers who already know a bit of Arduino and want cheap 5 GHz Wi-Fi.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wi-Fi gadgets that need the less crowded 5 GHz band
  • Wi-Fi scanners and signal strength tools
  • Small battery sensors that sleep most of the time
  • Bluetooth LE remote controls from a phone
  • Very low cost builds, with the bare module near five US dollars

Not great for

  • Total beginners, as the first upload can need a flash erase and button presses
  • Projects that need many pins, with only 13 on the BW16 board
  • Camera or sound projects
  • Finding lots of tutorials, as the community is small

Specs in plain words

  • Wi-Fi on two bands lets the board join a busy 2.4 GHz network or a quieter 5 GHz one.
  • The main core runs at 200 MHz, which is plenty for sensors and simple web pages.
  • 512 KB of memory holds a small web page and a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • 2 MB of flash storage on the BW16 module fits your program and its libraries.
  • Deep sleep draws about 10 microamps, which helps a battery last for months.
  • The BW16 dev board is about 5 by 2.5 centimeters, close to the size of a stick of gum.

Values are for the BW16 dev board and the RTL8720DN chip. The KM4 runs at up to 200 MHz and the KM0 at up to 20 MHz per the RTL872xD datasheet. The datasheet lists 512 KB main SRAM and no internal flash or PSRAM for the RTL8720DN. The 2 MB flash is the external SPI flash on the BW16 module per amebaiot.com, which says up to 4 MB is possible. gpio is the 13 pins in the Ameba BW16 pin table. adc_channels is the x3 ADC count for the QFN48 RTL8720DN in the datasheet package table. low_power_ua is the datasheet typical deep-sleep current, with sleep mode at 38 uA typical. The chip has a USB host or device block per the datasheet, but the BW16 boards do not break it out. Some BW16 boards use micro-USB and the BW16 Type-C uses USB-C. Size is from the Ameba BW16 docs. Seeed and CNX describe the cores as Cortex-M4F and Cortex-M0. By contrast, the Realtek datasheet says Cortex-M33 and Cortex-M23 compatible.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink the color light

    Upload a short Arduino sketch to the BW16 dev board. The RGB LED cycles through colors on command.

  • Home Wi-Fi band checker

    Scan the air for nearby networks on both bands. The board lists each network and its signal strength on your computer.

    Uses: Wi-Fi.

  • Room temperature web page

    Read a temperature sensor and serve the number on a small web page. Anyone on the home network can check it from a phone.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Phone-controlled desk lamp

    Pair your phone over Bluetooth LE and tap a button in an app. A relay turns a small lamp on or off.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: relay module, small lamp.

  • Garden soil alarm

    Check a soil moisture sensor every hour. The board sends a message over Wi-Fi once the plants get thirsty.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • Classroom Wi-Fi signal map

    Carry the board around the school and log signal strength in each room. Students turn the numbers into a heat map.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: battery pack.

  • Bluetooth tag finder

    Listen for nearby Bluetooth LE beacons and show how close each one is. It helps track down a lost bag or a pet collar tag.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Door open notifier

    Watch a door with a magnetic switch. The board sends an alert over Wi-Fi each time the door swings open.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: magnetic door switch.

  • Weather station on a battery

    Wake up every few minutes to read air pressure and humidity. The board posts the readings and goes back to deep sleep.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: weather sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Bird feeder visit counter

    Count each visit with a motion sensor near the feeder. The board sleeps between visits and reports a daily total.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack.

  • Scoreboard for game night

    Show team scores on a small screen. Players tap buttons on a phone over Bluetooth LE to add points.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Dual-band network monitor

    Ping a list of devices on both Wi-Fi bands around the clock. The board logs any drop and flags weak spots on a small screen.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: small screen.

  • Community air sensor node

    Build several air quality nodes for a block or a school yard. Each one reports dust levels to a shared online map.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: air quality sensor, weatherproof box, USB power supply.

How open is it?

Open Board

The Ameba Arduino package is MIT licensed and the AmebaD SDK is Apache-2.0. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth stacks ship as closed precompiled libraries. The Seeed Wio Terminal shares schematics and Gerbers under Apache-2.0, but the BW16 boards have no published design files and the chip design is closed.

Hardware license: Apache-2.0 (Seeed OSHW-WioTerminal repo), BW16 boards have no published design files or license found · Software license: MIT (Ameba Arduino package), Apache-2.0 (ameba-rtos-d SDK repo)
