Overview

The RW612 is a wireless microcontroller from NXP. It packs Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth Low Energy, and Thread or Zigbee radios onto one chip. It also adds wired Ethernet and a USB port that can act as a host. It suits people who want to build smart home devices and already know some C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Building Matter smart home devices that work with big home apps.
  • Making a Thread border router or a home gateway.
  • Projects that need both Wi-Fi and wired Ethernet.
  • Learning Zephyr on a cheap, well-equipped board.

Not great for

  • First-time coders. The only real paths are C with Zephyr or the NXP SDK.
  • Fans of Arduino or MicroPython. No port for this chip turned up in the research.
  • Fully open setups. The Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radios need closed firmware files.
  • Battery projects that need known sleep currents. The low-power numbers were not found.

Specs in plain words

  • A 260 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 core is quick enough to run radios, a small screen, and sensors together.
  • 1.2 MB of built-in memory holds a lot of live data for a microcontroller.
  • The chip has no built-in storage. The FRDM-RW612 board adds 64 MB of flash for your programs.
  • Dual-band Wi-Fi 6 works on both 2.4 and 5 GHz home networks.
  • The 802.15.4 radio speaks Thread and Zigbee, which many smart home gadgets use.
  • The RW610 is the same chip without the Thread and Zigbee radio.

ConnectorsUSB-C, Pin headers

Power5 V over USB-C on the FRDM-RW612. Chip runs from a single 3.3 V supply

The chip has no internal flash and runs from external QSPI flash. flash_kb and psram_kb are for the FRDM-RW612, which carries a 512 Mbit Winbond W25Q512JV flash and a 64 Mbit APS6404L PSRAM. The u-blox IRIS-W10 modules carry 8 MB or 16 MB of flash. ram_kb rounds the 1.2 MB of on-chip SRAM. The single core listed is the application core. GPIO count, ADC channels and low-power current were not found on the pages read. Zephyr lists ADC, DAC and comparator support on the FRDM-RW612. USB is listed from the Zephyr board page, which supports the SoC EHCI controller in device and host modes. Connectors and onboard parts are for the FRDM-RW612, which has two USB-C ports, Arduino, FRDM, Pmod and mikroBUS headers, Ethernet, an RGB LED, three buttons and a P3T1755 temperature sensor. Packages are TFBGA145, WLCSP151 and HVQFN116.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable, temperature and humidity sensor, light sensor.

    Listen for nearby Bluetooth sensor tags and log their readings. A shelf of potted plants can report in one place.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: Bluetooth plant sensors.

Openness level

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

NXP shares a schematic and design files for the FRDM-RW612, but they need a free account and no hardware license is stated. The MCUXpresso SDK core is BSD-3-Clause, and Zephyr support is Apache-2.0. The chip, the radio firmware, and the default flashing tools stay closed.
