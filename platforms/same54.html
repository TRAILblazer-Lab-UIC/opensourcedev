Overview

The SAME54 is a 32-bit Arm microcontroller from Microchip. It is the SAMD51 chip plus wired Ethernet and two CAN bus controllers. Hobbyists mostly meet it on the SAM E54 Xplained Pro kit. It suits makers who want wired networking or CAN bus and can work in C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Wired Ethernet projects in places where Wi-Fi is weak.
  • Learning CAN bus, the network found in cars and machines.
  • Practicing Zephyr or RIOT on a well-documented kit.
  • Simple CircuitPython projects with LEDs, buttons, and sensors.

Not great for

  • Tight budgets. The Xplained Pro kit costs over $100.
  • CircuitPython fans who want Ethernet or CAN. The CircuitPython build skips both.
  • Wireless projects. The chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
  • Quick shopping. Some distributors list very long lead times.

Specs in plain words

  • A 120 MHz Cortex-M4F core with a math unit handles sensor math and audio quickly.
  • 1 MB of flash holds a large program plus CircuitPython libraries.
  • 256 KB of memory holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 99 pins give room for many buttons, lights, and sensors on one board.
  • Two fast 12-bit ADCs read analog sensors and microphones in fine detail.
  • Two DACs can turn numbers into sound or smooth voltages.

Power1.71 to 3.63 V chip supply. Xplained Pro takes 5 V over micro USB

Values are for the 128-pin ATSAME54P20A on the SAM E54 Xplained Pro. It has 1024 KB flash, 256 KB SRAM, 99 I/O pins and 16 channels on each of its two ADCs. The 64-pin J and 100-pin N parts have fewer pins and ADC channels. The Xplained Pro adds 32 Mbit QSPI flash, an RJ45 jack with a KSZ8091RNA PHY, an ATA6561 CAN transceiver, an SD card slot, a QTouch button and a user LED. The camera_dvp entry reflects the parallel capture controller. The board size and the low-power current were not confirmed.

Things you could build

Starter ideas

  • Blink and button starter

    Load CircuitPython and blink the user LED with a few lines of code. Add the onboard button to toggle it.

  • Classroom sensor logger

    Read a temperature sensor with CircuitPython and save the readings to an SD card. Students can graph a full day of data.

    Add: temperature sensor, microSD card.

  • Plant light meter

    Measure light with a sensor on an analog pin and log it each hour. See which window gets the most sun for your plants.

    Add: light sensor, microSD card.

Builder ideas

  • Tone and melody maker

    Use the built-in DAC to play tones through a small speaker. Program a doorbell tune or a simple music box.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Sound level meter

    Sample a microphone with the fast ADC and show how loud the room is on LEDs. A class can see when the noise gets too high.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone module, LED bar.

  • USB keyboard game controller

    Read a USB keyboard through the host port and use it to drive a simple game on a screen. Build a classroom quiz buzzer.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, small display.

  • Greenhouse control panel

    Read soil and air sensors and switch a fan and pump on a schedule. Log every action to the SD card.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, relay module, small fan, small water pump, microSD card.

Expert ideas

  • Wired weather web page

    Serve a small web page over Ethernet that shows live weather sensor readings. Wired networking skips Wi-Fi trouble in a basement or barn.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable, temperature and humidity sensor.

  • CAN bus car data reader

    Read messages from a CAN bus with Zephyr or RIOT and print them on your computer. Explore how car parts and machines talk.

    Add: CAN bus adapter cable.

  • Network clock with time sync

    Use the Ethernet port's precise time stamps to keep a clock in step with a network time server. Show the time on a small screen.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable, small display.

  • Simple camera snapshot

    Hook a small parallel camera to the capture controller and grab still frames in C. It is a hands-on lesson in how images become data.

    Uses: Camera. Add: parallel camera module.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot confirmed
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The software path is fully open, with CircuitPython under MIT, Zephyr under Apache-2.0, and RIOT under LGPL-2.1. Code builds with GCC and flashes with open tools such as edbg and OpenOCD. Microchip offers design files for the Xplained Pro, but no hardware license was found. The chip itself is a closed Arm design.
