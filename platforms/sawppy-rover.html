Sawppy Rover

Roger Cheng (Roger-random, newscrewdriver.com)

A build-it-yourself model of NASA's Mars rovers made from printed parts.

Field marks: Look for a six-wheeled Mars rover replica with about 300 screws, ten servos all named ID 1, and a $500 receipt.

Overview

Sawppy is a free set of plans for a motorized model of NASA's Curiosity and Perseverance rovers. Roger Cheng designed it in 2018 as a cheaper take on the JPL Open Source Rover. It uses aluminum rails, 3D-printed brackets, and ten serial bus servos. A Raspberry Pi lets you drive it from a web page. Makers, clubs, and families build it as a big printing and robotics project.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Families and clubs who want a long, rewarding build together.
  • Showing how real Mars rover suspension works.
  • Practicing 3D printing, wiring, and mechanical assembly.
  • Learning ROS and robot simulation with community software.

Not great for

  • Quick weekend projects. The build takes many sessions.
  • People without a 3D printer. The rover needs about 3 kg of printed parts.
  • Heavy loads or rough outdoor use. The printed frame is light duty.
  • Buyers who want a kit. No official kit is sold.

Specs in plain words

  • Six wheels on a rocker-bogie suspension climb over rocks while all wheels stay down.
  • Four steerable corner wheels let the rover turn in place like the real one.
  • Ten LX-16A servos act as motors, gears, and position sensors in one part each.
  • A Raspberry Pi 3 with 1 GB of memory runs the web control page over Wi-Fi.
  • A 2S LiPo battery around 5200 mAh powers the whole rover.
  • The parts budget is about $500 in the US, and careful shoppers may get near $400.

The original build uses a Raspberry Pi 3 and a LewanSoul BusLinker USB-to-serial board to drive the ten servos. The Pi 3 clock and RAM values are the standard Pi 3 Model B figures. It is not stated in the Sawppy docs. The chassis box uses 385 mm and 245 mm extrusions, but overall rover dimensions and weight were not found. Roger estimated payload at roughly double a 2.3 kg laptop and called it an unreliable estimate. A separate ESP32 PlatformIO project targets Micro Sawppy and does not yet drive LX-16A servos.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Library parade float

    Dress the rover with a sign and lights for a community event or a STEM night. Let visitors take turns driving.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: LED strip, cardboard sign.

Builder ideas

  • Backyard Mars mission

    Drive the rover over rocks and grass from a phone's web browser. Plan a pretend Mars landing site for the family.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Rocker-bogie science demo

    Roll the rover over books and blocks to show how its suspension keeps all six wheels on the ground. It makes a great classroom lesson.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Rover camera tour

    Add a USB webcam and stream the view to a laptop. Kids can steer by screen alone like real rover drivers.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: USB webcam.

  • Joystick driving station

    Build the wired Arduino joystick backup controller. It lets young drivers steer without a phone.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: Arduino board, joystick module, long cable.

  • Garden patrol rover

    Mount a soil moisture probe on the rover and drive it between garden beds. Check which plants need water.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: soil moisture sensor, analog to digital converter board.

  • Weather sampling rover

    Carry a temperature and humidity sensor across a park or schoolyard. Map warm and cool spots on a laptop.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Battery ready, Runs Linux. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Rover sound effects

    Plug a USB speaker into the Raspberry Pi and play beeps and space sounds as it drives. Add a recorded mission briefing.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: USB speaker.

  • Club build season

    Split the printing, wiring, and servo setup across a robotics club. Each team owns one corner of the rover.

    Add: 3D printer, filament, hand tools.

Expert ideas

  • ROS 2 simulation practice

    Run the community ROS 2 software and drive a virtual Sawppy in Gazebo. Test ideas on screen before the real rover moves.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Self-driving obstacle stop

    Add a distance sensor and write Python that halts the rover before it bumps a wall. It is a first taste of robot autonomy.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: distance sensor.

  • Robotic arm add-on

    Design a small printed arm on the front of the rover using extra serial bus servos. Pick up light rocks like a real sample arm.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: serial bus servos, 3D printed arm parts.

The whole design is under the MIT license. The repo holds STL files for every printed part, a wiring diagram, build docs, and the software. The source CAD lives in a public Onshape model. The servos and the Raspberry Pi are closed commercial parts.
