Overview

The Grove Beginner Kit is one panel with an Arduino-style board and ten modules already wired up. It has an LED, a buzzer, a small screen, a button, a knob, and five kinds of sensors. Total beginners can run lessons with no wiring or soldering. Teachers like its 12-project course and low price.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First steps with Arduino code
  • Classrooms that need a quick setup
  • Science lessons with light, sound, temperature, and pressure readings
  • Block coding with Codecraft
  • Growing into the wider family of plug-in Grove modules

Not great for

  • Learning to wire circuits on a breadboard
  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth projects, as it has no radio
  • Big programs, as memory is very small
  • Projects in a tight space, as the panel is about the size of a paperback book

Specs in plain words

  • The 16 MHz chip handles one simple job at a time, like reading a sensor and beeping.
  • 2 KB of working memory holds a few dozen sensor readings at once.
  • 32 KB of storage fits a typical beginner program with room to spare.
  • The 0.96 inch screen is about as wide as a thumbnail and shows a few short lines of text.
  • 12 plug-in ports let you add more Grove sensors and parts with no soldering.

MCU part, 16 MHz crystal and modules come from the v1.1 KiCad schematic. Flash, SRAM and ADC counts are ATmega328P chip values from Wikipedia. The TQFP package has 8 ADC inputs. The wiki uses A0, A2 and A6 for the potentiometer, sound and light sensors. Size is the full kit PCB from the Seeed store page, before modules are broken off. imu means the LIS3DH 3-axis accelerometer at I2C 0x19. temperature means the DHT20 temperature and humidity sensor on D3. The air pressure sensor is SPA06-003 (BMP280 on kits before October 2025). The temperature sensor was DHT11 before October 2025. microphone means an analog sound sensor (KS-9745P mic with LM358 amp). It is not a digital microphone. Onboard extras outside the vocab are a red LED on D4, a passive buzzer on D5 and a rotary potentiometer on A0. The USB-serial bridge is a CP2102N (CH340T on early revisions per the schematic change log). The store page says the current version uses USB-C. The wiki still names a Micro USB cable for the old version.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blinking light and beeper

    Make the LED blink and the buzzer beep in time. It is the classic first project with zero wiring.

  • Room weather station

    Show the temperature, humidity, and air pressure on the little screen. Watch the numbers change through the day.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Night light that wakes up

    The light sensor notices the room getting dark. The LED switches on by itself.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Clap counter

    The sound sensor hears each clap. The screen keeps a running count.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Knob-tuned buzzer music

    Turn the knob to change the pitch of the buzzer. Press the button to play a short tune.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Plug a soil moisture sensor into a spare port. The buzzer beeps once the soil gets dry.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in soil moisture sensor.

  • Reaction time game

    The LED lights up at a random moment. Press the button fast and the screen shows your time.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Tilt maze on the screen

    Tilt the panel to roll a dot through a maze on the screen. The motion sensor reads each tilt.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Classroom noise traffic light

    The sound sensor checks how loud the room is. Add a plug-in LED strip that glows green, yellow, or red.

    Uses: Hears sound, Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in LED strip.

  • Weather change forecaster

    Track air pressure over a few hours. A falling number on the screen hints that rain may come.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Step counter science test

    Break off the motion sensor and tape it to a shoe. Count steps and compare walking with running.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Plug-in connectors. Add: tape, long connector cable.

  • Servo pointer gauge

    Plug in a servo motor with a paper arrow. The arrow swings to show the room temperature like an old dial.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Sensors built in. Add: plug-in servo motor, cardboard.

Expert ideas

  • Automatic plant waterer

    Add a soil sensor, a relay, and a small pump. The board waters the pot once the soil gets dry.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: plug-in soil moisture sensor, plug-in relay, small water pump, power supply.
