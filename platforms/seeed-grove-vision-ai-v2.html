Overview

The Grove Vision AI Module V2 is a tiny board from Seeed Studio that runs AI vision models on its own. A Raspberry Pi style camera plugs into it, and the board reports what it sees to a host such as a XIAO, Arduino or Raspberry Pi. You can load a ready model from a web page with no code at all. It suits makers and classes who want low-cost AI vision on a small budget.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Person and object detection on a tiny budget
  • Adding AI eyes to an Arduino or XIAO project
  • No-code model loading through SenseCraft AI
  • Learning about edge AI with Edge Impulse

Not great for

  • Projects that need Wi-Fi on the board itself, as it has no radio
  • Streaming video, as it sends results and not a live feed
  • Buyers who expect a camera in the box, as the bare module has none
  • Big custom models, as memory is small

Specs in plain words

  • Two Arm cores at 400 and 150 MHz handle the camera and the host link.
  • A built-in AI engine called an NPU runs small vision models fast for very little power.
  • About 2.4 MB of working memory fits small detection models like YOLOv8 nano.
  • 16 MB of flash stores the firmware and a model, about the space of a few phone photos.
  • The board is 40 by 20 millimeters, about the size of your thumb.
  • A Grove plug and XIAO headers let it talk to a host over I2C or UART.

clock_mhz is the big Cortex-M55 core. The little core runs at up to 150 MHz. ram_kb is the configurable system SRAM. There is also a 64 KB boot ROM. Flash is a 16 MB Winbond W25Q128JW seen on the schematic and reported by CNX Software. USB goes through a CH343P USB-to-UART bridge. Reset and BOOT/user buttons are on board. The camera is not included with the bare module. Size is from the Make review. No TOPS figure or sleep current was published on fetched pages.

Starter ideas

  • Person detector night light

    Flash the ready person detection model from the browser. A light turns on each time someone walks in.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board, LED strip.

  • Rock paper scissors game

    Play rock paper scissors against the board. It reads your hand and picks its own move on a little screen.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board, small OLED screen.

Builder ideas

  • Smart doorway counter

    Count people passing through a door at a library or club room. Show the total on a small screen.

    Uses: Camera, Plug-in connectors. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board, small OLED screen.

  • Garden pest watch

    Train a model to spot squirrels or birds in your garden bed. A host board sounds a buzzer to shoo them away.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board, buzzer, weatherproof box.

  • Gesture remote for music

    Use a hand gesture model to skip or pause songs. Rock, paper, and scissors can each mean a different command.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board.

  • Home Assistant presence sensor

    Send person detection results to Home Assistant through a XIAO ESP32 board. Lights and heat can react to who is home.

    Uses: Camera, Plug-in connectors. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO ESP32 board.

  • Classroom object sorter

    Train a model on three classroom objects with Edge Impulse. Students test how well the board tells them apart.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera.

  • Wildlife trail logger

    Detect animals passing a trail camera spot. Save the counts and times to a microSD card.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, microSD card, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Pet bowl watcher

    Spot your cat or dog at the food bowl. A host board logs each visit for a health check.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board.

  • Parking spot checker

    Point the camera at a driveway or bike rack. A colored light shows whether a space is free.

    Uses: Camera, Plug-in connectors. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO board, Grove LED.

Expert ideas

  • Robot that follows a person

    Feed the position of a detected person to a robot car. The car turns to keep the person in the middle of its view.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, small robot car kit, Arduino board.

  • Clap and look security sensor

    Listen for a loud sound with the onboard microphone. Then check the camera for a person before sending an alert.

    Uses: Hears sound, Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera, XIAO ESP32 board.

  • Custom model on the NPU

    Train your own small YOLO model and compile it for the built-in AI engine. Build new firmware with the Himax SDK.

    Uses: Camera. Add: Raspberry Pi camera.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Seeed publishes the full KiCad schematic and board files, but it names no hardware license. The Himax SDK and the Arduino library use the MIT license, and the SSCMA Micro code uses Apache 2.0. The image packing tool, the SenseCraft web service, and the Himax chip itself stay closed.
