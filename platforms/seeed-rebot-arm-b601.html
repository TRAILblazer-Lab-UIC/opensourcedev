Overview

The reBot Arm B601 is a desktop robot arm from Seeed Studio. It has six joints and a two-finger gripper. It comes in a DM version with Damiao motors and an RS version with RobStride motors. A PC or Jetson drives it for robot learning projects in LeRobot and ROS 2.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Robot learning research with LeRobot and imitation learning.
  • University and makerspace labs that need a capable open arm.
  • Practicing ROS 2, MoveIt2, and robot simulation.
  • Builders who want full CAD files and a screw-level parts list.

Not great for

  • Small budgets. The assembled arm costs over $1,500.
  • First robot projects. Setup needs Linux, CAN, and Python skill.
  • Standalone use. It has no onboard computer.
  • Unattended setups. The arm falls under gravity once torque is off.

Specs in plain words

  • Six joints let the arm reach and turn its gripper much like a human arm.
  • A reach of about 767 mm covers most of a desk.
  • The DM lifts 1.5 kg, about the weight of a large water bottle. The RS lifts 2.5 kg rated.
  • Repeatability of 0.2 mm on the DM means it returns to the same spot within the width of a few hairs.
  • The DM runs on 24 volts and the RS on 48 volts. Both need a big separate power supply.
  • The DM weighs about 4.5 kg and the RS about 6.7 kg.

Values are for the B601-DM unless noted. DM uses 4x Damiao DM-J4310-2EC and 3x DM-J4340P-2EC motors, weighs about 4.5 kg, and has 27 Nm on J1 to J3 and 7 Nm on J4 to J6 (CNX Software). RS uses 3x RobStride 06 and 4x RobStride 00, weighs about 6.7 kg and runs at 48 V (Seeed store page). CAN runs at 1 Mbps and UART at 921600 bps. The DM uses a Damiao serial bridge by default. By contrast, the RS needs native CAN (LeRobot docs). Structure is 5052 aluminium CNC parts plus ABS and PLA printed parts.

Builder ideas

  • Teach by demonstration

    Move a cheap leader arm by hand and watch the big arm copy you live. Record the motions as training data.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: leader arm.

  • Lab sample sorter

    Program the arm to move test tubes or vials between racks. A school science lab can practice automation safely.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: test tube rack.

  • Robot art painter

    Clamp a brush or marker in the gripper and draw shapes from a Python script. Turn simple sketches into wall art.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: marker or brush, paper, desk clamp.

  • Simulate before you buy

    Load the arm's open models into Isaac Sim or another simulator. Students can program a virtual arm with no hardware cost.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Garden seed planter

    Pick seeds from a tray and drop them into seed cells one at a time. A classroom can start a whole tray of seedlings.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: seed tray, small cup for seeds.

Expert ideas

  • Train a cup-stacking policy

    Record dozens of demos with LeRobot and train a model that stacks cups on its own. It is a hands-on taste of robot learning.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: leader arm, USB camera, plastic cups.

  • Chess playing helper

    Have the arm pick and place chess pieces from moves you type. Add a camera later to watch the board.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: chess set, USB camera.

  • Phone teleoperation

    Steer the arm by moving a phone or headset through a community teleop project. Try remote control from across the room.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: smartphone or VR headset.

  • MoveIt motion planning lab

    Plan smooth paths around obstacles with ROS 2 and MoveIt2. Watch the plan in a simulator before the arm moves.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Two-arm handoff

    Run two arms on separate CAN channels and pass objects between them. Learn how bimanual robots coordinate.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: second reBot arm, USB-CAN adapter.

  • Build the arm from parts

    Print the plastic parts and assemble the structure, motor, and gripper kits yourself. Learn every joint and screw.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: 3D printer, filament, hand tools.
