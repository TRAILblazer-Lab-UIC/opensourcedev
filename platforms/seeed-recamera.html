Overview

reCamera is a small AI camera from Seeed Studio that runs Linux inside a cube. It comes with ready AI models and a browser tool called Node-RED, where you connect blocks to build a camera project. Its core, sensor, and base boards swap out. You can change the lens or add power over Ethernet. It suits hobbyists, teachers, and smart home fans who want AI vision without writing much code.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Object detection and people counting right out of the box
  • Smart home cameras that talk over MQTT or RTSP
  • Learning AI vision with drag and drop blocks
  • Builders who like swappable, modular hardware

Not great for

  • Big custom models, as the NPU is modest at 1 TOPS
  • Wireless projects on the base 2002, as only the 2002w has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth
  • Tight budgets, as prices have climbed well above launch levels
  • Commercial products using YOLO11 without a separate license

Specs in plain words

  • A 1 TOPS AI engine runs small detection models smoothly on live video.
  • 256 MB of memory is enough for Linux, one model and a web dashboard at the same time.
  • 64 GB of built-in storage holds hours of saved clips or thousands of snapshots.
  • The cube is about 4 centimeters on each side, about the size of a big ice cube.
  • 100 Mbps Ethernet is plenty for one smooth video stream.
  • It runs on 5 V at 1 A over USB-C, about what a phone charger gives.

flash_kb is the 64 GB eMMC of the main reCamera 2002 64GB listing. An 8 GB eMMC version is also sold. Wi-Fi (2.4 and 5 GHz) and Bluetooth 4.2/5.0 exist only on 2002w core boards. Ethernet is 100 Mbps. CNX Software says the main core can be switched to an Arm Cortex-A53 mode. The 8051 core has 8 KB SRAM. The default sensor is an OV5647. Seeed lists it as 2MP 1080p in the spec table and 5MP elsewhere. Video encode is up to 5MP at 30 fps. The sensor board has four LEDs for a 0.3 W fill light. The base exposes a debug UART. The HQ PoE exposes three GPIO pins plus UART. The B401 gimbal base adds CAN and a gyroscope. sdio refers to extra SDIO lines the non-wireless C1 core passes to the base.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Spot objects in five minutes

    Plug in the camera and open the web dashboard. The preinstalled YOLO11 model labels people, cups, and chairs live.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • People counter for a club room

    Count how many people enter a room with a Node-RED flow. Show the total on a web page anyone can open.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

Builder ideas

  • Home security camera

    Stream video over RTSP to a home video recorder. Send an alert only when a person shows up.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wired network. Add: Ethernet cable.

  • Home Assistant eye

    Send detections over MQTT to Home Assistant. Lights can turn on as you walk into the kitchen.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Bird feeder visitor log

    Detect birds at the feeder and save a snapshot of each visit. Kids can check the photo log after school.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: weatherproof box, USB power supply.

  • Garden deer alarm

    Watch the garden at night for large animals. Trigger a light or a sound to send them on their way.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: weatherproof box, relay module, outdoor light.

  • Pose game for gym class

    Use the pose model to track arms and legs. Score students on how well they copy a dance move.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: TV or projector.

  • Posture reminder at your desk

    Use pose detection to notice slouching at your desk. A browser page nudges you to sit up straight.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip.

  • Parking lot space finder

    Mount the HQ PoE version over a small lot. A web page shows which spots are free.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wired network. Add: PoE network switch, Ethernet cable.

  • Sound and sight doorbell

    Listen with the built-in microphone for a knock. Then check the camera and send a phone alert with a snapshot.

    Uses: Hears sound, Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi.

  • Tracking camera for live streams

    Use the Gimbal version to follow a speaker across a stage. It keeps the person centered for a school stream.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: 12 V power supply.

  • Science fair animal behavior study

    Record how often a class pet visits its food or wheel. Export the counts as a chart for a science project.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • Robot vision over Bluetooth

    Send object positions from a 2002w camera to a small robot. The robot drives toward the target it sees.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Bluetooth. Add: small robot car kit with a Bluetooth board.

  • Litter spotting at the park

    Train a custom model to spot bottles and cans on a path. Map the hot spots for a community cleanup.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: battery pack.

  • Sorting line for recycling

    Detect paper, plastic, and metal on a small conveyor. Send a signal to a servo that pushes items into bins.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: small conveyor belt, servo motor, microcontroller board.

  • Your own C++ vision app

    Build a native app with the reCamera SDK and the RISC-V toolchain. Run your own converted model on the NPU.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux.

Seeed shares KiCad files for every base and sensor board under the Apache 2.0 license. The main core board has only a schematic PDF and no published layout. reCamera OS builds from source but has no license file, and the Sophgo chip itself is closed.
