Overview

The reComputer J4012 is a finished AI computer from Seeed Studio. It puts an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX 16GB module on Seeed's own carrier board inside a metal case with a fan. It ships with a 128 GB SSD and JetPack already installed. It suits builders who want Jetson power in a tidy box that is ready to mount.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • AI on live video from one or two cameras
  • Robots and smart cameras that need a sturdy case
  • Small local chat and voice models
  • Hobby add-ons that fit a Raspberry Pi style pin header

Not great for

  • Beginners and tight budgets
  • Wireless projects without an extra Wi-Fi card
  • The fastest Super power mode, which its cooling cannot handle
  • Battery gadgets or tiny sensors

Specs in plain words

  • 100 TOPS means it does 100 trillion small math steps each second for AI.
  • 16 GB of memory holds a small chat model or several vision models.
  • The 8-core 2 GHz processor runs Linux at desktop speed.
  • It draws 10 to 25 watts, about as much as a small desk lamp.
  • The 128 GB SSD holds the system plus many AI models and video clips.
  • The case is about 13 by 12 centimeters, close to a thick paperback book.

RadiosEthernet
ConnectorsUSB-A, USB-C, Pin headers, Camera connector, M.2 slot
Power9 to 19 V DC barrel jack (5.5 x 2.5 mm), 12 V 5 A adapter included, 10 to 25 W
Size130 x 120 mm

Size is with the aluminum case. Height is 58.5 mm. The J401 carrier PCB is 100 x 80 mm. Storage is the included 128 GB NVMe SSD, which the Seeed wiki flashes as the boot drive. As a result, flash_kb is null. Wi-Fi needs an optional M.2 Key E card. The CAN pins are TTL level and need an external transceiver. The 100 TOPS figure is the standard mode. Seeed says the J4012 does not support the 157 TOPS MAXN SUPER mode because the cooling is not enough.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

Seeed publishes the J401 carrier board schematic as a PDF, plus a 3D model and pinout datasheet. Layout files are not public and no hardware license is stated. The Orin NX module, CUDA, and TensorRT stay closed, though the Linux kernel is GPL-2.0 and Seeed's examples use the MIT license.
