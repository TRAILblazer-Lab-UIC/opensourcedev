Seeed reComputer R1000

Seeed Studio

A rugged Raspberry Pi gateway that talks to building and factory gear.

Overview

The reComputer R1000 is a fanless gateway built around the Raspberry Pi Compute Module 4. It clips onto a DIN rail or a wall. It has three isolated RS485 ports for building and factory equipment. It suits facility managers, automation students, and makers who want a tough Pi for long jobs.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Reading meters and sensors over Modbus and BACnet
  • Building automation and energy tracking
  • Running Node-RED, ThingsBoard, or Home Assistant all day
  • Places with odd power, as it takes 9 to 36 V DC or 12 to 24 V AC

Not great for

  • First electronics projects, as it has no simple pin header
  • Battery or pocket projects, as it needs wired power
  • Heavy AI on its own, as it needs an add-on AI kit for that
  • Tight budgets, as it costs about 210 to 390 US dollars

Specs in plain words

  • The Compute Module 4 runs at 1.5 GHz and feels like a Raspberry Pi 4.
  • 2 to 8 GB of memory runs a dashboard and a database at the same time.
  • 8 to 64 GB of built-in storage holds the system with no SD card to wiggle loose.
  • Three RS485 ports each talk to a chain of meters or sensors over long cables.
  • It uses about 3 to 6 watts, close to a small night light.
  • It works from minus 30 to 70 degrees Celsius, cold or hot enough for a garage or plant room.

RAM and eMMC vary by model. Values shown are for the R1025-10 (4 GB RAM, 32 GB eMMC), the first model in the series. The uart entry stands for the three isolated RS485 ports. No user GPIO header is listed. Wi-Fi 2.4 and 5 GHz comes from the CM4. BLE is mentioned in the product feature notes. LoRa (USB or SPI module), 4G LTE and Zigbee are optional add-on modules. As a result, they are not listed in radios. Seeed lists 2.88 W idle and 5.52 W full load, IP40, and -30 to 70 C operation. Onboard extras are six LEDs, a buzzer, a reset button, a hardware watchdog and an RTC. Dimensions were not listed on pages I read.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Energy meter dashboard

    Connect a power meter over RS485. The gateway reads its numbers and shows a live chart in a web page.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: Modbus power meter, twisted pair cable.

  • Node-RED building monitor

    Drag blocks in Node-RED to read room sensors. Send alerts by email once a room gets too warm.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: Modbus temperature sensors.

  • School solar panel tracker

    Read a solar inverter over Modbus. Students watch how much power the panels make each day.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi. Add: solar inverter with Modbus.

  • ThingsBoard sensor portal

    Run ThingsBoard on the gateway. Anyone on the network can view sensor graphs in a browser.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: Modbus sensors.

  • Machine status screen

    Plug a monitor into the HDMI port. Show the state of each pump or motor on one page.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux. Add: monitor.

  • Bluetooth tag finder

    Scan for Bluetooth tags in a room. The gateway logs which tools or carts are nearby.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Runs Linux. Add: Bluetooth tags.

  • Home Assistant hub on a rail

    Install Home Assistant on the gateway. Mount it in a power panel to run lights, sensors, and alarms.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi, Wired network.

Expert ideas

  • Greenhouse controller

    Read soil and air sensors on an RS485 line. Switch fans and vents on a schedule.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: RS485 soil sensors, RS485 relay module, fan.

  • Heating system log

    Listen to a boiler or heat pump over BACnet. Save its temperatures for a winter-long chart.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: BACnet heating controller.

  • Two-network bridge

    Use one Ethernet port for machines and one for the office network. The gateway passes only the data you choose.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: network cables.

  • Camera check on a factory line

    Add a USB camera and the Raspberry Pi AI Kit. A YOLO model flags missing parts on a conveyor.

    Uses: AI chip, Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux. Add: Raspberry Pi AI Kit, USB camera.

  • Farm water tank monitor

    Add the optional LoRa module and a level sensor at a far tank. The gateway logs the level from far away.

    Uses: Runs Linux. Add: LoRa add-on module, LoRa water level sensor.
