Seeed SenseCAP Solar Node P1

Seeed Studio

A solar powered outdoor Meshtastic relay that mounts on a pole or roof.

Overview

The SenseCAP Solar Node P1 is a weatherproof Meshtastic relay with a built-in solar panel. It comes with brackets for a wall or pole. A small LoRa radio and a low power controller sit inside with room for four 18650 batteries. It suits people who want to stretch a local mesh network across a neighborhood or farm.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Rooftop or mast relays for a Meshtastic mesh
  • Running for long stretches on sunlight
  • Outdoor use in rain and spray
  • A low cost finished outdoor case with brackets
  • Adding one plug-in sensor through the inside Grove port

Not great for

  • Handheld use, as it has no screen or keyboard
  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi
  • A box that works straight away, as the base P1 needs four batteries
  • Heavy computing, as it has a small, slow chip

Specs in plain words

  • The nRF52840 chip runs at 64 MHz and sips power. It suits a gadget that runs on sunlight.
  • 256 KB of working memory is plenty for relaying messages and reading a sensor.
  • The 5 watt solar panel charges four 18650 cells. Those cells look like fat AA batteries.
  • The LoRa radio covers 862 to 930 MHz. Set it to the licence-free band your country allows.
  • Seeed claims eight to nine kilometers of range in open country. That is about a two hour walk.
  • The IPX6 rating means it shrugs off heavy rain and spray. The case is about the size of a large dinner plate.

The XIAO nRF52840 Plus adds 2 MB of external QSPI flash to the 1 MB internal flash. GPS is fitted only on the P1-Pro (XIAO L76K, GPS, GLONASS, Galileo and BeiDou bands). The base P1 has a slot for it. radios lists gps for the Pro. Charging uses a CN3165 solar charger chip at up to 0.99 A. There are power, reset and user buttons and five status LEDs. The LoRa antenna is a 2 dBi rod on an RP-SMA cable. Seeed claims 8 to 9 km range in open areas. Sleep current and battery life were in a spreadsheet that was not read.

Starter ideas

  • Rooftop mesh relay

    Mount the node on your own roof or mast. It passes Meshtastic messages farther across your neighborhood.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: four 18650 batteries.

  • Hiking trail repeater

    Strap a node to a pole at a high spot on your own land. Hikers in the valley can reach each other.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: four 18650 batteries.

  • Farm field mesh link

    Put a node at the edge of a far field. Workers with handheld radios stay in touch across the farm.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: four 18650 batteries.

  • School mesh club project

    Students set up a node on the school roof with permission. They test how far messages travel across town.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth. Add: four 18650 batteries.

  • Phone app setup

    Pair the node with the Meshtastic phone app over Bluetooth. Pick its role and region from the app.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Mesh messaging.

  • Location beacon on the map

    Use the P1-Pro with its GPS module. The node shows its exact spot on everyone's mesh map.

    Uses: GPS, Mesh messaging.

Builder ideas

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Garden soil monitor

    Connect a soil moisture sensor through the Grove port. Get a mesh message once the garden needs water.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, LoRa long range radio. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Community emergency backbone

    Neighbors place nodes on their own roofs. The mesh keeps working during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: four 18650 batteries.

  • Wildlife area air sensor

    Mount a node near a pond or meadow you look after. It reports air pressure and temperature to a base station.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: air pressure sensor.

  • Try MeshCore firmware

    Flash MeshCore with the web flasher. Compare how it routes messages against Meshtastic on the same node.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

Expert ideas
