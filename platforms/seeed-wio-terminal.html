Overview

The Wio Terminal is a handheld development board from Seeed Studio. It packs a color screen, buttons, a joystick, and several sensors into a small case. It suits beginners who want visible results fast. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth come from a second chip on the board.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • First Arduino or CircuitPython projects with a screen built in
  • Sensor dashboards and small Wi-Fi gadgets
  • Simple handheld games
  • TinyML demos with motion or sound

Not great for

  • Wireless projects in CircuitPython or Zephyr, as Wi-Fi works only from Arduino
  • Battery projects out of the box, as there is no built-in battery
  • Big data or AI jobs, as memory is small
  • Buyers who want a long future, as some shops now list it as discontinued

Specs in plain words

  • The 120 MHz processor is quick enough for smooth screen updates and simple games.
  • The 192 KB of working memory holds a few screens of data at once.
  • The 2.4 inch screen is about the size of a large postage stamp, with 320 by 240 dots.
  • Dual-band Wi-Fi works on both 2.4 and 5 GHz home networks.
  • Two Grove ports let you snap on sensors with no soldering.
  • The case is about 72 by 57 mm, close to the size of a credit card.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorArm Cortex-M4F (ATSAMD51P19), 1 core at 120 MHz
Memory192 KB
Storage512 KB
Ways to connect partsI2C, SPI, Serial (UART), Digital audio (I2S), USB device, USB host, Screen bus
RadiosWi-Fi, Bluetooth Low Energy
ConnectorsUSB-C, Grove, Pin headers, microSD slot
Built inscreen, motion sensor, microphone, speaker, light sensor, buttons
Power5 V over USB-C
Size72 x 57 mm

The case is 72 x 57 x 12 mm. The 4 MB external SPI flash is not counted in flash_kb. Seeed says the clock can boost to 200 MHz. The microSD slot takes cards up to 16 GB. The speaker entry is a buzzer rated at 78 dB or more at 10 cm. The GPIO and ADC counts on the 40-pin header were not confirmed. The board also has a 20-pin FPC connector and a 940 nm IR emitter.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

    Press the buttons to play notes on the buzzer. Write a short tune and play it back.

    Uses: Makes sound.
