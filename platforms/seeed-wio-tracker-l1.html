Seeed Studio

Overview

The Wio Tracker L1 is a small radio board from Seeed Studio. It arrives with Meshtastic firmware already loaded. That lets you send texts and share your GPS spot over long-range LoRa radio with no cell network. It comes in four versions, from a bare board to a cased model with a battery. It suits campers, preppers, teachers and anyone curious about off-grid radio.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Texting friends far away with no cell service
  • Sharing GPS positions with a hiking or event group
  • Solar-powered relay nodes on a roof or fence post
  • Learning about radio range with zero code

Not great for

  • Projects that need Wi-Fi, as it has none
  • Fast data such as photos or voice
  • Rough weather on the Lite and L1, which ship with no case
  • Builders who need a known pin count, as Seeed has not published it

Specs in plain words

  • The LoRa radio sends small messages a long way on low power. It uses the license-free 868 or 915 MHz band for your region.
  • The GPS receiver works out where you stand anywhere under open sky.
  • 256 KB of working memory is plenty for a radio and a small screen. It cannot run big apps.
  • The 1.3 inch screen is about the size of a postage stamp. It shows messages and battery level.
  • Three power inputs let it run from USB-C, a solar panel or a lithium battery.
  • The Grove port lets you click in a sensor with no soldering.

Specs are for the L1 with OLED, from the Seeed product page and wiki. Zephyr's board page adds a 2 MB Puya P25Q16H QSPI NOR flash, an SH1106 OLED driver, a 5-way trackball, a user button, a user LED, a passive piezo buzzer and a CN3165 charger. The buzzer has no matching onboard vocabulary value. As a result, it is listed here only. The nRF52840 also has an 802.15.4 radio, but no L1 firmware uses Thread or Zigbee. As a result, it is left out of radios. The board size is not published. CNX Software gives 181 x 56 x 26 mm for the L1 Pro in its case. The 24-pin E-Ink connector is present on all versions, and the OLED uses a 30-pin FPC per the Meshtastic page. GPIO and ADC counts for the through-hole header were not stated.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Off-grid text messenger

    Pair the tracker with your phone over Bluetooth. Send short texts to friends miles away with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth.

  • Hiking group locator

    Each hiker carries a tracker. The app shows everyone's GPS spot on a map.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS. Add: small lithium battery.

  • Standalone message reader

    Read new messages on the built-in screen. Scroll with the little trackball and leave the phone at home.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen.

  • Neighborhood emergency network

    Give trackers to neighbors on your street. The group can still check in during a power or phone outage.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: small lithium battery.

  • Low-power e-paper badge

    Choose the E-Ink version for a screen that stays readable in sunlight. It sips power and runs for days.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Solar rooftop repeater

    Mount a tracker high up with a small solar panel. It passes messages along and stretches the range of your local mesh.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: small solar panel, lithium battery, weatherproof box.

  • Bike ride tracker

    Clip a tracker in a case to your bike. Friends at home can watch your route on the map.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Battery ready. Add: lithium battery, small case.

  • Garden weather station

    Plug a weather sensor into the Grove port. Meshtastic sends temperature and humidity readings to your phone across the yard.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Classroom radio science lab

    Students place trackers around the school field. They map how far signals travel past trees and walls.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Screen.

  • Lost pet collar finder

    Fit a small tracker on a large dog's harness. Its GPS spot shows up on your phone during a walk in the woods.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, GPS, Battery ready. Add: small lithium battery, small case.

  • Community event check-in

    Volunteers at a fair or race carry trackers. Organizers see who is where on one map.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, GPS.

  • Try MeshCore firmware

    Drag MeshCore firmware onto the board in place of Meshtastic. Compare how the two mesh systems route messages.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

Expert ideas

  • Pocket keyboard messenger

    Add a tiny keyboard and a solar panel in a printed case. You get a pocket messenger that needs no phone.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Screen, Plug-in connectors. Add: small keyboard, small solar panel, 3D printed case.

  • Reticulum radio node

    Load a community RNode port. The board becomes a radio link for the Reticulum network on a laptop.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio.

  • Custom sensor firmware

    Write your own program with Arduino or Zephyr. Read a Grove sensor and beep the buzzer at a set level.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Sensors built in. Add: Grove sensor.

How open is it?

Open Board

Seeed publishes a schematic PDF and 3D case files for the Pro. Board layout files and a hardware license are missing. The Meshtastic firmware is open under GPL-3.0, and Zephyr support is open under Apache-2.0. The Nordic chip and its usual Bluetooth driver stay closed.

Hardware license: none stated · Software license: Meshtastic firmware GPL-3.0, Zephyr Apache-2.0, Seeed wiki docs GPL-3.0
