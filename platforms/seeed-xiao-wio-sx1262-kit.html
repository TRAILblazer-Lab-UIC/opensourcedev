Seeed XIAO Wio-SX1262 Kit

Seeed Studio

A thumb-sized LoRa radio kit that runs Meshtastic out of the box.

Field marks: A LoRa node about 21 by 18 millimeters, shipped with Meshtastic preinstalled for under fifteen dollars, with an antenna wire several times longer than the board.

Overview

This kit stacks a tiny Seeed XIAO board on top of a LoRa radio board. It arrives with Meshtastic firmware ready to go. You get long-range text messaging for about the price of a pizza. One version uses an ESP32S3 with Wi-Fi, and the other uses a low-power nRF52840.

Specs in plain words

  • The LoRa radio sends small messages for miles on very little power. It uses the license-free 868 or 915 MHz band for your region.
  • The whole stack is about 21 by 18 mm, close to the size of a postage stamp.
  • The ESP32S3 version has 8 MB of extra memory. That gives room for Wi-Fi features and a web page.
  • The nRF52840 version sleeps on under 5 millionths of an amp. A small battery can last a long time.
  • Built-in charging lets a lithium battery refill from the USB-C port.

Power5 V over USB-C, or a 4.2 V lithium battery on the BAT pads with 100 mA charging
Size21 x 17.8 mm

Values are for the ESP32-S3 kit (SKU 102010611), the first and cheapest version. The 8 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM come from the Meshtastic page. The ESP32-S3R8 die holds the 8 MB PSRAM. The nRF52840 kit (SKU 102010710) has a 64 MHz Cortex-M4F, 1 MB flash, 256 KB RAM, Bluetooth 5, NFC, 11 GPIO, 6 ADC, SWD and standby under 5 uA. The Wio-SX1262 module datasheet gives 22 dBm output, about -137 dBm sensitivity at SF12 125 kHz, 1.62 uA sleep and 125 mA peak transmit. The 21 x 17.8 mm figure is the board footprint. The nRF52840 store page also lists 8 x 22 x 23 mm, which seems to include the stack height. The Wio-SX1262 add-on adds one user button. The XIAO adds reset and boot buttons, a user LED and a charge LED. The two add-on boards have different pin maps and are not interchangeable.

Things you could build

Starter ideas

  • Pocket mesh messenger

    Pair the kit with your phone over Bluetooth. Text friends in your mesh with no cell signal.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Bluetooth.

  • Window relay node

    Power a kit from a USB charger on a high windowsill. It passes along messages for radios around your area.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: USB charger, better LoRa antenna.

  • Battery mesh node in a tin

    Tuck the kit and a small battery into a mint tin. Carry a tiny mesh radio on a trip.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: small lithium battery, small tin case.

  • Classroom range test

    Students carry kits around the school grounds. They log how far a message travels past hills and buildings.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

Builder ideas

  • Solar fence post repeater

    Pick the nRF52840 kit for its very low power use. A small solar panel keeps it relaying messages all year.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Battery ready. Add: small solar panel, lithium battery, weatherproof box.

  • Garden soil sensor

    Wire a soil moisture sensor to the pins. The kit radios readings back to the house from far down the garden.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Mailbox alert

    Add a door switch to the mailbox. The kit sends a radio ping to your phone app each time the lid opens.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: magnetic door switch, small lithium battery.

  • Wi-Fi bridge for the mesh

    Use the ESP32S3 kit on home Wi-Fi. Mesh messages show up in a web browser on any computer in the house.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging, Wi-Fi.

  • Hiking tracker add-on

    Stack a small GPS add-on board under the kit. Your group can see each other on a map in the app.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging. Add: GPS add-on board.

  • Try MeshCore firmware

    Flash MeshCore onto a spare kit. Compare its message routing with Meshtastic on your own radios.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Mesh messaging.

Expert ideas

  • Farm water tank monitor

    Put a level sensor in a water tank. The kit reports the level over a LoRaWAN network you set up.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Battery ready. Add: water level sensor, small lithium battery.

  • Single-channel home gateway

    Run gateway firmware on the ESP32S3 kit. It collects your own LoRaWAN sensor readings and passes them to Wi-Fi.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Wi-Fi.

  • Bird box sound alert

    Add a tiny microphone to the ESP32S3 kit. It sends a short radio note to your phone each time chicks start to chirp.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Hears sound. Add: small digital microphone.

  • Mesh doorbell chime

    A button on one kit sends a radio ping. A second kit plays a chime on a small speaker.

    Uses: LoRa long range radio, Makes sound. Add: push button, small speaker.
