Overview

The LR2021 is a radio chip from Semtech and the first in its LoRa Plus line. A small computer board drives it and tells it what to send. It can talk over very long distances on little power. It suits radio tinkerers, mesh builders, and people making remote sensors.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Meshtastic and MeshCore mesh radios
  • Remote sensors that run for months on batteries
  • Trying several radio types with one chip
  • Fast short-range links with FLRC mode

Not great for

  • Beginners, as the chip needs a host board and some code
  • Location tracking alone, as it has no GPS
  • Full Bluetooth apps, as it only speaks the basic BLE radio layer
  • Plug-and-play use, as cheap modules need soldering

Specs in plain words

  • Sub-GHz coverage from 150 to 960 MHz means it works with LoRa bands around the world.
  • A sensitivity of -141.5 dBm lets it hear very faint signals, often miles away.
  • Output up to +22 dBm is about the strongest that most hobby LoRa radios send.
  • Sleep current of about 470 nA lets a small battery last a very long time.
  • FLRC at up to 2.6 Mbps is fast enough to send a small photo in a few seconds.
  • The chip is 5 by 5 mm, about the size of a pencil eraser end.

Specs are for the bare chip. CPU, memory and connectors come from the host board. The ble radio entry means the chip has a BLE PHY. It is not a full Bluetooth controller. The 2.4 GHz ISM band and S-band satellite mode have no vocab entry. TX current is about 105 mA at +22 dBm. Sleep is 470 nA without RTC and 1.28 uA with RTC per Seeed and the datasheet v2.2 (search snippet). Elecrow lists 0.54 uA for its module. Semtech lists 490 MHz, 868 MHz and 915 MHz regional kit variants, each paired with 2.4 GHz. Package is 5 x 5 mm QFN-48.

  • Multi-band range test

    Uses: LoRa long range radio. Add: two host boards, antennas, GPS module.
