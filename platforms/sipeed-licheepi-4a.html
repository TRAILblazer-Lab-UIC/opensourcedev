Sipeed LicheePi 4A

Sipeed

A RISC-V Linux computer with an AI helper chip for curious tinkerers.

Field marks: The LicheePi 4A plugs a RISC-V module into a carrier with two Ethernet jacks, four USB 3.0 ports, and three camera ports, then waits for your software to get ported.

Overview

The LicheePi 4A is a small Linux computer built on a RISC-V chip. A removable module holds the processor, memory, and storage. It suits developers and curious hobbyists who want to explore RISC-V. Its 4 TOPS AI chip and three camera ports make it stand out.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how Linux runs on RISC-V
  • Testing and porting open-source software
  • Camera projects with a built-in AI helper
  • Wired network projects with two Gigabit ports

Not great for

  • First-time computer users, as setup has several traps
  • Wireless projects, as the spec list shows no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth
  • Speed fans, as it trails the Raspberry Pi 5
  • Fully open hardware, as only the carrier schematic is shared

Specs in plain words

  • Four cores at up to 1.85 GHz give roughly Raspberry Pi 4 speed.
  • Up to 16 GB of memory runs a desktop with many browser tabs open.
  • Up to 128 GB of built-in storage holds the system and lots of files.
  • A 4 TOPS AI chip can spot objects in a camera feed without help from the cloud.
  • Three camera ports and one display port let it see and show more than most small boards.
  • Onboard microphones can record sound with no extra parts.

RAM and eMMC vary by version: 8 GB with 8 or 32 GB eMMC, or 16 GB with 128 GB eMMC. Values shown are the 16 GB and 128 GB top version, the one most often sold. The Sipeed wiki lists 1.85 GHz while CNX Software listed 2.0 GHz at announcement. The wiki value is used. The 20-pin header carries UART, I2C and SPI. The number of usable GPIO was not stated on a page I read. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth were mentioned in early CNX coverage, but the Sipeed spec list I read does not include them. As a result, radios lists only Ethernet. The dimension drawing text shows figures of 99.06, 84.45, 75.56 and 52.07 mm, but the overall board size could not be confirmed. As a result, size_mm is unknown.

Expert ideas

  • Home router experiment

    Load OpenWrt and use the two network ports as a small router. Learn how home networks move traffic.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: network cables.

  • Object spotting camera

    Run a small AI model on the NPU to name objects in live video. It can count cars or spot a pet at the door.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux. Add: camera module, cooling fan.

  • Garden pest watcher

    Point a camera at the vegetable bed and let the AI chip flag rabbits or deer. Check the alerts each morning.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Runs Linux, Wired network. Add: camera module, weatherproof case, long network cable.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Sensors built in, Runs Linux. Add: relay module.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Linux kernel and U-Boot are open source under GPL-2.0. Sipeed shares the carrier board schematic, placement maps, and a 3D model, but states no hardware license. The TH1520 chip, its GPU and AI stack rely on closed vendor parts, although the C910 core design is public under Apache-2.0.
