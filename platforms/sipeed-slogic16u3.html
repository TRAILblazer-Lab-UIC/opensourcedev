Overview

The SLogic16U3 is a logic analyzer from Sipeed. It records the on and off signals that chips use to talk, then shows them on your computer. It suits makers, students, and repair fans who need to see why a circuit misbehaves. It is one of the cheapest USB 3 analyzers at its speed.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Finding bugs in I2C, SPI, and serial wiring
  • Learning how digital signals work
  • Decoding more than 150 protocols with sigrok decoders
  • Testing boards that run up to 10 volts

Not great for

  • Measuring analog waves, as it only sees high and low
  • Full speed on Windows, as the driver halves the rate there
  • Use through a USB hub, as it needs a direct USB 3 port
  • Stock PulseView, as you need Sipeed's build for now

Specs in plain words

  • Sixteen channels let you watch sixteen wires at the same time.
  • 200 million samples a second on all channels catches signals far faster than most hobby chips send.
  • Inputs up to 10 volts work with 3.3 volt, 5 volt, and many older boards.
  • The 0 to 6 volt threshold sets where the tool decides a signal is high.
  • A 3.3 volt output on the header can power a small test circuit.
  • The case is 40 by 40 by 10 mm, about the size of a matchbox.

Sipeed does not name the chips. A Sipeed staff comment on CNX Software says a single Gowin 15K FPGA with high-speed SerDes implements USB 3 directly. The exact part is unconfirmed. The 16 GPIO entry counts the input channels. The rear 2x12 header also has 4 ground pins, 2 VCC pins and 2 reserved CK pins that are not implemented yet. The Windows build is throttled to 400 MS/s on 4 channels because of USB driver limits. Linux reaches the full rate. The button is a hidden MODE switch pressed with a SIM pin to enter DFU mode.
