Overview

The Tang Nano 20K is a small FPGA board from Sipeed built on a Gowin chip. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. It packs an HDMI port, a microSD slot, a speaker amplifier, and a screen connector onto a stick about the size of a pack of gum. It suits hobbyists who want retro game consoles or tiny RISC-V computers for very little money.

Specs in plain words

  • 20,736 logic cells is room for a small game console or a RISC-V processor.
  • 8 MB of memory inside the chip package holds game data or a small Linux.
  • 8 MB of flash keeps your design stored after the power goes off.
  • The HDMI port plugs into a TV or a computer monitor.
  • A built-in amplifier drives a small speaker with no extra board.
  • One USB-C cable handles power, uploads, and a serial link to your computer.

Specs from the Sipeed wiki and CNX Software. FPGA resources: 20,736 LUT4, 15,552 flip-flops, 41,472 bits shadow SRAM, 828 Kbit block SRAM in 46 blocks, 48 18x18 multipliers, 2 PLLs, 8 I/O banks. ram_kb is the 64 Mbit SDR SDRAM embedded in the GW2AR package. flash_kb is the 64 Mbit SPI flash that holds the bitstream. clock_mhz is the 27 MHz crystal. The MS5351 generator adds three more programmable clocks. GPIO count is unclear: LinuxGizmos quotes up to 32 and 34 in different places, and the wiki gives only a pin diagram. The BL616 is a 32-bit RISC-V MCU at up to 320 MHz with 480 KB SRAM, used only for debugging and clock control by default. The audio amplifier drives a 2-pin speaker header. No speaker is included. The display connector is a 40-pin RGB LCD FPC.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink the color LED

    Write a small Verilog design that cycles the RGB LED through colors. It is the classic first FPGA step.

  • Button and LED logic lab

    Use the two buttons as inputs for logic gates you design. Watch the six LEDs show each result.

  • Color bars over HDMI

    Try the Sipeed HDMI example to draw color bars on a monitor. It shows how video signals work.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Hello over the serial port

    Build a serial sender and print a message in a terminal on your computer. The USB-C cable carries the text.

Builder ideas

  • Play retro games on a TV

    Load the NESTang core and turn the board into a retro game console. Plug it into a TV over HDMI.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, TV or monitor, game controllers, microSD card.

  • Chiptune music box

    Make retro game sounds in hardware and play them through a small speaker. Each button can start a new tune.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Build a RISC-V computer

    Load the PicoRV32 soft core example and run small C programs on it. You assemble the processor yourself.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a strip of color LEDs with patterns you design in logic. Hang it over a desk or a shelf.

    Add: LED strip, 5 V power supply, jumper wires.

  • Science class tone generator

    Play exact musical pitches through the speaker header. Students can hear how frequency changes the note.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Servo sweep controller

    Make a precise pulse signal that swings a servo back and forth. Use it to wave a flag or point a little sign.

    Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

  • Try the fully open toolchain

    Build a blinky design with Yosys, nextpnr, and Apicula. Load it with openFPGALoader and skip the vendor tools.

Expert ideas

  • Pong on the big screen

    Build Pong fully in logic and show it over HDMI. Play it with the board buttons.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Pocket picture frame

    Show pictures from the microSD card on a small color LCD panel. Press a button to flip to the next image.

    Uses: Screen. Add: 40-pin RGB LCD panel, microSD card.

  • Boot Linux on a soft CPU

    Follow the LiteX example to build a RISC-V computer in the chip. Boot a small Linux and log in over serial.
