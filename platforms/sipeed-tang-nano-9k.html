Overview

The Tang Nano 9K is a small and cheap board built around a Gowin FPGA. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. The board adds an HDMI port, screen connectors, and a built-in USB programmer. It suits students and hobbyists who want a first FPGA with open design tools.

Specs in plain words

  • 8,640 logic units is room for a small processor plus a video circuit.
  • 8 MB of fast memory inside the chip package can hold a full screen picture or a soft processor's program.
  • 4 MB of flash on the board stores your design and extra data.
  • The 27 MHz clock ticks 27 million times each second, and two built-in timers can speed it up.
  • The board is about 65 by 23 millimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.

Clock is the onboard 27 MHz crystal. The FPGA has 2 PLLs. ram_kb is the 468 Kbit of block SRAM (26 blocks). psram_kb is the 64 Mbit in-package PSRAM. flash_kb is the 32 Mbit SPI flash. The FPGA also has 608 Kbit of user flash and 17,280 bits of shadow SRAM. The board breaks out I/O on two 24-pin 2.54 mm header rows. The usable user I/O count was not stated on the wiki. The FPGA has no ADC. Six user LEDs are on board. Size is the approximate figure from CNX Software. The official dimensional drawing was not read.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink your first LED

    Write a tiny Verilog design that blinks one of the six LEDs. It shows how hardware design differs from normal code.

  • Binary counter on six LEDs

    Count upward in binary across the row of LEDs. Students can watch each bit flip at its own speed.

  • Button logic gate lab

    Link the two buttons to the LEDs through gates you design. Build AND, OR and XOR by hand and test them.

Builder ideas

  • Hello over the serial port

    Build a simple serial sender in Verilog. Your message appears in a terminal on your computer through the USB-C cable.

  • Color bars on a TV

    Draw a test pattern on a monitor through the HDMI port. It teaches how a screen is drawn line by line.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor or TV.

  • Tiny LCD picture frame

    Show a picture or a moving pattern on a small screen plugged into the LCD connector. Keep it on a desk as a little art piece.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small LCD screen.

  • Reaction time game

    Light a random LED and time how fast a player presses a button. Show the best score as a pattern on the LEDs.

  • Tone and melody maker

    Make square wave tones on a pin and play a short tune. Each button can trigger a different note.

    Add: small speaker, small amplifier board.

  • Servo sweep controller

    Make a precise pulse signal that swings a servo back and forth. Use it to wave a flag or point a little sign.

    Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Pong on the big screen

    Build the classic Pong game fully in logic and show it over HDMI. Play it with the two board buttons.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor or TV.

  • Build a RISC-V computer

    Load the PicoRV32 soft core from the official examples. Then run small C programs on a processor you built from logic.

  • Retro game console screen

    Build a small tile and sprite engine that draws a retro game over HDMI. It is a big but rewarding project for skilled builders.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor or TV, push buttons.
