SO-101 Robot Arm

The Robot Studio and Hugging Face

A low-cost 3D-printed robot arm pair for teaching robots by example.

Field marks: Two 3D-printed arms with six servos each, one moved by hand and one copying it, for a parts bill of about $230 plus printing.

Overview

The SO-101 is a six-joint robot arm made from 3D-printed parts and small smart servos. It was designed by The Robot Studio with Hugging Face for the LeRobot library. Most people build a pair. You move the leader arm by hand and the follower copies it. The recorded moves become lessons that teach the arm new tasks.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how robots learn from human demos
  • Makers who own a 3D printer and enjoy a build
  • University and club projects in robot AI
  • Teleoperation demos where one arm copies another

Not great for

  • People who want a robot that works out of the box
  • Heavy lifting or precise factory work
  • Running AI on the arm itself. A separate computer does all the thinking.
  • Shoppers in a hurry, as US kits often sell out

Specs in plain words

  • Six degrees of freedom means six joints, counting the gripper. The arm can bend, twist, and grab much like a human arm.
  • Each arm uses six STS3215 smart servos. These motors report their own position, which lets the computer track every joint.
  • The follower servos give about 16.5 kilogram centimeters of force. That is enough to lift a sock or a small block.
  • The arm has no brain of its own. A laptop, desktop, or Jetson runs the LeRobot software over a USB cable.
  • Each arm needs a 5 volt 4 amp power adapter. The Pro follower with 12 volt servos needs a 12 volt supply.
  • Builder parts cost about 230 dollars for a pair before printing. Kits start around 199 dollars.

The arm has no processor. Policies run on a laptop, desktop GPU or a Jetson. The follower uses six STS3215 7.4 V servos at 1/345 gearing (16.5 kg.cm at 6 V). The Pro follower uses 12 V servos rated 30 kg.cm. The leader uses one 1/345, two 1/191 and three 1/147 servos so it is easy to move by hand. The bus servo adapter links USB-C to the half-duplex serial servo bus. Arm dimensions were not found on a fetched page.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Copycat arm show

    Move the leader arm by hand and watch the follower copy you. It makes a great first demo for friends.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Robot drawing artist

    Clamp a marker in the gripper and guide the leader arm across paper. The follower draws a copy of your doodle.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: marker, paper.

  • Remote helping hand

    Use the leader and follower pair to pick up objects at arm's length. It can reach items on a cluttered desk.

    Uses: Drives motors.

Builder ideas

  • Sock sorting helper

    Record a few demos of picking up socks and dropping them in a basket. Train the arm to tidy socks on its own.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera, small basket.

  • Classroom robot learning lab

    Students take turns recording demos with the leader arm. The class trains a policy and tests how well it learned.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera.

  • Block stacking challenge

    Teach the arm to stack wooden blocks into a tower. Count how many blocks it stacks before the tower falls.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera, wooden blocks.

  • Seedling pot mover

    Train the arm to lift small seedling pots onto a sunny tray. It helps with a tabletop garden.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera, small plant pots.

  • Chess piece mover

    Record demos of moving chess pieces between squares. Play a slow game against a friend with the arm as your hand.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: chess set.

  • Wrist camera point of view

    Mount a small camera on the wrist and record what the arm sees. The video makes training data for smarter policies.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small USB camera, printed camera mount.

Expert ideas

  • Talking arm with a vision-language model

    Train a vision-language-action policy on your demos. Type a short instruction and the arm tries the task.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera, computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Science sample tray loader

    Teach the arm to move small cups between trays. A school science club can study how many demos it needs.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: USB camera, small plastic cups.

  • Two-armed mobile helper

    Mount two arms on a small rolling base to build the XLeRobot design. It can drive around and grab things.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: second follower arm, mobile robot base, battery pack.
