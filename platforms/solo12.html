Overview

Solo12 is an open four-legged robot built for research labs. It weighs about 2.5 kg and has twelve joints that can sense and control force. Every design file, circuit board, and line of control code is published. Sales have ended. You build it yourself from the files.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • University labs studying walking, jumping, and balance
  • Researchers testing force control and learning methods on real legs
  • Teams that want to change any part of the robot down to the circuit boards
  • Students who want to study a complete open robot design

Not great for

  • Beginners or anyone new to electronics
  • Small budgets, with parts estimated above 7,000 US dollars
  • Quick projects, as the build takes many weeks of skilled work
  • Buyers who want a ready robot, as no store sells one now

Specs in plain words

  • Twelve force-controlled joints let each leg feel the ground and push softly.
  • A 9 to 1 belt drive keeps the legs light and springy to the touch.
  • The body is about 45 by 30 cm and the legs fold for carrying.
  • An ESP32 board talks to the PC a thousand times each second over Ethernet or Wi-Fi.
  • A real-time Linux PC does the thinking and sends commands to the legs.
  • The original robot runs on a 24 volt supply through a tether wire.

The ESP32 core count and clock are the standard ESP32 values. The repo does not list the module variant. The master board has 4 free GPIO, two used for the IMU UART, and up to 8 SPI slaves. The IMU is a Lord Microstrain 3DM-CX5-25 set to +/-900 deg/s and +/-20 g. Each actuator uses a T-Motor Antigravity 4004 300 kV motor, a Broadcom AEDT-981x optical encoder and two 3:1 belt stages. The original v1 robot is tethered for power and Ethernet. A v1.1 autonomy upgrade with onboard batteries and wireless control is documented in the repo. Weight is 2.5 kg per the repo. The 2019 paper cites 2.2 kg for the 8-DoF Solo.

Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Practice in simulation first

    Load the open robot model into a physics simulator and tune a gait there. You protect the real hardware from early mistakes.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Build one actuator module

    Assemble a single leg joint from the open files and drive it from a PC. You learn torque control on one motor before building twelve.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: brushless motor, optical encoder, timing belts, 3D printed parts.

Expert ideas

  • Teach a robot to walk

    Write a simple trotting gait in Python and test it on the real robot. You see how leg timing turns into steady walking.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: real-time Linux PC.

  • Self-righting after a fall

    Program the robot to roll back onto its feet after a tumble. The fully rotating knees and hips make this move possible.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Jumping experiments

    Plan a crouch and a push-off to make the robot hop. You measure jump height and landing forces from the joint data.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Balance on a tilting board

    Use the motion sensor to keep the body level on a moving platform. The legs adjust their push many times each second.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: tilting platform.

  • Gentle push recovery

    Give the robot a light shove and let it catch itself. Soft force control lets the legs absorb the bump.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Learning to walk by trial

    Train a walking policy with reinforcement learning in simulation. Then test the result on the real robot.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Cut the tether

    Follow the documented upgrade to add onboard batteries and wireless control. The robot can then roam a lab floor freely.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors. Add: robot batteries, wireless parts from the upgrade guide.

  • University robotics lab demo

    Run a live walking demo for visitors with joint data on a big screen. Students see real control research in action.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: large monitor.
