Overview

The SparkFun Inventor's Kit is a boxed Arduino starter kit for classrooms and curious beginners. It centers on the RedBoard Qwiic, which works like an Arduino Uno. The kit adds a breadboard, LEDs, sensors, a text screen, a servo, motors, and a printed guidebook. Learners wire 16 circuits by hand and finish by building a rover.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning real wiring on a breadboard
  • Middle school, high school, and adult beginners
  • Classrooms that want a printed step-by-step guide
  • A first taste of robots with motors and a distance sensor

Not great for

  • Children under ten, as the wiring is fiddly
  • Wi-Fi or Bluetooth projects, as it has no radio
  • Tight budgets, as the full kit costs about 100 USD
  • Large programs, as memory is very small

Specs in plain words

  • The 16 MHz chip handles one simple job at a time, like reading a sensor and moving a servo.
  • 2 KB of working memory holds a few dozen sensor readings at once.
  • 32 KB of storage fits every program in the guide with room to spare.
  • 20 input and output pins give room for many LEDs, buttons and sensors.
  • The 16x2 screen shows two short lines of text, about the length of a text message preview.
  • The Qwiic port takes plug-in sensor boards with no soldering.

Board values are for the RedBoard Qwiic (DEV-15123) from its SparkFun product page. SRAM is the ATmega328P chip value from Wikipedia. gpio is the 20 digital I/O pins (6 PWM). Adc_channels is the 6 analog inputs. The board has a CH340C USB-serial bridge and an AP2112 3.3 V regulator rated to 600 mA. The onboard list covers loose kit parts: the 16x2 LCD, the TB6612FNG motor driver board, tactile buttons, a photocell and a TMP36 temperature sensor. Other kit parts outside the vocab are an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, a servo, two gearmotors, a piezo speaker, LEDs, a tricolor LED, a trimpot and an AA battery holder. Board size was not stated on the product page.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blinking LED first circuit

    Wire one LED on the breadboard and make it blink. It is the first step in the printed guide.

  • Light-sensing night light

    The light sensor notices the room getting dark. An LED switches on by itself.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Color mood lamp

    Turn the knob to mix colors on the tricolor LED. Put a paper cup on top for a soft glow.

    Add: paper cup.

  • Button piano

    Wire the buttons to play notes on the piezo speaker. Compose a short tune for the class.

  • Room thermometer screen

    Read the temperature sensor and show the result on the text screen. Compare the window with the middle of the room.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Distance alarm

    The ultrasonic sensor measures how close someone stands. The speaker beeps faster as they get closer.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Servo gauge dial

    The servo swings a paper arrow to show the temperature. It works like an old car dial.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: cardboard.

  • Quiz buzzer game

    Each player gets a button. The first press lights that player's LED and shows the name on the screen.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Remote-controlled rover

    Build the kit's rover from two gearmotors and the motor driver. Steer it with commands typed on a computer.

    Uses: Drives motors.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Add a soil moisture sensor to the breadboard. The text screen shows a warning once the soil gets dry.

    Uses: Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor.

  • Science fair data logger

    Record light and temperature every minute. Send the numbers to a computer and graph a full day.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Plug-in sensor upgrade

    Connect a plug-in sensor board to the Qwiic port. Show air quality or humidity readings on the screen.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Screen. Add: plug-in air quality sensor, connector cable.

  • Mini drawbridge model

    Build a cardboard bridge and lift it with the servo. A button raises it and a light warns the cars.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: cardboard, glue.

Expert ideas

  • Self-driving rover

    Add the ultrasonic sensor to the rover. It drives around and turns away from walls on its own.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The RedBoard Qwiic is certified open hardware with the OSHWA number US000802. SparkFun shares its design files under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license. The kit sketches use the same license, and the Arduino tools are open. The main chip and the stock parts stay closed.

Hardware license: CC-BY-SA-4.0 (RedBoard repo LICENSE.md), OSHWA list shows CERN hardware, MIT software and CC BY-SA documentation licenses · Software license: CC-BY-SA-4.0 (SIK-Guide-Code sketches)

Key terms on this page

Breadboard
A breadboard is a plastic block full of small holes linked in rows.
CC BY-SA
CC BY-SA is a Creative Commons license that asks for credit and sharing alike.
Driver
A driver is software that lets a computer or chip use a piece of hardware.
LED
An LED is a small light that turns on with very little power.
Open hardware
Open hardware is a physical device whose design files are public.
OSHWA certification
OSHWA certification is a free check run by the Open Source Hardware Association.
Qwiic
Qwiic is a small four-pin plug system from SparkFun.
Rover
A rover is a robot that drives around on wheels or tracks.
Servo motor
A servo motor turns to an exact angle and holds it.
Sketch
A sketch is the Arduino name for a program.
Soldering
Soldering joins metal parts with a melted metal called solder.
USB-to-serial chip
A USB-to-serial chip lets a computer talk to a board's UART over USB.
