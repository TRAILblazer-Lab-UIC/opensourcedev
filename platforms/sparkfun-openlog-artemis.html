Overview

The OpenLog Artemis is a data logger that works out of the box. It saves readings from sensors to a microSD card as simple spreadsheet files. You set it up from a text menu with no programming. Teachers, scientists, and field workers use it to collect data for days or months.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Collecting sensor data with no code
  • Long outdoor studies on a battery
  • Classroom science experiments
  • Recording motion with the built-in motion sensor
  • Saving serial output from another gadget

Not great for

  • Sending data live over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, as the firmware uses no radio
  • Reading analog signals above 2 volts
  • Sensors outside SparkFun's supported list, without custom firmware

Specs in plain words

  • The microSD slot takes cards up to 32 GB, enough for years of slow readings.
  • The 9-axis motion sensor tracks tilt, turning, and direction up to about 250 times each second.
  • The clock and its backup battery stamp each reading with the real time.
  • Deep sleep draws about 18 microamps, which lets a small battery last a long time.
  • The Qwiic connector plugs in sensors with a single cable and no soldering.
  • Logging speed ranges from 500 readings each second to one reading each day.

CPU, clock, RAM and flash are Apollo3 / Artemis module values from the apollo3 card (96 MHz with TurboSPOT burst, 384 KB SRAM, 1 MB flash). The OpenLog Artemis page itself lists the board-level items. The four analog inputs are 14-bit with a 2 V maximum, and ADC channels sample up to 1900 Hz. Power draw is about 20 mA running, 80 uA in sleep and 18 uA in deep sleep, per the SparkFun product page. microSD cards up to 32 GB are supported. The page mentions FAT32 and FAT16 in one place and exFAT in another. The README says exFAT works from firmware v1.11. USB serial goes through a CH340E bridge. The Apollo3 has a BLE radio, but the logger firmware does not use it. As a result, radios is empty. The board has a 2x5 SWD header. Dimensions are only shown in a drawing. As a result, size is unknown.

Starter ideas

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: temperature sensor with Qwiic cable, microSD card.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: small LiPo battery, microSD card, case.

  • Science class experiment logger

    Log temperature, light, or pressure during a lab with no code at all. Students graph real data from the microSD card.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors. Add: Qwiic sensors, microSD card.

    Add a Qwiic GPS module and record your path on a hike. Load the file into a map tool at home.

    Uses: Plug-in connectors, Battery ready. Add: GPS module with Qwiic cable, LiPo battery, microSD card.

    Wear the board on a wrist and record motion during a routine. Compare two dancers by graphing their moves.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: wrist strap, small LiPo battery.

    Log carbon dioxide levels through a school day. Students see how fresh air changes with open windows.
