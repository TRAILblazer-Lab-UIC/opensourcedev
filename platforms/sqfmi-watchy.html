Overview

Watchy is an open-source smartwatch kit from SQFMI. It has a paper-like e-paper screen, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a step counter. You program it in the Arduino IDE with the Watchy library. Its makers call it a development platform first and a watch second.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Making custom watch faces with Arduino code
  • Pulling weather and other web data over Wi-Fi
  • Learning about low-power gadgets and e-paper screens
  • Printing and sharing your own watch cases
  • Fully open design files for study and remixing

Not great for

  • People who want a finished watch with no tinkering
  • Heart rate or health tracking, with no heart sensor on board
  • Wiring extra sensors, with no pin header exposed
  • Long battery life with Wi-Fi in heavy use

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-S3 has two cores at 240 MHz. It handles watch faces, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth with room to spare.
  • 512 KB of working memory is plenty for watch faces and small web lookups.
  • 8 MB of storage holds your program, fonts, and pictures.
  • The 1.54 inch e-paper screen is about the size of a large postage stamp. It keeps its picture with almost no power.
  • The 200 mAh battery lasts about five to seven days for plain timekeeping. Heavy Wi-Fi use cuts that to two or three days.
  • The watch weighs about 13 grams, about as much as three sheets of printer paper.

Values are for the current v3 board. The ESP32-S3FN8 has 8 MB of in-package flash and no PSRAM, which matches the 8 MB flash setting in the getting-started guide. Clock and SRAM are ESP32-S3 datasheet values. v1 to v2 used an ESP32-PICO-D4 with 4 MB flash and a CP2104 or CP2102 USB-serial chip. v1 had a DS3231 RTC and v1.5 to v2 a PCF8563 RTC. v3 uses the ESP32-S3 internal RTC with an external 32 kHz crystal. The v3 BOM lists a Micro USB connector, a TP4054 LiPo charger, a BMA423 accelerometer and a Molex battery connector. The vibration motor does not fit the onboard vocabulary. No GPIO header is exposed. The v3 board has SDA, SCL, TX and RX test points. The size is 46 x 35.5 x 9.5 mm and the weight is 13 g, per the hardware page. Battery life is quoted as 5 to 7 days for timekeeping without Wi-Fi, per CNX Software.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Build and wear the kit

    Put together the board, screen, battery, and strap. You end up with a working watch you made yourself.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Your own watch face

    Copy an example watch face and change the font, numbers and pictures. Upload it from the Arduino IDE.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Weather on your wrist

    Fetch the local weather over Wi-Fi a few times a day. Today's temperature shows on the screen beside the time.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Daily step tracker

    Show your step count on the watch face. Set a daily goal and watch the number climb on a walk.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Class pixel art watch faces

    Students draw small black and white pictures and turn them into watch faces. The e-paper screen shows them sharply in sunlight.

    Uses: Screen.

Builder ideas

  • Plant care reminder

    Add a buzz and a note at set times to water the garden. The paper-like screen stays readable outdoors.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Bus and train times

    Fetch the next departure times over Wi-Fi from a public transit feed. Glance at your wrist on the way out the door.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Phone notification display

    Send short messages from a phone over Bluetooth to the screen. The motor buzzes to get your attention.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Wrist game of reflexes

    Write a simple button game with the four buttons. Track a high score and challenge your friends.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Moon phase and sunrise face

    Show the moon phase and sunrise time for your town. It is a good fit for stargazers and early birds.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Tilt to wake gesture

    Use the accelerometer to wake the screen with a wrist tilt. It saves battery and feels like a real smartwatch.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready.

  • Design your own case

    Model a new case in a free 3D design app and print it. Share the file with the Watchy community.

    Add: 3D printer filament.

  • Community event countdown

    Show a countdown to a local fair, game, or club meeting. Update the date over Wi-Fi from a simple web page.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Screen.

  • Try CircuitPython on the watch

    Load CircuitPython and write watch code in Python. It is a friendly path for learners who know a little Python.

    Uses: Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Music remote over Bluetooth

    Make the watch act as a Bluetooth remote for a computer. The buttons play, pause, and skip songs.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Sleep motion logger

    Record wrist movement through the night and upload it over Wi-Fi in the morning. Chart how restless you slept.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Wi-Fi, Battery ready.

The KiCad files, Gerbers, parts list, and case files are published under the MIT license. The Watchy Arduino library is also MIT, and the design is OSHWA certified as US000936. The ESP32-S3 chip and its Wi-Fi and Bluetooth drivers stay closed.
