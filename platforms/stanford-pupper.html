Stanford Pupper

Stanford Student Robotics and Hands-On Robotics

An open-source robot dog you build yourself from a free parts list.

Overview

Stanford Pupper is a four-legged robot dog that you build from free plans. It grew out of Stanford robotics classes and is now developed with Hands-On Robotics. The current version 3 uses twelve strong motors, a Raspberry Pi 5, a camera, a speaker and a small screen face. It suits university classes, robot clubs and skilled makers who want to learn how walking robots work.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning motor control, robot math and reinforcement learning with a real course
  • Building a capable robot dog for far less than a commercial one
  • Voice and AI projects with the microphone, speaker and camera
  • Remixing the body with the open CAD files
  • Robot clubs that enjoy a long team build

Not great for

  • Quick weekend projects, as the build takes many hours
  • Small budgets, with parts near 2,160 US dollars or a kit at 2,480 to 3,400 US dollars
  • Beginners who have never soldered or used Linux
  • Rough outdoor ground or heavy loads

Specs in plain words

  • Twelve motors give three joints on each leg. That is enough to walk, turn and do tricks.
  • The Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB of memory works like a small desktop computer inside the robot.
  • A second control board talks to the motors many times each second.
  • The robot weighs about 3 kilograms, about the same as a house cat.
  • Crouched, it is about 25 by 22 by 20 centimeters, about the size of a shoebox.
  • It runs on 20 volt power tool batteries, and the parts list includes two packs.

Tech specs list 12 DoF, 3 kg weight and 25 x 22 x 20 cm in the crouched pose. Pi 5 core count, clock and RAM come from the raspberry-pi-5 card. The docs say Pi 5 8GB. The Pi boots from a USB flash drive. As a result, flash_kb is null. Actuators are 4005 brushless motors with 10:1 planetary gearboxes, about 1.0 Nm continuous and 30 rad/s. Two 9 g servos move the ears. Interfaces are inferred from the parts used (Pi hat control board, PCIe M.2 adapter, CSI camera, HDMI screen).

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Walk it with a game controller

    Pair a wireless controller over Bluetooth and steer Pupper around the room. It trots and turns on command.

    Uses: Drives motors, Bluetooth. Add: wireless game controller.

  • Make a new face

    Draw your own eyes and moods for the screen. Pupper can look happy, sleepy or surprised.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux.

  • Ear wiggle tricks

    Program the ear servos to wiggle on cue. Pair the ears with a face change for a cute greeting.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Science fair gait study

    Time how fast Pupper walks on carpet, tile and grass. Chart which surface works best.

    Uses: Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: stopwatch, tape measure.

Builder ideas

  • Chat with your robot dog

    Talk to Pupper and hear it answer through its speaker. A large language model handles the conversation over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux.

  • Follow the ball

    Use the fisheye camera to spot a bright ball. Pupper turns and walks toward it.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: bright colored ball.

  • Balance and tilt lab

    Read the motion sensor and graph how the body tips with each step. Students see how robots keep their balance.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Runs Linux.

  • Single leg motor lab

    Command one leg joint and plot its angle and speed. This is the first lesson in the Pupper course.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Garden patrol pup

    Send Pupper along a flat garden path on battery power. It streams camera views of the plants to your laptop.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Battery ready, Drives motors.

  • Robot dance routine

    Choreograph steps, wiggles and ear flicks to a song. Pupper plays the music through its speaker.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound, Runs Linux.

  • Hallway greeter

    Park Pupper by a door at an open house. It waves, shows a smile and says hello to visitors.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound, Drives motors, Camera.

  • Custom 3D printed shell

    Remix the CAD files and print a new body cover. Your pup can look like a fox, a cat or a rover.

    Add: 3D printer filament.

Expert ideas

  • Retrain how it walks

    Open the Colab notebook and train a new walking style in simulation. Load the new policy and watch the real robot try it.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Fast object spotting

    Add an AI accelerator to the M.2 slot and run a vision model. Pupper can name people, pets and chairs in view.

    Uses: AI chip, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: AI accelerator board.

  • Voice command tricks

    Say sit or spin and have Pupper do the move. The microphone listens and the robot acts.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Map the room

    Plug in a USB sensor and build a rough map as Pupper walks. Use ROS 2 tools to view the map.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: USB depth sensor.

How open is it?

Open Board

The CAD models, 3D print files, circuit board files and full parts list are free to download. The Raspberry Pi software is open under the GPL-3.0 license and uses ROS 2. No hardware license is named, and the control board and motor driver firmware come only as ready-made files without source.
