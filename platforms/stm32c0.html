Overview

STM32C0 is the lowest-cost family of 32-bit chips from STMicroelectronics. ST made it to replace older 8-bit chips in fans, pumps, and smoke alarms. Hobbyists use it on Nucleo boards with the Arduino IDE. It is a good step up for makers who want a cheap, simple chip.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Small gadgets with buttons, lights, and simple sensors
  • Learning about STM32 chips on a low budget
  • Custom USB devices with the C071 chip
  • Designing your own tiny circuit boards
  • Rust, Zephyr, and C programming practice

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Big programs or graphics, with at most 36 KB of memory
  • First-time coders who want a browser-based start

Specs in plain words

  • A 48 MHz Cortex-M0+ core handles buttons, sensors, and lights with ease.
  • 16 KB to 256 KB of flash holds a small program with room for settings.
  • 6 KB to 36 KB of memory is enough for simple sensor jobs.
  • A 12-bit ADC reads knobs and analog sensors in fine steps.
  • The smallest chip has just eight pins and fits on a fingertip.
  • The C071 adds USB with no crystal, and the C092 adds a CAN FD bus for cars and machines.

Power5 V over USB-C or 3.3 V, 5 V or 7 to 12 V external (NUCLEO-C071RB)

Values are for the STM32C071RB used on the NUCLEO-C071RB, per CNX Software and the Zephyr board page. It has 128 KB flash, 24 KB SRAM, up to 61 GPIOs and up to 19 ADC channels. The C011 has 32 KB flash and 6 KB SRAM with up to 18 GPIOs. The C031 has 32 KB flash and 12 KB SRAM with up to 45 GPIOs. The C092RC has 256 KB flash, 30 KB SRAM and CAN FD per the Wikipedia Nucleo table. I2S is shared with SPI on the C011 and C031. USB is only on the C071 and C09x lines. CAN FD is only on the C092. Low-power current figures were not found in the sources read.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink a light

    Upload the Blink example to a Nucleo board. The onboard LED flashes and you know your setup works.

  • Button doorbell chime

    Press a button and play a short tune on a small speaker. Hang it by a bedroom door.

    Add: push button, small buzzer.

  • Plant thirst light

    Read a soil moisture sensor and light a red LED on dry days. Your houseplant gets water on time.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, LED.

  • Night light with a sensor

    A light sensor checks the room. A strip of LEDs glows as the room gets dark.

    Add: light sensor, LED strip.

  • Reaction time game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a friend presses a button. The fastest score wins.

    Add: push button, LEDs.

Builder ideas

  • Tiny desk fan controller

    Turn a knob to set the speed of a small fan. A temperature sensor can speed it up on hot days.

    Add: small DC fan, motor driver, knob, temperature sensor.

  • Custom USB keyboard

    Build a small macro pad on an STM32C071 board. Each key sends a shortcut to your computer.

    Add: board with the C071 chip, key switches.

  • Digital microphone level meter

    Read an I2S microphone and light more LEDs as sound gets louder. Use it to track classroom noise.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: I2S microphone, LEDs.

  • Simple tone player

    Send audio out over I2S to a small amplifier. Play short sound effects for a toy or game.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: I2S amplifier, small speaker.

  • Weather display

    Show temperature and humidity on a small screen. Put it on a kitchen shelf.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

  • Smoke and gas alarm prototype

    Read a gas sensor on an analog pin and sound a buzzer at high levels. This mirrors the job ST designed the chip for.

    Add: gas sensor, buzzer.

  • Servo cat toy

    Swing a feather wand back and forth on a servo motor. A button starts and stops the play time.

    Add: servo motor, push button, feather wand.

Expert ideas

  • USB keyboard reader

    Use the C071's USB host mode to read a USB keyboard. The board can type into a gadget that has no keyboard port.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB adapter.

  • Rust on a tiny chip

    Write firmware in Rust with the embassy framework. Blink lights and read sensors with modern tools.

  • Make your own tiny board

    Design a board in KiCad around an eight-pin C011 chip. Open designs such as the STM32C0-Nano show the way.

    Add: custom circuit board, SWD programmer.

  • CAN FD sensor network

    Link several C092 boards over a CAN FD bus. Model railways and robots use buses like this to share sensor data.

    Add: CAN FD transceivers, twisted pair wire.

ST's STM32CubeC0 driver code is BSD-3-Clause, and code builds with open tools such as GCC and OpenOCD. Community boards such as the STM32C0-Nano share KiCad files under CC BY-SA 4.0. The chip design is closed, and ST's own IDE is free but closed.
