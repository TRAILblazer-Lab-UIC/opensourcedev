Overview

The STM32C5 is an entry-level microcontroller family from STMicroelectronics, launched in March 2026. It runs an Arm Cortex-M33 core at up to 144 MHz with up to 1 MB of flash storage. The cheapest chips cost well under a dollar in volume. Its 48-pin parts fit the classic Blue Pill board layout, and makers already share open C5 boards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Upgrading old Blue Pill projects
  • Sensor projects and data logging
  • USB gadgets such as game controllers
  • CAN FD networks for robots and vehicles
  • Wired Ethernet projects on the larger chips

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Absolute beginners, as guides are still thin
  • MicroPython or CircuitPython fans, as neither supports it yet
  • People who want mature open flashing tools today

Specs in plain words

  • 144 MHz runs simple programs many times faster than a classic UNO.
  • 128 KB of working memory on the common boards holds thousands of sensor readings at once.
  • 512 KB of flash storage fits a large program with room to spare.
  • The 12-bit ADC reads up to 28 analog inputs such as knobs and light sensors.
  • Ethernet and the most memory come only on the C59x and C5A3 chips.

Power5 V over USB-C (Nucleo-64)

Core, flash and RAM are for the STM32C562RE on the NUCLEO-C562RE and on the common WeAct STM32C552 boards (512 KB flash, 128 KB RAM). The C531 and C542 have 64 KB SRAM and 128 KB to 256 KB flash. The C59x and C5A3 have 256 KB SRAM and up to 1 MB flash. adc_channels is the family maximum of 28 external channels. The family has up to 118 I/Os, but the count for the LQFP64 Nucleo part was not found. As a result, gpio is null. Ethernet is only on the C59x and C5A3, used on the NUCLEO-C5A3ZG. CNX gives dynamic power under 80 uA/MHz. No Stop or Standby current was found.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blinking light

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE to a supported C5 board. The light on the board flashes on and off.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

  • Room temperature display

    Read a temperature sensor every few seconds. A small screen shows the number for your desk or classroom.

    Add: temperature sensor, small screen.

Builder ideas

  • Science class data logger

    Sample light, sound, or voltage with the 12-bit ADC. Send the readings over USB to a spreadsheet for graphs.

    Add: light sensor, jumper wires.

  • USB game controller

    Wire buttons and a joystick to the board. The computer sees it as a gamepad for your favorite games.

    Add: arcade buttons, joystick module.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the board's host port. Each key press triggers lights or sounds for a party game.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB socket breakout, LED strip.

  • Tiny synth box

    Send sound through an I2S amplifier board. Knobs change the pitch and tone of a simple synthesizer.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: I2S amplifier board, small speaker, knobs.

  • Noise level meter

    Listen through a digital microphone in a library or classroom. A light bar grows as the room gets louder.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital I2S microphone, LED strip.

Expert ideas

  • Blue Pill upgrade

    Build an open C5 Blue Pill board from its KiCad files. Swap it into an old STM32F103 project for more speed and memory.

    Add: soldering iron, solder paste, small parts kit.

  • Wired weather station

    Use the Ethernet port on the Nucleo-C5A3ZG to serve a small web page. It shows outdoor temperature and humidity on your home network.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: network cable, weather sensor.

  • CAN bus model train

    Link several boards with CAN FD wiring along a model railway. Each board controls its own signals and switches.

    Add: CAN transceiver boards, twisted pair wire, servo motors.

  • Pen plotter robot

    Drive two stepper motors to move a pen across paper. The board draws shapes you design on a computer.

    Add: stepper motors, stepper driver boards, pen holder.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

ST's STM32CubeC5 drivers are BSD-3-Clause, and the code builds with the open GCC compiler. The community C5 Blue Pill shares KiCad files under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and the tenko eval board uses MIT. ST's IDE and programmer tools are free but closed, and the chip design stays closed.
