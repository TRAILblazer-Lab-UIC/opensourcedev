STM32F0

STMicroelectronics

A simple, low-cost STM32 family that makes a gentle first step into 32-bit Arm.

Field marks: STM32F0 chips hide inside candleLight and CANable sticks, using crystal-less USB to skip one tiny quartz part on a board already priced for pocket change.

Overview

The STM32F0 is the entry family of Arm Cortex-M0 microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, first launched in 2012. Chips run at up to 48 MHz with 16 KB to 256 KB of flash. The F042 and F072 parts add USB and CAN with no extra crystal. That made them the standard chips inside open USB-to-CAN adapters such as candleLight. Hobbyists also meet it on ST Nucleo boards and in QMK keyboards.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning 32-bit Arm on a cheap, simple chip
  • USB gadgets such as keypads on the F042 and F072
  • Open USB-to-CAN adapters for cars, robots, and 3D printers
  • Small battery projects that sleep most of the time
  • Step-by-step debugging with the debugger built into each Nucleo

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • MicroPython on most parts, as only the larger F091 is supported
  • Big programs, graphics, or heavy math
  • Brand new designs, as ST now points people to the STM32G0 and STM32C0

Specs in plain words

  • 48 MHz is fast enough to read sensors and blink lights hundreds of times a second.
  • 16 KB of working memory on the F072 holds a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • 128 KB of flash storage fits a medium Arduino sketch with a couple of libraries.
  • The 12-bit ADC can read 16 different analog sensors.
  • Standby draws about 1 microamp, which lets a coin cell last a long time.
  • The built-in USB bootloader accepts new code over a plain USB cable.

Values are for the STM32F072RB in LQFP64, used on the NUCLEO-F072RB and STM32F072B-DISCO. It runs at 48 MHz with 128 KB flash and 16 KB SRAM with parity. The datasheet lists 51 GPIOs for LQFP64 and up to 87 on LQFP100, and one 12-bit ADC with up to 16 external channels. low_power_ua is the Standby IDD typical at 3.3 V with LSI and IWDG off. The datasheet adds about 2.5 uA IDDA when VDDA monitoring is on. The Nucleo-64 uses a mini-USB connector for its ST-LINK per Wikipedia. Nucleo-32 boards use micro-USB. Family range is 16 KB to 256 KB flash and 4 KB to 32 KB SRAM. The low-cost F030 lacks USB and CAN.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blink on a Nucleo

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE. The green LED on the Nucleo flashes on and off.

  • Button reaction game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a player presses the button. The score prints on your computer.

    Add: push button, LED.

  • Plant thirst light

    Read a soil moisture sensor every few minutes. A red LED turns on once the pot gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, LED.

Builder ideas

  • Night light with a dimmer

    Read a light sensor and a knob. The board fades an LED strip up as the room gets dark.

    Add: light sensor, knob, LED strip.

  • Small macro keypad

    Wire a few keys to a board with USB, such as the F072. It types shortcuts into your computer like a tiny keyboard.

    Add: push buttons.

  • Simple tone player

    Send a tune out of the built-in DAC to a small amplifier. The board plays a doorbell chime or a short song.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Loud noise meter

    Read a microphone module on the analog input. A row of LEDs shows how loud the classroom is.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: microphone module, LEDs.

  • Touch pad music box

    Use the capacitive touch pins on a Discovery kit as keys. Each touch plays a different note through a speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small amplifier board, small speaker, copper tape.

  • Coin cell step counter

    Count steps with a motion sensor on a coin cell. The board naps in standby between steps to save power.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, coin cell holder.

  • Servo pet feeder

    Turn a servo motor at set times each day. A small door drops dry food into a bowl.

    Add: servo motor, real-time clock module.

Expert ideas

  • Build a USB-to-CAN adapter

    Flash candleLight firmware onto an F042 or F072 board with a CAN transceiver. Linux then talks to car and robot networks.

    Add: CAN transceiver board, DB-9 connector.

  • Klipper printer CAN bridge

    Use an F072 USB-to-CAN board to link a 3D printer toolhead to its main computer. It cuts the bundle of wires down to a few.

    Add: CAN cable, 3D printer toolhead board.

  • Garden data logger

    Wake on a timer to read temperature and soil moisture. The board saves each reading and sleeps for weeks on batteries.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, soil moisture sensor, memory chip, battery pack.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open

The candleLight adapter shares KiCad files under CERN OHL v1.2. The FalCAN Probe shares KiCad files under CC BY-SA 4.0. ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, but its USB and touch libraries only allow use on ST chips. The chip design and ST's own IDE stay closed.
