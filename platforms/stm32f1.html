Overview

The STM32F1 is the first STM32 series from STMicroelectronics, launched in 2007 on an Arm Cortex-M3 core. Its best known chip is the STM32F103, found on the very cheap Blue Pill board. That board made 32-bit Arm chips affordable for hobbyists. It suits makers who already know some Arduino and want to learn how Arm chips work.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Motor control with its special timer for motors
  • CAN projects such as model railways and robot networks
  • Learning bare-metal Arm programming in C
  • Low-cost USB gadgets
  • Battery loggers that sleep most of the time

Not great for

  • Total beginners who just bought a Blue Pill
  • Projects that need wireless links
  • Big programs or lots of data with only 20 KB of working memory
  • Math-heavy work, as the chip lacks a floating point unit

Specs in plain words

  • The 72 MHz processor is several times faster than a classic Arduino UNO.
  • 20 KB of working memory holds a few hundred sensor readings at once.
  • 64 KB of flash storage on the Blue Pill fits a medium program.
  • Standby draws about 2 microamps, small enough for long battery life.
  • Most pins accept 5 volt signals, which helps with older parts.
  • The Blue Pill is about 5 by 2 centimeters, close to a stick of gum.

Values are for the STM32F103C8T6 in LQFP48 on the Blue Pill, the most common F1 board. gpio is the 37 I/O of the 48-pin package from the datasheet list of 26/37/51/80 I/O by package. adc_channels is the datasheet maximum across packages. The 48-pin part exposes fewer. low_power_ua is the typical Standby current at 3.3 V with oscillators and watchdog off from the F103x8/xB datasheet. The Nucleo-F103RB and Olimexino-STM32 use the STM32F103RB with 128 KB flash. Other F1 lines range from 24 to 72 MHz, 4 to 96 KB SRAM and 16 KB to 1 MB flash, per Wikipedia.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blink on a Nucleo

    Plug a Nucleo board into USB and upload the Blink sketch from the Arduino IDE. The green LED starts to flash.

Builder ideas

  • Reaction timer game

    Light an LED at a random moment and time a button press. The fastest friend wins the round.

    Add: push button, LED.

  • Motor speed knob

    Turn a knob to change how fast a small motor spins. The motor timer makes the speed smooth and steady.

    Add: motor driver board, small DC motor, knob potentiometer.

  • USB shortcut box

    Wire a few buttons and let the board act as a USB keyboard. Each press can launch an app or paste a phrase.

    Add: push buttons.

  • Classroom noise meter

    Read a microphone module with the fast analog input. A strip of lights grows taller as the room gets louder.

    Add: microphone module, LED strip.

  • Weather station with a screen

    Read temperature and humidity on the porch. A small screen shows the numbers and the high for the day.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Self-balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor many times each second. The board nudges two motors to keep a two-wheeled robot upright.

    Add: motion sensor, motor driver board, small DC motors, battery pack.

  • Motorized slider for time-lapse photos

    Drive a stepper motor very slowly along a rail. A phone rides along and films a smooth time-lapse of a garden.

    Add: stepper motor, stepper driver board, rail kit.

  • Model railway on a CAN network

    Put a board with a CAN transceiver in each station. They share signals and switch tracks over two wires.

    Add: CAN transceiver modules, twisted pair wire.

  • Battery garden logger

    Wake the board once an hour to record soil temperature on a memory card. It sleeps on tiny power the rest of the time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature sensor, microSD card module, battery pack.

  • Bird house visit counter

    Mount a motion sensor at the door of a bird house. The board sleeps between visits and tallies each one.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Bare-metal Arm lessons

    Blink an LED by writing straight to the chip registers in C. You learn how a microcontroller works under the hood.

    Add: ST-Link programmer.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design
  • OSHWACertified open hardware

The Olimexino-STM32 shares Eagle design files under CC BY-SA 3.0 and is certified open hardware. ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, and GCC, OpenOCD, and STM32duino give a fully open toolchain. The chip design stays closed, and the generic Blue Pill has only a community schematic.
