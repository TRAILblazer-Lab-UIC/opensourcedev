STM32F4

STMicroelectronics

A fast STM32 family with built-in math power for sound, motors and MicroPython.

Field marks: Look for the Black Pill, a gum-stick board carrying one member of a chip family that runs anywhere from 84 to 180 MHz depending on the part number.

Overview

The STM32F4 is a family of Arm Cortex-M4F microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, first announced in 2011. It adds a floating point unit and DSP instructions for fast math on sound and sensor data. Chips run from 84 MHz up to 180 MHz with up to 2 MB of flash. Hobbyists know it from the WeAct Black Pill, ST Nucleo boards, the MicroPython pyboard, and the Adafruit Feather STM32F405.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sound projects such as synths, effects, and tuners
  • Motor control and balancing robots
  • MicroPython and CircuitPython on a fast board
  • Learning real debugging on a Nucleo board
  • A step up from 8-bit Arduino boards

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Absolute beginners who dislike pressing boot buttons to upload
  • Cameras or screens on the small F411 chip
  • Running Linux or big AI models

Specs in plain words

  • The 100 MHz F411 on the Black Pill does heavy math many times faster than a classic UNO.
  • 128 KB of working memory holds a short sound clip or thousands of sensor readings.
  • 512 KB of flash storage fits a large program plus a few libraries.
  • The floating point unit handles decimal math in hardware, which helps with sound and motion.
  • Standby draws under 2 microamps for long battery life.
  • The Black Pill is about 5 by 2 centimeters, roughly the size of a stick of gum.

Values are for the STM32F411CEU6 on the WeAct Black Pill V3.1, the most common hobby board in this family. It runs at 100 MHz with 512 KB flash and 128 KB SRAM. Adafruit lists 10 ADC inputs on this board, and Universal Solder lists 32 I/O with 14 analog. As a result, gpio and adc counts are approximate. low_power_ua is the STM32F411 datasheet Standby figure without RTC at 25 C and 1.7 V, seen in a search snippet of datasheet Rev 7. The family runs 84 MHz (F401) up to 180 MHz (F42x, F446, F469), with up to 2 MB flash and up to 384 KB SRAM. The F405 and F407 boards (Feather, pyboard, MicroMod) run at 168 MHz with 1 MB flash and 192 KB RAM. CAN, Ethernet, camera and LCD interfaces exist only on larger parts and are not on the F411.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • MicroPython lab board

    Load MicroPython or CircuitPython and type commands live over USB. Blink lights and read sensors in a few lines.

Builder ideas

  • Greenhouse helper

    Read temperature and humidity in a small greenhouse. The board turns on a fan once the air gets too warm.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, relay module, small fan.

  • Science data logger

    Save sensor readings to a memory card on the Feather STM32F405. Students open the file in a spreadsheet later.

    Add: microSD card, temperature sensor.

  • Heart rate graph

    Clip a pulse sensor to a finger. A small screen draws each beat as a moving line.

    Add: pulse sensor, small screen.

  • Pocket synthesizer

    Send sound to a small digital amplifier and speaker. Knobs change the pitch and tone of a homemade instrument.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: digital audio amplifier board, small speaker, knobs.

  • Clap counter

    Listen through a digital microphone and count loud claps. A light flashes each time the board hears one.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED.

  • Music-reactive light show

    Split the sound from a microphone into bass and treble. An LED strip dances along with the music.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED strip.

  • USB music knob box

    Wire knobs and buttons to the board. It acts as a USB music controller for recording apps.

    Add: knobs, push buttons.

  • Pen plotter art robot

    Drive two stepper motors to move a pen across paper. The board draws patterns and pictures you design.

    Add: stepper motors, stepper driver boards, pen holder.

  • Step through code with breakpoints

    Use the built-in debugger on a Nucleo board. You can pause your program and inspect each value.

Expert ideas

  • Guitar echo pedal

    Pass a guitar signal through an audio codec board. The floating point math adds echo and plays it back out.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, audio jacks, foot switch.

  • Instrument tuner

    Listen to a string through a microphone and find its pitch. A small screen shows the note and how far off it is.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, small screen.

  • Self-balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor hundreds of times each second. The board adjusts two motors to keep the robot upright.

    Add: motion sensor, motor driver board, small DC motors, battery pack.

  • Library barcode checkout

    Plug a USB barcode scanner into the board through an adapter. Each scan logs which book left the class library.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB barcode scanner, USB OTG adapter.

  • Battery wildlife logger

    Wake on a motion sensor near a den or a nest. The board records the time and sleeps on tiny power between visits.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

How open is it?

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

The Adafruit Feather STM32F405 Express shares Eagle files under CC BY-SA and is certified open hardware. The MicroPython pyboard design is CC BY 3.0. ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, but its USB middleware only allows use on ST chips. The chip design and ST's own IDE stay closed.
