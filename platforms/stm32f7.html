STM32F7

STMicroelectronics

A fast STM32 family with Ethernet, screen and camera support for big projects.

Overview

The STM32F7 is a family of Arm Cortex-M7 microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, first introduced in 2014. Chips run at up to 216 MHz with up to 2 MB of flash and 512 KB of working memory. Larger parts add Ethernet, a camera interface and a screen controller. Makers use it on ST Nucleo-144 and Discovery boards for touchscreen menus, audio and wired network gadgets.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Touchscreen menus and dashboards on the Discovery kits
  • Audio effects, recorders and sound analysis
  • Wired Ethernet projects that need to be reliable
  • MicroPython and CircuitPython on a fast board
  • Fast control loops for robots

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on Nucleo boards, as the chip has no radio
  • Battery gadgets, as low power figures were not found
  • Total beginners, as the cache and big feature list can confuse
  • Tight budgets, as Discovery kits cost far more than Nucleo boards

Specs in plain words

  • 216 MHz runs heavy math for sound and motion many times faster than a classic UNO.
  • 512 KB of working memory holds a short sound clip or a screen full of graphics.
  • 2 MB of flash storage fits a large program with pictures and fonts.
  • The Ethernet jack on the NUCLEO-F767ZI joins a wired network with a plain cable.
  • The screen controller and graphics helper draw smooth menus on a touchscreen.
  • Three 12-bit ADCs can read up to 24 analog sensors.

Values are for the STM32F767ZI on the NUCLEO-F767ZI, the most common F7 hobby board. Zephyr lists 216 MHz, 2 MB flash, 512 KB SRAM, three 12-bit ADCs with 24 channels and GPIO up to 168 for the family. The exact GPIO count for the LQFP144 package was not confirmed. As a result, gpio is null. The Nucleo-F767ZI has an RJ45 Ethernet jack, which is recorded under radios as wired Ethernet. Smaller parts differ. The STM32F722 has 512 KB flash and 256 KB RAM, and the STM32F746 has 1 MB flash and 320 KB RAM. Low-power figures were not found because the ST datasheets could not be fetched.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • MicroPython on a Nucleo

    Flash MicroPython onto a NUCLEO-F767ZI and type commands live over USB. Blink the onboard LEDs in a few lines.

  • Room thermometer on the wire

    Read a temperature sensor and serve the number on a web page over Ethernet. Anyone on the home network can check it.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: temperature sensor, network cable.

Builder ideas

  • Touchscreen light switch

    Draw big buttons on the Discovery kit touchscreen. Each tap turns a relay and a lamp on or off.

    Uses: Screen. Add: relay module, small lamp.

  • Kitchen recipe screen

    Show recipes on the 4.3 inch touchscreen of the F746 Discovery kit. Swipe through each step while you cook.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microSD card.

  • Greenhouse dashboard

    Read soil moisture, light and temperature sensors. A touchscreen shows live graphs and the board waters plants on a schedule.

    Uses: Screen. Add: soil moisture sensor, light sensor, temperature sensor, small water pump, relay module.

  • Classroom voice recorder

    Record short clips with the microphones on the F746 Discovery kit. Students play them back to hear their reading practice.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: headphones, microSD card.

  • Drum machine with pads

    Tap pads on the touchscreen to trigger drum sounds. The board mixes them and plays the beat through headphones.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • USB keyboard game console

    Plug a USB keyboard into the USB host port. Play a simple maze game drawn on the Discovery kit screen.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, USB OTG adapter.

  • School network clock

    Fetch the exact time over Ethernet and show it on a large screen. The bell schedule lights up as each period changes.

    Uses: Wired network, Screen. Add: network cable, large screen.

Expert ideas

  • Guitar effects box

    Pass a guitar signal through an audio codec board. The fast math adds echo or distortion and plays it back out.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, audio jacks, foot switch.

  • Bird feeder snapshot camera

    Wire a small camera module to the camera interface on a board that exposes it. The board saves a photo to a memory card each time motion is seen.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, motion sensor, microSD card.

  • Science lab spectrum analyzer

    Sample a microphone thousands of times each second. The screen draws the sound as bars from low to high pitch.

    Uses: Hears sound, Screen.

  • Wired sensor hub for a garden plot

    Gather readings from several sensors around a community garden. The board shares them on a web page over Ethernet.

    Uses: Wired network. Add: temperature sensors, soil moisture sensors, network cable, weatherproof box.

  • USB memory stick logger

    Plug a USB stick into the host port. The board writes weather readings to a file you can open on any computer.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB memory stick, USB OTG adapter, weather sensor.

  • Self-balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor hundreds of times each second. The board adjusts two motors to keep the robot upright.

    Add: motion sensor, motor driver board, small DC motors, battery pack.

  • Smart home control panel

    Build a wall panel with a touchscreen GUI made in LVGL. It controls lights and shows sensor data over the network.

    Uses: Screen, Wired network. Add: relay modules, network cable, power supply.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The satshakit-M7 community board shares Eagle files under CC BY-SA 4.0. ST's HAL driver code is BSD-3-Clause, but some Cube parts and the STemWin graphics library only allow use on ST chips. Free open tools such as GCC and OpenOCD can build and flash code. The chip design and ST's own IDE stay closed.
