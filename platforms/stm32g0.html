STM32G0

STMicroelectronics

A low-cost STM32 chip family for simple gadgets, USB-C power, and CAN networks.

Overview

The STM32G0 is a family of budget Arm Cortex-M0+ microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, launched in 2018. It runs at up to 64 MHz and replaces the older STM32F0 in new designs. The bigger G0B1 and G0C1 parts add USB, a USB-C power controller, and two CAN FD ports. Makers meet it on ST Nucleo boards and tiny WeAct core boards.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Cheap gadgets that read buttons and sensors
  • Learning on a Nucleo board with a built-in debugger
  • CAN FD network projects with G0B1 chips
  • Battery projects that sleep most of the time
  • A step up from 8-bit Arduino boards

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Heavy math such as audio effects or machine learning
  • CircuitPython, which has no G0 board yet
  • Absolute beginners who want a ready-to-go app

Specs in plain words

  • 64 MHz is a few times faster than a classic UNO.
  • The G0B1 has 144 KB of working memory, enough for MicroPython and thousands of sensor readings.
  • 512 KB of flash storage on the G0B1 fits a large program and several libraries.
  • The small G030 and G031 parts have only 8 KB of memory, enough for simple control jobs.
  • Standby draws about 0.2 microamps, small enough for years on a battery between wake-ups.
  • The ADC reads 16 analog inputs for sensors and knobs.

Values are for the STM32G0B1 (datasheet DS13560), used on the Nucleo-G0B1RE and the WeAct STM32G0B1 core board. It has up to 512 KB flash and 144 KB SRAM, of which 128 KB has parity. The datasheet lists up to 94 I/Os depending on package. As a result, gpio is left null. low_power_ua is the typical Standby current with RTC off at 3.0 V and 25 C. The small G030 and G031 parts have 32 KB to 64 KB flash and 8 KB SRAM and lack USB and CAN. The Nucleo-G071RB uses a G071RB with 128 KB flash and 32 KB to 36 KB SRAM. Nucleo boards use a USB connector for the onboard ST-LINK. By contrast, WeAct boards use USB-C.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blinking light

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE. The green light on the Nucleo board flashes on and off.

  • Classroom reaction timer

    Light an LED at a random moment and time how fast a student presses a button. The result prints on the computer.

    Add: LED, push button.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Room temperature display

    Read a temperature sensor every few seconds. A small screen shows the reading for the whole class.

    Add: temperature sensor, small screen.

  • Garden watering timer

    Turn a small pump on for a few minutes each morning. Seedlings get water on schedule.

    Add: small water pump, relay module, tubing.

  • Night light with a dimmer

    Read a light sensor and a knob. An LED strip glows softly once the room gets dark.

    Add: light sensor, knob, LED strip.

  • Simple game controller

    Wire buttons and a joystick to a G0B1 board. It shows up on a computer as a USB game pad.

    Add: push buttons, joystick module.

  • Tiny tone player

    Send simple tunes to a digital amplifier board. A small speaker plays a doorbell chime or a game sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: digital audio amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Noise level meter

    Listen through a digital microphone and measure how loud a room is. A row of LEDs climbs as the noise grows.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LEDs.

Expert ideas

  • Coin cell weather sensor

    Wake once an hour to log temperature and humidity. The chip sleeps on tiny power the rest of the time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, coin cell holder.

  • USB keyboard reader

    Plug a USB keyboard into the G0B1 host port. The board turns key presses into commands for a robot or light show.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keyboard, USB OTG adapter.

  • CAN bus sensor network

    Link several boards with the CAN FD ports on G0B1 chips. Each node reports its sensor to one main board.

    Add: CAN transceiver boards, twisted pair wire, sensors.

  • Small DC motor controller

    Drive a small motor with a speed knob and a direction switch. A model train or a fan runs at the pace you set.

    Add: motor driver board, small DC motor, knob, switch.

How open is it?

Open Board

ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, and the code builds with free open tools such as GCC and OpenOCD. The USB and USB-C power libraries use ST's own license, and the USB-C power core is a closed precompiled file. WeAct shares PDF schematics with no license, and the chip design stays closed.

Hardware license: No license stated on WeAct files, ST Nucleo schematics come under ST evaluation board terms · Software license: BSD-3-Clause
