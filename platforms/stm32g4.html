Overview

The STM32G4 is a family of Arm Cortex-M4F microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, launched in 2019. It runs at up to 170 MHz and adds math helpers for fast motor and signal work. Each chip packs fast ADCs, DACs, op-amps, and comparators. Makers use it on ST Nucleo boards, the B-G431B-ESC1 motor kit, and tiny WeAct boards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Brushless motor drives and robots
  • Power supplies and battery chargers
  • Fast signal sampling and sound math
  • CAN FD networks for robots and vehicles
  • MicroPython on a G474 board

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • MicroPython on the small G431, which has only 32 KB of memory
  • CircuitPython, which has no G4 board yet
  • Absolute beginners who want a guided first project

Specs in plain words

  • 170 MHz handles heavy math many times faster than a classic UNO.
  • The floating point unit and math helpers solve motor angles in hardware.
  • The G431 has 32 KB of working memory, enough for a motor program and a few thousand readings.
  • The G474 has 512 KB of flash storage and 128 KB of memory for bigger programs.
  • The ADC samples millions of times each second, fast enough to watch a motor current change.
  • The WeAct board is about 36 by 28 millimeters, roughly the size of a postage stamp.

Values are for the STM32G431CB (datasheet DS12589) on the WeAct STM32G4 core board. It has 128 KB flash and 32 KB SRAM, made of 22 KB main SRAM plus 10 KB CCM SRAM. adc_channels is the chip maximum of 23 across two ADCs, and the datasheet lists up to 86 I/Os by package. As a result, gpio is left null. low_power_ua is the typical Standby current of 120 nA at 3 V and 25 C without the watchdog. The board size and the 3.3 V to 20 V USB-C input come from CNX Software. The G474 parts have 512 KB flash, 128 KB SRAM and the HRTIM, and Zephyr lists 28 nA Standby for the G474RE. Nucleo boards use a USB connector for the onboard ST-LINK in place of USB-C.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blinking light

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE. The light on the board flashes on and off.

  • Live MicroPython lab

    Load MicroPython on a G474 board and type commands over USB. Blink lights and read sensors in a few lines.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Brushless motor spinner

    Use the B-G431B-ESC1 kit to spin a drone motor smoothly. A knob sets the speed.

    Add: brushless motor, LiPo battery, knob.

  • Greenhouse fan control

    Read temperature in a small greenhouse. The board speeds up a fan as the air warms.

    Add: temperature sensor, fan, motor driver board.

  • Pocket signal scope

    Sample a voltage millions of times each second with the fast ADC. A computer app draws the wave on screen.

    Add: probe wires.

  • Heart rate graph

    Clip a pulse sensor to a finger. A small screen draws each beat as a moving line.

    Add: pulse sensor, small screen.

  • Tone generator for science class

    Make steady sound waves with the built-in DAC. Students hear and measure different pitches.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: digital audio amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Clap counter

    Listen through a digital microphone and count loud claps. A light flashes each time the board hears one.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED.

  • Pen plotter art robot

    Drive two stepper motors to move a pen across paper. The board draws patterns you design.

    Add: stepper motors, stepper driver boards, pen holder.

Expert ideas

  • Electric skateboard drive

    Run a big brushless motor with smooth field-oriented control. The rider sets the speed with a handheld trigger.

    Add: brushless motor, motor driver board, large battery pack, throttle.

  • Bench power supply

    Use the fast timers to switch power at high speed. The board holds a steady voltage for your other projects.

    Add: power stage board, inductor, display.

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Pass a guitar signal through an audio codec board. The math helpers add echo and distortion in real time.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio codec board, audio jacks, foot switch.

  • Self-balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor hundreds of times each second. The board adjusts two motors to keep the robot upright.

    Add: motion sensor, motor driver board, small DC motors, battery pack.

  • Solar panel tracker

    Compare two light sensors on a panel. A motor tilts the panel toward the sun and the board sleeps between moves.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: light sensors, servo motor, small solar panel.

  • CAN bus robot arm

    Give each joint its own G4 board on a CAN FD network. A main board sends the moves to every joint.

    Add: CAN transceiver boards, servo motors, twisted pair wire.

How open is it?

Wild Open

ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, and the code builds with free open tools such as GCC and OpenOCD. The USB libraries use ST's own license, and the motor tools are free but closed. The analog-toolkit board shares KiCad files under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, and the chip design stays closed.

Hardware license: CERN-OHL-S-2.0 (analog-toolkit), WeAct core boards carry no license, ST boards use ST terms · Software license: BSD-3-Clause
