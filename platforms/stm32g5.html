Overview

The STM32G5 is a mixed-signal microcontroller family from STMicroelectronics, launched in November 2026. It runs an Arm Cortex-M33 core at up to 280 MHz and replaces the popular STM32G4. Each chip packs fast ADCs, comparators, DACs, and a very precise timer for motors and power supplies. It suits engineers and advanced makers who already know C.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Brushless motor drives and robots
  • Power supplies, chargers, and solar controllers
  • Fast signal sampling
  • CAN FD networks for robots and vehicles

Not great for

  • Beginners, as it needs C and ST's new tools
  • Arduino or MicroPython fans, as neither supports it yet
  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Tight budgets, as the Nucleo board costs about 48 US dollars

Specs in plain words

  • 280 MHz handles heavy motor math about twice as fast as the STM32G4.
  • 112 KB of working memory holds a motor program plus thousands of readings.
  • 512 KB of flash storage fits a large control program.
  • The ADC samples five million times each second, fast enough to track motor current.
  • The 111 picosecond timer steps let power circuits switch with very fine control.
  • The chips have no radio, and they talk over USB, CAN FD, or serial wires.

Core, flash and RAM are for the STM32G564RE on the NUCLEO-G564RE (512 KB flash, 112 KB RAM). gpio and adc_channels are family maximums for the G55x and G56x. The G53x and G54x have up to 62 GPIOs, 23 ADC channels, 60 KB SRAM and one FDCAN. The family also has I3C, XSPI and two position encoder interfaces on the larger parts. Zephyr's default setup runs the Nucleo at 160 MHz from a 24 MHz crystal. No low-power current figure was found.

Builder ideas

  • First blinking light

    Build the Zephyr Blinky sample for the NUCLEO-G564RE. The light on the board flashes on and off.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

  • Greenhouse fan control

    Read temperature in a small greenhouse. The board speeds up a fan as the air warms.

    Add: temperature sensor, fan, motor driver board.

  • Pocket signal scope

    Sample a voltage five million times each second with the fast ADC. A computer app draws the wave for a science class.

    Add: probe wires.

  • USB keypad shortcuts

    Plug a small USB keypad into the host port. Each key starts a light show or a motor move.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB keypad, USB socket breakout.

Expert ideas

  • Brushless motor spinner

    Use the B-G564E-MC1S motor kit to spin a drone motor smoothly. A knob sets the speed.

    Add: brushless motor, battery pack, knob.

  • Electric bike drive

    Run a large brushless motor with field-oriented control. The rider sets the speed with a thumb throttle.

    Add: brushless motor, motor driver board, large battery pack, throttle.

  • Lab bench power supply

    Use the high-resolution timer to switch power very fast. The board holds a steady voltage for your other projects.

    Add: power stage board, inductor, small screen.

  • Solar battery charger

    Track the best working point of a solar panel. The board charges a battery bank for a garden shed.

    Add: solar panel, power stage board, battery pack.

  • Self-balancing robot

    Read a motion sensor hundreds of times each second. The board adjusts two motors to keep the robot upright.

    Add: motion sensor, motor driver board, small DC motors, battery pack.

  • CAN bus robot arm

    Give each joint its own board on a CAN FD network. A main board sends moves to every joint.

    Add: CAN transceiver boards, servo motors, twisted pair wire.

  • Camera slider for nature videos

    Drive a stepper motor along a rail very slowly. Your camera glides past flowers for smooth time-lapse shots.

    Add: stepper motor, stepper driver board, linear rail.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot confirmed
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

ST's STM32CubeG5 drivers are BSD-3-Clause, and the code builds with the open GCC compiler. The open bootloader uses ST's own license, and ST's flashing tools are free but closed. Board design files were not confirmed, and the chip design stays closed.
