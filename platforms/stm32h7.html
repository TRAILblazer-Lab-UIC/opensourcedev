STM32H7

STMicroelectronics

A very fast microcontroller family for sound, screens, and simple vision.

Field marks: At 480 MHz this microcontroller runs vision, audio, and screens, usually from a Mega-sized board with more connectors than spare desk space.

Overview

STM32H7 is the fastest family of STM32 chips from STMicroelectronics. Some parts pair a 480 MHz main core with a second helper core. Hobby boards with it include the Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi, the Portenta H7, the OpenMV Cam, and the Daisy Seed. It suits makers who want sound, screens, cameras, or simple machine learning on a small board.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Audio projects such as synths and guitar effects
  • Small color screens and touch displays
  • Simple camera and machine vision projects
  • Fast motor control and robot arms
  • Wi-Fi and Bluetooth projects on the GIGA and Portenta boards

Not great for

  • A very first project, as the setup has more pieces than simpler boards
  • Running a full desktop system like Linux
  • Battery projects that must last for months
  • Tiny wearables, as most boards are large

Specs in plain words

  • 480 MHz is very fast for a microcontroller. It can mix sound and update a screen together.
  • Two cores on the H747 let one side run the screen and the other run sensors.
  • About 1 MB of memory on the chip holds a small photo or a few seconds of sound.
  • The GIGA board adds 8 MB of extra memory for bigger images and buffers.
  • 2 MB of flash holds a large program with fonts and pictures.
  • 76 pins on the GIGA give plenty of room for buttons, lights and sensors.

Power5 V over USB-C or 6 to 24 V on VIN

Values are for the STM32H747XI on the Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi, the most widely sold hobby board in this family. The chip has 2 MB flash and 1 MB RAM. The board adds 8 MB SDRAM (shown as psram_kb) and 16 MB QSPI NOR flash. GPIO, ADC, connectors, radios and power input are GIGA board values from its datasheet. Single-core parts such as the H743 (OpenMV, Nucleo) have only the Cortex-M7. The H750 on the Daisy Seed and WeAct board is a small-flash value part. Wikipedia gives 600 MHz as the family maximum for the Cortex-M7. Which lines reach it was not confirmed from ST. The CAN port needs an external transceiver on the GIGA.

Starter ideas

  • Hello on the screen

    Run an Arduino example that draws shapes and text on a display. It is a friendly first step with this board.

Builder ideas

  • Desktop synthesizer

    Turn knobs to shape a tone and play it through the audio jack. The chip has plenty of speed for rich sounds.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: knobs, speakers or headphones, push buttons.

  • Weather station with a screen

    Read temperature and humidity and draw a live chart on a touch screen. Hang it in the kitchen to plan your day.

    Uses: Screen, Wi-Fi. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, touch screen.

  • USB keyboard note taker

    Plug a USB keyboard straight into the board. Type notes that show up on a small screen.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Screen. Add: USB keyboard, screen.

  • Classroom data dashboard

    Collect sensor readings from the room and post them over Wi-Fi. Students watch the numbers change on a web page.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: temperature sensor, light sensor.

  • Phone-controlled light show

    Send colors from a phone over Bluetooth. A long LED strip changes to match.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: LED strip, power supply.

  • Drum machine

    Tap pads to record beats and loop them through speakers. Build a full rhythm for a school band.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: touch pads or buttons, speakers.

  • Smooth robot arm

    Drive several servos at once with smooth motion. The arm picks up and stacks small blocks.

    Add: robot arm kit, servo motors, power supply.

  • Talking plant monitor

    Read a soil sensor and play a short recorded clip once the pot gets dry. The plant asks for a drink.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: soil moisture sensor, small speaker.

  • Room sound meter

    Show how loud the classroom is on a big colored bar. Kids learn to keep the noise level green.

    Uses: Screen. Add: microphone module, screen.

Expert ideas

  • Guitar effects pedal

    Feed a guitar signal in and add echo or distortion. Stomp a foot switch to change the sound.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio input circuit, foot switch, sturdy box.

  • Bird feeder camera

    Snap a photo each time something moves at the feeder. Check the pictures later to learn who visits.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, motion sensor, weatherproof box.

  • Color sorting machine

    A camera spots the color of each bead or candy. A servo flips it into the right cup.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, servo motor, chute and cups.

  • Retro game console

    Write small arcade games and show them on a screen. Plug in a USB game controller to play.

    Uses: Screen, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: screen, USB game controller.

  • Gesture-sensing lamp

    Wave a hand in front of a camera to switch a lamp on or off. A small model learns your gestures.

    Uses: Camera. Add: camera module, relay module, lamp.

  • Science lab oscilloscope

    Sample a signal fast and draw the wave on a screen. Students see sound and electricity as shapes.

    Uses: Screen. Add: screen, probe wires.
