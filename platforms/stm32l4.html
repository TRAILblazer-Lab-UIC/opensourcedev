STM32L4

STMicroelectronics

A low-power STM32 chip family for battery sensors, loggers, and audio gadgets.

Field marks: This low-power chip family idles near 130 nanoamps and goes by several boards, from white Nucleo kits to the Blues Swan and Cygnet.

Overview

The STM32L4 is a family of ultra-low-power Arm Cortex-M4F microcontrollers from STMicroelectronics, launched in 2015. It runs at 80 MHz, and the newer L4+ parts reach 120 MHz with up to 2 MB of flash. The chips sleep on about a tenth of a microamp. Makers know it from ST Nucleo boards and the Blues Swan and Cygnet Feather boards.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Battery sensor nodes that last months
  • Data loggers for science and nature
  • Sound projects with digital microphones
  • CircuitPython on the Blues Swan or Cygnet
  • Cellular projects paired with a Blues Notecard

Not great for

  • Wireless projects on their own, as the chips have no radio
  • Cameras or video
  • Running Linux or big AI models
  • Absolute beginners who want a plug-and-play board

Specs in plain words

  • 80 MHz with a floating point unit handles sound and sensor math with ease.
  • The Nucleo-L476RG has 128 KB of working memory, enough for a short sound clip or thousands of readings.
  • 1 MB of flash storage fits a large program plus several libraries.
  • The Blues Swan has 640 KB of memory, enough room for CircuitPython or a small sound model.
  • Standby draws about 0.13 microamps, small enough for years on a battery between wake-ups.
  • The built-in microphone filter reads digital microphones with no extra chip.

Values are for the STM32L476RG on the Nucleo-L476RG, the most common L4 board. It runs at 80 MHz with 1 MB flash and 128 KB SRAM. The chip has up to 114 I/Os by package, and the board pin and ADC counts were not confirmed. As a result, gpio and adc_channels are null. low_power_ua is the 130 nA Standby figure listed by Zephyr from ST data. i2s stands for the SAI audio ports, and lcd stands for the segment LCD driver. The Blues Swan uses an STM32L4R5 at 120 MHz with 2 MB flash and 640 KB SRAM, and CNX Software lists 55 GPIOs on its castellated edges. The Nucleo-L432KC uses an STM32L432KC with 256 KB flash and 64 KB SRAM.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Live CircuitPython lab

    Load CircuitPython on a Blues Swan or Cygnet. Type commands over USB to blink lights and read sensors.

  • First blinking light

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE to a Nucleo board. The green light flashes on and off.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Classroom weather station

    Read temperature, humidity, and air pressure every minute. A small screen shows the numbers for the class.

    Add: weather sensor, small screen.

  • Science data logger

    Save sensor readings to a memory card for a week. Students open the file in a spreadsheet later.

    Add: microSD card adapter, microSD card, temperature sensor.

  • Battery bird box monitor

    Wake on a motion sensor inside a nest box. The board logs each visit and sleeps on tiny power between them.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Segment display clock

    Drive a simple segment LCD with the built-in driver. The clock runs for months on a small battery.

    Uses: Screen, Sips power. Add: segment LCD, coin cell holder.

  • Clap counter

    Listen through a digital microphone and count loud claps. A light flashes each time the board hears one.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED.

  • Talking doorbell

    Play a short sound clip through a speaker. Visitors hear a greeting each time they press the button.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: digital audio amplifier board, small speaker, push button.

  • Heart rate graph

    Clip a pulse sensor to a finger. A small screen draws each beat as a moving line.

    Add: pulse sensor, small screen.

Expert ideas

  • Keyword listener

    Train a small sound model with Edge Impulse on a Blues Swan. A light turns on each time it hears your chosen word.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone module, LED.

  • Cellular river level sensor

    Measure water height at a creek with a distance sensor. A Blues Notecard sends the reading over the cell network.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: cellular module, distance sensor, battery pack, weatherproof box.

  • Bee hive scale

    Weigh a hive each hour with load cells. The board tracks honey gains across the season on battery power.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: load cells, load cell amplifier, battery pack.

  • Machine motion watcher

    Read a vibration sensor on a pump or a fan. A small model spots odd shaking before a part fails.

    Add: motion sensor.

  • USB barcode library checkout

    Plug a USB barcode scanner into the board through an adapter. Each scan logs which book left the class library.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB barcode scanner, USB OTG adapter.

  • Woodshop digital readout

    Read a linear scale on a saw fence or a mill. A display shows the exact position in millimeters.

    Uses: Screen. Add: linear scale sensor, display.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The Blues Swan and Cygnet share schematics, Gerbers, and board files under the MIT license. ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, and the code builds with free open tools such as GCC and OpenOCD. ST's USB and touch libraries use ST's own license, and the chip design stays closed.
