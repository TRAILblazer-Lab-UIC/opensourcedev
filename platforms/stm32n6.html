Overview

The STM32N6 is the most powerful STM32 microcontroller from STMicroelectronics. It pairs a fast Arm Cortex-M55 core with ST's own neural processing unit for AI. The chip also has camera inputs, an image processor, and a video encoder. It suits makers who want a small, low-power smart camera, and the OpenMV N6 is the friendliest board to start with.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Smart cameras that spot people, animals, or objects
  • Running vision AI models on a small, cool chip
  • Live video with labels on the Discovery kit screen
  • Wired video streaming over Gigabit Ethernet
  • MicroPython vision projects on the OpenMV N6

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, with most boards costing 70 to 220 US dollars
  • Total beginners on the ST Nucleo board, which needs signing tools and boot jumpers
  • Projects that need a full Linux computer
  • Parts that send 5 volt signals into the OpenMV N6 pins

Specs in plain words

  • The 800 MHz processor is many times faster than a classic Arduino UNO.
  • The AI unit is rated at 600 billion simple math steps each second. That is enough for object detection at about 30 frames per second.
  • 4.2 MB of working memory holds a few camera frames at once.
  • The chip has no built-in storage for programs. Boards add a separate flash chip, often 32 MB or more.
  • The camera system handles a 5 megapixel sensor at 30 frames per second.
  • The video encoder records smooth 1080p video at 30 frames per second.

Chip values come from CNX Software and Zephyr. The chip has 4.2 MB SRAM (shown as 4300 KB) and no internal flash. Wikipedia lists 4424 KB SRAM. GPIO (165) and ADC channels (20) are chip maximums for the largest package. PSRAM, flash, radios, connectors, onboard parts and power are OpenMV N6 values. It has 64 MB SDRAM, 32 MB octal flash, a CYW43439 Wi-Fi and BLE 5.1 module and Gigabit Ethernet. The Nucleo-N657X0-Q has no PSRAM and no camera. The STM32N6570-DK has 128 MB octo-SPI flash and 32 MB PSRAM. Zephyr runs the core at 400 MHz by default. The 800 MHz figure is ST's maximum. The 0.6 TOPS value is ST's peak 600 GOPS claim.

Things you could build

17 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First face finder

    Open the OpenMV IDE and run the face detection example on an OpenMV N6. Boxes appear around faces in the live video.

    Uses: Camera.

  • Color blob tracker

    Teach the camera one bright color and watch it follow a ball across the table. A dot on screen marks the ball.

    Uses: Camera.

  • QR code door sign

    Point the camera at visitor badges with QR codes. The board reads each code and logs the name.

    Uses: Camera.

Builder ideas

  • Bird feeder visitor counter

    Aim a camera at a feeder and run an object detection model. The board counts each bird and saves a snapshot to a memory card.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: microSD card, weatherproof box.

  • Hallway people counter

    Mount the camera above a doorway and count people walking in and out. The totals help a library or club see busy hours.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: wall mount.

  • Garden pest spotter

    Watch the vegetable bed for rabbits or deer with a detection model. The board sends an alert over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Wi-Fi. Add: weatherproof box, power supply.

  • Recycling sorter helper

    Show the camera a can, a bottle, or a paper cup. The board names the bin and lights a matching LED.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: LEDs.

  • Smart touchscreen camera

    Use the Discovery kit with its 5-inch touch screen. Live video plays with labels drawn on each object it spots.

    Uses: Camera, Screen, AI chip.

  • Battery trail camera

    Run an OpenMV N6 from a small battery on a nature trail. It saves photos of passing animals to a memory card.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready. Add: lithium battery, microSD card, weatherproof box.

  • Gesture-controlled music box

    Wave your hand in front of the camera to play, pause or skip a song. A small speaker plays the music.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Makes sound. Add: small speaker, amplifier board.

  • Clap and voice trigger

    Listen with the onboard microphone for a clap or a short sound. The board snaps a photo at that moment.

    Uses: Hears sound, Camera.

  • Science lab motion tracker

    Film a rolling cart or a swinging pendulum. The board tracks the object and logs its position frame by frame.

    Uses: Camera. Add: microSD card.

Expert ideas

  • Wired security camera

    Stream compressed video over the Ethernet port to a computer on the home network. The hardware encoder keeps the picture smooth.

    Uses: Camera, Wired network. Add: network cable.

  • Sign language letter reader

    Train a small model on hand shapes for a few letters. The board shows each letter on a screen as you sign it.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Screen. Add: small screen.

  • Robot that follows a person

    Detect a person in each frame and steer two motors toward them. The robot trails you across the room.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip. Add: motor driver board, small DC motors, robot chassis, battery pack.

  • Phone app for a smart camera

    Send each detection to a phone over Bluetooth. A simple app shows a running list of what the camera saw.

    Uses: Camera, AI chip, Bluetooth.

  • Custom model on the NPU

    Pick a vision model from the STM32 model zoo and compile it with ST Edge AI Core. Then run it on a Nucleo board in C.

    Uses: AI chip. Add: camera module.

Getting started checklist

How open is it?

Open Board

ST's HAL code, board support files, and examples are BSD-3-Clause, and Zephyr and MicroPython support the chip. Some libraries, the flashing tools, and the AI model compiler stay closed, and the chip design is closed too. The Ohm Lab Neuro N6 shares a schematic with no license, and no full open layout was found for any N6 board.

Hardware license: None stated (Ohm Lab Neuro N6 schematic PDF), OpenMV N6 publishes no design files, ST board CAD license not verified · Software license: BSD-3-Clause
