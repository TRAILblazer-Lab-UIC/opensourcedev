Overview

The STM32U0 is an ultra-low-power microcontroller family from STMicroelectronics, launched in 2024. It runs an Arm Cortex-M0+ core at up to 56 MHz and sleeps on a tiny trickle of power. Some chips drive segment LCD screens like the ones on meters and thermostats. It suits battery and solar sensor projects for hobbyists and students.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Battery and solar sensors that run for months
  • Segment LCD gadgets such as timers and meters
  • Simple USB devices such as knobs and buttons
  • Touch buttons and small control jobs

Not great for

  • Color screens, as the LCD controller drives only segment displays
  • Wireless projects, as the chips have no radio
  • Big programs, as memory tops out at 40 KB
  • MicroPython fans, as there is no official port

Specs in plain words

  • 56 MHz is about three times the speed of a classic UNO.
  • 40 KB of working memory holds a sensor program and a few thousand readings.
  • 256 KB of flash storage fits a medium program with room for logged data.
  • 160 nanoamps in standby is a tiny trickle that a coin cell can supply for a very long time.
  • The segment LCD controller drives up to 208 segments, enough for digits and symbols.
  • USB works with no extra crystal on the U073 and U083 chips.

Chip values are for the STM32U083RC on the Nucleo-U083RC with 256 KB flash and 40 KB SRAM. GPIO (69) is the maximum per the Zephyr page. The U031 has 16 to 64 KB flash and 12 KB SRAM and no LCD controller per CNX Software. low_power_ua is the 160 nA Standby figure with RTC. Standby without RTC is 30 nA and Shutdown is 16 nA. CNX Software's spec list gave 54 MHz while ST and Zephyr give 56 MHz. 56 MHz is used. The lcd entry stands for the segment LCD controller. It is not a TFT interface. There is no I2S or CAN. Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blinking light

    Upload the Blink example from the Arduino IDE. The green light on the Nucleo board flashes on and off.

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Read a soil moisture sensor in a flower pot. A buzzer chirps once the soil gets dry.

    Add: soil moisture sensor, buzzer.

  • Night light with a light sensor

    Read a light sensor at dusk. An LED turns on in the dark and off at sunrise.

    Add: light sensor, LED.

Builder ideas

  • Segment LCD thermometer

    Show room temperature on the STM32U083C-DK segment display. The built-in temperature sensor does the reading.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Coin cell weather logger

    Wake once a minute to log temperature and humidity. The board sleeps the rest of the time and runs for months on a small battery.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, coin cell holder, memory chip.

  • Solar garden light meter

    Run the board from a small solar cell in the garden. It records how much light each bed gets through the day.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: small solar panel, light sensor, supercapacitor.

  • Step counter badge

    Count steps with a motion sensor clipped to a backpack. The segment display shows the daily total.

    Uses: Screen, Sips power. Add: motion sensor, coin cell holder.

  • Kitchen timer

    Turn a knob to set minutes on the segment display. A buzzer sounds once the time runs out.

    Uses: Screen. Add: rotary knob, buzzer.

  • Touch button doorbell

    Use the touch sensing pins as a doorbell pad. A chime plays through a small buzzer.

    Add: copper tape, buzzer.

  • USB volume knob

    Turn a knob to change your computer's volume. The board shows up as a USB device with no extra drivers.

    Add: rotary knob.

  • Bird box temperature watch

    Place a sensor inside a nesting box in the yard. The board logs the inside temperature for months on one battery.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: waterproof temperature sensor, battery holder, memory chip.

Expert ideas

  • Water meter reader

    Count pulses from a water meter sensor. The segment display shows how many liters your home used today.

    Uses: Screen, Sips power. Add: pulse sensor, battery holder.

  • Two-factor key fob

    Build a small key fob with an e-paper screen and an infrared link. It shows login codes for your computer.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: small e-paper screen, infrared LED, infrared sensor, coin cell holder.
