Overview

The STM32U3 is a line of microcontroller chips from STMicroelectronics. It is built to use as little power as possible. ST says it does almost twice the work per unit of power of its older U5 line. It suits makers and engineers who build battery or solar sensors.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Sensors that must run for months or years on a coin cell
  • Energy-harvesting projects with small solar cells
  • Always-on sound detection with a digital microphone
  • Learning modern Arm chips through Arduino, Zephyr, or ST's own tools

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth radio
  • First-time coders, as most guides assume embedded experience
  • Breadboard projects, as no cheap small breakout board was found

Specs in plain words

  • A 96 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 core is plenty fast for sensors, timers, and simple sound work.
  • 256 KB of memory on the U385 holds thousands of sensor readings at once.
  • 1 MB of flash storage fits large programs with room left for saved data.
  • A 1.6 microamp sleep mode lets a coin cell keep the chip waiting for a very long time.
  • The bigger U3C5 adds a signal processor for small sound and image models.
  • USB, CAN, and audio ports let it talk to computers, car-style networks, and microphones.

Chip values are for the STM32U385RG on the Nucleo-U385RG-Q with 1 MB flash and 256 KB SRAM. GPIO (82) is the maximum per the Zephyr page. The U3C5ZI on the Nucleo-U3C5ZI-Q has 2 MB flash, 640 KB SRAM, up to 114 GPIO, two CAN FD and the HSP accelerator. low_power_ua is the 1.6 uA Stop 3 figure with 8 KB SRAM kept. Wikipedia gives 110 nA for a lower power mode, which was not confirmed. The I2S entry stands for the SAI audio port. The PDM entry stands for the audio digital filter. Neither U3 Nucleo has USB host. accelerator is empty because the common U385 has no HSP. ST gives no TOPS figure for the HSP. The ADC channel count was not found.

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and button first steps

    Use the Arduino IDE to blink an LED and read the user button. It is a gentle way to meet the Nucleo board.

  • Door open alarm

    Sleep until a magnetic switch opens and then sound a buzzer. A small battery can last a very long time.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, buzzer.

Builder ideas

  • Coin cell weather logger

    Log temperature and humidity every few minutes to flash memory. The chip sips power and can run for a long time on a small coin cell.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: temperature and humidity sensor, coin cell holder.

  • Garden soil moisture tracker

    Check soil moisture a few times an hour and light an LED for dry soil. Long sleep periods keep the battery going all season.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • Clap or knock detector

    Listen through a digital microphone and flash a light at a sharp sound. It shows how always-on audio listening works.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone.

  • Simple tone player

    Play short tunes or alert beeps through the audio port. Students can code their own melodies note by note.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio amplifier board, small speaker.

  • Classroom power meter lab

    Measure how much current the board draws in run and sleep modes. Students see real numbers for each power setting.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: USB power meter.

  • Water meter pulse counter

    Count pulses from a water or electricity meter and store daily totals. The chip can sit by the meter for months.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: reed switch or light sensor, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Solar powered sensor node

    Run a light or temperature sensor from a small solar cell and a capacitor. It is a fun test of how little power the chip needs.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: small solar cell, supercapacitor, light sensor.

  • Step counter wearable

    Read a motion sensor and count steps through the day. The low run current suits a device worn on the wrist.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: motion sensor, small battery.

  • Bird song sound level logger

    Record how loud the dawn chorus gets each morning in a backyard. Save the sound levels to memory for later charts.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sips power. Add: digital microphone, battery pack.

  • Keyword spotter on the U3C5

    Train a tiny sound model and run it on the hardware signal processor of the bigger U3C5 board. It can light an LED for a chosen sound.

    Uses: Hears sound. Add: digital microphone.
