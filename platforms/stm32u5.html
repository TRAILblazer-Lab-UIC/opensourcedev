Overview

The STM32U5 is STMicroelectronics' low-power microcontroller line built around an Arm Cortex-M33 core. It sleeps on tiny amounts of power and adds strong security features. It also brings precise analog inputs, touch sensing, and a camera port. It suits makers building battery sensors, small screen gadgets, and secure devices, and it serves as the helper chip on the Arduino UNO Q.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Battery sensors that sleep for months
  • Precise measurements with the 14-bit analog input
  • Touch buttons and small color screen gadgets
  • Secure devices that guard passwords or keys
  • Learning MicroPython on a cheap board

Not great for

  • Wireless projects, as the chip has no radio of its own
  • Total beginners, as boards need firmware flashing or extra tools first
  • Heavy AI or video work
  • Shoppers who want a board from a big hobby store

Specs in plain words

  • The 160 MHz processor is about ten times faster than a classic Arduino UNO.
  • 786 KB of working memory holds thousands of sensor readings at once.
  • 2 MB of flash storage fits a large program with room for small pictures.
  • Standby draws only 0.3 microamps. A coin cell could keep it waiting for years.
  • The 14-bit analog input splits a voltage into about 16,000 steps for fine readings.
  • The WeAct Core Mini is about 5 by 2 centimeters, close to a stick of gum.

Chip values are for the STM32U575ZI and STM32U585, which share 2 MB flash and 786 KB SRAM (722 KB with ECC on). The U5A5 and U5G9 lines have up to 4 MB flash and about 3 MB SRAM, and some U5 parts add a NeoChrom GPU and an LCD controller. GPIO (136) is the maximum for the LQFP144 part per Zephyr. The ADC channel count was not found. Connectors, onboard parts and power are Nucleo-U575ZI-Q values. The WeAct Core Mini is 52.8 x 20.8 mm. low_power_ua is the 300 nA Standby figure. Shutdown is 110 nA. The I2S entry stands for the two SAI audio ports. The PDM entry stands for the multi-function digital filter.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • First blink in MicroPython

    Flash MicroPython onto a WeAct Core Mini and open Thonny. Type one line and the onboard LED turns on.

  • Button doorbell chime

    Wire a push button and a small buzzer. Each press plays a short tune.

    Add: push button, buzzer.

  • Desk thermometer

    Read a temperature sensor and print the value every few seconds. Watch the room warm up in the afternoon.

    Add: temperature sensor.

Builder ideas

  • Year-long soil logger

    Wake the board once an hour to measure soil moisture. It saves the reading to a memory card and sleeps on tiny power.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, microSD card module, battery pack.

  • Touch-sensitive art panel

    Paint shapes with conductive paint and wire them to the touch pins. Each shape lights a different LED color.

    Add: conductive paint, LED strip.

  • Precise science scale

    Read a load cell with the 14-bit analog input. The board shows weights to a fraction of a gram for lab work.

    Add: load cell, load cell amplifier, small screen.

  • USB music controller

    Turn knobs and buttons into a USB MIDI device. Music apps on a laptop respond to each twist.

    Add: knob potentiometers, push buttons.

  • Tiny weather display

    Show indoor temperature, humidity, and a small graph on a color screen. The graphics engine draws the chart quickly.

    Add: temperature and humidity sensor, small color screen.

  • Sound level badge

    Listen with a digital microphone and light a bar of LEDs by loudness. Wear it during band practice.

    Uses: Hears sound, Sips power. Add: digital microphone, LED bar, coin cell holder.

  • Talking reminder box

    Play short recorded messages through a speaker at set times. It reminds someone to take medicine or water plants.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker, amplifier board, microSD card module.

Expert ideas

  • Wildlife snapshot camera

    Connect a small camera module to the camera port. A motion sensor wakes the board to save a low-resolution photo.

    Uses: Camera, Sips power. Add: camera module, motion sensor, microSD card module, battery pack.

  • USB memory stick reader

    Plug a USB stick into the board and read a text file of settings. The board uses them to set up a sensor project.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: USB host adapter cable.

  • Secure door keypad

    Store a passcode in the secure part of the chip. A keypad and a small lock open only for the right code.

    Add: keypad, servo motor or small lock.

  • Model car network on CAN

    Link several boards with CAN transceivers inside a model car or robot. They share speed and sensor data over two wires.

    Add: CAN transceiver modules, twisted pair wire.

How open is it?

Wild Open

ST's HAL code and board support files are BSD-3-Clause, and open tools such as OpenOCD, pyOCD, and dfu-util can flash the boards. The Arduino UNO Q shares full CAD files under CC BY-SA 4.0, and WeAct shares a schematic with no license. The chip design stays closed.
