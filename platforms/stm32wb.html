Overview

The STM32WB is a wireless microcontroller line from STMicroelectronics. It has two cores. A Cortex-M4 runs your program and a Cortex-M0+ runs the radio. The radio speaks Bluetooth Low Energy, Thread, Zigbee, and Matter. It suits makers building Bluetooth sensors and smart home devices, and it is the brain of the Flipper Zero.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Bluetooth sensors that report to a phone
  • Zigbee, Thread, and Matter smart home gadgets
  • Battery devices that sleep on tiny power
  • Cheap wireless experiments with the WeAct board
  • MicroPython Bluetooth projects with no C code

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi radio
  • Total beginners, as the radio firmware setup can be confusing
  • Color screens or camera work
  • People who need a fully open radio stack

Specs in plain words

  • The 64 MHz main processor is about four times faster than a classic Arduino UNO.
  • The second core runs the radio. Your program keeps the main core to itself.
  • 256 KB of working memory holds a few thousand sensor readings at once.
  • 1 MB of flash storage fits a large program with the radio stack.
  • Standby draws about 0.6 microamps with the clock running. A coin cell can last a very long time.
  • The WeAct Core Board is about 5.6 by 2 centimeters, close to a stick of gum.

Chip values are for the STM32WB55RG/CG with 1 MB flash and 256 KB SRAM, as used on the Nucleo-WB55RG, WeAct board and Flipper Zero. The Zephyr page also says up to 320 KB SRAM including parity RAM. Wikipedia and CNX give 256 KB. Zephyr gives both 72 and 114 as the I/O maximum. 72 is shown. The ADC channel count was not confirmed. The WeAct board breaks out 10 analog inputs. Connectors, onboard parts and power are Nucleo-WB55RG values. It also has three user LEDs and a PCB antenna. low_power_ua is the 600 nA Standby figure with RTC and 32 KB RAM. The I2S entry stands for the SAI audio port. The PDM entry stands for the sigma-delta digital filters. The chip also drives a segment LCD of up to 8 x 40, which is not a TFT interface.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Bluetooth LED switch

    Run a MicroPython Bluetooth example on a Nucleo-WB55. Tap a button in a phone app to turn the LED on.

    Uses: Bluetooth.

  • Phone thermometer

    Read a temperature sensor and send the value over Bluetooth. A free phone app shows the reading.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Coin cell step counter

    Count steps with a motion sensor and save power between steps. The board runs for a long time on a coin cell.

    Uses: Sips power, Battery ready. Add: motion sensor, coin cell.

Builder ideas

  • Plant thirst alarm

    Check soil moisture a few times a day and send a Bluetooth alert to your phone. The board sleeps the rest of the time.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, coin cell.

  • Bluetooth game controller

    Wire buttons and a joystick to the board. A computer or tablet sees it as a wireless game pad.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: push buttons, joystick module, battery pack.

  • Classroom quiz buzzers

    Give each team a board with a big button. The first press reaches the teacher's laptop over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: large push buttons, coin cells.

  • Lost item finder tag

    Attach a small board to a backpack or keys. A phone shows how close the tag is by its signal strength.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: coin cell holder.

  • Bluetooth heart rate display

    Read a pulse sensor and send the beats to a phone app. Watch your heart rate climb during a jog.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: pulse sensor, battery pack.

  • Segment display clock

    Drive a simple segment LCD straight from the chip. The clock runs for months on a coin cell.

    Uses: Sips power, Battery ready. Add: segment LCD, coin cell.

  • Baby sound monitor

    Listen with a digital microphone and measure the loudness. The board sends a Bluetooth alert on loud crying.

    Uses: Hears sound, Bluetooth. Add: digital microphone.

Expert ideas

  • Zigbee door sensor

    Build a battery sensor for a door or window. It joins a Zigbee smart home hub and reports each opening.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power, Battery ready. Add: magnetic reed switch, coin cell holder, small case.

  • Thread mesh garden network

    Spread several boards around a garden to share soil readings. Each node passes messages along the mesh to a home hub.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensors, battery packs, weatherproof boxes.

  • Matter smart lamp

    Make a lamp that a phone smart home app can control. The board speaks Matter over Thread.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee. Add: LED strip, power supply.

  • Bluetooth audio chime

    Send a short tone to a speaker through the audio port. A phone triggers the chime over Bluetooth.

    Uses: Makes sound, Bluetooth. Add: small speaker, amplifier board.

  • Study the Flipper Zero firmware

    Read the open Flipper Zero firmware to see how a real gadget uses this chip. Then build a small app of your own for it.

    Add: Flipper Zero.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

ST's HAL code is BSD-3-Clause, and the app code builds with GCC and flashes with OpenOCD or pyOCD. The Bluetooth and Thread stacks run as closed binary firmware on the radio core, and the wireless middleware uses ST's own license. SparkFun shared full design files under CC BY-SA 4.0 for its retired MicroMod board, and the chip design stays closed.

Quick facts

Made bySTMicroelectronics
KindMicrocontrollers
Code you need*Beginner code
MicroPython builds and the STM32duinoBLE library let beginners copy and tweak short Bluetooth examples.
Ease of use*3 of 5
The radio core needs ST's wireless stack flashed through a special update service before Bluetooth works, and this step trips up many beginners.
Effort to get going*Light
A Nucleo board or a cheap WeAct board and a USB cable give a first result within an hour.
Docs quality*3 of 5
Zephyr, MicroPython and STM32duinoBLE document the boards, but ST pages could not be read and the radio setup is poorly explained for newcomers.
Community*Medium
The ST Cube repo has about 288 stars, and the Flipper Zero firmware gives the chip a large real-world codebase.
