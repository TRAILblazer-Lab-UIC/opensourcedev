STM32WBA

STMicroelectronics

A low-power STM32 chip with Bluetooth, Thread, Zigbee, and Matter radio support.

Overview

The STM32WBA is a wireless microcontroller line from STMicroelectronics. One Arm core runs both your program and the radio. The radio speaks Bluetooth Low Energy, Thread, Zigbee, and Matter. It suits makers who build smart home gadgets and battery sensors.

How the scores work

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Bluetooth sensors that talk to a phone
  • Matter, Thread, and Zigbee smart home devices
  • Battery gadgets that must sleep for long stretches
  • Secure products that need encryption and secure boot

Not great for

  • Wi-Fi projects, as the chip has no Wi-Fi radio
  • Tight budgets, as official boards start around 50 dollars
  • First-time coders, as the radio tools assume embedded experience
  • Fully open builds, as the radio code ships as closed files

Specs in plain words

  • A 100 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 core runs your program and the radio together.
  • 128 KB of memory on the WBA55 is enough for a sensor program plus its wireless stack.
  • 1 MB of flash storage holds your program and the radio software.
  • A 140 nanoamp standby mode lets a coin cell last a very long time.
  • The 2.4 GHz radio reaches across a typical home and can join a mesh.
  • The newer WBA65 has 2 MB of storage and 512 KB of memory for bigger programs.

Chip values are for the STM32WBA55CG with 1 MB flash and 128 KB SRAM, as used on the NUCLEO-WBA55CG and the STM32duino variant. The STM32WBA65RI has 2 MB flash, 512 KB SRAM, up to 86 I/Os, four UARTs, three SPIs and four I2C ports. The WBA55 I/O count and ADC channel count were not read from an ST datasheet. The ADC is 12-bit at 2.5 Msps on both lines. low_power_ua is the 140 nA Standby figure for the WBA55 from the Zephyr board page. Connectors and onboard parts are Nucleo values with three user LEDs, three user buttons and a PCB antenna. The ST eStore lists USB-C on the NUCLEO-WBA55CG. By contrast, the Zephyr page lists Micro-B. The eStore lists Micro-B for the NUCLEO-WBA52CG.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and button warm-up

    Use the Arduino IDE to blink the board's LEDs and read its three buttons. It is a calm first step before any radio work.

Builder ideas

  • Bluetooth temperature beacon

    Read a temperature sensor and broadcast the value over Bluetooth. Any phone app that scans Bluetooth can show it.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: temperature sensor.

  • Bluetooth keyboard gadget

    Turn a few buttons into a tiny Bluetooth keyboard for slides or shortcuts. ST publishes a keyboard example to start from.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: push buttons.

  • Plant thirst alert

    Check soil moisture and send a Bluetooth alert to your phone for dry soil. A small battery can last a long time.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: soil moisture sensor, battery pack.

  • Phone controlled fan

    Switch a small fan on and off from a phone over Bluetooth. ST shares a fan control demo as a model.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: small fan, motor driver board.

  • Heart rate relay

    Read a pulse sensor and send each beat to a phone over Bluetooth. It makes a simple health monitor for a science class.

    Uses: Bluetooth. Add: pulse sensor.

  • Mailbox notifier

    Sense the mailbox door and send a Zigbee or Bluetooth message to the house. A long sleep keeps the battery alive for months.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: tilt switch, battery pack.

Expert ideas

  • Matter smart light switch

    Build a wall switch that joins a Matter smart home over Thread. It can turn a smart lamp on and off.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging. Add: push button, small enclosure.

  • Zigbee door sensor

    Report a door opening to a Zigbee home hub. The chip sleeps between events and saves battery.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Sips power. Add: magnetic door switch, battery pack.

  • Thread mesh garden network

    Spread several sensor nodes around a garden and link them in a Thread mesh. Each node passes messages along to the next.

    Uses: Thread or Zigbee, Mesh messaging, Sips power. Add: extra boards, temperature sensors, battery packs.

  • Classroom attendance tag

    Give each student a small tag that a hub can detect over Bluetooth. It shows how wireless presence sensing works.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sips power. Add: coin cell holder, extra boards.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The STM32Cube drivers are open under BSD-3-Clause, and Zephyr support is under Apache-2.0. The Bluetooth, Zigbee, and link layer code uses ST's own license and ships as closed files. A community breakout board has KiCad files under the MIT license, but the chip design is closed.

Hardware license: MIT (community STM32WBA55CGU Breakout KiCad files), ST Nucleo and Discovery CAD license not verified · Software license: BSD-3-Clause (HAL, BSPs), Apache-2.0 (CMSIS, Zephyr), ST SLA (STM32WPAN BLE, link layer, Zigbee and examples)
