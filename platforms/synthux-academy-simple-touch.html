Synthux Academy Simple Touch

Synthux Academy (Stichting PTM Academy)

A touch-pad synth kit that becomes a new instrument with each firmware swap.

Overview

The Synthux Touch 2 is a small synth from Synthux Academy, a non-profit in Rotterdam. It uses an Electrosmith Daisy Seed audio board as its brain. You play it by touching twelve pads and turning knobs and faders. A new firmware file and a new faceplate turn it into a different instrument.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Musicians who want several instruments in one box
  • Learners who want to study synth programming with video courses
  • Live looping, drums, bass, strings, and effects
  • Makers who enjoy building a solder kit

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as it costs about 300 to 400 dollars
  • Eurorack modular systems, as strong signals need to be turned down first
  • Fully open hardware fans, as the main board files are unpublished

Specs in plain words

  • A 480 MHz processor is fast enough to run rich synth sounds and effects at once.
  • 64 MB of memory holds long audio loops and samples.
  • 24-bit, 96 kHz stereo audio means studio-grade sound in and out.
  • Twelve touch pads act like keys you play with a light touch.
  • Six knobs, two faders, and two switches let you shape the sound by hand.

Compute specs come from the Daisy Seed docs. psram_kb holds the 64 MB SDRAM, and flash_kb is the 8 MB QSPI flash. The MPR121 touch controller is inferred from the official firmware headers, which reference it, and from the Synthmas 2023 brief. The jack type, USB connector and power input were not stated on fetched pages. The onboard field lists buttons for the two switches. Knobs and faders have no allowed value.

Starter ideas

  • Play the bass instrument

    Load the ready-made bass firmware and play deep lines on the touch pads. It is a fun first hour with no code at all.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones.

  • Live looper jam

    Load the looper firmware and stack short loops on top of each other. It turns one player into a small band.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone or instrument cable.

  • Drum pad beat maker

    Tap out beats with the drum firmware and shape them with the faders. Kids can make rhythms in minutes.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: small speaker.

  • Guitar effects box

    Run a guitar or keyboard through the effects firmware. Add echo, filters, and other sound changes live.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: instrument cable.

  • Design your own faceplate

    Draw a custom faceplate on the blank plate for a new instrument. Label each pad and knob for your own sound.

    Add: printed faceplate.

  • String drone instrument

    Play the TouchString firmware for plucked and bowed string sounds. Hold pads to build a slow evolving drone.

    Uses: Makes sound.

Builder ideas

  • Sample slicer

    Chop a recorded sound into pieces and trigger each slice from a pad. It is a good way to learn how samplers work.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound.

  • Build the kit yourself

    Solder the DIY kit and then follow the Academy video lessons. You end up with an instrument you understand inside and out.

    Add: soldering iron, solder.

  • Your first synth sketch

    Change a sound in the open Arduino instrument code and upload it over USB. Hear how one number changes the tone.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Classroom sound science lab

    Show students how pitch, volume, and filters change a wave. Pair the synth with a free oscilloscope app on a laptop.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: audio cable, laptop.

  • Ambient nature soundscape

    Mix recorded bird song or rain with soft drones through the effects firmware. It makes a calm backdrop for a room.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: audio player.

Expert ideas
