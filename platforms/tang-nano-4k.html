Overview

The Tang Nano 4K is a small FPGA board from Sipeed. An FPGA is a chip whose wiring you design yourself with code. This one also hides a small Arm processor inside the same chip. It suits hobbyists and students who want to learn hardware design with video and camera projects.

  • Storing files, as it has no microSD slot

Specs in plain words

  • 4,608 logic units give room for small games, video patterns, and counters.
  • A built-in Arm Cortex-M3 core can run C programs next to your logic.
  • 8 MB of fast memory can hold a few video frames.
  • 4 MB of flash storage keeps your design after power off.
  • 44 input and output pins let you connect lights, buttons, and sensors.
  • At about 60 by 23 millimeters, it is about the size of a stick of gum.

cores counts the single hard Cortex-M3. Its clock speed was not stated on the wiki, Gowin's page or CNX Software. Make's board guide lists 100 MHz, which is unverified. ram_kb is the 180 Kbit block SRAM. psram_kb is the 64 Mbit in-package PSRAM. flash_kb is the 32 Mbit NOR flash, per Gowin and CNX Software. gpio is the up to 44 user I/O stated by Sipeed and Gowin. The HDMI pins are shared with header I/O, and the wiki warns external pull-ups can affect them. I/O is broken out on two 22-pin headers. The FPGA has 2 PLLs and no ADC. Size is from the wiki.

Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Blink an LED in Verilog

    Write a tiny hardware design that blinks the onboard LED. It shows how chip design differs from normal code.

  • Button controlled light

    Wire the two buttons to the LED through logic you design. Try making AND and OR gates by hand.

  • HDMI color bars

    Send a color bar test pattern to a TV or monitor. It teaches how screens are drawn line by line.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Live camera to HDMI

    Stream video from the small camera sensor straight to a monitor. Sipeed ships an example to start from.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: OV2640 camera sensor, HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Digital logic class lab

    Build counters, timers, and shift registers on the board. Students see their logic run in real hardware.

    Add: LEDs, breadboard, jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Bouncing ball game on a TV

    Draw a ball and paddle on a monitor and move the paddle with the buttons. It is a classic first video game in hardware.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Motion detector camera

    Compare camera frames and highlight pixels that change. Watch a bird feeder for visitors on a screen.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: OV2640 camera sensor, HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Retro text terminal

    Show scrolling text on a monitor from a font you store in the chip. It works like an old computer screen.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Mix the Arm core with logic

    Run a C program on the built-in Cortex-M3 that talks to your own logic blocks. It needs the free Gowin IDE.

  • Camera color tracker

    Find a bright colored ball in the camera image and draw a box around it on screen. It is a hands-on intro to machine vision.

    Uses: Camera, Screen. Add: OV2640 camera sensor, HDMI cable, monitor.
