Overview

Thymio II is a small educational robot designed at EPFL with the ECAL art school. The non-profit Mobsya makes it. It comes ready to roll with dozens of sensors and lights built in. Children can play with its built-in behaviors right away and then program it with blocks or text.

Specs in plain words

  • Seven proximity sensors face forward and back. They help Thymio sense walls, hands and other robots about a hand's width away.
  • Two ground sensors look down at the floor. They let it follow a dark line or spot a table edge.
  • About 39 lights glow in many colors. Your code can turn each one on or off.
  • The 16 KB of working memory is tiny. It holds simple programs for driving, sensing and lights.
  • A rechargeable battery charges over micro-USB. The robot rolls around with no cable.
  • The body is about 11 centimeters across, roughly the size of a sandwich.

MCU, RAM and flash come from the official BOM (PIC24FJ128GB106, 128 KB flash, 16 KB SRAM, 8 MHz crystal). The core clock was not confirmed on a fetched page. The accelerometer is an ST LIS2DE12 per the BOM. The wireless version adds a radio module and USB dongle. The radio type was not confirmed and is left out of radios. Battery, size, 270 g weight and speed figures come from retailer search snippets. The API doc gives about 20 cm/s at full motor speed. The IR proximity sensors also do robot-to-robot messaging over about 15 cm.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Explore the built-in moods

    Press the arrow buttons to pick a ready-made behavior. Watch Thymio follow your hand or flee from it with no computer at all.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Line follower race track

    Draw a thick black line on white paper. Program Thymio with blocks to follow the track and race your friends.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: large paper, black marker or tape.

  • Clap to go robot

    Clap your hands and Thymio drives forward. Clap again and it stops.

    Uses: Hears sound, Drives motors.

  • Obstacle dodging explorer

    Program Thymio to turn away from walls and boxes. Build a cardboard maze and test whether it finds the exit.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: cardboard boxes.

  • Musical robot

    Make each button play a different note through the speaker. Compose a short tune and share it with the class.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Mood light color changer

    Touch the buttons to change the color of the top and bottom lights. Use it as a gentle night light.

    Uses: Battery ready.

  • Tilt alarm guard

    Use the accelerometer to sense a bump or lift. Thymio plays an alarm on any pickup.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Makes sound.

Builder ideas

  • Classroom temperature logger

    Read the temperature sensor every few minutes. Show warm and cool times with the colored lights.

    Uses: Sensors built in. Add: microSD card.

  • LEGO bulldozer

    Snap LEGO bricks onto the attachment points to build a plow. Program Thymio to push small blocks into a corner.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in. Add: LEGO bricks.

  • Garden path patrol

    Draw a path on a board around potted plants. Thymio follows the line and checks on each pot.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: large board, black tape.

  • Robot dance party

    Program a dance with spins, lights and sounds. Line up several robots and start them together.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

  • Robot messenger game

    Two Thymios send short signals to each other with their infrared sensors. Build a game of robot tag.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: second Thymio robot.

  • Table edge safety bot

    Use the ground sensors to spot the edge of a table. Thymio stops and backs away before it falls.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

Expert ideas

  • Shepherd and sheep simulation

    Write Aseba code to make one robot herd others into a pen. It shows how animals move in groups.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: several Thymio robots, cardboard pen.

How open is it?

Open Board

The electronics and shell designs are published under the CC BY-SA 3.0 license. The Aseba language and Thymio Suite are open under LGPL-3.0. Building the robot firmware needs closed Microchip compiler tools, and the chip itself is closed.
