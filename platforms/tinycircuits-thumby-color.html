Overview

Thumby Color is a tiny handheld game console from TinyCircuits in Ohio. It has a color screen, nine buttons, a buzzer, a rumble motor, and a built-in battery. You write games in MicroPython. A game engine handles sprites, physics, and sound. It suits kids, teachers, and anyone who wants a fun first step into coding.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning to code by making real games
  • Playing over 100 free community games
  • Classroom game jams and coding clubs
  • Gifts for curious kids and teens

Not great for

  • Add-on sensors or wires, with no pin headers to plug into
  • Long play sessions, with about 2 hours per charge
  • Wireless projects, with no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth on board
  • People who want open hardware files, which are not published yet

Specs in plain words

  • The RP2350 chip runs at 150 MHz and can speed up for busy games.
  • 520 KB of working memory is plenty for small sprite games.
  • 13 MB of storage holds dozens of games and their art.
  • The 0.85 inch screen is smaller than a postage stamp but shows full color.
  • The 110 mAh battery gives about 2 hours of play and charges in about 2 hours.
  • The whole console is about 5 cm long, about the size of a matchbox.

Flash is split into 1 MB firmware, 2 MB game scratch and 13 MB filesystem. The docs site lists the clock as 150 to 300 MHz. As a result, games can raise it. The screen is a 0.85 inch 128 x 128 16-bit backlit TFT. Audio is a 4 kHz magnetic buzzer, and haptics come from a small DC vibration motor. No user GPIO headers were described. As a result, gpio and adc_channels are null. The FAQ says a full charge takes about 2 hours.

Starter ideas

  • Change a game's colors

    Open a community game in the browser editor and swap its sprite colors. You see your change on the screen in seconds.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Bouncing ball first game

    Make a ball bounce around the tiny screen and change direction with the d-pad. It teaches loops and movement.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Pocket reaction timer

    Flash a color and time how fast a player presses A. Buzz the winner with the buzzer.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Homework flash cards

    Build a quiz app with spelling words or math facts. Kids can practice on the bus.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Tiny music box

    Program short tunes on the buzzer and pick songs with the buttons. It is a first lesson in notes and timing.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Nature walk tally counter

    Count birds, trees, or bugs you spot with different buttons. Review the totals on screen at home.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

Builder ideas

  • Pixel art sketchpad

    Move a cursor with the d-pad and paint pixels on the 128 by 128 screen. Save your drawings to the onboard storage.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Breathing exercise pacer

    Grow and shrink a colored circle to guide slow breathing. A gentle buzz marks each breath.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Platformer with physics

    Use the game engine's sprites and 2D physics to make a jumping game. Add levels and a score counter.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Club game jam console

    Run a game jam where each student makes a tiny game. Load the winners onto every console in the room.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Port a classic Thumby game

    Bring an original Thumby game over with the legacy library and add color art. Share it in the community games collection.

    Uses: Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Simple 3D maze

    Use the engine's simple 3D features to build a first-person maze. Players turn with the shoulder buttons.

    Uses: Screen.

How open is it?

Openness level

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsFully open

The game engine, the MicroPython fork, and the docs site are open under GPL-3.0. They build with the open Pico SDK and GCC. The Thumby Color schematics and board files are not published, and the RP2350 chip is closed.
