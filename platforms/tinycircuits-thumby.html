Overview

Thumby is a tiny game console about the size of a keychain. It uses the Raspberry Pi RP2040 chip with a small black and white screen, a speaker and a battery. Kids, teachers, and hobbyists use it to learn game coding. You can code it with blocks, MicroPython, or Arduino C++ in a free web editor.

Not great for

  • Projects that need sensors or extra pins
  • Detailed graphics on its tiny one-color screen
  • Long play sessions on one charge
  • Games that need lots of storage

Specs in plain words

  • The 72 by 40 pixel screen is small, close to a single line of a phone text.
  • Two megabytes of storage hold a handful of small games at once.
  • The 40 mAh battery gives about two hours of play and charges in about an hour.
  • The case is about 30 by 18 millimeters, smaller than most thumbs.
  • The RP2040 chip runs MicroPython with room for simple game logic.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorRaspberry Pi RP2040 (dual Arm Cortex-M0+), 2 cores at 133 MHz
Memory264 KB
Storage2 MB
Ways to connect partsUSB device
ConnectorsMicro USB
Built inscreen, speaker, buttons, battery charger
Power5 V over micro USB, or the built-in 40 mAh LiPo battery
Size29.5 x 18 x 8.5 mm

Core count, 133 MHz clock and 264 KB SRAM are RP2040 chip values and were not stated on the Thumby pages. Flash is the 2 MB chip on the board, and the FAQ says storage cannot be expanded. The screen is a 72 x 40 monochrome OLED. No user GPIO headers are exposed. As a result, gpio and adc_channels are null. The micro USB port is used for programming, charging and the multiplayer link cable. The FAQ says a full charge takes about 1 hour and an LED shows charging.

Starter ideas

  • Your first dodge game

    Make a little ship that dodges falling rocks. Build it from blocks in the web editor.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Pocket pet

    Raise a pixel pet that gets hungry and sleepy. Press buttons to feed it and play.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready.

  • Reaction speed test

    Wait for the screen to flash and press a button fast. Compare scores with friends.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Beep melody maker

    Assign a note to each button. Play simple tunes on the tiny speaker.

    Uses: Makes sound.

  • Study flash cards

    Show a spelling word or math fact on the screen. Press a button to see the answer.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Homework timer

    Set a study timer with the buttons. The speaker beeps once the time is up.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

Builder ideas

  • Two-player pong

    Link two consoles with a cable. Each player controls a paddle on their own screen.

    Uses: Screen. Add: link cable, second console.

  • Tiny maze explorer

    Draw a maze and guide a dot through it with the d-pad. Add levels as you learn.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Pixel art sketchpad

    Move a cursor with the d-pad and turn pixels on or off. Make tiny drawings to show friends.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Rhythm tap game

    Notes scroll across the screen in time with beeps. Tap the button on the beat to score.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound.

  • Share on the arcade

    Polish a game and share it on the Thumby Arcade. Other players can load it onto their own console.

    Uses: Screen.

Expert ideas

    Write a side-scrolling jumper game in Arduino C++. Learn how real game loops and sprites work.

How open is it?

Wild Open

TinyCircuits shares the schematic, board files, and case STL files on GitHub. The README names CC BY-SA 3.0, but the repo also holds a GPL-3.0 license file. The Code Editor is GPL-3.0, and the RP2040 chip runs with no closed blobs, though the chip design itself is closed.
