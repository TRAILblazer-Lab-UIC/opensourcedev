Overview

The TinyFPGA BX is a small FPGA board that plugs straight into a breadboard. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become your own digital circuit. The BX uses a Lattice iCE40 chip that works with a fully open toolchain. It suits students and makers who want to learn Verilog or run soft processors.

Specs in plain words

  • 7,680 logic cells are room for small processors, counters, and many simple circuits at once.
  • 128 Kbit of block RAM is about 16 KB, enough for small buffers and tiny programs.
  • 41 user pins let you wire up lots of buttons, LEDs, and other parts.
  • 8 Mbit of flash stores the bootloader and your design.
  • The 16 MHz clock can be sped up with the built-in PLL.
  • The board is 36 by 18 mm, about the size of a stick of gum.
Full spec sheet
ProcessorLattice iCE40LP8K-CM81 FPGA (no hard CPU core)
Memory16 KB
Storage1 MB
Pins41 pins
Ways to connect partsUSB device, SPI
ConnectorsMicro USB, Pin headers
Power5 V over micro USB, or Vin pin. Onboard 3.3 V (300 mA) and 1.2 V (150 mA) LDOs
Size36 x 18 mm

Clock is the onboard 16 MHz MEMS oscillator. The PLL can make faster clocks. ram_kb is the 128 Kbit of block RAM. flash_kb is the 8 Mbit SPI flash shared by the bootloader and the user bitstream. gpio is the 41 user I/O pins from the README. The tinyfpga.com table splits them into 31 dedicated and 10 shared pins. USB is implemented in FPGA logic by the bootloader. As a result, user designs that want USB must include a soft USB core. The board has a power LED, a bootloader LED and a user LED. It has a reset button that returns to the bootloader.

Things you could build

10 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink the user LED

    Load the official blink template and change the blink pattern. It is the hello world of hardware design.

Builder ideas

  • Breadboard logic gate lab

    Wire buttons and LEDs on a breadboard and design AND, OR, and XOR gates in Verilog. Students see logic turn into real lights.

    Add: breadboard, push buttons, LEDs, resistors.

  • Seven segment counter

    Count button presses on a number display. It teaches counters, clocks, and button debouncing.

    Add: seven segment display, push button, breadboard.

  • Reaction time game

    Light a random LED and time how fast a player hits the button. Show the score on a number display.

    Add: LEDs, push button, seven segment display.

  • Light show for art

    Drive a row of LEDs with patterns you design in logic. Each pattern runs at exact speeds with no flicker.

    Add: LEDs, resistors, breadboard.

  • Simple tone generator

    Make square-wave notes on a small speaker from a design you write. Add buttons to play a tiny keyboard.

    Add: small speaker, push buttons, transistor.

  • Servo sweep controller

    Generate the timed pulses that move a servo arm. It shows how precise hardware timing works.

    Add: servo motor, separate 5 V supply.

Expert ideas

  • Serial message sender

    Build a serial transmitter in Verilog and read its message on your computer. Use a small USB serial adapter to listen.

    Add: USB serial adapter.

  • Run a RISC-V soft CPU

    Load the PicoSoC example and run a small RISC-V processor inside the FPGA. You can then write C programs for a CPU you built.

  • VGA pattern display

    Design a circuit that draws color bars on an old VGA monitor. It is a classic lesson in video timing.

    Add: VGA connector breakout, resistors, VGA monitor.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 3 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The KiCad schematic and board layout are published under CERN OHL v1.2. The USB bootloader is open under Apache-2.0, and the whole build flow uses open tools. The Lattice iCE40 chip itself is closed, though Project IceStorm documents its bitstream.
