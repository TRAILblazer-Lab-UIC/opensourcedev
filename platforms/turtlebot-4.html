TurtleBot 4

Clearpath Robotics with Open Robotics and iRobot

A ready-built research robot that maps rooms and drives itself with ROS 2.

Field marks: TurtleBot 4 stacks a Raspberry Pi, a spinning lidar, and an OAK-D camera on an iRobot Create 3 base, then asks a price north of 1,800 dollars.

Overview

TurtleBot 4 is the reference robot for learning ROS 2, the popular open robot software. Clearpath Robotics built it with Open Robotics and iRobot. It sits on an iRobot Create 3 base and carries a Raspberry Pi 4, a spinning lidar and a depth camera. Universities and research labs use it to teach mapping and self-driving.

Specs in plain words

  • A Raspberry Pi 4 with 4 GB of memory is the robot's brain. It runs Linux and ROS 2 much like a small desktop computer.
  • The lidar spins and measures distances all around up to 12 meters away. That data becomes a floor map.
  • The OAK-D depth camera sees in 3D. It can judge how far away a person or object is.
  • The robot can carry 9 kilograms, about the weight of a full laundry basket.
  • The battery lasts about 2.5 to 4 hours. The robot can drive back to its dock to charge.
  • The Standard model is about 35 centimeters tall and wide, roughly the size of a small trash can.

Specs describe the Standard model. The Lite is 192 mm tall, weighs 3270 g against 3945 g, has no UI board display or user buttons, and has 2 USB 2.0 plus 2 USB 3.0 Type-A ports. The Standard has 2 USB 2.0 Type-A, 1 USB 3.0 Type-A and 4 USB 3.0 Type-C ports on the UI board. Raspberry Pi lists the BCM2711 at 1.8 GHz. Early boards ran at 1.5 GHz. The Pi boots from a microSD card. As a result, flash_kb is null. The OAK-D camera's Myriad X VPU was not checked on a fetched page. Ai_tops left null. Max speed is 0.31 m/s in safe mode and 0.46 m/s without. Payload 9 kg. The 2D lidar is not in the onboard vocabulary.

Things you could build

Builder ideas

  • Drive it from a laptop

    Use the keyboard teleop example to steer the robot around a room. It is the first step for every TurtleBot owner.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Map your home

    Drive the robot through each room. Its lidar builds a floor map along the way. Save the map for later trips.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

  • Self-driving room tour

    Click a spot on the saved map and the robot plans a route there on its own. It steers around chairs and feet.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Try it in the simulator first

    Run the free Gazebo simulation on a computer. A class can practice mapping and driving before touching the real robot.

    Uses: Runs Linux.

  • Auto-docking night routine

    Program the robot to finish a patrol and return to its charging dock. It stays ready for the next morning.

    Uses: Battery ready, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Library book delivery

    Load a book on the top plate and send the robot to a desk on the map. Students learn about delivery robots.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux, Wi-Fi.

  • Status messages on the screen

    Show the robot's battery level and current task on the small display. Use the buttons to start a patrol.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux.

  • Bump and cliff science lab

    Log every bumper hit and cliff warning during a drive. Students graph how the base senses its world.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Runs Linux.

  • Phone remote over Bluetooth

    Pair a game controller or phone app over Bluetooth to drive the robot. Visitors can take a turn at the wheel.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors. Add: Bluetooth game controller.

Expert ideas

  • Person follower

    Use the depth camera to spot a person and follow at a safe distance. It can carry a small basket behind you.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small basket.

  • Hallway security patrol

    Send the robot along a set route at night and stream camera video to a laptop. You can check on a lab or office from afar.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Object finder game

    Train the camera to spot a colored ball. The robot searches the room and stops beside it.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: colored ball.

  • Greenhouse plant checker

    Map the rows of a greenhouse and visit each plant on a schedule. The camera takes photos to track growth.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors, Battery ready.

  • Air quality rover

    Plug a small sensor into a USB port and log readings on each trip. The map shows which rooms feel stuffy.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: USB air quality sensor.

  • Robot tag for a class

    Two robots share their map positions over Wi-Fi. One robot chases and the other runs away.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: second TurtleBot.

  • Gesture-controlled robot

    Wave a hand in front of the depth camera to start, stop or turn the robot. It makes a fun museum demo.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Robot arm add-on

    Bolt a small robot arm to the top plate and wire it to a USB port. The robot can drive to a cup and pick it up.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: small robot arm.
