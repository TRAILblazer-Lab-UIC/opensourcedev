Overview

TurtleBot3 is a small mobile robot kit from ROBOTIS. It is built to teach ROS, the robot software many labs use. A Raspberry Pi 4 runs Linux, and a spinning laser sensor sees the room in every direction. It suits college classes, robot clubs, and makers who want to learn real robot skills.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning ROS 2 with a robot many teachers already use
  • Building maps of a room and driving through them on autopilot
  • Classes that mix one real robot with free simulation on laptops
  • Track races and line following with the camera kit
  • Research projects in navigation and robot learning

Not great for

  • People who want a quick toy robot to play with
  • Tight budgets, with the smaller kit at around 780 US dollars
  • Outdoor rough ground, as the wheels are small and the robot is low
  • Anyone without a second computer running Ubuntu

Specs in plain words

  • The Raspberry Pi 4 with 4 GB of memory works like a small desktop computer riding on the robot.
  • The laser sensor spins all the way around. It measures the distance to walls and furniture many times each second.
  • The OpenCR board is a second, smaller brain. It runs the motors, reads the motion sensor, and can be coded in the Arduino IDE.
  • The smaller Burger kit is about the size of a shoebox standing on end and weighs about one kilogram.
  • A battery charge lasts about two to two and a half hours of driving.
  • The bigger Waffle Pi kit adds a camera and stronger motors and costs about 1,900 US dollars.

Cores, clock, RAM and interfaces describe the Raspberry Pi 4 main computer. The 1800 MHz clock is the Pi 4 value used on this catalog's Raspberry Pi 4 card. Older Pi 4 boards run at 1500 MHz. RAM is 4 GB for the current ROBOTIS US Burger kit. Waffle Pi kits ship with 2 GB or 4 GB. The Pi boots from microSD. As a result, flash_kb is null. OpenCR 1.0 adds an STM32F746 Cortex-M7 at 216 MHz, an ICM-20648 6-axis IMU, TTL and RS485 DYNAMIXEL ports, two UARTs, CAN and 32 Arduino-style extension pins. OpenCR measures 105 x 75 mm. Size is the Burger (138 x 178 x 192 mm, 1 kg). Waffle Pi is 281 x 306 x 141 mm and 1.8 kg. Burger runs about 2.5 hours and tops out at 0.22 m/s. Waffle Pi runs about 2 hours at up to 0.26 m/s. Only the Waffle Pi has a camera (Raspberry Pi Camera v2.1). The features page calls the IMU 9-axis but the spec table lists only gyro and accelerometer.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Drive it from a keyboard

    Steer the robot around the room with arrow keys on your laptop. It is the first test every new owner runs.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Practice robot in a simulator

    Run a virtual TurtleBot3 on a computer with no hardware at all. A class can share one real robot and many virtual ones.

  • Robot dance party

    Program a short routine of spins and wiggles to music. Two robots can perform side by side.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: speaker.

Builder ideas

  • Map your house

    Let the robot roll through each room. The laser scans the walls as it goes. You get a floor plan on your screen.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

  • Self-driving hallway trip

    Click a spot on the saved map. The robot plans a path and drives there on its own and dodges chairs.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Follow the line

    Lay colored tape on the floor. The camera watches the tape and the robot steers along it.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: colored floor tape.

  • Hallway patrol camera

    Send the robot on a loop through a building after hours. It streams camera video to a laptop on the same network.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Snack delivery robot

    Place a small tray on top and send it between two desks. The bigger kit can carry a heavy load.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small tray.

  • Tilt and bump detector

    Use the motion sensor to notice a bump or a tip. The robot stops and backs away by itself.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors.

  • Science fair room survey

    Measure how big each room is from the saved map. Compare the robot result to a tape measure.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: tape measure.

Expert ideas

  • Mini road race track

    Build a small track with lanes, signs, and a traffic light. The robot follows the lanes and obeys the signals.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: tape for lane lines, printed road signs.

  • Teach it with trial and error

    Train the robot to reach a goal by rewarding good moves. Students watch it get smarter over many tries.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Garden path explorer

    Map a flat patio or greenhouse aisle. Add a soil or air sensor and log readings at each stop.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux, Battery ready. Add: air temperature sensor.

  • Custom motor firmware

    Open the motor control board in the Arduino IDE. Write your own code for the wheels and lights.

    Uses: Drives motors. Add: LED strip.

  • Web dashboard for the robot

    Show battery level and position on a web page. Anyone on the network can watch the robot work.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wi-Fi, Battery ready.

The main software is open under the Apache-2.0 license. ROBOTIS publishes the chassis 3D models and the OpenCR board schematic, Gerbers and parts list. No hardware license is named for those files. The Raspberry Pi boot firmware and the laser sensor firmware stay closed.
