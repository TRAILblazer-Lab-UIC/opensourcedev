Overview

The Ubertooth One is an open 2.4 GHz radio board from Great Scott Gadgets. It plugs into a computer's USB-A port and quietly listens to Bluetooth and Bluetooth Low Energy traffic. Security researchers used it for years to study how wireless gadgets talk. Great Scott Gadgets retired it in December 2022, and most new units today are clones built from the open design.

Not great for

  • Using as a normal Bluetooth adapter for headphones or keyboards
  • Beginners who have never used a terminal
  • Buyers who want a genuine unit with maker support
  • Radio work outside the 2.4 GHz band

Specs in plain words

  • The 2.4 GHz radio covers the same band as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and many toys.
  • Its transmit power and hearing range match a Class 1 Bluetooth device, which reaches farther than a typical phone.
  • An Arm Cortex-M3 chip runs the firmware and talks to your computer over USB.
  • The RP-SMA port lets you swap the included antenna for a bigger one.
  • The board is about 62 by 19 millimeters, close to the size of a pack of gum.

Official docs name the MCU only as LPC175x. As a result, clock, SRAM and flash are left null. The CC2400 is a 2.4 GHz transceiver used to sniff and inject Bluetooth packets. It is not a full Bluetooth controller. The radios field marks bt_classic and ble because that is what it monitors. It cannot act as a normal Bluetooth host adapter. Interfaces lists the ISP serial header as uart and the Cortex Debug header as swd_jtag. Size from SparkFun and Core Electronics.
