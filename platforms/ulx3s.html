ULX3S

Radiona.org (Zagreb Makerspace) with EMARD and FER University of Zagreb

A fully open FPGA board with Wi-Fi, video, audio, and plenty of memory.

Field marks: Look for a credit card sized board with a long row of buttons, a sprinkle of eleven LEDs and an ESP32 module riding along, often seen running old arcade games.

Overview

The ULX3S is an FPGA board built around a Lattice ECP5 chip. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. The board was made for a university logic course in Zagreb. It packs video out, an audio jack, analog inputs, lots of memory, and an ESP32 for Wi-Fi. It suits students, retro gaming fans, and makers who want a big, fully open FPGA.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Running retro computer and arcade game cores on a TV
  • Teaching digital logic with fully open tools
  • Building soft RISC-V computers with room to grow
  • Video and audio projects that need lots of memory

Not great for

  • Complete beginners, as most projects need a hardware language
  • Tight budgets, as it costs well over 100 US dollars
  • Quick setup, as the tools take some time to install

Specs in plain words

  • 12K to 84K logic units is enough for a whole retro computer on the larger versions.
  • 32 MB of memory can hold many screens of pictures or a large soft processor program.
  • 56 pins give lots of room for buttons, sensors and add-on boards.
  • Eight analog inputs read changing voltages, such as a knob or a light sensor.
  • 5 microamps in standby is a tiny trickle of power, good for long battery life.
  • The board is about 94 by 51 millimeters, close to a credit card.

ram_kb is the 32 MB 16-bit SDRAM (MT48LC32M16, 166 MHz) on current v3.1.x boards. Older docs list 8 to 64 MB options. Quad-SPI flash is 4 to 16 MB depending on build. As a result, flash_kb is left null. The video port is GPDI, electrically LVDS and compatible with most HDMI monitors. It is mapped to hdmi here. The OLED is an optional 0.96 inch SSD1331 SPI display on a header placeholder. Two micro USB ports, one to an FTDI FT231XS for JTAG and serial, one direct to the FPGA. The 25 MHz crystal is the main clock. The ADC is a MAX11125, 12-bit, 1 MSa/s. 11 LEDs and an MCP7940N RTC are on board.

Things you could build

16 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink and count with LEDs

    Write a small Verilog design that counts in binary across the eleven LEDs. It is a gentle first step into hardware design.

  • Button logic gate lab

    Link the seven buttons to the LEDs through gates you design. Students can build and test AND, OR and XOR by hand.

Builder ideas

  • Color bars on a monitor

    Draw a test pattern through the video port. It teaches how a screen is drawn line by line.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor or TV.

  • Retro computer clone

    Load a community core that recreates a classic home computer. Then play old games and type in old programs on your TV.

    Uses: Screen. Add: HDMI cable, monitor or TV, USB keyboard.

  • Chiptune synth

    Design a sound chip in logic and play bleepy tunes through the audio jack. Each button can play a different note.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: headphones or small powered speaker.

  • Program it over Wi-Fi

    Let the ESP32 load new designs onto the FPGA without a cable. It is handy for a board mounted out of reach.

    Uses: Wi-Fi.

  • Voltage logger for science class

    Read up to eight voltages with the analog inputs. Save the readings to the microSD card for later graphs.

    Add: microSD card, jumper wires.

  • Mini OLED dashboard

    Show live readings or a tiny animation on the small OLED screen. Use it as a status display on your desk.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small OLED display.

  • Servo puppet theater

    Generate precise pulses for several servos at once. Make a small puppet nod, wave, and turn its head.

    Add: servo motors, 5 V power supply, jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Arcade cabinet brain

    Run a retro arcade game core and wire real joysticks and buttons to the pins. Build it into a small wooden cabinet.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: HDMI cable, monitor, arcade joystick, arcade buttons.

  • Build a RISC-V computer

    Load a soft RISC-V core into the FPGA and run C programs on it. The large memory leaves room for bigger programs.

  • Classroom logic analyzer

    Turn the board into a tool that records fast digital signals. Students can inspect what other circuits are really doing.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Wireless garden monitor

    Read soil and temperature sensors through the pins. Send the readings to your phone over Wi-Fi.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: soil moisture sensor, temperature sensor.

  • Bluetooth game controller bridge

    Use the ESP32 to receive a Bluetooth controller. Pass the button presses to a game running on the FPGA.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen. Add: Bluetooth game controller, HDMI cable, monitor.

  • Sleepy timed wake-up project

    Use the clock chip and the low standby power to wake the board on a schedule. It can take a reading and drift back to sleep.

    Uses: Sips power. Add: battery pack.

  • Music visualizer

    Read sound levels with the analog inputs and draw moving bars on a monitor. Hang it near a speaker at a party.

    Uses: Screen. Add: small microphone module, HDMI cable, monitor.
