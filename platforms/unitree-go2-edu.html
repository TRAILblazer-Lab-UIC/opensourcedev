Overview

The Go2 EDU is the research and education version of the Unitree Go2 robot dog. It arrives fully built with twelve strong joint motors, a 4D LiDAR, cameras, and foot force sensors. Only the EDU versions let you run your own code on the robot. It suits university labs and advanced schools that study walking, navigation and robot learning.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Research on legged walking and reinforcement learning
  • Navigation and mapping projects with ROS 2
  • AI vision with the optional NVIDIA Jetson Orin module
  • Labs that want a working robot on day one with no build
  • Outdoor tests that use GPS and a 4G link

Not great for

  • Most budgets, with stores asking about 11,000 to 16,000 US dollars
  • People who want open hardware they can repair or remix
  • Beginners, as coding needs Linux and network setup
  • Cheaper Go2 versions, as the AIR and PRO cannot run your own code

Specs in plain words

  • The robot weighs about 15 kilograms, about the same as a beagle.
  • Twelve motors give three joints per leg, with about 45 Nm of peak force at each joint.
  • The 15,000 mAh battery runs the robot for about two to four hours.
  • The optional Jetson module adds 40 to 100 TOPS of AI speed for vision and speech models.
  • The LiDAR spins a laser around the robot and measures the distance to walls and objects.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and a 4G module with GPS keep it connected indoors and out.

Unitree lists 70 x 31 x 40 cm standing, 76 x 31 x 20 cm crouching and about 15 kg with battery. EDU payload is about 8 kg typical and 12 kg max. Unitree does not name the 8-core CPU or the exact Orin module. Resellers describe a 40 TOPS EDU (Orin Nano) and a 100 TOPS EDU Plus (Orin NX 16GB), and ai_tops uses the base EDU value. Unitree lists the L2 4D LiDAR while one reseller names the L1. The SDK connects to the robot over Ethernet at 192.168.123.x. The IMU, microphone and speaker are inferred from the voice and locomotion features and were not listed as parts.

Things you could build

13 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Walk it with high-level commands

    Send simple commands like stand, walk forward, and turn from a Python script. The robot handles its own balance.

    Uses: Wired network, Drives motors. Add: Ethernet cable.

  • Delivery dog

    Strap a small box on its back and send it across a lab. The EDU can carry about 8 kilograms.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: small cargo box, straps.

  • Wildlife camera walk

    Send the robot along a quiet path on battery power. It records video of birds and deer for later review.

    Uses: Camera, Battery ready, Drives motors.

  • Robot dance show

    Script a routine of steps and poses set to music. A class can program a group number for an event.

    Uses: Drives motors, Makes sound.

Expert ideas

  • Map a building with LiDAR

    Use the 4D LiDAR and ROS 2 tools to build a map of a hallway. View the map live on your laptop.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Wired network, Sensors built in.

  • Self-driving patrol route

    Mark points on a saved map and send the robot between them. It plans a path and steps around obstacles.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Person follower

    Run a vision model on the Jetson module to spot a person. The robot walks behind them at a safe distance.

    Uses: AI chip, Camera, Drives motors.

  • Train a new gait in simulation

    Train a walking policy with reinforcement learning on a computer. Then test it on the real robot with low-level motor control.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors.

  • Campus tour guide

    Lead visitors along a route and play short talks through the speaker. The GPS helps track the route outdoors.

    Uses: GPS, Makes sound, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Remote field scout

    Drive the robot over rough trails and stream camera views over 4G. Scientists can check a site from far away.

    Uses: Cellular, Camera, Battery ready, Drives motors.

  • Stair and slope study

    Log the foot force sensors as the robot climbs steps and ramps. Graph how the load shifts between legs.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Runs Linux.

  • Voice command demo

    Speak a command and have the robot sit, stand, or wave. Pair the microphone with a speech model on the Jetson.

    Uses: Hears sound, AI chip, Drives motors.

  • Network safety lesson

    Put the robot on its own isolated network and study what it sends. Students learn why robot security matters.

    Uses: Wired network, Wi-Fi, Runs Linux. Add: spare network router.

How open is it?

Open Code

The coding tools unitree_sdk2, unitree_sdk2_python, and unitree_ros2 are on GitHub under the BSD-3-Clause license, with open headers and examples. The core of the SDK ships as a prebuilt library without source. The hardware design, robot firmware, and motion controller are all closed.
