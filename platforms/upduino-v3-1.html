Overview

The UPduino v3.1 is a small FPGA board from tinyVision.ai. An FPGA is a chip you rewire with code to become almost any digital circuit. All its pins sit on a stick that fits a breadboard. It is a favorite in university logic classes. Both the board design and the tools are fully open.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning digital logic in class or at home
  • Building circuits with drag and drop blocks in IceStudio
  • Breadboard projects with buttons, LEDs and sensors
  • Studying a fully open board and tool flow

Not great for

  • Big designs, as the chip holds about 5,000 logic cells
  • Wireless projects, as it has no radio
  • Plug and play use, as the headers ship loose and need soldering
  • Powering hungry add-ons, as the 3.3 V pin should stay under 200 mA

Specs in plain words

  • 5,280 logic cells is room for small circuits or a very small processor.
  • 128 KB of fast memory inside the chip holds data for simple projects.
  • 4 MB of flash stores your design and keeps it after power goes off.
  • 32 pins on 0.1-inch headers plug straight into a breadboard.
  • A 12 MHz clock ticks twelve million times a second to keep circuits in step.

Specs from the GitHub README, the Lattice partner page, the tinyVision store page and LinuxGizmos. ram_kb is the 1 Mbit of single-port SRAM (SPRAM) inside the FPGA. The 120 Kbit of dual-port block RAM is extra. flash_kb is the 4 MB SPI flash. clock_mhz is the onboard 12 MHz oscillator. On v3.0 it was reached by a jumper. gpio is 32 per the README and Lattice. The Tindie listing says 39 GPIO, which likely counts the FT232H and LED pins brought out on the headers. The iCE40UP5K has hard I2C and SPI blocks. UART is soft logic or the FT232H bridge. The USB connector type is inferred from the Tindie note that a micro USB cable is not included. size_mm is from LinuxGizmos (2.2 x 6.2 x 0.5 cm). Project power from the 3.3 V pin should stay under 200 mA (Tindie).

Things you could build

12 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Blink the RGB LED

    Build a blink circuit in IceStudio with drag and drop blocks. Watch the LED cycle through colors.

  • Logic gate playground

    Wire buttons to LEDs through AND, OR and XOR blocks. Students see logic gates work in real hardware.

    Add: push buttons, LEDs, breadboard, jumper wires.

  • Traffic light model

    Build a traffic light controller that steps through red, yellow and green. Add a crosswalk button for extra fun.

    Add: LEDs, push button, breadboard, jumper wires.

Builder ideas

  • Dice roller

    Press a button to roll a random number from one to six. Show it on LEDs arranged like dice dots.

    Add: LEDs, push button, breadboard, jumper wires.

  • Seven segment counter

    Count button presses on a number display. It is a classic first step into counters and timers.

    Add: seven segment display module, push button.

  • Hello over the serial port

    Send a message from the FPGA to your computer through the USB chip. Watch it appear in a terminal.

  • Tone and melody maker

    Make square wave tones and play a short tune through a buzzer. Each button can play a different note.

    Add: small buzzer, push buttons.

  • Servo sweep controller

    Make a precise pulse that swings a servo back and forth. Use it to wave a flag or point at a sign.

    Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

  • LED strip light show

    Drive a strip of color LEDs with moving patterns. Light up a shelf or an art piece.

    Add: LED strip, 5 V power supply.

Expert ideas

  • Video pattern on a monitor

    Draw color bars on a VGA screen with a simple video add-on. It teaches how screens draw pictures line by line.

    Add: VGA PMOD module, VGA monitor.

  • Tiny RISC-V computer

    Fit a minimal soft RISC-V core into the chip. Run very small programs on a processor you built.

  • Garden temperature logger

    Read a temperature sensor over I2C and send readings to a computer. Track how warm your garden bed gets.

    Add: temperature sensor, jumper wires.

Breadboard
A breadboard is a plastic block full of small holes linked in rows.
CPU
The CPU is the main chip part that runs program steps.
Digital
A digital signal has only two states, on or off.
FPGA
An FPGA is a chip whose wiring you can rearrange with code.
