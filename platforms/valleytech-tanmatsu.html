Overview

Tanmatsu is an open pocket terminal from Nicolai Electronics in the Netherlands. It pairs an ESP32-P4 chip with a color screen, a QWERTY keyboard, and a built-in battery. Radios cover Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth LE, Thread or Zigbee, and LoRa for long-range mesh messages. It suits hackers, badge fans, and curious beginners who want a ready-made gadget to explore.

Specs in plain words

  • The ESP32-P4 chip has two cores and 32 MB of extra memory, enough for smooth graphics and small games.
  • The 800 by 480 screen is about 4 inches across, a bit smaller than a phone screen.
  • The ESP32-C6 helper chip handles Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth LE, and Thread or Zigbee.
  • The LoRa radio sends short messages for miles with an SMA antenna. Pick 868 MHz for Europe or 915 MHz for North America.
  • The 2500 mAh battery charges over USB-C.
  • The whole device is about 135 by 120 millimeters, close to the size of a paperback book.

The product page says 400 MHz. The Tanmatsu docs say 360 MHz, which may reflect early ESP32-P4 silicon limits. Battery is 2500 mAh per the docs and the 2026 Hackaday review. The 2025 CNX preview said 2000 mAh. The docs name an Ebyte E22-900M22S LoRa module (850 to 930 MHz). The CNX preview named Ai-Thinker Ra-01S modules, and the product page offers a 433 MHz option. CNX named the WCH CH32V203C8T6 housekeeping MCU for keyboard and power. Size and the 215 g weight are from CNX, with case and battery. The headers entry stands for the CATT side port (PMOD, SAO, JTAG) and the rear expansion port.
