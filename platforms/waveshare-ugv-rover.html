Overview

The UGV Rover is a six-wheel robot from Waveshare with a strong aluminum body. A Raspberry Pi runs the camera, web app, and AI demos. A second ESP32 board drives the motors and reads the sensors. It suits hobbyists and classrooms that want a ready robot for learning computer vision.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Driving a camera robot from any web browser
  • Learning computer vision with step-by-step notebooks
  • Indoor and patio exploring on its sturdy wheels
  • Moving on to ROS2 and LiDAR mapping later

Not great for

  • Tight budgets. Kits start around 245 dollars before batteries.
  • Learning from a huge community. Most help comes from Waveshare alone.
  • Using right out of the box. The three batteries are sold separately.
  • Rough off-road trips over rocks and deep grass

Specs in plain words

  • The Raspberry Pi is a small Linux computer. It can run Python, show a web page, and stream video at once.
  • The ESP32 is a helper board that keeps the motors steady. It takes orders from the Pi as short text messages.
  • The 5 megapixel camera sees 160 degrees wide. That is nearly everything in front of the rover.
  • Six 80 millimeter wheels are each about as wide as a coffee mug. Four of them are driven by motors.
  • The 9-axis motion sensor tracks tilt, turning, and direction. It helps the rover drive straight.
  • The rover weighs about 2 kilograms. That is close to a bag of sugar.

Host values are for the Pi 5 4GB kit. The Pi 4B kit uses a 1.8 GHz Cortex-A72, and Acce kits ship with no Pi. The host boots from microSD with a preinstalled Debian Bookworm image. The ESP32 module variant is not named on the fetched pages. The board is the Waveshare ROS Driver for Robots with a TB6612FNG motor driver. The product page gives up to 1.3 m/s. By contrast, CNX Software's 2024 article gives 0.5 m/s. Weight is about 1874 g or 2190 g depending on the version. The 254.53 mm height is for the pan-tilt version and 122.55 mm for the base version. The ESP32 also offers ESP-NOW and its own Wi-Fi web app.

Things you could build

15 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Living room explorer

    Drive the rover around the house from a browser on your phone. Watch the live camera view as it rolls.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Wi-Fi.

  • Pet check camera

    Send the rover to peek at the cat while you are in another room. The pan-tilt head lets you look around.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Wi-Fi.

  • Color chasing game

    Use the color tracking tutorial to follow a bright ball. Kids can lead the rover around with it.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: bright colored ball.

  • Face following buddy

    Turn on face tracking and let the camera head follow you. It feels like the robot is paying attention.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux.

Builder ideas

  • Tape line racer

    Lay a tape track on the floor and run the line tracking demo. Time each lap and tune it for speed.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: colored floor tape.

  • Hand gesture driver

    Use the gesture tutorial to steer the rover with hand signs. A thumbs up can mean go forward.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux.

  • Garden patrol

    Drive the rover along garden paths and check plants on camera. The shock-absorbing wheels handle small bumps.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Wi-Fi, Battery ready.

  • Classroom robot coding lab

    Students open the JupyterLab notebooks and change the vision code step by step. Each group shows off its own behavior.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Camera.

  • Battery health dashboard

    Show the battery level and tilt readings on a web page. You can see how far a charge really goes.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready, Runs Linux.

  • Status screen messages

    Write short Python code to show your own messages on the small screen. It can greet visitors by name.

    Uses: Screen, Runs Linux.

  • Science sensor cart

    Plug a USB sensor into the Pi and log readings around a building. A science club can map warm and cool spots.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: USB temperature sensor.

  • Wired lab rover

    Connect the Pi over an Ethernet cable for steady access during testing. It helps on a busy school network.

    Uses: Wired network, Runs Linux. Add: Ethernet cable.

Expert ideas

  • Security round robot

    Program a short patrol loop and save photos at each stop. Check the pictures on your computer later.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux.

  • Room mapping with LiDAR

    Mount a LiDAR sensor on the plate and run ROS2 to draw a map of a room. The rover can then plan its own path.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: LiDAR sensor.

  • Object spotting with AI

    Run an object detection model on the Pi and announce what the camera sees. It can spot cups, chairs, and people.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux.

How open is it?

Open Board

The Raspberry Pi app and the ESP32 firmware are on GitHub under the GPL-3.0 license. Waveshare shares a driver board schematic and 3D chassis models. No hardware license is stated and the PCB layout files are not posted. The chips and the Wi-Fi firmware stay closed.
