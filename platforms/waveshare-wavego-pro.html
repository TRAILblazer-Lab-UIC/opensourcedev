Overview

The WAVEGO Pro is a four-legged robot dog from Waveshare with twelve moving joints. An ESP32 board handles walking and balance, and it serves a control page over its own Wi-Fi hotspot. You can add a Raspberry Pi and camera for face tracking, color tracking, and gesture control. It suits families, clubs, and classrooms looking for a working robot on day one.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Driving a walking robot from a phone or laptop browser with no code
  • Teaching how legs, servos, and balance sensors work together
  • Vision projects with a Raspberry Pi and camera on the EX or PI5 kits
  • Recording custom poses and dance moves
  • Tinkering with walking code in Arduino or Python

Not great for

  • Tight budgets. The base kit costs about 243 dollars.
  • Vision projects on the BASIC kit, which has no camera
  • Rebuilding or repairing the circuit board, as no schematic is published
  • Heavy lifting. The small servos carry only light loads.

Specs in plain words

  • Twelve smart servos move the legs and report their position, speed, and voltage.
  • The nine-axis motion sensor feels tilt and turning, and it keeps the body level.
  • Two rechargeable 18650 cells hold 5200 mAh for untethered walks.
  • The robot weighs about 0.7 kilograms and is about as long as a large smartphone is tall.
  • The optional Raspberry Pi 5 adds enough computing power for live face and gesture detection.

Values are for the ESP32 sub-controller in the BASIC kit. The 520 KB SRAM is the ESP32-D0WDQ6 chip figure, and module flash was not stated. The PI5 KIT adds a built-in Raspberry Pi 5 4GB, and the EX kit is for a user-supplied Pi 4B or Pi 5. The camera is only in the EX and PI5 kits. Size is the BASIC footprint from CNX Software. The EX and PI5 kits are 179.6 x 70.27 mm. The product page gives a weight of 0.722 kg. Servo locked-rotor torque reaches 5.2 kg-cm. The body is 5052 aluminum and PA12 nylon.

Starter ideas

  • Browser-driven robot pet

    Join the robot's own Wi-Fi hotspot and steer it from a phone browser. It walks, turns, and poses with no code.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Living room obstacle course

    Build a course from books and boxes. Friends take turns steering the dog through it.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors.

  • Self-balancing demo

    Turn on balance mode and tilt the floor under the robot. The motion sensor keeps its body level.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Drives motors. Add: tray or board to tilt.

Builder ideas

  • Recorded dance routine

    Record a string of poses as an action file. The robot plays the dance back on cue for a school show.

    Uses: Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

  • Battery health screen

    Show battery voltage and a friendly name on the small OLED. Owners know when to charge.

    Uses: Screen, Battery ready, Sensors built in.

  • Two-robot remote mode

    Pair a second ESP32 board as a handheld controller with ESP-NOW. Drive the dog with no Wi-Fi router.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Drives motors. Add: ESP32 board, joystick module.

  • Face-following pet

    Add a Raspberry Pi and camera. The dog turns to face anyone who walks into the room.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, camera module.

  • Color ball chaser

    The camera tracks a bright colored ball. The dog walks toward it during a game of fetch.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, camera module, colored ball.

  • Home patrol camera

    Send the dog on a walk around the house. It streams video to a browser page.

    Uses: Camera, Wi-Fi, Drives motors, Battery ready. Add: Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, camera module.

  • Motion alarm sentry

    The dog sits still and watches with its camera. It flashes its lights once something moves.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, camera module.

  • Robotics class mascot

    Use the dog to teach how joints, sensors, and code work together. Each group adds one new trick.

    Uses: Drives motors, Wi-Fi.

Expert ideas

  • Hand gesture commands

    Wave or point at the camera. The dog sits, walks, or turns based on the gesture.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors. Add: Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, camera module.

  • Custom gait lab

    Change leg timing in the ESP32 code and test new walking styles. Students compare speed and stability.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Bluetooth gamepad control

    Write code to read a Bluetooth controller on the ESP32. Drive the dog like a video game character.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Drives motors. Add: Bluetooth gamepad.
