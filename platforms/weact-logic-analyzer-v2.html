Overview

The WeAct Logic Analyzer V2 is a small USB box that records digital signals from up to sixteen wires. A free PulseView-based app decodes UART, I2C, SPI, CAN, and many more protocols. A button switches it into a USB serial adapter and debug probe. It costs about 10 dollars and suits makers who need to see what their circuits are really saying.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Finding out why a sensor or screen will not talk to your board
  • Decoding serial, I2C, and SPI messages into readable values
  • Showing students what digital signals look like
  • Serving as a three-port USB serial adapter and SWD debug probe

Not great for

  • Mac users. The app has no macOS version.
  • Analog signals such as audio waves, which need an oscilloscope
  • Very fast signals. A reviewer estimated usable bandwidth near 50 MHz.
  • People who want fully open firmware, as the main analyzer code is closed

Specs in plain words

  • Sixteen channels let you watch sixteen wires at the same time.
  • A 200 MHz sample rate takes 200 million snapshots each second on every wire.
  • USB 3.0 streams the data to your computer for about 45 seconds per capture at full speed.
  • The input level adjusts from 1.0 to 3.3 volts to match most hobby boards.
  • The box is about the size of a matchbox.

The brain is a WCH CH32H417. Chip values come from CNX and the CH32H417 card. The V3F core runs at 144 MHz per CNX. The 16 GPIO are the D0 to D15 terminals on a 20-pin header with 4 ground pins. The USB-C port wires straight to the chip's USB 3.0 SuperSpeed port. Sample rate is 200 MHz. A CNX commenter estimated the usable signal bandwidth at about 50 MHz. Flash is split into a 24 KB IAP bootloader, three 304 KB app slots and a 24 KB config area. Size includes the enclosure. JTAG and SWO are not supported by the DAP app.

Things you could build

11 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Starter ideas

  • Find a broken sensor wire

    Clip the probes onto the data lines of an I2C sensor. The app shows whether the sensor ever answers.

    Add: jumper wires, I2C sensor.

  • Read serial messages

    Watch the UART messages a board sends. The decoder turns the pulses into readable text.

    Add: jumper wires.

  • Signals lesson for class

    Show students what a button press or a blinking light looks like as a signal. The screen draws each high and low.

    Add: jumper wires, breadboard, button, LED.

Builder ideas

  • Check servo pulse timing

    Measure the control pulses sent to a servo. See why a robot arm twitches or drifts.

    Add: servo motor, jumper wires.

  • Decode a TV remote

    Connect an infrared receiver and press buttons on an old remote. The app shows each button's pulse pattern.

    Add: infrared receiver, jumper wires, TV remote.

  • Debug an LED strip

    Capture the fast data signal going into an LED strip. Spot timing errors that cause wrong colors.

    Add: LED strip, jumper wires.

  • Spy on a screen bus

    Record the SPI traffic to a small display. Learn how a picture gets sent one byte at a time.

    Add: small SPI screen, jumper wires.

  • Three-port serial adapter

    Switch to the second firmware slot. Talk to three boards at once over USB serial ports.

    Add: jumper wires.

Expert ideas

  • Flash a microcontroller over SWD

    Use the built-in CMSIS-DAP probe to load and debug code on an Arm board.

    Add: Arm microcontroller board, jumper wires.

  • Retro game controller study

    Probe the wires of an old game controller. Decode how it reports button presses.

    Add: old game controller, jumper wires.

  • Write your own slot 2 app

    Load your own program into the third firmware slot. The RISC-V chip becomes a small dev board.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 1 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansNot published
  • Hardware licenseNot confirmed
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The host app is a GPL-3.0 fork of sigrok PulseView with full source. The firmware upgrade tool and DAPFlasher app are also GPL-3.0. The analyzer firmware ships only as closed binary files, and no schematic or hardware license is published. The repo includes a 3D model of the board and case, and the third slot runs your own code.
