Overview

The PGB-1 is a pocket groovebox, synthesizer, and MIDI sequencer from Wee Noise Makers in France. It has eight sound tracks, a sampler, a small screen, 30 keys, a speaker, a microphone, and a battery. It is ready to make music out of the box. Its firmware is open, and coders can write their own in C, C++, Rust, Ada, or CircuitPython.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Making beats and songs on the go with no computer
  • Sampling sounds with the built-in microphone
  • Driving other synths through MIDI in and out
  • Musicians who want to hack their own instrument firmware

Not great for

  • Tight budgets. It costs about 275 dollars or more.
  • Studio work that needs many tracks or long samples
  • Beginner coders hoping to start in the stock firmware, as Ada is rare

Specs in plain words

  • The RP2040 chip has two cores at up to 133 MHz for synth voices and the sequencer.
  • 16 MB of storage holds the firmware and your samples.
  • The sequencer has 16 tracks with 16 patterns of 16 steps each.
  • The 850 mAh battery gives about five hours of play.
  • The case is about 10 by 6.6 centimeters, close to the size of a phone.

Flash is the W25Q128 (16 MB) on the Noise Nugget 2040 schematic. The audio codec is a TLV320AIC3105. The main schematic shows a STEMMA QT I2C connector and an SWD debug port. Clock is the RP2040 rated speed. The firmware clock was not confirmed. Size is from Synth Anatomy (about 10 x 6.6 x 1.5 cm). MIDI is on 3.5 mm TRS jacks.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansSchematics only
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsFully open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The stock firmware is open source under GPL-3.0. The main board schematic and the Noise Nugget 2040 module schematic are published under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. The front panel has KiCad files and Gerbers, but the main board layout is not public. The RP2040 chip is closed, though its toolchain is fully open.
