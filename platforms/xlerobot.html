Overview

XLeRobot is an open design for a two-armed household robot. It puts two SO-101 arms and a camera head on an IKEA cart with a rolling base. It runs on the Hugging Face LeRobot software from a laptop. It suits makers and students who want a low-cost robot for learning home chores.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning how robots learn tasks from human demos
  • Makers who own a 3D printer and enjoy a long build
  • University and club research on two-armed robots
  • Driving a robot with a game controller or VR headset
  • Testing ideas in simulation before building

Not great for

  • Beginners with no Python, Ubuntu, or Git practice
  • Homes with thick rugs or bumpy floors. The wheels need a very flat floor.
  • Heavy lifting. Each arm carries about 600 to 1000 grams.
  • People who want a robot that works out of the box

Specs in plain words

  • The robot has no computer of its own. A laptop runs the software over USB cables.
  • It uses 17 smart servo motors. Twelve move the arms, three drive the base, and two turn the head.
  • Each arm reaches about 40 centimeters. That is about the length of a forearm and hand.
  • The robot weighs about 12 kilograms. That is close to a full suitcase.
  • A portable power station runs it for 10 hours or more per charge.
  • The basic parts cost about 660 dollars before printing, tools and shipping.

The robot has no processor of its own. The basic build tethers to a laptop. A Raspberry Pi 5 is a $79 add-on. The docs give about 12 kg weight, about 40 cm reach per arm and 600 to 1000 g payload per arm. The 17 servos are 12 for the arms, 3 for the base and 2 for the head. The STS3250 is an optional higher-torque upgrade. Cameras are two USB wrist cameras plus a single RGB, stereo RGB or RealSense D415 head camera. Overall dimensions were not found on a fetched page.

Things you could build

14 ideas sized to what this board can do. Starter ideas suit first-time builders.

Builder ideas

  • Game controller robot drive

    Drive the cart and move both arms with an Xbox controller. It makes a fun first demo for family and friends.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: game controller.

  • Try it in simulation first

    Open the MuJoCo simulation or the browser demo and drive a virtual XLeRobot. You can practice before you print a single part.

  • Snack delivery cart

    Drive the robot to a friend and hand over a snack with one arm. The cart shelves carry the extras.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux.

  • Two-handed block building

    Use both arms to pass blocks and stack them. It shows how two hands work together on one job.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: wooden blocks.

  • Robot camera tour

    Drive around the house and stream the head camera to your laptop. Friends can see the robot's point of view.

    Uses: Camera, Drives motors, Runs Linux.

Expert ideas

  • VR remote helper

    Wear a VR headset and steer the arms with your hands. The robot copies your moves across the room.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: VR headset.

  • Laundry basket loader

    Record demos of picking up clothes and dropping them in a basket. Train a policy to tidy the floor on its own.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: laundry basket.

  • Table wiping practice

    Teach the arms to push a cloth across a table. Count how many demos it needs to learn the job.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: cleaning cloth.

  • Watering the houseplants

    Teach one arm to tip a small watering can over a pot. The robot rolls from plant to plant.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: small watering can.

  • University robot learning lab

    Students collect demos with the arms and train their own policies. The class compares which tasks the robot learns best.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Talking chore robot

    Train a vision-language-action policy and type a short instruction. The robot tries to do the chore you asked for.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: computer with a strong graphics card.

  • Toy cleanup game

    Scatter soft toys on the floor and teach the robot to collect them. Kids can race it to see who tidies faster.

    Uses: Drives motors, Camera, Runs Linux. Add: toy bin.

  • Depth camera upgrade

    Swap the head camera for a stereo or depth camera. The robot can judge how far away objects sit.

    Uses: Camera, Runs Linux. Add: stereo or depth camera.

  • Onboard Raspberry Pi brain

    Add a Raspberry Pi to the cart and cut the laptop cord. The robot can roll farther on its own.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: single-board computer.

How open is it?

Wild Open

The 3D-printed parts, CAD files, and simulation models are free on GitHub under the Apache-2.0 license. The LeRobot software is also Apache-2.0. The servos, driver boards, and cameras are closed off-the-shelf parts with closed firmware.
