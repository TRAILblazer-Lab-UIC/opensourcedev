Overview

The ROSMASTER X3 is a robot car kit from Yahboom for learning ROS, the Robot Operating System. It rolls on four mecanum wheels that can drive in any direction. You pick a Raspberry Pi 5 or an NVIDIA Jetson as its brain. Higher kits add a LiDAR scanner and a depth camera for mapping and vision.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Learning ROS 1 and ROS 2 with ready courses
  • SLAM mapping and self-driving navigation
  • Robot vision with OpenCV and MediaPipe
  • University labs and advanced high school robotics clubs

Not great for

  • Tight budgets, as kits start around 659 US dollars without a host board
  • Total beginners to Linux
  • Custom setups, as Yahboom supports only its own courses and images
  • Long runs, as the battery lasts about 1.5 hours on the Pi version

Specs in plain words

  • Mecanum wheels have angled rollers that let the robot slide sideways like a crab.
  • A Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB of memory is a small Linux computer that runs ROS and the camera.
  • The STM32 expansion board drives the four motors and reads the motion sensor.
  • The 9-axis IMU senses tilt, turning, and direction.
  • A 12.6 volt battery with 6,000 mAh powers the whole robot for about an hour and a half.
  • The LiDAR spins a laser to measure distances all around the robot.

Host values are for the Raspberry Pi 5 8GB version (quad Cortex-A76 at 2.4 GHz), the cheapest host option. Jetson versions differ. Kits are sold with or without the host board. The Standard kit lacks the LiDAR and depth camera. Advanced adds an RPLIDAR A1 and Astra Pro Plus. Battery runtime is about 1.5 hours for the Pi version per openelab. One manual snippet says 12 V 6000 mAh. OLED, light bar and voice module are listed in the Advanced kit contents. Net dimensions and weight were not published on fetched pages. The shipping box is about 39.2 x 25.8 x 21.8 cm per a retailer snippet.

Starter ideas

Builder ideas

  • Map your home with LiDAR

    Drive the robot through each room and build a floor map with SLAM. Save the map for later trips.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: LiDAR sensor.

  • Robot butler for the kitchen

    Click a spot on the saved map and let the robot drive there alone. Carry a snack tray from the kitchen to the couch.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: LiDAR sensor, small tray.

  • Follow the line race

    Tape a track on the floor and have the robot follow it with its camera. Hold timed races in a classroom.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: depth camera, colored tape.

  • Person follower

    Have the robot spot a person and stay a few steps behind. It can carry a light load down the hall.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: depth camera.

  • Talk to your robot

    Give spoken commands such as "go forward" or "turn left" through the voice module. Hear the robot answer back.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Drives motors. Add: voice interaction module.

  • Gesture-controlled rover

    Use MediaPipe pose detection to steer with hand signals. Wave left and the robot turns left.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: depth camera.

  • Garden path patrol

    Map a patio or greenhouse aisle and send the robot on a loop. Log temperature and humidity at each stop.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Drives motors, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: LiDAR sensor, temperature and humidity sensor.

  • Tilt and bump science lab

    Read the 9-axis IMU while the robot drives over ramps and bumps. Graph the tilt and shaking for a physics lesson.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Runs Linux. Add: small ramp.

Expert ideas

  • Tune the motor control

    Adjust the PID settings on the STM32 board and watch the speed curve change. It shows how robots hold a steady speed.

    Uses: Drives motors, Sensors built in.

  • Library book finder

    Teach the robot to recognize book covers or colored tags. Have it drive to the shelf with the right one.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: depth camera, printed tags.

  • LLM voice assistant on wheels

    Connect the voice module to a large language model on a Jetson host. Ask a question and the robot answers out loud.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Hears sound, Makes sound, Wi-Fi. Add: voice interaction module.

  • Robot hide and seek

    Have the robot explore unknown rooms on its own and mark open floor on a map. Play a game of finding a hidden toy.

    Uses: Runs Linux, Plugs in USB gadgets, Drives motors. Add: LiDAR sensor, depth camera.

  • Add a robot arm

    Use the serial servo ports to mount a small arm on top. Pick up blocks and sort them by color.

    Uses: Drives motors, Runs Linux. Add: serial servo arm, depth camera.

  • Two-robot traffic test

    Run two robots on one ROS 2 network and keep them from bumping. It is a simple start in robot teamwork.

    Uses: Wi-Fi, Runs Linux, Drives motors. Add: second robot kit.
