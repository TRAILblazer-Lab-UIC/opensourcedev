Overview

ZSWatch is an open-source smartwatch built from scratch on the Zephyr real-time system. It uses a Nordic nRF5340 chip with Bluetooth and a 1.28-inch round touch screen. Most people start with the WatchDK, a larger development board with the same electronics. It suits embedded developers who want a watch they can fully read, change, and rebuild.

Specs in plain words

  • Two 128 MHz cores split the work, with one core running your app and the other handling Bluetooth.
  • 512 KB of memory is room for a watch app, its screen buffer, and a few sensor logs.
  • 64 MB of extra flash stores pictures, fonts, and watch faces.
  • The 240 by 240 pixel round screen is about the size of a large coin.
  • The nPM1300 chip charges a small battery and manages power.
  • The watch board is 38 millimeters across, a little wider than a quarter.

Specs are from the README and the hardware overview page. The watch and the WatchDK share the same electronics. clock_mhz is the 128 MHz app core. flash_kb is internal flash. A 64 MB Macronix MX25U51245G QSPI flash holds images and XIP code. The display uses a GC9A01 driver with capacitive touch. A vibration motor and an RV-8263 RTC are on board. The BMP581 pressure sensor and LIS2MDL magnetometer do not fit the onboard vocabulary. Connectors and headers listed are for the WatchDK, which has breakout headers, a 10-pin SWD header, power-measurement headers and an I2S speaker header. The watch routes SWD, reset and UART out through its USB-C port, reached with the Minimal Dock board. A speaker needs the HR extension board, which is still in development.

Builder ideas

  • Design your own watch face

    Draw a custom clock face with your colors and favorite fonts. Flash it to the round touch screen.

    Uses: Screen.

  • Phone notifications on your wrist

    Pair the watch with Gadgetbridge or the ZSWatch app. Read texts and calendar alerts on the round screen.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

  • Step counter and activity log

    Use the motion sensor to count steps through the day. Show a daily bar chart on the watch.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Pocket compass for hikes

    Read the magnetometer and draw a compass needle on the screen. Take it on a nature walk.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Altitude and weather tracker

    Log air pressure from the pressure sensor every few minutes. Watch for falling pressure before a storm.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Screen.

  • Music remote control

    Use the touch screen to pause, skip, and change volume on your phone's music app. Leave the phone in your bag.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Screen.

Expert ideas

  • Stair climbing counter

    Combine pressure and motion readings to count floors climbed. Compare totals with friends at school.

    Uses: Sensors built in.

  • Sleep and light diary

    Record wrist movement and room light through the night. Graph your sleep pattern in the morning.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Battery ready. Add: small LiPo battery.

  • Voice memo watch

    Record short voice notes with the onboard microphone. Store them in the 64 MB flash and send them to your phone later.

    Uses: Hears sound, Bluetooth.

  • Fall alert for a grandparent

    Detect a sudden fall with the motion sensor and buzz the watch. Send an alert to a paired phone.

    Uses: Sensors built in, Bluetooth.

  • Gesture remote for slides

    Flick your wrist to move to the next slide in a presentation. The watch acts as a Bluetooth controller.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in.

  • Build a wearable watch

    Order the watch circuit boards and print the case. Assemble a watch you can wear and repair yourself.

    Uses: Battery ready. Add: watch circuit boards, 3D printed case, small LiPo battery, watch strap.

  • Learn Zephyr on the devkit

    Use the WatchDK to practice drivers, threads, and Bluetooth in Zephyr. Each sensor makes a small lab exercise.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Sensors built in. Add: debugger, SWD cable.

How open is it?

Openness level

Open CodeOpen BoardWild Open

Level 2 of 3. Wild Open shares the most.

  • Board plansFull design files
  • Hardware licenseOpen license
  • Coding toolsMostly open
  • The chip itselfClosed chip design

The watch, devkit, dock, and extension boards are all published as KiCad files under GPL-3.0. The case is shared as STEP and STL files, and the Zephyr firmware is GPL-3.0 too. The nRF5340 chip is closed, and the setup also uses closed Nordic and SEGGER tools.
