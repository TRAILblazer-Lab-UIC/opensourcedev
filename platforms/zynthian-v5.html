Overview

Zynthian V5 is an open synth workstation kit built around a Raspberry Pi. It works as a sound module, effects unit, MIDI router, groovebox, and small recording studio. The current V5.1 kit puts a Pi 5 in an aluminum case with a touchscreen, four knobs, and 20 lit buttons. It suits musicians who like open tools and want one box for many jobs.

Good fit or not?

Good for

  • Guitar and vocal effects with free plugins
  • Looping, sequencing, and multitrack recording
  • Connecting older MIDI gear through DIN ports

Not great for

  • Buyers in a hurry, as the shop showed it not available for sale
  • US buyers, as it ships only from Spain
  • People who want a ready-made box, as you assemble the kit yourself
  • Tight budgets, as the kit plus a Pi costs over 500 euros

Specs in plain words

  • A Raspberry Pi 5 with 4 or 8 GB of memory runs many synths and effects at once.
  • The Burr-Brown audio chips record and play at 24-bit and 192 kHz, which is studio quality.
  • Balanced 6.35 mm jacks are the big quarter-inch plugs found on studio gear.
  • DIN MIDI in, thru, and out ports talk to older keyboards and drum machines.
  • The 5-inch touchscreen is about the size of a small phone screen.
  • The power input takes 9 to 48 volts, which covers most adapters musicians already own.

Starter ideas

  • Sound module for a keyboard

    Plug a MIDI keyboard into the Zynthian and pick a piano, organ, or synth sound. It turns a plain controller into a full instrument.

    Uses: Plugs in USB gadgets, Makes sound. Add: USB MIDI keyboard, headphones or speakers.

  • Guitar effects pedalboard

    Run your guitar through reverb, delay, and distortion plugins. Save each chain as a preset for a song.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: guitar, instrument cable, amplifier or speakers.

  • Live looper for jam nights

    Record a phrase and loop it while you play over the top. Layer drums, bass, and melody one at a time.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound. Add: microphone or instrument, audio cables.

  • Step sequencer beats

    Build drum patterns and bass lines with the step sequencer and the 20 lit buttons. Chain patterns into a full song.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: headphones or speakers.

  • Classroom music lab

    Let students try different synth engines and hear how each one shapes sound. Compare a sine wave, a sawtooth, and a sampled piano.

    Uses: Screen, Makes sound. Add: USB MIDI keyboard, headphones.

Builder ideas

  • Multitrack home recorder

    Record vocals, guitar, and synths on separate tracks. Mix them into a demo without a laptop.

    Uses: Hears sound, Makes sound, Plugs in USB gadgets. Add: microphone, audio cables, USB drive.

  • MIDI hub for old gear

    Use the DIN ports to route notes between vintage synths and drum machines. Add MIDI filters to split or transpose parts.

    Add: DIN MIDI cables, older synths or drum machines.

  • Play from a phone or tablet

    Open the Zynthian web interface over Wi-Fi to tweak presets and manage files. Adjust sounds from across the room.

    Uses: Wi-Fi. Add: phone or tablet.

  • Ambient sound garden

    Set up slow evolving synth pads and a long reverb for a gallery or garden party. Leave it playing for hours.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: powered speakers.

  • Wireless MIDI controller setup

    Pair a Bluetooth MIDI controller and play the synths with no cables. It works well on a small stage.

    Uses: Bluetooth, Makes sound. Add: Bluetooth MIDI controller.

  • Choir practice backing tracks

    Program accompaniment parts and play them back with the sequencer. Singers can practice with a steady beat.

    Uses: Makes sound. Add: powered speakers.

Expert ideas

  • Network jam between rooms

    Connect two units over Ethernet and send MIDI between them. Two players can share one groove.

  • Write your own plugin
