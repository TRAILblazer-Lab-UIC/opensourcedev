Radios Explained

Many boards have a built-in radio. A radio lets your gadget talk with no wires. Each kind of radio trades distance, speed, and battery life.

RadioHow far it reachesHow much it can sendBattery lifeGood for
Wi-FiAcross a houseA lot, even videoShortSending data to the internet
Bluetooth Low EnergyAcross a roomA littleLongTalking to a phone

Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi joins your home or school network. It can reach the internet. It sends lots of data fast. It also drains batteries quickly. Most Wi-Fi projects plug into the wall.

Bluetooth Low Energy

Bluetooth Low Energy talks to phones, tablets, and laptops nearby. It uses very little power. It is great for wearables, game controllers, and fitness trackers.

Thread and Zigbee

Thread and Zigbee build mesh networks for smart homes. Each device passes messages to the next one. A message can hop across the whole house. Many smart lights and sensors use them.

LoRa

LoRa stands for long range. A LoRa radio sends tiny messages over miles. It needs very little power. A sensor in a field can report for a year on a small battery. LoRa sends short notes only. It cannot carry photos or video.

Mesh networks and Meshtastic

A mesh network has no central tower. Every radio passes messages along to its neighbors. The network grows stronger with each new radio.

Meshtastic is a free, open-source project that builds a text-message mesh on LoRa radios. You pair a small radio with your phone over Bluetooth. Then you can text friends miles away with no cell service. Hikers, campers, and emergency volunteers use it. Many cheap boards run Meshtastic with no coding.

A note on rules

Radios must follow local laws. LoRa uses different frequencies in different countries. The US uses 915 MHz. Europe uses 868 MHz. Buy the version made for your region.
